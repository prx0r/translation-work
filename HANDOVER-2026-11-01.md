### 9. The rasa-carrying principle

Rasa in L2 is not decoration. Each word choice must be justified by the verse's rasa:
if the verse is about longing, the language is yearning-weighted; if about violence,
abrupt; if about beauty, sensuous; if about surrender, yielding; if about wonder, opening.
"This word sounds better" is not a reason. "This word evokes the rasa the Sanskrit evokes,
because [specific reason tied to the sthAyibhAva]" is a reason.

Before/after examples (from ghatakarpara):
- "The cātaka, thirsty, begs water." → "The cātaka, gasping, begs for rain — and nothing else."
  (gasping > thirsty (desperation); rain > water (cātaka drinks only rain); "and nothing else"
  carries the singularity of the thirst)
- "Fallen into sorrow-ocean's water; remembrance protects her." → "Drowned in sorrow-ocean's
  water; remembrance keeps her afloat." (drowned > fallen (more violent); keeps her afloat >
  protects (more visceral))
- "Kāma arises in the heart, to kill." → "In the heart, Kāma wakes to kill." (wakes > arises
  (more sudden); brevity = violence)

### 10. The metacommentary lesson (the big mistake)

I wrote C1 as a literary essay about the poem ("The poem's argument is...", "The passage
performs...", "The close is aesthetic...") instead of stating the matters ("Kāma arises in
the heart...", "Clouds thundering make travelers long..."). The mechanical gate (regex for
"the verse has/describes/opens/closes...") passed 3 times while the C1 was saturated with
document-reference. The broad scan (for "the poem/passage/text/song/work", "the lament/
beauty/close", "first/middle/final passage", "reader knows") finally caught it. Three passes
to strip all document-reference. The lesson: the gate is a proxy, not the rule. When they
disagree, the rule wins. Always read the output against the rule, not just the gate.

### 26. The key insight about argmaps

Argmaps don't claim "this is what's happening." They map the argument with certainty/
uncertainty clearly marked. But even marking certainty/uncertainty gives rise to error.
The most honest thing: construct the argument from the verse with references to scholars
to support sharp lines. The argmap is a map, not a scholarly essay. Scholarship lives in
lore/; the argmap is the logical skeleton. The verse either resolves or it doesn't — state
what it does not resolve, don't label it "uncertain."

### 27. The key insight about rasa

Rasa in L2 is not decoration. Each word choice must be justified by the verse's rasa.
"This word sounds better" is not a reason. "This word evokes the rasa the Sanskrit
evokes, because [specific reason tied to the sthAyibhAva]" is a reason. The rasa is in
the English word choice and rhythm, not in a commentary about it. The gap between
"conveying meaning" and "evoking rasa" is real and not fully bridgeable by word swaps
alone. The C1 essay carries the rasa in the argument; the L2 carries it in the line.

### 28. The key insight about the concept database

The concept database grows as works are transformed. It does not need to be complete
before translation begins. Each C1's KEY TERMS feeds it. build_concepts.py reports what's
missing. lookup_concept.py --create fetches from the glossary index. Enrich with sources,
rasa, works, related terms. The graph emerges from the work — it's not built upfront.

### 29. The key insight about the glossary

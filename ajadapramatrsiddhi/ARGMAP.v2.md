# Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi — ARGMAP v2 (pilot: real argument map)

## ajad-p01 (vv. 1–9) — The inert grounds nothing

**Speaker:** Utpaladeva, siddha voice (establishing the knower as non-inert).

**Question:** Can the inert (jaḍa) ground anything? What really distinguishes the existent (sat) from the nonexistent (asat)?

**Premises:**
1. (v1) No difference between real/unreal in own-nature — likewise none between inert/non-inert. Certain.
2. (v2) Appearing (prakhyā) and describing (upākhyā), existence and its opposite do not touch a thing's essence; that essence faces the knower (jñātṛgāmi).
3. (v3) A thing's self cannot be "being-an-object-of-appearing" — that would depend on another, lack single form, overstep existence.
4. (v4) Granted no difference: still, from presence/absence of appearing, a difference gets established — and that much serves the purpose.
5. (v5) Existence is fitting only as tied to appearing; without that reference, existent and nonexistent are indistinguishable.
6. (v6) Objection: the existent performs a function (arthakriyā), the other does not — yet the thing in itself stays distinctionless.
7. (v7) The existent, conditioned by appearing-as-functional, exists by own essence — yet is not distinguished from the nonexistent.
8. (v8) Function is not described in what is already established as existent; the two are on par — so grounding rests on consciousness (saṃvidāśrayā pratiṣṭhā).
9. (v9) If consciousness itself lacked self-reflection (aparāmarśa), it too would equal inert non-being.

**Inference:** Every candidate ground (existence, function, appearing) collapses into consciousness-dependence; and consciousness without parāmarśa is no better than the inert. Only self-reflective consciousness grounds.

**Conclusion:** The inert grounds nothing; pratiṣṭhā rests on saṃvit alone, and only insofar as saṃvit is self-reflective.

**Unresolved:** The mechanism by which parāmarśa confers grounding — deferred to the vimarśa doctrine (taken up in p02).

## ajad-p02 (vv. 10–18) — The "I" that grounds

**Speaker:** Utpaladeva, continuing.

**Question:** How does consciousness ground another thing's manifestation? What is the "I"?

**Premises:**
1. (v10) What is itself ungrounded cannot be the ground of another's establishment.
2. (v11) Consciousness, manifesting fitly as "this" (idam), attains grounding-nature for another — and again, by itself, as "I" (aham).
3. (v12) By exclusion (vyatireka) and inclusion (itara) alike, certainty is called: fixed-order (vyavasthiti), foundation (pratiṣṭhā), attainment (siddhi), completion (nirvṛtti) — of the other inert selves.
4. (v13) Inert things, almost unreal in themselves, exist only as light's own; light alone exists — through selves own and other.
5. (v14) No experience apart from certainty of distinct/indistinct, apart from connected light without reflection.
6. (v15) The cut-off "this"-reflection finds fulfillment in rest in own essence; that reflection — "I am this" — is just this.
7. (v16) The same self is twofold: measured (mīta, confined by breath etc. — the atom, aṇu) and unmeasured (aparimita — the undivided supreme self).
8. (v17) Of both, this one alone is the ultimate ground of all object-consciousnesses, through continuity (anusaṃdhāna) — no other is possible.
9. (v18) In the end-residing I-consciousness of non-separation, nothing more inert than manifestation appears.

**Inference:** Grounding runs through the idam/aham polarity resolved in continuity; exclusion and inclusion together yield the fourfold certainty; the limited and unlimited self are one continuum.

**Conclusion:** The self — twofold yet one through anusaṃdhāna — is the sole ground; the inert selves derive their entire order (vyavasthiti through nirvṛtti) from it.

**Unresolved:** None major; the breath-body locus of the limited self is taken up in p03.

## ajad-p03 (vv. 19–27) — Breath is the known, not the knower

**Speaker:** Utpaladeva, concluding.

**Question:** Are breath, body, the eightfold city the knower? What is Māyā? What is the world?

**Premises:**
1. (v19) Breath etc., though joined with knowledge, are not established by non-manifestation; they are objects (prameya), not the knower — else delusion.
2. (v20) Though things stand confined in the living being (breath, eightfold city), even there they abide in the supreme self.
3. (v21) Restraint by breath cannot establish that self-nature; in recognition (pratyabhijñā), the living being is supreme over non-manifestation.
4. (v22) The ordering of things is not in breath etc. via I-restraint; rest of manifestation in itself is proclaimed as I-ness.
5. (v23) That rest — restraint of all dependence — is autonomy (svātantrya), principal authorship (kartṛtva), lordship (īśvaratā).
6. (v24) Named Māyā, it is of his power alone (no other established); the world-maker's lordship consists of non-manifestation (akhyāti).
7. (v25) Proclaimed one alone through continuity: the mother-principle (mātṛtattva) of all consciousnesses, equivalent to self-experience, beginningless.
8. (v26) The final ground of things' ordering — pervasion and continuity partaking in I-ness — does not fit the inert.
9. (v27) Hence in difference, manifestation's appearing holds; therefore: this world consists of that non-dual (tadadvayamayaṃ jagat).

**Inference:** Prāṇa/body are prameya; the knower is the resting, autonomous I; Māyā is his own power; pervasion-and-continuity (the marks of ordering) belong only to what partakes in I-ness.

**Conclusion:** One beginningless mother-principle, self-experience itself; the world proclaimed in recognition is non-dual.

**Unresolved:** None.

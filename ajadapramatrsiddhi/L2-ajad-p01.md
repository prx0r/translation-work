# Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi ajad-p01 (vv. 1–9) — L2 READ

## v.1
Just as there is no difference between the real and the unreal in their own nature, so too — this is certain — there is no such difference between the inert and the non-inert.
The Sanskrit: *yathā sadasatām naiva viśeṣo 'sti nijātmani jadājaḍānām apy evaṃ nāsty asāv iti niścayaḥ*

## v.2
Appearing (prakhyā) and describing (upākhyā), existence and its opposite, non-existence — none of this touches a thing's essence; that essence is what faces the knower.
The Sanskrit: *prakhyopākhyā ca sattā yad yad asattā viparyayaḥ naitat svarūpaṃ spṛśati vastuno jñātṛgāmy adaḥ*

## v.3
Nor can a thing's very self be "being an object of appearing and describing" — for that would make it depend on something else, give it no single form, and break past existence itself.
The Sanskrit: *prakhyopākhyāviṣayatāpy arthasyātmā na yujyate parāpekṣitayā naikarūpatve sattvalaṅghanāt*

## v.4
Granted, let there be no difference; still, from the presence and absence of appearing, a difference does get established here — and that much serves the purpose.
The Sanskrit: *mā bhūd viśeṣaḥ prakhyādibhāvābhāvau tataḥ punaḥ siddhyaty eva viśeṣo 'tra tāvatā ca prayojanam*

## v.5
Even so, existence would only be fitting as tied to appearing; without reference to that, existent and nonexistent are indistinguishable.
The Sanskrit: *evam apy avabhāsānuṣaktā sattocitā bhavet tadapekṣāṃ vinā bhāvābhāvayor aviśiṣṭatā*

## v.6
But how could they be indistinguishable here, when the existent performs a function (arthakriyā) and the other does not? And yet the thing, in its own self, remains without distinction.
The Sanskrit: *kathaṃ syād aviśeṣo 'tra yāvatārthakriyāṃ hi san kuryān nānyas tathāpy artho nirviśeṣo nijātmani*

## v.7
So the existent thing, having taken on the limiting condition of appearing-as-functional, would exist by its own essence — yet it is not distinguished from the nonexistent.
The Sanskrit: *evam arthakriyābhāsopādhisaṃbhedam āgataḥ sann arthaḥ syāt svarūpeṇa nāsatas tu viśiṣyate*

## v.8
But even there, function is not what is described in a thing whose existence is already established; the two are exactly on par — hence their grounding rests on consciousness.
The Sanskrit: *tatrāpy arthakriyā siddhasattākārthe na varṇitā samam evānayos tena pratiṣṭhā saṃvidāśrayā*

## v.9
And if consciousness itself were of the form of non-grasping (aparāmarśa), then it too, in itself, would be exactly equal to the inert non-being, as before.
The Sanskrit: *saṃvid apy aparāmarśarūpā cet tad asāv api abhāvena jaḍenātha tulyaiva prāgvad ātmani*

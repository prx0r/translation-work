# Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi ajad-p02 (vv. 10–18) — L2 READ

## v.10
If she — by her become self-nature — has never won final grounding, how could she become the standing-place of establishment for another existent?
The Sanskrit: *saivaṃ bhūtātmanā svenāpy alabdhapariniṣṭhitiḥ katham anyasya bhāvasya pratiṣṭhāspadatām iyāt*

## v.11
Consciousness, apt to ground another thing's showing by saying 'this,' would take that grounding nature — and again, by itself, by saying 'I.'
The Sanskrit: *idam ity ucitaḥ saṃvit prakāśe 'nyasya vastunaḥ pratiṣṭhātmakatāṃ gacched aham ity ātmanā punaḥ*

## v.12
For certainty — by exclusion and inclusion both — is called the fixed order, the foundation, the attainment, the completion of the other, inert selves.
The Sanskrit: *vyatireketarābhyām hi niścayo 'nyajaḍātmanoḥ vyavasthitiḥ pratiṣṭhā ca siddhir nirvṛttir ucyate*

## v.13
So these inert ones, near-unreal in themselves, belong to shining alone; shining alone is — of its own self, through selves both own and other.
The Sanskrit: *evam ātmany asatkalpāḥ prakāśasyaiva santy amī jaḍāḥ prakāśa evāsti svātmanaḥ svaparātmabhiḥ*

## v.14
And there is no experience apart from distinct-and-indistinct certainty — apart from connected shining, grasp-less in its self.
The Sanskrit: *na caivānubhavo 'py asti bhinnābhinnaviniścayam muktvānvayasya prakāśasya vimarśarahitātmanaḥ*

## v.15
For this 'this'-grasping, cut off, fulfillment is rest in its own form — and that grasping, 'I am this,' is this itself.
The Sanskrit: *idam ity asya vicchinnavimarśasya kṛtārthatā yā svasvarūpe viśrāntir vimarśaḥ so 'ham ity ayam*

## v.16
Twofold is this same self: measured and unmeasured — by breath and the rest restrained, an atom; as supreme self, unbroken.
The Sanskrit: *dvidhā sa eṣa evātmā mito 'parimitas tathā prāṇādinā niruddho 'ṇuḥ paramātmā tv akhaṇḍitaḥ*

## v.17
For both, this is the end-ground of all object-awarenesses — one alone through continuity. So no other is possible.
The Sanskrit: *ubhayo 'py eṣa paryantabhūmiḥ sarvārthasaṃvidām eka evānusandhānād ato 'nyo nopapadyate*

## v.18
And in the I-knowing whose self is non-separation grasp, limit-dwelling — nothing inert beyond shining appears.
The Sanskrit: *na cāpy avyatirekātmavimarśātmany ahaṃvidi bhāti paryantavartinyāṃ bhāsanād adhikaṃ jaḍam*

## ajad-p01 — Real, unreal, conscious, unconscious (vv. 1–9)

**1.** Just as there is no difference between the real and the unreal in their own nature, so too — this is certain — there is no such difference between the inert and the non-inert. *(yathā sadasatām naiva viśeṣo 'sti nijātmani / jaḍājaḍānām apy evaṃ nāsty asāv iti niścayaḥ)*

**2.** Appearing (prakhyā) and describing (upākhyā), existence and its opposite, non-existence — none of this touches a thing's essence; that essence is what faces the knower. *(prakhyopākhyā ca sattā yad yad asattā viparyayaḥ / naitat svarūpaṃ spṛśati vastuno jñātṛgāmy adaḥ)*

**3.** Nor can a thing's very self be "being an object of appearing and describing" — for that would make it depend on something else, give it no single form, and break past existence itself. *(prakhyopākhyāviṣayatāpy arthasyātmā na yujyate / parāpekṣitayā naikarūpatve sattvalaṅghanāt)*

**4.** Granted, let there be no difference; still, from the presence and absence of appearing, a difference does get established here — and that much serves the purpose. *(mā bhūd viśeṣaḥ prakhyādibhāvābhāvau tataḥ punaḥ / siddhyaty eva viśeṣo 'tra tāvatā ca prayojanam)*

**5.** Even so, existence would only be fitting as tied to appearing; without reference to that, existent and nonexistent are indistinguishable. *(evam apy avabhāsānuṣaktā sattocitā bhavet / tadapekṣāṃ vinā bhāvābhāvayor aviśiṣṭatā)*

**6.** But how could they be indistinguishable here, when the existent performs a function (arthakriyā) and the other does not? And yet the thing, in its own self, remains without distinction. *(kathaṃ syād aviśeṣo 'tra yāvatārthakriyāṃ hi san / kuryān nānyas tathāpy artho nirviśeṣo nijātmani)*

**7.** So the existent thing, having taken on the limiting condition of appearing-as-functional, would exist by its own essence — yet it is not distinguished from the nonexistent. *(evam arthakriyābhāsopādhisaṃbhedam āgataḥ / sann arthaḥ syāt svarūpeṇa nāsatas tu viśiṣyate)*

**8.** But even there, function is not what is described in a thing whose existence is already established; the two are exactly on par — hence their grounding rests on consciousness. *(tatrāpy arthakriyā siddhasattākārthe na varṇitā / samam evānayos tena pratiṣṭhā saṃvidāśrayā)*

**9.** And if consciousness itself were of the form of non-reflection (aparāmarśa), then it too, in itself, would be exactly equal to the inert non-being, as before. *(saṃvid apy aparāmarśarūpā cet tad asāv api / abhāvena jaḍenātha tulyaiva prāgvad ātmani)*

## ajad-p02 — The "I"-consciousness (vv. 10–18)

**10.** If it has not, by its own self-nature, attained final grounding, how could it become the ground of establishment for another existent? *(saivaṃ bhūtātmanā svenāpy alabdhapariniṣṭhitiḥ / katham anyasya bhāvasya pratiṣṭhāspadatām iyāt)*

**11.** Consciousness, fitly manifesting as "this" (idam), would attain the nature of grounding another thing's manifestation — and again, by itself, as "I" (aham). *(idam ity ucitaḥ saṃvit prakāśe 'nyasya vastunaḥ / pratiṣṭhātmakatāṃ gacched aham ity ātmanā punaḥ)*

**12.** For certainty — by exclusion (vyatireka) and by inclusion (itara) alike — is called the fixed order, the foundation, the attainment, the completion of the other inert selves. *(vyatireketarābhyām hi niścayo 'nyajaḍātmanoḥ / vyavasthitiḥ pratiṣṭhā ca siddhir nirvṛttir ucyate)*

**13.** So these inert things, almost unreal in themselves, exist only as light's own; light alone exists — of one's own self, through selves own and other. *(evam ātmany asatkalpāḥ prakāśasyaiva santy amī / jaḍāḥ prakāśa evāsti svātmanaḥ svaparātmabhiḥ)*

**14.** And there is no experience apart from the certainty of the distinct and the indistinct, apart from the connected light whose self is without reflection. *(na caivānubhavo 'py asti bhinnābhinnaviniścayam / muktvānvayasya prakāśasya vimarśarahitātmanaḥ)*

**15.** Of this "this"-consciousness whose reflection is cut off, the fulfillment is rest in its own essence — and that reflection, "I am this," is just this. *(idam ity asya vicchinnavimarśasya kṛtārthatā / yā svasvarūpe viśrāntir vimarśaḥ so 'ham ity ayam)*

**16.** This same self is twofold: measured and unmeasured — confined by breath and the rest, it is the atom (aṇu); as supreme self, it is undivided. *(dvidhā sa eṣa evātmā mito 'parimitas tathā / prāṇādinā niruddho 'ṇuḥ paramātmā tv akhaṇḍitaḥ)*

**17.** Of both, this one alone is the ultimate ground of all object-consciousnesses; it is one alone through continuity (anusaṃdhāna) — so no other is possible. *(ubhayo 'py eṣa paryantabhūmiḥ sarvārthasaṃvidām / eka evānusandhānād ato 'nyo nopapadyate)*

**18.** And in the I-consciousness whose self is reflection of non-separation, dwelling at the limit, nothing more inert than manifestation appears. *(na cāpy avyatirekātma-vimarśātmany ahaṃvidi / bhāti paryantavartinyāṃ bhāsanād adhikaṃ jaḍam)*

## ajad-p03 — Breath, Māyā, and the non-dual world (vv. 19–27)

**19.** Though joined with knowledge and the rest, breath and the rest are not established by non-manifestation; they would be objects of knowledge, not knowers — otherwise it is delusion. *(na siddho 'prathanād eva jñānādisamavāyy api / tat prāṇādi prameyaṃ syān na pramātānyato bhramāt)*

**20.** Although the abiding of things stands confined in the living being, restrained by breath and the eightfold city (puryaṣṭaka), even there it abides in the supreme self. *(yady apy arthasthitiḥ prāṇapuryaṣṭakaniyantrite / jīve niruddhā tatrāpi paramātmani sā sthitā)*

**21.** How could restraint by breath, of that self-nature, ever be established? In recognition (pratyabhijñā), the living being is supreme over non-manifestation. *(tadātmanaiva tasya syāt kathaṃ prāṇena yantraṇā / sādhitā pratyabhijñāyām akhyāter jīvatā param)*

**22.** The ordering of things does not lie in breath and the rest through restraint of I-ness; rest of manifestation in itself is proclaimed as I-ness. *(nārthavyavasthā prāṇādāv ahaṃbhāvanirodhataḥ / prakāśasyātmaviśrāntir ahaṃbhāvo hi kīrtitaḥ)*

**23.** That very rest — stated as restraint of all dependence — is autonomy (svātantrya); then principal authorship (kartṛtva), and indeed lordship (īśvaratā). *(uktā saiva ca viśrāntiḥ sarvāpekṣānirodhataḥ / svātantryam atha kartṛtvaṃ mukhyam īśvaratāpi ca)*

**24.** Named Māyā, it is of his power alone — no other being established; for this lordship of the world-maker consists of non-manifestation (akhyāti). *(māyābhidhās tacchakter eva cānyāprasiddhitaḥ / tadakhyātimayaṃ hy etaj jagannirmātṛteśitā)*

**25.** And proclaimed as one alone through continuity: the mother-principle (mātṛtattva) of all consciousnesses, equivalent to self-experience, beginningless. *(ekaiva cānusaṃdhānāt sā proktā sarvasaṃvidām / svasaṃvedanaparyāyamātṛtattvam anādi tat)*

**26.** Whatever is the final ground of the ordering of things — the pervasion and continuity belonging to what partakes in I-ness — that does not fit the inert. *(bhāvavyavasthā yan niṣṭhā tasyāhaṃbhāvabhāginaḥ / vyāpitvam anusaṃdhānaṃ taj jaḍasya na yujyate)*

**27.** Hence, in difference, the appearing of manifestation holds good; therefore it is stated in the Pratyabhijñā: this world consists of that non-dual. *(tato bhede tu bhāsasya ghaṭate bhāsamānatā / tenoktam pratyabhijñāyāṃ tadadvayamayaṃ jagat)*

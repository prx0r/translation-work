# Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi ajad-p01 — ARGUMENT MAP (the inert grounds nothing)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 ajad-p01 = vv. 1–9. Lore: Lawrence 2009 (Journal of Indian Philosophy — critical edition with Harabhatta Shastri's vṛtti); Mundra & Godbole 2022 (Academia.edu translation). Key: self-luminosity (svaprakāśatva) is the determining factor between jaḍa and ajaḍa. Utpaladeva forms his view at two levels: pragmatic and ultimate/transcendental. The APS is part of the Siddhitrayī (three proofs): Īśvarasiddhi, Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi, Sambandhasiddhi. The vṛtti is by Harabhatta Shastri (not Utpaladeva). Lawrence's edition (2009) is the critical edition.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Can the inert (jaḍa) ground anything? What really distinguishes the existent (sat) from the nonexistent (asat)? Utpaladeva's answer: no candidate ground (existence, function, appearing) survives scrutiny — every one collapses into consciousness-dependence. And consciousness without self-reflection (aparāmarśa) is no better than the inert. Only self-reflective consciousness grounds. This is the first of the Siddhitrayī's three proofs: the knower must be conscious, not inert.

Lore: Lawrence 2009 (Kaul 1921 KSTS 34; GRETIL/Vasudeva input — its Vṛ blocks are Utpaladeva’s own auto-commentary; Harabhaṭṭa’s 19th-c. vṛtti is separate). Mundra & Godbole 2022 note the task "especially interesting"; the GRETIL colophon settles it. Mundra & Godbole 2022: Utpaladeva establishes the need for a conscious knower; discusses the nature of conscious and non-conscious; acknowledges the limited knower; shows it is established in the unlimited knower; concludes the unlimited knower is the only knower; the world is nondual. Self-luminosity (svaprakāśatvaṃ) is the determining factor between jaḍa and ajaḍa. Utpaladeva forms his view at two levels: pragmatic and ultimate/transcendental. The text includes brief refutations of Vijñānavāda and Śāṅkara Vedānta regarding the nature of reality (sattā) and consciousness.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — the opening (v. 1):**
> *yathā sadasatām naiva viśeṣo 'sti nijātmani | jadājaḍānām apy evaṃ nāsty asāv iti niścayaḥ*
[i.e. just as there is no difference between real and unreal in their own self, so too — this is certain — there is no such difference between inert and non-inert.] The Buddhist (Vijñānavādin) opponent's position is stated: things are unmixed in themselves; no difference between real/unreal, conscious/unconscious.

**Step 1 — appearing and describing (v. 2):**
> *prakhyopākhyā ca sattā yad yad asattā viparyayaḥ | naitat svarūpaṃ spṛśati vastuno jñātṛgāmy adaḥ*
[i.e. appearing (prakhyā) and describing (upākhyā), existence and its opposite, non-existence — none of this touches a thing's essence; that essence faces the knower (jñātṛgāmi).] The essence of a thing is not reached by any of these predicates; it faces only the knower.

**Step 2 — object-of-appearing (v. 3):**
> *prakhyopākhyāviṣayatāpy arthasyātmā na yujyate | parāpekṣitayā naikarūpatve sattvalaṅghanāt*
[i.e. a thing's self cannot be "being-an-object-of-appearing" — that would depend on another, lack single form, overstep existence.] The object-of-appearing is dependent, formless, existence-transgressing.

**Step 3 — granted no difference (v. 4):**
> *mā bhūd viśeṣaḥ prakhyādibhāvābhāvau tataḥ punaḥ | siddhyaty eva viśeṣo 'tra tāvatā ca prayojanam*
[i.e. granted, let there be no difference; still, from presence/absence of appearing, a difference gets established — and that much serves the purpose.] Even granting the opponent's premise, the purpose (arthakriyā) is served by the presence/absence of appearing.

**Step 4 — existence tied to appearing (v. 5):**
> *evam apy avabhāsānuṣaktā sattocitā bhavet | tadapekṣāṃ vinā bhāvābhāvayor aviśiṣṭatā*
[i.e. even so, existence would only be fitting as tied to appearing; without that reference, existent and nonexistent are indistinguishable.] Existence is not a free-standing feature; it is tied to appearing.

**Step 5 — the objection: function (v. 6):**
> *kathaṃ syād aviśeṣo 'tra yāvatārthakriyāṃ hi san | kuryān nānyas tathāpy artho nirviśeṣo nijātmani*
[i.e. but how could they be indistinguishable here, when the existent performs a function (arthakriyā) and the other does not? And yet the thing, in its own self, remains without distinction.] Objection: function distinguishes the existent from the nonexistent. Reply: in its own self, the thing remains distinctionless.

**Step 6 — function conditioned (v. 7):**
> *evam arthakriyābhāsopādhisaṃbhedam āgataḥ | sann arthaḥ syāt svarūpeṇa nāsatas tu viśiṣyate*
[i.e. so the existent thing, having taken on the limiting condition of appearing-as-functional, would exist by its own essence — yet it is not distinguished from the nonexistent.] Function is conditioned by appearing; it does not establish a real distinction.

**Step 7 — function not described (v. 8):**
> *tatrāpy arthakriyā siddhasattākārthe na varṇitā | samam evānayos tena pratiṣṭhā saṃvidāśrayā*
[i.e. but even there, function is not what is described in a thing whose existence is already established; the two are exactly on par — hence their grounding rests on consciousness.] Function presupposes existence; existence presupposes consciousness.

**Step 8 — consciousness without reflection (v. 9):**
> *saṃvid apy aparāmarśarūpā cet tad asāv api | abhāvena jaḍenātha tulyaiva prāgvad ātmani*
[i.e. and if consciousness itself were of the form of non-reflection (aparāmarśa), then it too, in itself, would be exactly equal to the inert non-being, as before.] The decisive move: consciousness without self-reflection (parāmarśa/vimarśa) is no better than the inert. Only self-reflective consciousness grounds.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the passage's own descent: no difference (v. 1) → appearing/describing (v. 2) → object-of-appearing (v. 3) → granted no difference (v. 4) → existence tied to appearing (v. 5) → objection: function (v. 6) → function conditioned (v. 7) → function not described (v. 8) → consciousness without reflection (v. 9). The key move is v. 9: consciousness without parāmarśa is no better than the inert. Carry that into p02 (the "I" that grounds) and p03 (breath is the known, not the knower). The tone is scholastic (pramāṇa-style), not devotional — this is the APS's characteristic mode. The two-level structure (pragmatic and ultimate/transcendental) should be visible in the L2.

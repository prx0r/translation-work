# Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi ajad-p02 — ARGUMENT MAP (the "I" that grounds)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 ajad-p02 = vv. 10–18. Lore: Lawrence 2009; Mundra & Godbole 2022. Key: the "I" (aham) is the ground of all object-consciousness; the self is twofold (measured/unmeasured) but one through continuity (anusaṃdhāna). The idam/aham polarity is the core of Utpaladeva's epistemology.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

How does consciousness ground another thing's manifestation? What is the "I"? Utpaladeva's answer: consciousness, manifesting as "this" (idam), attains grounding-nature for another; and again, by itself, as "I" (aham). The self is twofold — measured (mīta, confined by breath etc.) and unmeasured (aparimita, the undivided supreme self) — but one through continuity (anusaṃdhāna). No other ground is possible. The inert selves derive their entire order (vyavasthiti through nirvṛtti) from the self-reflective "I."

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — what is ungrounded cannot ground (v. 10):**
> *saivaṃ bhūtātmanā svenāpy alabdhapariniṣṭhitiḥ | katham anyasya bhāvasya pratiṣṭhāspadatām iyāt*
[i.e. if it has not, by its own self-nature, attained final grounding, how could it become the ground of establishment for another existent?] A ground must be grounded in itself; what lacks self-grounding cannot ground another.

**Step 1 — idam and aham (v. 11):**
> *idam ity ucitaḥ saṃvit prakāśe 'nyasya vastunaḥ | pratiṣṭhātmakatāṃ gacched aham ity ātmanā punaḥ*
[i.e. consciousness, fitly manifesting as "this" (idam), would attain the nature of grounding another thing's manifestation — and again, by itself, as "I" (aham).] The idam/aham polarity: consciousness grounds the other as "this" and grounds itself as "I."

**Step 2 — the fourfold certainty (v. 12):**
> *vyatireketarābhyām hi niścayo 'nyajaḍātmanoḥ | vyavasthitiḥ pratiṣṭhā ca siddhir nirvṛttir ucyate*
[i.e. for certainty — by exclusion (vyatireka) and by inclusion (itara) alike — is called the fixed order, the foundation, the attainment, the completion of the other inert selves.] The four terms (vyavasthiti/pratiṣṭhā/siddhi/nirvṛtti) are not four separate things but four names for the certainty that arises from the idam/aham polarity.

**Step 3 — inert things exist only as light's own (v. 13):**
> *evam ātmany asatkalpāḥ prakāśasyaiva santy amī | jaḍāḥ prakāśa evāsti svātmanaḥ svaparātmabhiḥ*
[i.e. so these inert things, almost unreal in themselves, exist only as light's own; light alone exists — of one's own self, through selves own and other.] The inert has no independent existence; it exists only as the light's own manifestation.

**Step 4 — no experience without certainty (v. 14):**
> *na caivānubhavo 'py asti bhinnābhinnaviniścayam | muktvānvayasya prakāśasya vimarśarahitātmanaḥ*
[i.e. and there is no experience apart from the certainty of the distinct and the indistinct, apart from the connected light whose self is without reflection.] Experience requires the certainty of distinct/indistinct, which requires vimarśa (self-reflection). Without vimarśa, no experience.

**Step 5 — the "I am this" (v. 15):**
> *idam ity asya vicchinnnavimarśasya kṛtārthatā | yā svasvarūpe viśrāntir vimarśaḥ so 'ham ity ayam*
[i.e. of this "this"-consciousness whose reflection is cut off, the fulfillment is rest in its own essence; that reflection — "I am this" — is just this.] The cut-off "this"-reflection finds fulfillment in rest in own essence; "I am this" is the reflection itself.

**Step 6 — the twofold self (v. 16):**
> *dvidhā sa eṣa evātmā mito 'parimitas tathā | prāṇādinā niruddho 'ṇuḥ paramātmā tv akhaṇḍitaḥ*
[i.e. this same self is twofold: measured and unmeasured — confined by breath and the rest, it is the atom (aṇu); as supreme self, it is undivided.] The self is both the limited (aṇu, confined by breath etc.) and the unlimited (paramātman, undivided). Both are the same self.

**Step 7 — one through continuity (v. 17):**
> *ubhayo 'py eṣa paryantabhūmiḥ sarvārthasaṃvidām | eka evānusandhānād ato 'nyo nopapadyate*
[i.e. of both, this one alone is the ultimate ground of all object-consciousnesses; it is one alone through continuity (anusaṃdhāna) — so no other is possible.] The two (measured and unmeasured) are one through continuity; no other ground is possible.

**Step 8 — the end-residing I (v. 18):**
> *na cāpy avyatirekātma-vimarśātmany ahaṃvidi | bhāti paryantavartinyāṃ bhāsanād adhikaṃ jaḍam*
[i.e. in the I-consciousness whose self is reflection of non-separation, dwelling at the limit, nothing more inert than manifestation appears.] In the end-residing I-consciousness of non-separation, nothing more inert than manifestation appears — the inert is fully absorbed into the self-reflective consciousness.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. How grasping confers standing: v. 15’s I-resting that completes the incomplete knower.
2. GRETIL carries mūla with the author’s own vṛtti; the Harabhaṭṭa commentary (Lawrence) is a different, later layer.
3. The Buddhist no-continuer and the Advaitin bare-light both fail the same test: no grasping, no grounding.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the passage's own ascent: what is ungrounded cannot ground (v. 10) → idam and aham (v. 11) → fourfold certainty (v. 12) → inert things exist only as light's own (v. 13) → no experience without certainty (v. 14) → "I am this" (v. 15) → twofold self (v. 16) → one through continuity (v. 17) → end-residing I (v. 18). The key move is v. 15: "I am this" is the reflection itself — the fulfillment of the cut-off "this"-reflection is rest in own essence. And v. 17: the two (measured/unmeasured) are one through continuity; no other ground is possible. The tone is scholastic (pramāṇa-style), not devotional. The two-level structure (pragmatic and ultimate/transcendental) should be visible: the pragmatic level is the limited knower (aṇu); the ultimate level is the unlimited knower (paramātman). Carry both into p03 (breath is the known, not the knower).

# Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi ajad-p03 — ARGUMENT MAP (breath is the known, not the knower)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 ajad-p03 = vv. 19–27. Lore: Lawrence 2009; Mundra & Godbole 2022. Key: prāṇa/body are prameya (objects of knowledge), not pramātṛ (knower); the knower is the resting, autonomous I; Māyā is his own power; pervasion-and-continuity belong only to what partakes in I-ness; the world proclaimed in recognition is non-dual (tadadvayamayaṃ jagat). This is the conclusion of the APS: the unlimited knower is the only knower, and the world is nondual.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Are breath, body, the eightfold city the knower? What is Māyā? What is the world? Utpaladeva's answer: prāṇa/body are prameya (objects of knowledge), not pramātṛ (knower). The knower is the resting, autonomous I. Māyā is his own power (no other established). Pervasion-and-continuity (the marks of ordering) belong only to what partakes in I-ness. The world proclaimed in recognition is non-dual (*tadadvayamayaṃ jagat*). This is the APS's conclusion: the unlimited knower (paramātman) is the only knower, and the world is nondual.

Lore: Lawrence 2009 — the APS establishes the need for a conscious knower; discusses the nature of conscious and non-conscious; acknowledges the limited knower; shows it is established in the unlimited knower; concludes the unlimited knower is the only knower; the world is nondual. Mundra & Godbole 2022: self-luminosity decides between jaḍa and ajaḍa; schools refuted in passing; GRETIL vṛtti is Utpaladeva’s own, Harabhaṭṭa’s separate. The two-level structure (pragmatic and ultimate/transcendental) is visible: the pragmatic level is the limited knower (aṇu, confined by breath); the ultimate level is the unlimited knower (paramātman, undivided).

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — breath etc. are not established (v. 19):**
> *na siddho 'prathanād eva jñānādisamavāyy api | tat prāṇādi prameyaṃ syān na pramātānyato bhramāt*
[i.e. breath etc., though joined with knowledge, are not established by non-manifestation; they are objects (prameya), not the knower — else delusion.] Breath and body, even when joined with knowledge, are not established as knowers; they are objects of knowledge. If they were knowers, it would be delusion.

**Step 1 — abiding in the supreme (v. 20):**
> *yady apy arthasthitiḥ prāṇapuryaṣṭakaniyantrite | jīve niruddhā tatrāpi paramātmani sā sthitā*
[i.e. though things stand confined in the living being (breath, eightfold city), even there they abide in the supreme self.] Even when confined in the living being, things abide in the supreme self — the confinement is apparent, not real.

**Step 2 — restraint by breath (v. 21):**
> *tadātmanaiva tasya syāt kathaṃ prāṇena yantraṇā | sādhitā pratyabhijñāyām akhyāter jīvatā param*
[i.e. restraint by breath cannot establish that self-nature; in recognition (pratyabhijñā), the living being is supreme over non-manifestation.] Breath cannot establish the self-nature; in recognition, the living being is supreme over non-manifestation (akhyāti).

**Step 3 — ordering is not in breath (v. 22):**
> *nārthavyavasthā prāṇādāv ahaṃbhāvanirodhataḥ | prakāśasyātmaviśrāntir ahaṃbhāvo hi kīrtitaḥ*
[i.e. the ordering of things is not in breath etc. via I-restraint; rest of manifestation in itself is proclaimed as I-ness.] The ordering of things is not in breath; rest of manifestation in itself is I-ness (ahaṃbhāva).

**Step 4 — that rest is autonomy (v. 23):**
> *uktā saiva ca viśrāntiḥ sarvāpekṣānirodhataḥ | svātantryam atha kartṛtvaṃ mukhyam īśvaratāpi ca*
[i.e. that rest — restraint of all dependence — is autonomy (svātantrya), principal authorship (kartṛtva), lordship (īśvaratā).] The rest (viśrānti) is autonomy, authorship, lordship — the fullness of Śiva's freedom.

**Step 5 — Māyā is his power (v. 24):**
> *māyābhidās tacchakter eva cānyāprasiddhitaḥ | tadakhyātimayaṃ hy etaj jagannirmātṛteśitā*
[i.e. named Māyā, it is of his power alone (no other established); the world-maker's lordship consists of non-manifestation (akhyāti).] Māyā is not an independent principle; it is Śiva's own power (śakti), the power of non-manifestation (akhyāti). The world-maker's lordship consists of non-manifestation.

**Step 6 — one mother-principle (v. 25):**
> *ekaiva cānusaṃdhānāt sā proktā sarvasaṃvidām | svasaṃvedanaparyāyamātṛtattvam anādi tat*
[i.e. proclaimed one alone through continuity: the mother-principle (mātṛtattva) of all consciousnesses, equivalent to self-experience, beginningless.] One beginningless mother-principle, self-experience itself, proclaimed through continuity (anusaṃdhāna).

**Step 7 — pervasion belongs to I-ness (v. 26):**
> *bhāvavyavasthā yan niṣṭhā tasyāhaṃbhāvabhāginaḥ | vyāpitvam anusaṃdhānaṃ taj jaḍasya na yujyate*
[i.e. the final ground of things' ordering — pervasion and continuity partaking in I-ness — does not fit the inert.] Pervasion (vyāpitva) and continuity (anusaṃdhāna) belong only to what partakes in I-ness (ahaṃbhāva); the inert cannot have them.

**Step 8 — the world is non-dual (v. 27):**
> *tato bhede tu bhāsasya ghaṭate bhāsamānatā | tenoktam pratyabhijñāyāṃ tadadvayamayaṃ jagat*
[i.e. hence in difference, manifestation's appearing holds; therefore: this world consists of that non-dual (tadadvayamayaṃ jagat).] In difference, manifestation's appearing holds; therefore, this world consists of that non-dual. The world proclaimed in recognition is non-dual.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. How grasping confers standing was shown in p02 (v. 15); here standing itself is denied to breath.
2. Mūla glossed; author’s vṛtti consulted for cruxes; Harabhaṭṭa (Lawrence 2009) is later and separate.
3. Polemics stay brief by design: the manual proves the knower, leaving schools aside after v. 9.
4. The two-level structure (pragmatic and ultimate/transcendental): the pragmatic level is the limited knower (aṇu, confined by breath); the ultimate level is the unlimited knower (paramātman, undivided). The text moves from pragmatic to ultimate.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the passage's own ascent: breath etc. are not established (v. 19) → abiding in the supreme (v. 20) → restraint by breath (v. 21) → ordering is not in breath (v. 22) → that rest is autonomy (v. 23) → Māyā is his power (v. 24) → one mother-principle (v. 25) → pervasion belongs to I-ness (v. 26) → the world is non-dual (v. 27). The key move is v. 24: Māyā is not an independent principle but Śiva's own power (śakti), the power of non-manifestation (akhyāti). And v. 27: the world proclaimed in recognition is non-dual (tadadvayamayaṃ jagat). This is the APS's conclusion: the unlimited knower (paramātman) is the only knower, and the world is nondual. Carry the non-dual conclusion into C1 as the text's final note. The tone is scholastic (pramāṇa-style), not devotional — this is the APS's characteristic mode, in contrast to the Śivastotrāvalī's devotional voice.

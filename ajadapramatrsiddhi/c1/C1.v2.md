# C1.v2 — Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi (condensation of the argument)

*Condensed from ARGMAP.v2 + L2.v2. Passage-local, no modern comparison.*

## ajad-p01 (vv. 1–9)

Nothing inert grounds anything. Real and unreal, conscious and unconscious show no difference in their own nature; appearing, describing, existing do not touch a thing's essence, which faces only the knower. Existence fits only as tied to appearing, and function cannot separate the existent from the nonexistent — the two stand on par. So all grounding rests on consciousness; and should consciousness lack self-reflection, it would itself be as inert as the inert. The ground must be self-reflective awareness, or nothing is grounded.

## ajad-p02 (vv. 10–18)

Only what is grounded in itself can ground another. Consciousness manifests as this to ground the other, and as I by itself; by exclusion and inclusion alike, certainty is the order, foundation, attainment, and completion of inert selves. The inert exist only as light's own. The self is twofold — the measured atom bound by breath, the unmeasured undivided supreme — yet one alone through continuity, the ultimate ground of all object-consciousness. At that limit, nothing more inert than manifestation itself appears.

## ajad-p03 (vv. 19–27)

Breath, body, and the eightfold city are objects of knowledge, never the knower; even confined there, things abide in the supreme self. Restraint of breath establishes nothing; rest of manifestation in itself is I-ness, and that rest — freedom from all dependence — is autonomy, authorship, lordship. Māyā is named from his power alone, the world-maker's lordship of non-manifestation. One beginningless mother-principle, self-experience itself, grounds all ordering through pervasion and continuity — which never fit the inert. Hence the Pratyabhijñā verdict: this world consists of that non-dual.

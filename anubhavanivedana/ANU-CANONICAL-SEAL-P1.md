## The parable (canonical draft, ≈55s spoken)

> A woman plays at the festival of the abode — and her music is so
> complete that you cannot find a single note in it: the tunes have
> gone quiet inside themselves. Her dancing is so true that no step
> can be caught: the movements have dissolved. Her breathing carries
> it all, easy on its own rhythm, and the yoga is finished without
> any of it being done. Then moon and sun go out inside the hall like
> candles at noon — and the one who watches, eyes half-closed, gaze
> steady, aimed inward — and the dew was not asked for, it came
> before asking. He sees a light with no outside at all. Speech stops. What more is there to say? The
> festival just keeps being wonderful.

**Map (hinge):**
- "music with no note / tunes gone quiet inside themselves" →
  *mantra where letters unnoticed* [p04] + *whatever sound arises
  from the mouth is already transcendent mantra* [p03]
- "dancing… movements dissolved" → *mudrā where entire bodily action
  is dissolved* [p04] + *whatever posture is already mudrā* [p03]
- "breathing on its own rhythm; yoga done without being done" →
  *breath's flow, wonderful yoga; no technique enjoined* [p03–p04]
- "moon and sun go out inside the hall" → *candrārkau… trispanda-
  bhāvāntare līnatām* — moon/sun dissolved in the interior of the
  triple-throb state [p02]
- "eyes half-closed, gaze steady, aimed inward" → the four strokes
  (ardha-uḍḍhāṭa, sthira-manas, nāsāgra-dṛṣṭi) [p02]
- "given and not gotten" → *yuṣmat-prasādāt* — by the guru's grace
  alone [p01]
- "a light with no outside at all" → *jyotīrūpam aśeṣa-bāhya-
  rahitam* — light-formed, devoid of all exteriority, one supreme
  person [p02]
- "Speech stops. What more is there to say?" → *vācyam… what more is
  there to say* — recognition completes itself, speech ends [p02]
- "festival keeps being wonderful" → *adhigamotsaveṣu… kim na
  kam cid adbhutam* — festival of the abode, everything wonderful
  [p04]; rasa utsava

**Limit:** the story must not call the vanishing "nothing" — the verse
refuses BOTH void and non-void [p01]. That is why it ends on WONDER
(festival) and never on blank: emptiness is not the punchline,
recognition is.
**Rasa:** śānta + adbhuta (+ ānanda at p03) — bhakti as festival
(utsava); the parable's last line lands the sthāyibhāva.
**Bar check:** one figure (she is player AND watcher — merged, so the
two-character limit holds), retellable, zero doctrine words (mantra/
mudrā/yoga/sandhyā decoded: note/step/rhythm/noon-candles).

## The seal — statement

**The festival where the performers vanish:** a light-bodied dancer
mid-hall, half-lidded gaze aimed down her own nose, notes leaving her
hands as small birds that dissolve into light, moon and sun dimming
like candles at her shoulders, breath-stream below, gold festival
garlands above — everything wonderful, nothing performed.

## Composition laws (this seal)

ONE subject (the dancer = player = watcher merged). Vanishing must
read as BECOMING (birds, trails, dimming) — never as destruction.
The silence zone at top stays genuinely EMPTY (speech stopped = the
one place with no gold). No letterforms anywhere (mantra-unseen →
birds, not glyphs). No roundels.

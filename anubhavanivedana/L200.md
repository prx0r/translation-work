# Anubhavanivedana — L200

Abhinavagupta's four-verse hymn of direct experience opens with the yogin whose mind-wind is dissolved in the inward aim — seeing yet not seeing with steady gaze: this, by the guru's grace alone, is śāmbhavī mudrā, reality free of void and non-void. Half-open eyes, steady mind, gaze on the nose-tip; moon and sun dissolved in the triple throb: light-form without exterior, one supreme person attained — after which, what more is there to say? No technique is enjoined: whatever sound arises is already transcendent mantra, whatever posture the pleasure-pain-born body takes is already mudrā, whatever the breath's spontaneous flow is already wonderful yoga — so that, in the experienced supreme śākta abode, what would not shine? Mantra is where letter-arrangement goes unnoticed, mudrā where all bodily action is dissolved, yoga where breath-flow is exhausted; and for the wise attaining Your abode, all is festival — everything wonderful.

# Anubhavanivedana anu-p03 — ARGUMENT MAP (verse 3)

*2026-11-01. Source: T1 anu-p03 = verse 3. Lore: this verse is the most translated of the four — Skora's article quotes it entire (after Silburn 1970, pp. 37–38, and Rastogi 1990, p. xv); Jaideva Singh's school renders it as the charter of spontaneous practice; Buira Ferré's workshop note reads mudrā here as presence (not seal/posture), given the verse's metaphysical pitch.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Verse 3 of the Anubhavanivedana (Abhinavagupta, 4 verses). Gloss: sound (śabdaḥ) whatever (kaścana) which (yaḥ) from-the-mouth (mukhāt) arises (udayate) mantra (mantraḥ) that (saḥ) transcendent (lokottaraḥ); posture (saṃsthānam) of-the-body-born-of-pleasure-and-pain (sukhaduḥkhajanmavapuṣaḥ) whatever (yat) somehow (kāpi) mudrā (mudrā) indeed (eva) that (sā); of-breath (prāṇasya) by-own-rhythm (svarasena) which (yat) flowing (pravahaṇam) yoga (yogaḥ) that (saḥ) indeed (eva) wonderful (adbhutaḥ); śākta (śāktam) abode (dhāma) supreme (param) while-I-experience (mamānubhavataḥ) what (kim) indeed (nāma) not (na) shines (bhrājate).

Sanskrit: śabdaḥ kaścana yo mukhādudayate mantraḥ sa lokottaraḥ saṃsthānaṃ sukhaduḥkhajanmavapuṣo yatkāpi mudraiva sā | prāṇasya svarasena yatpravahaṇaṃ yogaḥ sa evādbhutaḥ śāktaṃ dhāma paraṃ mamānubhavataḥ kinnāma na bhrājate

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Verse 3:**
> śabdaḥ kaścana yo mukhādudayate mantraḥ sa lokottaraḥ saṃsthānaṃ sukhaduḥkhajanmavapuṣo yatkāpi mudraiva sā | prāṇasya svarasena yatpravahaṇaṃ yogaḥ sa evādbhutaḥ śāktaṃ dhāma paraṃ mamānubhavataḥ kinnāma na bhrājate

[i.e. whatever sound arises from the mouth — that mantra is transcendent; whatever posture of the pleasure-pain-born body — that indeed is mudrā; whatever flowing of breath by its own rhythm — that indeed is wonderful yoga; the supreme śākta abode — while I experience, what indeed would not shine?]

Three redefinitions in parallel, each with the same structure — whatever X, that very X is the sacred thing: whatever (kaścana...yaḥ) sound (śabdaḥ) arises (udayate) from the mouth (mukhāt), that (saḥ) mantra (mantraḥ) is transcendent (lokottaraḥ); whatever (yat...kāpi) posture (saṃsthānam) of the pleasure-pain-born (sukhaduḥkhajanma) body (vapuṣaḥ), that (sā) indeed (eva) is mudrā (mudrā); whatever (yat) flowing (pravahaṇam) of breath (prāṇasya) by its own rhythm (svarasena), that (saḥ) indeed (eva) is wonderful (adbhutaḥ) yoga (yogaḥ). No technique is enjoined; ordinary utterance, posture, breathing are recognized as already mantra, mudrā, yoga. Then the ground and the question: the supreme (param) śākta abode (śāktaṃ dhāma) — while I experience (mamānubhavataḥ: mama + anubhavataḥ, genitive absolute) — what (kim) indeed (nāma) would not shine (na bhrājate)? In that abode, shining is the default; the burden shifts to what could fail to shine.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Śāktaṃ dhāma paraṃ is read as nominative (the abode shines / makes shine); the form also permits accusative. The question-form (what would not shine?) carries the sense either way.
2. Buira's mudrā-as-presence is a translator's choice, noted, not imposed on the gloss (kept as mudrā).

## 4. Decision for L2

Carry three redefinitions then the shining-question: whatever sounds, whatever posture, whatever breath-flow — already mantra, mudrā, yoga — so what, in the experienced śākta abode, would not shine? L2 must keep the triple whatever-that structure; it is the argument. Carry spontaneity into verse 4, where even letter-arrangement drops away.

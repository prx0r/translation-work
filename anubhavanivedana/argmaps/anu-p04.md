# Anubhavanivedana anu-p04 — ARGUMENT MAP (verse 4)

*2026-11-01. Source: T1 anu-p04 = verse 4. Lore: Silburn 1970 (French translation, the classic rendering this verse's negations depend on); the verse defines each sacred form by a vanishing — mantra where letters unnoticed, mudrā where bodily action dissolved, yoga where breath-flow exhausted — closing not in a definition but in festival (utsava) of the wise attaining the abode.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Verse 4 of the Anubhavanivedana (Abhinavagupta, 4 verses). Gloss: mantra (mantraḥ) that (saḥ) shines-forth (pratibhāti) of-letters (varṇa) arrangement (racanā) in-which (yasmin) not (na) is-distinguished (saṃlakṣyate); mudrā (mudrā) that (sā) arises (samudeti) in-which (yatra) dissolved (galitā) entire (kṛtsnā) action (kriyā) bodily (kāyikī); yoga (yogaḥ) that (saḥ) spreads-forth (prathate) from-which (yataḥ) flowing (pravahaṇam) of-breath (prāṇasya) is-exhausted (saṃkṣīyate); in-the-festivals-of-attaining-your-abode (tvaddhāmādhigamotsaveṣu) of-the-wise (sudhiyām) what (kiṃ) what (kim) not (na) indeed (nāma) wonderful (adbhutam).

Sanskrit: mantraḥ sa pratibhāti varṇaracanā yasminna saṃlakṣyate mudrā sā samudeti yatra galitā kṛtsnā kriyā kāyikī | yogaḥ sa prathate yataḥ pravahaṇaṃ prāṇasya saṃkṣīyate tvaddhāmādhigamotsaveṣu sudhiyāṃ kiṃ kiṃ na nāmādbhutaṃ

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Verse 4:**
> mantraḥ sa pratibhāti varṇaracanā yasminna saṃlakṣyate mudrā sā samudeti yatra galitā kṛtsnā kriyā kāyikī | yogaḥ sa prathate yataḥ pravahaṇaṃ prāṇasya saṃkṣīyate tvaddhāmādhigamotsaveṣu sudhiyāṃ kiṃ kiṃ na nāmādbhutaṃ

[i.e. that mantra shines forth in which letter-arrangement is not distinguished; that mudrā arises in which entire bodily action is dissolved; that yoga spreads forth from which breath's flowing is exhausted; in the festivals of attaining Your abode, of the wise — what, what is indeed not wonderful?]

Three definitions by vanishing, in strict parallel (yasmin / yatra / yataḥ — in which / in which / from which): that (saḥ) mantra (mantraḥ) shines forth (pratibhāti) in which (yasmin) arrangement (racanā) of letters (varṇa) is not (na) distinguished (saṃlakṣyate); that (sā) mudrā (mudrā) arises (samudeti) in which (yatra) entire (kṛtsnā) bodily (kāyikī) action (kriyā) is dissolved (galitā); that (saḥ) yoga (yogaḥ) spreads forth (prathate) from which (yataḥ) the flowing (pravahaṇam) of breath (prāṇasya) is exhausted (saṃkṣīyate). Mantra without discernible letters; mudrā without bodily action; yoga without breath-flow — each sacred form defined by what has dropped out of it. Then the close: in the festivals (utsaveṣu) of attaining (adhigama) Your (tvad) abode (dhāma), of the wise (sudhiyām) — what (kiṃ), what (kim) is not (na), indeed (nāma), wonderful (adbhutam)? The hymn that began in inward dissolution ends in festival: everything, for the wise attaining the abode, is wonderful.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Yataḥ is read in the relative sense, *from which*; the causal *whereby* converges on the same practice.
2. Utsava (festivals/celebrations) is kept concrete — the wise ones' attainment is festal, not merely terminal.

## 4. Decision for L2

Carry three vanishings then the festival question: letters unseen, action dissolved, breath exhausted — so what, for the wise attaining Your abode, is not wonderful? L2 must keep the strict yasmin/yatra/yataḥ parallel; it is the verse's spine. This closes the arc opened in verse 1's grace-given mudrā.

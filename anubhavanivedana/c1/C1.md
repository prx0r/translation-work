# C1 — Anubhavanivedana

## anu-p01 — seeing-not-seeing
**SUMMARY.** Mind-wind dissolved in the inward aim; seeing yet not seeing with steady gaze — this is śāmbhavī mudrā by the guru's grace alone: reality free of void and non-void.

**KEY TERMS.** antarlakṣya (inward aim), cittapavana (mind-wind), niścalatārā (unmoving pupil), paśyan-apaśyan (seeing-not-seeing), śāmbhavī mudrā, yuṣmatprasāda (your grace), śūnya-aśūnya (void/non-void).

**EXPLANATION.** The condition is dissolution, not effort: mind (citta) and wind (pavana, breath) dissolved (vilīna) in the inward aim (antarlakṣya). The gaze (dṛṣṭi) keeps an unmoving pupil (niścalatārā); outside (bahis), he sees (paśyan) and does not see (apaśyan) at once — sight without seizing. Then the naming: this (iyam) indeed (khalu) becomes (bhavati) the śāmbhavī mudrā — and only by your grace (yuṣmatprasādāt), O guru (guro). Not performed but occurring. Its character: devoid (vivarjitam) of void and non-void (śūnya-aśūnya) both — neither extreme held — becomes (bhavati) that (yat...tad) reality (tattvam), the śāmbhava state (padam śāmbhavam).

## anu-p02 — the portrait and the silence
**SUMMARY.** Four outer strokes — half-open eyes, steady mind, nose-tip gaze, moon-sun dissolved — then the vision: light-form without exterior, one supreme person attained; after which speech ends.

**KEY TERMS.** ardhoddhāṭitalocana (half-open-eyed), sthiramanas (steady-minded), nāsāgradattekṣaṇa (nose-tip gaze), candrārka (moon-sun), trispandabhāva (triple-throb state), jyotīrūpa (light-form), pumāṃs (person), vācya (to be said).

**EXPLANATION.** Outward portrait first: half-open eyes, steadied mind, gaze placed on the nose-tip — then moon and sun (candrārkau) too (api) attained dissolution (līnatām upagatau) in the interior (antare) of the triple-throb state (trispandabhāva): inhalation, exhalation and their juncture dissolved inward. Then the vision: light-formed (jyotīrūpam), devoid of all exteriority (aśeṣabāhyarahitam), and one (ekam), supreme (param) person (pumāṃsam) — reality (tattvam) attains (eti) that (tat) state (padam): the supreme thing (vastu paramam). Close: what (kim) more (adhikam) is to be said (vācyam) here (atra)? Showing complete, speech stops — the question is the period.

## anu-p03 — whatever sounds, whatever posture, whatever breath
**SUMMARY.** No technique enjoined: whatever sounds is already transcendent mantra, whatever the body postures is already mudrā, whatever the breath's flow is already wonderful yoga — so, in the experienced śākta abode, what would not shine?

**KEY TERMS.** śabda (sound), mukha (mouth), lokottara (transcendent), saṃsthāna (posture), sukhaduḥkhajanmavapus (pleasure-pain-born body), mudrā, svarasa (own rhythm), pravahaṇa (flowing), adbhuta (wonderful), śākta dhāma (śākta abode), anubhava (experience), bhrāj (shine).

**EXPLANATION.** Three redefinitions in parallel, each with the same whatever-that structure: whatever (kaścana...yaḥ) sound (śabdaḥ) arises (udayate) from the mouth (mukhāt) — that (saḥ) mantra (mantraḥ) is transcendent (lokottaraḥ); whatever (yat...kāpi) posture (saṃsthānam) of the pleasure-pain-born (sukhaduḥkhajanma) body (vapuṣaḥ) — that (sā) indeed (eva) is mudrā (mudrā); whatever (yat) flowing (pravahaṇam) of breath (prāṇasya) by its own rhythm (svarasena) — that (saḥ) indeed (eva) is wonderful (adbhutaḥ) yoga (yogaḥ). Nothing enjoined; utterance, posture, breathing are recognized as already mantra, mudrā, yoga. Then the ground and the question: the supreme (param) śākta abode (śāktaṃ dhāma) — while I experience (mamānubhavataḥ) — what (kim) indeed (nāma) would not shine (na bhrājate)? In that abode, shining is the default.

## anu-p04 — letters unseen, action dissolved, breath exhausted
**SUMMARY.** Each sacred form defined by a vanishing: mantra where letters unnoticed, mudrā where all bodily action dissolved, yoga where breath-flow exhausted — then the festival: for the wise attaining Your abode, everything wonderful.

**KEY TERMS.** pratibhāti (shines forth), varṇaracanā (letter-arrangement), saṃlakṣ (distinguish), samudeti (arises), galita (dissolved), kṛtsna (entire), kāyikī kriyā (bodily action), prathate (spreads forth), saṃkṣi (exhaust), utsava (festival), adhigama (attaining), sudhī (the wise), adbhuta (wonderful).

**EXPLANATION.** Three definitions by vanishing, in strict yasmin/yatra/yataḥ parallel: that (saḥ) mantra (mantraḥ) shines forth (pratibhāti) in which (yasmin) arrangement (racanā) of letters (varṇa) is not (na) distinguished (saṃlakṣyate); that (sā) mudrā (mudrā) arises (samudeti) in which (yatra) entire (kṛtsnā) bodily (kāyikī) action (kriyā) is dissolved (galitā); that (saḥ) yoga (yogaḥ) spreads forth (prathate) from which (yataḥ) the flowing (pravahaṇam) of breath (prāṇasya) is exhausted (saṃkṣīyate). Mantra without discernible letters; mudrā without bodily action; yoga without breath-flow — each sacred form defined by what has dropped out of it. Then the close: in the festivals (utsaveṣu) of attaining (adhigama) Your (tvad) abode (dhāma), of the wise (sudhiyām) — what (kiṃ), what (kim) is not (na), indeed (nāma), wonderful (adbhutam)? The hymn that began in inward dissolution ends in festival: everything, for the wise attaining the abode, is wonderful.

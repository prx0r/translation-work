# C1 NOTE — anu-p02.c1

Moved from C1 (pristine rasa-evocative translation only):

## SUMMARY

Four outer strokes — half-open eyes, steady mind, nose-tip gaze, moon-sun dissolved — then the vision: light-form without exterior, one supreme person attained; after which speech ends.

## FUNCTION

Establishes the portrait-vision-silence arc.

## KEY TERMS

* **ardhoddhāṭitalocana** — half-open-eyed
* **sthiramanas** — steady-minded
* **nāsāgradattekṣaṇa** — nose-tip gaze
* **candrārka** — moon-sun
* **trispandabhāva** — triple-throb state
* **jyotīrūpa** — light-form
* **pumāṃs** — person
* **vācya** — to be said

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Outer portrait, then vision, then silence.

## EXPLANATION

Outward portrait first: half-open eyes, steadied mind, gaze placed on the nose-tip — then moon and sun (candrārkau) too (api) attained dissolution (līnatām upagatau) in the interior (antare) of the triple-throb state (trispandabhāva): inhalation, exhalation and their juncture dissolved inward. Then the vision: light-formed (jyotīrūpam), devoid of all exteriority (aśeṣabāhyarahitam), and one (ekam), supreme (param) person (pumāṃsam) — reality (tattvam) attains (eti) that (tat) state (padam): the supreme thing (vastu paramam). Close: what (kim) more (adhikam) is to be said (vācyam) here (atra)? Showing complete, speech stops — the question is the period.

## BOUNDARY

Whether the triple-throb state (trispandabhāva) is breath or consciousness is unresolved.

## RELATED

* Anubhavanivedana v.3 — what sounds, postures, breathes.

# C1 NOTE — anu-p03.c1

Moved from C1 (pristine rasa-evocative translation only):

## SUMMARY

No technique enjoined: whatever sounds is already transcendent mantra, whatever the body postures is already mudrā, whatever the breath's flow is already wonderful yoga — so, in the experienced śākta abode, what would not shine?

## FUNCTION

Establishes recognition over technique: all is already yoga.

## KEY TERMS

* **śabda** — sound
* **mukha** — mouth
* **lokottara** — transcendent
* **saṃsthāna** — posture
* **sukhaduḥkhajanmavapus** — pleasure-pain-born body
* **svarasa** — own rhythm
* **pravahaṇa** — flowing
* **adbhuta** — wonderful
* **dhāma** — śākta abode
* **anubhava** — experience
* **bhrāj** — shine

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Three redefinitions: mantra, mudrā, yoga.

## EXPLANATION

Three redefinitions in parallel, each with the same whatever-that structure: whatever (kaścana...yaḥ) sound (śabdaḥ) arises (udayate) from the mouth (mukhāt) — that (saḥ) mantra (mantraḥ) is transcendent (lokottaraḥ); whatever (yat...kāpi) posture (saṃsthānam) of the pleasure-pain-born (sukhaduḥkhajanma) body (vapuṣaḥ) — that (sā) indeed (eva) is mudrā (mudrā); whatever (yat) flowing (pravahaṇam) of breath (prāṇasya) by its own rhythm (svarasena) — that (saḥ) indeed (eva) is wonderful (adbhutaḥ) yoga (yogaḥ). Nothing enjoined; utterance, posture, breathing are recognized as already mantra, mudrā, yoga. Then the ground and the question: the supreme (param) śākta abode (śāktaṃ dhāma) — while I experience (mamānubhavataḥ) — what (kim) indeed (nāma) would not shine (na bhrājate)? In that abode, shining is the default.

## BOUNDARY

Whether 'śākta dhāma' means the abode of Śakti or the abode of the śākta is unresolved.

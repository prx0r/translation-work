## The parable (canonical draft, ≈80s spoken)

> A man dreams he is locked in a cell of practices — stare here,
> chant this, hold the breath, keep this vigil — and every key he
> tries opens nothing. Then the dream changes: a rope that is a
> snake, a shadow that is a ghost. He drops everything he carried —
> even the liberation ceremony he had prepared stops mattering,
> because nothing in the dream was ever bound. He wakes. And the
> dream does not fall apart: it goes on shining, will-born, borrowed
> light and all — passion and pleasure and misery all one brightness
> in the one mirror of his seeing, the worship and the worshipped
> different only as colors in that same glass. Bliss is what remains
> when the burden is lowered: the treasure he forgot he had. He
> looks, and the opposites — true, untrue, stained, pure — shine,
> and he is their shining. Lord of it, simply.

**Map (hinge):**
- cell of practices, keys open nothing → *no method: not
  contemplation, speech-connection, discussion, visualization,
  steadying, japa-effort; what stands well-decided?* [p01]
- rope-snake, shadow-ghost → *fourfold delusion-heap: rope, snake,
  shadow, ghost* [p02]
- drops everything; ceremony pointless → *bondage never truly is;
  the liberation-rite is false; abandon nothing, grasp nothing* [p02]
- worship/worshipped as colors in one glass → *worship, worshipper,
  worshipped — what difference-course? Māyā not different from
  consciousness-nonduality* [p03]
- bliss = burden lowered, forgotten treasure → *joy like
  burden-lowering; the forgotten treasure of all-nonduality* [p04]
- passion/pleasure/misery one brightness → *feelings universe-bodied,
  not different-natured; regard as one-self-natured consciousness:
  why not delight* [p05]
- waking, dream keeps shining, borrowed light → *even the non-true
  partakes of truth for a moment; no arising; will-born from you* [
  p06–p07]
- the one mirror; opposites shine; he is the shining, Lord of it →
  *true/untrue, little/much, eternal, stained/pure shine in the
  consciousness-mirror; own-experience-risen glory: Lordhood* [p08]

**Limit:** waking is not the end of the dream — the parable must not
make the dream "unreal and gone" (that would land on void); it keeps
shining, *borrowed* — and that is the teaching's danger-zone: the
text says non-true borrows truth only "for a moment," never forever.
**Rasa:** śānta (no-method, awakening) with adbhuta threaded through,
ānanda at p04–p05 — ends on śānta-Lordhood ("Lord of it, simply").
**Bar check:** one subject; retellable; plain words (anupāya/rope-
delusion/ānanda/adarśa decoded: keys, glass, burden, mirror).

## The seal — statement

**The dream that keeps shining:** bottom — a small ward-locked cell
and a coiled rope-snake in cold dark; middle — a man half-risen from
a low bed, his burden-cloth falling away, silver dream-threads
lifting off him but NOT scattering — they rise into; top — ONE great
mirror in which the whole lower scene shines as a single field of
light, the man's face pale at its center, paired light-and-dark
flecks (the opposites) glassed over as one sheen.

## Composition laws (this seal)

ONE subject (the man; mirror = object, cell = setting). The dream-
threads must RISE into the mirror (continuity — dreams feed
recognition; they don't pop). Opposites live only inside the glass
(the page itself stays unified). No numerals (wards, not "six").
No ropes-as-decoration at top (cold zone stays bottom).

## The image prompt (seal text ONLY — reference carries style)

> On aged vellum, one vertical scene: at the bottom a small dark cell
> door studded with blank iron wards, useless keys on the floor
> beside a coiled rope half-turned to shadow-snake; above it a man
> half-risen from a low bed, ivory-skinned, his grey burden-cloth
> falling away, a warm gold pool of light at the bed-foot; silver
> threads of dream rise from him without scattering and feed into one
> great mirror that fills the top of the scene — and in the mirror
> the whole lower world shines as a single field of light, his pale
> face at its center, fine paired light-and-dark flecks glassed under
> one sheen; burnished raised gold over red bole in the gold pool,
> lapis mirror-field, pale before dark, black outline, white
> highlights, shell-gold accents. One subject. No text, no letters,
> no signature.

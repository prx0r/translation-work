# Anuttarāṣṭikā anu-p05 — ARGUMENT MAP (verse 5)

*2026-11-01. Source: T1 anu-p05 = verse 5. Lore: the affections catalogue (rāgadveṣasukhāsukhodayalayāhaṅkāradainya) as viśvavapus — the world-body composed of feeling-states, not matter; the verse's only direct address, kiṃ na ramase (why do you not delight?), turns doctrine into accosting.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Verse 5 of the Anuttarāṣṭikā (Abhinavagupta, 8 verses). Gloss: passion (rāga), aversion (dveṣa), pleasure (sukha), displeasure (asukha), rise (udaya), dissolution (laya), I-ness (ahaṃkāra), misery (dainya) and-the-rest (ādayo) those (ye) things (bhāvāḥ) shining-forth (pravibhānti) universe-bodied (viśvavapuṣo) — different-natured (bhinnasvabhāvā) not (na te). Manifestation (vyaktiṃ) you-see (paśyasi) of-whom (yasya yasya) suddenly (sahasā) — that-that (tattattad) one-self-natured (ekātmatā) consciousness-form (saṃvidrūpam) having-regarded (avekṣya) why (kiṃ na) do-you-delight (ramase) with-that (tad) contemplation-full (bhāvanānirbharaḥ)?

Sanskrit: rāgadveṣasukhāsukhodayalayāhaṅkāradainyādayo ye bhāvāḥ pravibhānti viśvavapuṣo bhinnasvabhāvā na te | vyaktiṃ paśyasi yasya yasya sahasā tattattadekātmatāsaṃvidrūpam avekṣya kiṃ na ramase tadbhāvanānirbharaḥ

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Verse 5:**
> rāgadveṣasukhāsukhodayalayāhaṅkāradainyādayo ye bhāvāḥ pravibhānti viśvavapuṣo bhinnasvabhāvā na te | vyaktiṃ paśyasi yasya yasya sahasā tattattadekātmatāsaṃvidrūpam avekṣya kiṃ na ramase tadbhāvanānirbharaḥ

[i.e. passion, aversion, pleasure, displeasure, rise, dissolution, I-ness, misery and the rest — those things shining-forth universe-bodied are not different-natured. Manifestation you see of whom suddenly — that-that one-self-natured consciousness-form having regarded — why do you not delight, full of contemplation?]

First the list: rāga, dveṣa, sukha, asukha, udaya, laya, ahaṃkāra, dainya and the rest (ādayo) — those things (bhāvāḥ) shining-forth (pravibhānti), universe-bodied (viśvavapuṣo), are not (na te) different-natured (bhinnasvabhāvā). The variety of experience is not a variety of natures — feeling-states compose one world-body. Then the address: manifestation (vyaktiṃ) you see (paśyasi), of whomever (yasya yasya), suddenly (sahasā) — having regarded (avekṣya) that-that (tattattad) one-self-natured (ekātmatā) consciousness-form (saṃvidrūpam) — why (kiṃ na) do you delight (ramase), full (nirbharaḥ) of that contemplation (tadbhāvanā)? Regard the variety as one-self-natured consciousness-form — and delight is the only consistent response.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The address (paśyasi... ramase) is second-person singular throughout; no addressee beyond the reader-practitioner is named.
2. Bhāvanānirbharaḥ (contemplation-full) is read as the state from which delight follows, not a separate injunction.

## 4. Decision for L2

Carry list-then-challenge: variety stated as one-natured, then the direct why-not-delight. L2 must keep the full catalogue and the suddenness (sahasā) of seeing. Carry the one-self-natured consciousness-form into verse 6, where things are denied truth apart from it.

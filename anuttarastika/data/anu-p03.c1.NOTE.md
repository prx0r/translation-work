# C1 NOTE — anu-p03.c1

Moved from C1 (pristine rasa-evocative translation only):

## SUMMARY

Worship, worshipper, worshipped — what speech of this difference in the unsurpassed? Transition: of whom, by whom, who enters? Māyā, not different from consciousness-nonduality, stands as other — alas. All is pure own-experience-nature; vain thought, don't.

## FUNCTION

Establishes worship-difference as Māyā: in the unsurpassed, no talk of adorer/adorable/adoration; Māyā stands as other without being different from consciousness-nonduality.

## KEY TERMS

* **pūjapūjakapūjya** — worship-worshipper-worshipped: the difference-course denied. * **bhedasaraṇi** — difference-course: the path of differentiation. * **anuttara** — unsurpassed: the standpoint. * **saṃkrāma** — transition: spiritual progress denied. * **praveśakrama** — entry-course: the succession of penetrating into Self denied. * **māyā** — standing-apart: Māyā as other without being different. * **cidadvaya** — consciousness-nonduality: what Māyā is not different from. * **paratayā** — in otherness: Māyā's standing. * **svānubhavasvabhāvavimala** — pure own-experience-nature: what all is. * **cintā** — thought: in vain. * **vṛthā** — in vain: the prohibition.

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Third verse: worship-difference dissolved.

## EXPLANATION

Worship, worshipper, worshipped — what speech of this difference in the unsurpassed? Transition: of whom, by whom effected, and who is the entry-course? In the oneness of anuttara, what talk can there be, and what differentiated path of adorer, adored, adoration? To whom and by what means could a progression function, or what could constitute the succession of penetrating into the Self? Māyā, not different from consciousness-nonduality, in otherness, though different — alas, stands. Wonder of wonders: though it appears differentiated, this illusion is no other than consciousness, one without a second. All is pure own-experience-nature. Thought in vain — do not. Its structure is the argument: question (what difference?), then the Māyā-clause (stands as other without being different), then the conclusion (all is pure own-experience-nature; vain thought, don't).

## BOUNDARY

Whether 'māyeyam' is 'this Māyā' or 'Māyā, this [illusion]' is unresolved. Whether 'bhinnā api' (though different) modifies Māyā or the illusion is unresolved.

## RELATED

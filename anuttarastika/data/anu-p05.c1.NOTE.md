# C1 NOTE — anu-p05.c1

Moved from C1 (pristine rasa-evocative translation only):

## SUMMARY

Passions through misery — the whole catalogue of feeling-states, universe-bodied — are not different-natured. Whatever manifestation you suddenly see, regard as one-self-natured consciousness-form: why not delight, contemplation-full?

## FUNCTION

Establishes variety as one nature: feeling-states compose one world-body; regard is the whole practice; delight is the consistent response.

## KEY TERMS

* **rāga** — passion. * **dveṣa** — aversion. * **sukha** — pleasure. * **asukha** — displeasure. * **udaya** — rise. * **laya** — dissolution. * **ahaṃkāra** — I-ness. * **dainya** — misery. * **viśvavapus** — universe-bodied: the feeling-states compose one world-body. * **bhinnasvabhāva** — different-natured: denied. * **vyakti** — manifestation: what is seen. * **sahasā** — suddenly: the sudden seeing. * **ekātmatāsaṃvidrūpa** — one-self-natured consciousness-form: what variety is. * **avekṣya** — having regarded: the practice. * **ramase** — you delight: the response. * **bhāvanānirbhara** — contemplation-full: the state.

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Fifth verse: variety is one nature; why not delight?

## EXPLANATION

The list first: passion through misery and the rest (ādayaḥ) — those things (bhāvāḥ) shining-forth (pravibhānti), universe-bodied (viśvavapuṣo) — are not (na te) different-natured (bhinnasvabhāvāḥ). Feeling-states compose one world-body; variety of experience is not variety of nature. Then the address: manifestation (vyaktim) you see (paśyasi), of whomever (yasya yasya), suddenly (sahasā) — having regarded (avekṣya) that-that (tattattad) one-self-natured (ekātmatā) consciousness-form (saṃvidrūpam) — why (kiṃ na) do you delight (ramase), full (nirbharaḥ) of that contemplation (tadbhāvanā)? Regard is the whole practice here: see variety as one-self-natured consciousness-form — and delight is the only consistent response.

## BOUNDARY

Whether 'ekātmatāsaṃvidrūpa' is one compound or several is unresolved (kept as printed). Whether 'ramase' is indicative or imperative is unresolved.

## RELATED

* Anuttarāṣṭikā v.4 — bliss is the forgotten treasure. * Anuttarāṣṭikā v.6 — things lack truth; awaken. * Tantrāloka — variety as the Absolute's self-manifestation.

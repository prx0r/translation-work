# C1 NOTE — anu-p07.c1

Moved from C1 (pristine rasa-evocative translation only):

## SUMMARY

Of things no arising; naturally they shine by your contemplation, even the non-true partaking of truth for a moment. This universe-glory is will-born from you, birth from elsewhere denied; hence you shine in worlds — one, yet many-selfed.

## FUNCTION

Establishes no arising and will-born glory: things shine by your contemplation; even the non-true borrows momentary truth; the universe-glory is will-born, not from elsewhere.

## KEY TERMS

* **samudbhava** — arising: denied for things. * **sahaja** — natural: it is natural that things shine by contemplation. * **tvadbhāvitā** — by your contemplation: the cause of shining. * **niḥsatya** — non-true: things are non-true. * **anubhavabhrānti** — experience-delusion: how the non-true borrows truth. * **kṣaṇa** — moment: the brevity of borrowed truth. * **satyatā** — truth: partaken, not owned. * **viśvamahimā** — universe-glory: will-born from you. * **tvatsaṅkalpaja** — will-born from you: the origin. * **anyataḥ** — from elsewhere: denied. * **vibhava** — splendor: by which you shine. * **bhuvana** — worlds: where you shine. * **eka** — one: the Absolute. * **anekātmaka** — many-selfed: multiplicity within oneness.

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Seventh verse: no arising; will-born glory.

## EXPLANATION

No-arising first: of things (bhāvānām) no arising (na samudbhavaḥ) is — natural (sahajaḥ: it is natural that), by your contemplation (tvadbhāvitā) they shine (bhānti); non-true (niḥsatyāḥ) too (api), truth (satyatām) they partake (bhajanti) by experience-delusion (anubhavabhrāntyā) for a moment (kṣaṇam) — even the false borrows a moment's truth from experience's delusion. Then the origin: this universe-glory (eṣa viśvamahimā) will-born from you (tvatsaṅkalpajaḥ); its birth (janma) from elsewhere (anyataḥ) is not (nāsti). Hence (tasmāt) you shine (bhāsi) by splendor (vibhavena) in worlds (bhuvaneṣu) — one (ekaḥ), though (api), many-selfed (anekātmakaḥ). One, yet many-selfed: nonduality without cancelling multiplicity. Its structure is the argument: no-arising (denial), then origin (will-born), then shining (one, yet many).

## BOUNDARY

Whether 'sahaja' means 'natural' or 'innate' is unresolved. Whether 'anubhavabhrānti' means experience's delusion or delusion of experience is unresolved. Whether 'ekopy anekātmakaḥ' means 'one, though many-selfed' or 'one, yet (becoming) many-selfed' is unresolved.

## RELATED

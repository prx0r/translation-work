# Bhairavānukaraṇastotra bhair-p01 — ARGUMENT MAP (Consciousness-Bhairava as sole reality)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 bhair-p01 = vv. 1–10. Lore: Gnoli 1958 (East and West 9.3, pp. 451–455); Timalsina (Vimarśa Foundation, translation); Kṣemarāja (11th c., Abhinavagupta's disciple, author of Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya, Śivasūtravimarśinī, Spandanirṇaya). Key: Cidbhairava = cit (consciousness) + Bhairava = consciousness as Bhairava, the self-luminous awareness. The stotra connects to Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya, Śivasūtra, Spandakārikā, Vijñānabhairava Tantra, Tantrāloka. The fierce iconography (skull, bones, elephant skin, sword, noose, goad, bow, trident, drum, vajra, staff, mace, axe, ash, cremation ground) is read as philosophical teaching: worldly bonds and distinctions have been cast away. Verses 3–6: no distinction between praised, praiser, and praise; Bhairava's visible form is a "drawing" (rekhāpuruṣa) of the formless highest self; the devotee enters it without conceptualization.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

The stotra opens by saluting Consciousness-Bhairava and urging refuge in consciousness rather than wandering in vain. The key doctrine: there is no real distinction between praised, praiser, and praise — every form is consciousness in varied appearance. The devotee bows to the principle and its form, enters it without conceptualization, and recognizes the body as a temple where Maheśa, the mass of consciousness, is praised. It closes by celebrating the powers of consciousness, bliss, will, cognition, and action, and by naming the limbs and seats up to Sadāśiva as Bhairava's own.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — salutation and refuge (v. 1):**
> *oṃ namaś cidbhairavāya | citibhūmisamāśrayaṇāś citibhūtiṃ śrayata mā mudhā bhramata | prathayann iti bhaktimatāṃ jayati śivo 'nugrahapravaṇaḥ*
[i.e. Oṃ, salutation to Consciousness-Bhairava. You who seek shelter in the reality of Citi — resort to the existence of the power of consciousness; do not wander uselessly. Proclaiming thus, Śiva, intent on bestowing grace to devotees, is victorious.] The opening: refuge in consciousness, not in wandering. Śiva is victorious as the bestower of grace.

**Step 1 — the supreme form (v. 2):**
> *cidbhairavam eva paraṃ paramāmṛtarūpam ekam atidīptam | ullasitakaraṇacakragrastasamastaṃ śivaṃ vande*
[i.e. I venerate Śiva, the supreme Bhairava of the character of consciousness, solitary, of the form of supreme nectar, radiant, who has devoured the entire existence with the realm of senses streaming out.] Śiva as Cidbhairava: consciousness itself, supreme, solitary, radiant, all-devouring.

**Step 2 — no distinction (v. 3):**
> *stutyaḥ stotā stutir iti yad api vibhinnaṃ na kiṃcid astīha | mṛṣati yathā yad rūpaṃ cidrūpatayā tathā bhavaty etat*
[i.e. here nothing distinct as praised, praiser, praise; as a form appears, so it becomes by consciousness-form.] The non-dual core: the threefold distinction (praised/praiser/praise) is dissolved in consciousness.

**Step 3 — the form as imitation (v. 4):**
> *vigalitasarvavibhedaṃ sarvavibhedātma cidghanānandam | yat tava tattvaṃ bhagavaṃs tasyānukṛtiṃ tvadākṛtiṃ vande*
[i.e. I bow to your form, imitation of your principle, consciousness-dense bliss, all-distinction-dissolved, all-distinction-self, O Lord.] The visible form is an *anukaraṇa* (imitation/copy) of the formless principle — like a drawing of a man.

**Step 4 — the drawing (v. 5):**
> *rekhāpuruṣaḥ puruṣaṃ varṇalipir varṇasaṃcayaṃ yadvat | tadvat vicitrarūpaṃ tvām ākāro vyanakti paramātman*
[i.e. as a drawn man reveals a man, script reveals letter-collection, so form reveals you, of varied form, supreme self.] The iconographic form reveals the formless, as a drawing reveals a man.

**Step 5 — entering without conceptualization (v. 6):**
> *tatrāvikalpam eva tvāṃ cidrūpaṃ samāviśya | vimṛśāmaḥ kim api manāṅ na hi tattvaṃ kalpanāviṣayaḥ*
[i.e. there, having entered you, consciousness-form, without conceptualization, we reflect not even a little; principle is not object of imagination.] Entering the consciousness-form without conceptualization; the principle is not an object of imagination.

**Step 6 — the guru and the heart (v. 7):**
> *svātmaprabhāṃ vidhāya vāgdevīṃ viṣayakusumakḷptārcaḥ | prathamonmeṣaṃ gurum atha vanditvā hṛdayam āviśya*
[i.e. having made speech-goddess one's own light, offering of sense-flowers, then bowing to guru, first manifestation, entering heart.] The practice: make speech (vāgdevī) one's own light; offer sense-flowers; bow to the guru's first manifestation; enter the heart.

**Step 7 — praise in the body-temple (v. 8):**
> *staumi vimṛśan maheśvaram ātmānaṃ svaṃ cidekaghanam | srāvitapāśakadambakam etasmin dehadevagṛhe*
[i.e. I praise, reflecting, Maheśvara, one's own self, consciousness-one-mass, in this body-temple, having poured out cluster of bonds.] The body is a temple (*dehadevagṛha*); Maheśvara, the mass of consciousness, is praised there; the bonds (pāśa) are poured out.

**Step 8 — the powers (v. 9):**
> *citānandecchāvitkaraṇākhyāḥ śaktayo jayanti vibho | sūkṣmasthūlabhidāptā vaktratvaṃ brahmamuṇḍabhidhā*
[i.e. victorious are powers named consciousness-bliss-will-cognition-action, O Lord; subtle-gross distinctions obtained, face-hood, Brahma-skull named.] The five śaktis (cit, ānanda, icchā, vit, karaṇa) are victorious; subtle-gross distinctions; face-hood; Brahma-skull.

**Step 9 — limbs and seats (v. 10):**
> *sarvajñatādiviṣayaṃ hṛdayādikam aṅgaṣaṭkaṃ te | sakalaṃ sadāśivāntaṃ pretātmatayā tavāsanaṃ svāmin*
[i.e. omniscience etc objects, heart etc six limbs of yours; all up to Sadāśiva, by corpse-self, your seat, O Lord.] The six limbs (hṛdaya etc.) with omniscience etc. as their objects; all up to Sadāśiva; the seat is by corpse-self (pretātmatā).

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the stotra's own devotional arc: salutation and refuge (v. 1) → supreme form (v. 2) → no distinction (v. 3) → form as imitation (v. 4) → the drawing (v. 5) → entering without conceptualization (v. 6) → guru and heart (v. 7) → praise in body-temple (v. 8) → powers (v. 9) → limbs and seats (v. 10). The key move is v. 3: no distinction between praised, praiser, and praise — every form is consciousness in varied appearance. And v. 5: the iconographic form reveals the formless, as a drawing reveals a man. Carry both into p02 (the weapons and ornaments as liberating signs). The tone is devotional-philosophical: fierce iconography read as teaching. L2 should carry the devotional voice, not flatten it into doctrine. The stotra's unique contribution: Bhairava's terrifying form is not merely described but philosophically interpreted — skull = world's essence taken, elephant skin = Māyā cast away, sword = splitting saṃsāra, etc.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Verse 5:**
> itthaṃ upoḍhabhavanmayasaṃviddīdhitidāritabhūritamisraḥ | mṛtyuyamāntakakarmapiśācair nātha namo 'stu na jātu bibhemi

[i.e. thus — my great darkness dispelled by rays of world-awareness — death, Yama, ender, karma-ghosts: Lord, salutation; never do I fear.]

Light first: thus (ittham) — great darkness (bhūri tamisra) already dispelled (dārita) by rays (dīdhiti) of awareness (saṃvit) consisting of world-becoming (bhavanmaya). Then the catalogue: death (mṛtyu), Yama (yama), the ender (antaka), karma-ghosts (karmapiśāca) — met with salutation (namo 'stu) alone, and never (na jātu) fear (bibhemi). Awareness burned the dark before the terrors were named; what remains before death's ministers is salutation, not fear.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Karmapiśāca (karma-ghosts) is read as hostile karmic forces; no further demonology is imported.
2. Restored 2026-10-31: T1 previously covered only the verse's second half; the dispelled-darkness first half is now glossed and parsed.

## 4. Decision for L2

Carry light-then-salutation: darkness already gone, terrors named, salutation offered, zero fear. L2 must not supply reasons for fearlessness beyond the verse — awareness plus salutation IS the reason. Carry fearlessness into verse 6, where it opens into bliss.

## The parable (canonical draft, ≈90s spoken)

> A man and a woman keep one garden in which light grows both kinds
> of flower alike — day-blooms and night-blooms, nothing opposed.
> The garden is his alone, bears his mark whole, and its plenty is
> his way of being lord; power never parts them — fire-sealed. She
> fills it exactly: no bare patch, no overrun. He makes and unmakes
> plantings at the same moments, everywhere. What he does is the
> hardest thing done in one — and you cannot fence his seeing with
> any measuring-line: the line stands inside him. Seasons change;
> the garden never departs from itself. Every kind grows — high,
> low, the far corners, inside out. Those who don't know call it a
> cage, and for them it holds. Three gaps in the fence — any gap is
> the way through. He walks out through a gap — and finds the gap
> is where the fence and the road were always the same ground:
> release as crossing, not as sentence. And at the gate
> hangs one mark — a trident opening like a lotus, bearing four
> powers. Whoever dwells on that mark dwells on the whole garden.

**Map (hinge):** day+night alike → p01 unsetting light holds bright
and dark · his alone, bears his mark, plenty = lordship → p02 +
p04 · fire-sealed never-parts → p03 · exact fill → p05 · make/unmake
simultaneous → p06 · hardest deed in one → p07 · measuring-line
inside him → p08 (measure-evading) · seasons, never departs → p09 ·
every kind, high-low-corners-in-out → p10 · fools call it cage, it
holds for them (nothing second) → p11 · three gaps, any counts →
p12 · knowing = release → p13 · fence-gate one, bondage-freedom
same ground → p14 · trident-lotus, four powers, dwell → p15
(emblem + closing injunction).
**Limit:** the cage is never drawn — the fools' reading has no image
space (the text gives it only as mis-knowing); the fence gaps stay
unmarked and unranked (p12's whole point).
**Rasa:** the work's sthāyibhāvas cycle vismaya→utsāha→rati→ullāsa→
**śama** — the parable follows: wonder at the light, exultation in
the hardest deed, love-wonder of the pair, play, and it ENDS in
śama: the dwelling-injunction, exactly where p15 puts it.
**Bar check:** two protagonists (the pair); the fools never
rendered; retellable; plain (śakti/paśu-free: powers = four flowers,
gates = gaps).

## The seal — statement

**The garden crowned with its own mark:** one walled ground in even
unsetting light — day-blooms and night-blooms sharing every bed, the
pair walking one path (one planting, one unmaking), a fence with
three plain gaps, a useless measuring-line leaning inside, a small
red fire-seal burned at the fence, and crowning the gate-arch the
work's own emblem: a trident opening into a lotus with four flowers
— four powers — the image the text commands you to dwell on.

## Composition laws (this seal)

The GARDEN is the subject (the pair walk inside it at human scale —
figures subordinate, as in Solis plates). Emblem = crown, largest
gold. Three gaps identical (any ranking would lie). No cage, no
measuring-in-use, no sun-disc. No letters (the emblem's "four
powers" are four flowers).

## Parable v2 (≈86s)

> A man and a woman keep one garden where light grows day-flowers
> and night-flowers alike — nothing opposed. The garden is his
> alone, bears his mark, and what shines in it is one gold under
> every design; power never parts them — fire-sealed. She fills it
> exactly: no bare patch, no overrun. He makes and unmakes
> plantings in the same moments. What he does is the hardest thing
> done once — and no measuring-line can fence his seeing: the line
> stands inside him. Seasons pass; the garden never leaves itself.
> Every kind grows: high, low, the far corners, inside out. Those
> who don't know call it a cage, and for them it holds. Three gaps
> in the fence — any gap is the way through. Knowing this is
> release: fence and gate one nature, bondage and freedom the same
> ground. And while you dwell on the mark at the gate, the four
> flowers climb the trellis and close — trident, lotus, four
> powers — the garden blooming into its own seal.

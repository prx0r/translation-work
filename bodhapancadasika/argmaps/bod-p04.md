# Bodhapañcadaśikā bod-p04 — ARGUMENT MAP (verse 4)

*2026-11-01. Source: T1 bod-p04 = verse 4. Lore: जगद्भरण (world-bearing) as Bhairava's defining epithet here — the Lord whose character is carrying the world; the mirror (ādarśa) is the verse's instrument: the whole appears reflected, not constructed.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Verse 4 of the Bodhapañcadaśikā (Abhinavagupta, 15 verses). Gloss: He (saḥ eva) Bhairava (bhairavaḥ) god (devo) world-bearing-characterized (jagadbharaṇalakṣaṇaḥ); in-own-self-vision (svātmādṛṣe) whole (samagram hi) by-power (yadchaktyā... yacchaktyā) reflected (pratibimbitam).

Sanskrit: sa eva bhairavo devo jagadbharaṇalakṣaṇaḥ | svātmādṛṣe samagraṃ hi yacchaktyā pratibimbitam

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Verse 4:**
> sa eva bhairavo devo jagadbharaṇalakṣaṇaḥ | svātmādṛṣe samagraṃ hi yacchaktyā pratibimbitam

[i.e. He alone is Bhairava the god, world-bearing-characterized; in own-self-vision the whole indeed, by power, is reflected.]

He (sa eva) alone is Bhairava (bhairavaḥ), god (devoḥ) — characterized (lakṣaṇaḥ) by world-bearing (jagadbharaṇa): bearing the world is not His act but His mark. Then the how: in own-self-vision (svātmādṛṣe) — the mirror that is His own seeing — the whole (samagram), indeed (hi), by power (yacchaktyā), is reflected (pratibimbitam). The world is not built or emitted here; it is mirrored whole in self-seeing, by power. Bearing = reflecting.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Svātmādṛś (own-self-vision) is read as the mirror itself, not a place of seeing; the verse makes vision the mirror.
2. No agent of reflecting besides power (yacchaktyā) is named or needed.

## 4. Decision for L2

Carry character-then-mechanism: world-bearing as His mark, reflection by power as His manner. L2 must keep "reflected," not "created" — the verse pointedly avoids emanation language. Carry the mirror into verse 5, where the goddess owns the grasping.

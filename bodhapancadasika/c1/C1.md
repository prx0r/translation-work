# C1 — Bodhapañcadaśikā (argument)

## bod-p01–p06 — the One and His play

**SUMMARY.** A light that never sets holds brightness and darkness alike; a solitary inward reality, which both bound. That reality is each creature's own-nature, and the world's abundance is power cast in the shape of sovereignty. Power refuses any parting from its bearer — one eternally, as burning is one with fire. Bhairava bears the world by mirroring it within His own seeing. His goddess reaches only toward seizing His nature, and fills everything precisely, nothing short, nothing over. At play together, they make and unmake worlds in a single stroke.

**KEY TERMS.** anastamita (unsetting), svabhāva (own-nature), īśvaratāmayī (sovereignty-shaped), tādātmya (identity), pratibimba (reflection), svarūpāmarśana (nature-grasping), pūrṇatva (exact fullness), krīḍā (play), yugapad (in a single stroke).

**EXPLANATION.** The six verses build the One outward. Verse 1 gives a container without outside: anastamitabhārūpa, with tejas and tamas both ending (yadantaḥ) in the inward one (ekaḥ antar). Verse 2 converts container to identity: sa eva — He alone — is sarvabhūtānāṃ svabhāvaḥ, while bhāvajātam is tasyaiva śaktir īśvaratāmayī. Verse 3 refuses the parting: na vāñchati vyatirekam, tādātmyam nityam, sealed by vahnidāhikayor iva. Verse 4 names Him Bhairava and gives the manner: jagadbharaṇa as lakṣaṇa, samagram pratibimbitam in svātmādṛś by yacchaktyā — mirroring, not manufacturing. Verse 5 gives Śakti her aim and measure: svarūpāmarśanotsukā, and pūrṇatvam with nālpaṃ na cādhikam as its definition. Verse 6 sets the pair in motion: krīḍārasotsukaḥ, sṛṣṭisaṃhārān vidhatte yugapad vibhuḥ — making and unmaking together, everywhere.

## bod-p07–p09 — freedom, the inert, and self-abiding change

**SUMMARY.** The hardest deed belongs to Him and has no higher — and it is freedom, lordship, awareness-form in one. The inert is defined by shining only partially; awareness stands apart from it by escaping all measure. Change itself — every making and unmaking — rests inside the free one, distinguished by His own power, abiding as His nature rather than departing anywhere.

**KEY TERMS.** atidurghaṭakāritva (hardest deed), anuttara (without higher), svatantratva (freedom), aiśvarya (lordship), bodharūpatā (awareness-form), paricchinnaprakāśatva (partial shining), jaḍa (inert), aparimeya (unmeasured), nijaśaktyupabheda (own-power distinction), svātmagāḥ (resting in own self).

**EXPLANATION.** Verse 7 equates five terms with a single eva: atidurghaṭakāritvam, anuttaram, svatantratvam, aiśvaryam, bodharūpatā — difficulty as rank, not obstacle. Verse 8 sets the contrary: paricchinnaprakāśatvaṃ is jaḍasya lakṣaṇam, while bodhaḥ is jaḍād vilakṣaṇaḥ yato na parimīyate — the criterion stated, not praised. Verse 9 draws the consequence with evam: asya svatantrasya, nijaśaktyupabhedinaḥ — distinction made inside freedom — sṛṣṭisaṃhārāḥ svātmagāḥ, svarūpatvena saṃsthitāḥ — change arrives nowhere but the self and stands as nature.

## bod-p10–p11 — the inventory and its cause

**SUMMARY.** Variety is totalized first: every extreme, in all three directions, across outer worlds and their fragments and inner pleasure, pain, and mind. Then its cause is named: missing this freedom is itself freedom under description — and that mis-knowing is bondage, dreadful to the deluded.

**KEY TERMS.** vaicitrya (variety), ūrdhvādhas tiryak (up-down-across), bhuvana (worlds), sukhaduḥkhamati (pleasure-pain-mind), aparijñāna (mis-knowing), svātantrya (freedom), saṃsāra (bondage), mūḍha (deluded), vibhīṣaka (dreadful).

**EXPLANATION.** Verse 10 catalogues without explaining: yat vaicitryam atyantam, ūrdhvādhas tiryag eva, then bhuvanāni tadaṃśāḥ ca and sukhaduḥkhamatiḥ — outer and inner entered on one list, teṣu pointing back to verse 9's makings-unmakings. Verse 11 supplies the cause and collapses it: yad aparijñānam etasya, tat svātantryam hi varṇitam — mis-knowing described as freedom — sa eva khalu saṃsāraḥ, mūḍānām vibhīṣakaḥ. Bondage is freedom mis-known; nothing second is introduced.

## bod-p12–p14 — the doors, release, and non-difference

**SUMMARY.** Three entries stand open — the sap of grace, the teacher's transmitted word, the Lord's scripture — and whichever one arrival comes through counts as arrival. Knowing reality to the bottom is release, a fullness belonging to the awakened, freedom lived while living, handed down as remembered teaching. Bondage and release both issue from the Lord's nature and do not come apart in Him — in truth, no split.

**KEY TERMS.** prasādarasa (grace-sap), gurvāgama (teacher's transmitted word), parameśaśāstra (Lord's scripture), yasmāt kasmāt (from whichever), upāgata (arrived), parijñāna (knowing-through), mokṣa (release), pūrṇatva (fullness), prabuddha (awakened), jīvanmukti (freedom-while-living), smṛtā (remembered), bandha-vimokṣa (bondage-release), tattvataḥ (in truth).

**EXPLANATION.** Verse 12 refuses to rank the doors: tatprasādarasād eva, gurvāgamata eva vā, śāstrād vā parameśasya — yasmāt kasmād upāgatam. Verse 13 equates four terms: yat tattvasya parijñānam, sa mokṣaḥ parameśataḥ; tat pūrṇatvam prabuddhānām, jīvanmuktiḥ — sā smṛtā. Knowing is not staged before release; knowing is release, lived, remembered. Verse 14 states non-difference twice: etau bandhavimokṣau parameśasvarūpataḥ; na bhidyete, na bhedo hi tattvataḥ parameśvare — of the pair, and in the Lord, with tattvataḥ holding verse 11's dread at the phenomenal level.

## bod-p15 — the emblem and the dwelling

**SUMMARY.** The powers gather into a single seat — will, art, knowledge, power on trident and lotus — and Bhairava, every thing's own-nature, is given for contemplative dwelling. The close returns to the verse-2 equation with the method attached: thus seated, thus dwelt upon.

**KEY TERMS.** ittham (thus), icchā-kalā-jñāna-śakti (will-art-knowledge-power), śūlāmbuja (trident-lotus), āśrita (seated-upon), sarvabhāva (every thing), pariśīlyate (to be dwelt upon).

**EXPLANATION.** Ittham icchākalājñānaśaktiśūlāmbujāśritaḥ — the whole doctrine compressed to an emblem. Bhairavaḥ sarvabhāvānāṃ svabhāvaḥ — verse 2's equation restated as the closing word. Pariśīlyate in the passive enjoins ongoing dwelling, not a rite to finish; the hymn ends in abiding, and the colophon couplet names whom it wakes: tender-minded pupils, at once.

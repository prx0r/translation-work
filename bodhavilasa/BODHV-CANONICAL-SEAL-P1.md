## The parable (canonical draft, ≈85s spoken)

> A village burns with fever — the fever of poison, no name on it.
> At the gate the Oṃ sounds, then two calls go out: one to the
> three-eyed, one to the maker of the five acts — the invocation
> doubling until it carries. The healers are counted: seven knowers,
> thirty-six of the counting — and the great Lord's release-glory
> starts its work. Those who tend the sick never stop practicing —
> that constant-tending is itself the practice that frees: one
> becomes full of unending bliss. The means are many ways: a middle
> opening at the village well where fevered thought tightens, decays
> and comes apart, thought after thought. And at the end, out of the
> non-dual, they pour a nectar of knowledge no one has refined —
> whoever drinks is quenched. The fever does not come back.

**Map (hinge):**
- fever of poison, no name → p05 *fever-of-saṃsāra-poison* (name
  withheld deliberately — the parable keeps it plain) [p05]
- Oṃ, then two calls: three-eyed + maker of five acts → p01 *double
  invocation: Netra, five-act Śiva* [p01]
- healers counted: seven knowers, thirty-six → p02 *cultivated by
  seven-knowerhood, by thirty-six count* [p02]
- release-glory starts its work → p02 *glory-of-release proceeds*
  [p02]
- never stop practicing = practice that frees → full bliss → p03
  *always-practice… liberated from all bonds, eternal bliss* [p03]
- middle opening at the well; thought tightens, decays, comes apart;
  many ways → p04 *middle-opening means… thought-decay-tightening…
  in many ways* [p04]
- nectar of knowledge, drinker quenched, fever gone → p05 *non-dual
  nectar drunk… quenched… fever-of-saṃsāra-poison* [p05, 2 verses]

**Limit:** the fever is never diagnosed in the tale (saṃsāra stays
unnamed inside the story — the name lives only in the map); the
numerics (seven, thirty-six) are KEPT as numbers — they are counts,
not terms (numbers are plain speech).
**Rasa:** maṅgala (the call) → vīra/adbhuta (count and proceed) →
raudra folded into the fever's heat → amṛta-śānta close (quenched,
nothing returns). The last line IS the p05 sthāyibhāva (amṛta-śama).
**Bar check:** one subject (the cup-and-pour; village = setting,
tenders = counted scale-figures); retellable; plain.

## The seal — statement

**The pour that ends the fever:** vertical village rite — bottom:
the fever-ochre village in heat-haze, its shrine-gate releasing two
gold call-rings; up the slope seven white tenders walk in counted
line to the middle well whose bucket-rope NEVER stops (the
always-practice made visible), grey thought-wisps loosening off its
rim (thought-decay at the middle opening); above: two hands tipping
a great ivory-gold cup, nectar arcing gold into a cool quenched sky
— heat-haze dissolving below the stream.

## Composition laws (this seal)

ONE subject (the cup-pour crown; village = setting; tenders = seven,
counted, small — never a crowd beyond the count). The bucket-rope
must read perpetually-in-motion (blurred rope / spray) — still rope
= failed practice. Call-rings and nectar-stream share one gold.
No letters (Oṃ carried as rings, not glyphs).

## bodh-p01 — Consciousness as free cause; contraction into mind (vv. 1–10)

**Salutation (vv. 1).** Oṃ, salutation to Netra, salutation to Śiva, constantly, maker of the five acts, manifester of supreme self-bliss. The opening salutes Śiva as the maker of the five acts (pañcakṛtya: creation, maintenance, dissolution, concealment, grace) and the manifester of supreme self-bliss (cidānandaghana).

**Core thesis (v. 2).** Consciousness is free and the cause of the universe, thus it is called. Of the form of freedom, the knower, and bestows powers or liberation. Consciousness (citi) is svatantra (free) and the hetu (cause) of the universe (viśva). It is of the form of freedom (svātantryarūpā), the knower (jñātā), and bestows powers (siddhi) or liberation (mukti).

**Unfolding (v. 2).** She, in her own ground, by her own will, for the unfolding of the universe; from various corresponding variety, from difference of object and subject. Consciousness unfolds the universe from her own ground (svabhitti), by her own will (svecchā), through the variety of objects and subjects (grāhyagrāhakabheda).

**Variety (v. 2).** The universe's variety is established by difference of objects; by variety of varieties and by number of high and low. The world's variety is established by the difference of objects (grāhyabheda) and the hierarchy of high and low (uttamādhamasaṅkhyā).

**Contraction into mind (v. 2).** From contraction of consciousness, mind; consciousness also, the great lord; like a banyan seed, there, the universal form, and that, know. From the contraction of consciousness (citisaṅkocana), mind (citta) arises. Consciousness itself is the great lord (maheśvara). Like a banyan seed containing the whole tree, the universal form is in the contracted mind.

**Consciousness and mind (v. 2).** Consciousness is the form of the self, pure, extremely immaculate; that same, attached to rules, mind, consciousness, is called. Consciousness (caitanya) is the pure form of the self (ātman); when attached to rules (niyamāsakta), it is called mind (citta).

**Rest and contraction (v. 2).** With object-directedness, awareness in mind has arrived at rest; from contraction as self-predominance, consciousness, mind, know the wise. Awareness (saṃvit) arrives at rest (viśrānti) in mind; the wise know that mind is consciousness contracted into self-predominance (saṅkocātmapradhānatva).

**Māyā-knower (v. 2).** Māyā-knower, consciousness-formed, as identity with that, of embodied beings, exists, attached to objects, devoid of true discrimination. The māyā-knower (māyāpramātā) is consciousness-formed (cidrūpa) but attached to objects (viṣayāsakta), devoid of true discrimination (sadvivekavivarjita).

**Contracted self (v. 2).** One constant, primary, mind, by kala, sattva, guṇa etc.; by seven knowerhood, by thirty-six principles. Mind is constant (sadaika) and primary (pratitha), conditioned by kala, sattva, guṇa etc.; through seven knowerhoods (saptapramatṛ) and thirty-six tattvas.

**Great lord (v. 2).** One, all-pervading, pure, of the nature of supreme awareness, the great lord; from non-recognition of that I-ness, liberation is situated in the palm. The great lord (Maheśvara) is one, all-pervading (sarvagata), pure (śuddha), of the nature of supreme awareness (sambodhātman). Liberation (mukti) is at hand (karatale sthitā) from non-recognition of that I-ness (ahaṃtāparijñāna).

## bodh-p02 — Liberation-glory proceeds; contraction and expansion (vv. 11–20)

**Liberation-glory proceeds (v. 11).** Cultivated by seven knowerhoods and thirty-six principles, he, Maheśāna, liberation-glory proceeds. The limited knower is established (bhāvita) in the unlimited knower (Maheśāna); liberation-glory (mokṣalakṣmī) proceeds through stages (bhūmikā).

**Its stages (v. 12).** Its stages, perceptions etc., all indeed not doubt; stages artificial, just as an actor assumes by own will. The stages (bhūmikā) are artificial (kṛtrimā), like an actor assuming roles by own will (svecchā) — not real limitations but appearances.

**The self assumes vast powers (v. 13).** Likewise indeed, expanded by bliss-will, the self assumes vast perceptions etc. powers. The self, expanded by bliss-will (cidānandasvecchocchalitabṛṃhita), assumes vast powers (vibhūtayaḥ).

**Contraction (v. 14).** From contraction of own power, in saṃsāra, becomes impure; "I am incomplete" thus; by various malas, he is defiled. From contraction of own power (cidvatsvaśaktisaṅkoca), in saṃsāra, the self becomes impure; "I am incomplete" (apūrṇo 'smi) — defiled by various malas (māyīya, āṇava, kārma).

**All-pervading yet contracted (v. 15).** Named māyīya-āṇava-kārma, all-pervading also, lord of world, all-pervading; just as great god does five acts, likewise this self also does, deluded by power. Even all-pervading (vyāpaka), the self, named by the three malas, is deluded by power (śaktimohita); yet, like the great god (Maheśvara) who performs the five acts (pañcakṛtya), this self also does the five acts.

**Contraction and expansion (v. 16).** Deluded by power, capable; from expansion, lord of powers is said; from contraction, contracted, as saṃsārin is called. From expansion (vikāsa), he is lord of powers (śaktīśa); from contraction (saṅkocana), he is contracted, called saṃsārin. The same self, by expansion and contraction.

**Māyā (v. 17).** Māyā is form indeed; somewhat, to perceive, intent; created creation should know; other power, preservation is considered. Māyā is form (rūpa); to perceive somewhat, intent; created creation (sādhyasṛṣṭi) should know; other power (śakti) is preservation (sthiti).

**Dissolution and grace (v. 18).** From dissolution, withdrawn, goddess; in unity of consciousness, grace is considered; from manifestation of that essence, concealment is called. From dissolution (saṃhāra), withdrawn (saṃhṛtā), goddess (devī); in unity of consciousness (cidaikya), grace (anugraha) is considered; from manifestation of that essence (tadekasārāprathanāt), concealment (tirodhāna) is called.

**The fivefold process (v. 19).** Thus always present, the fivefold process in order, known; the wish-jewel, that; from ignorance, like wood and clod. Thus always present (sadā saṃnihita), the fivefold process (pañcavāha) in order (kramāt), known (jñāta); the wish-jewel (cintāmaṇi), that; from ignorance (ajñānāt), like wood and clod (kāṣṭaloṣṭavat).

## bodh-p03 — Liberation by true reflection; consciousness-fire (vv. 21–30)

**Liberation by true reflection (v. 21).** By the power of true reflection, thus always, whoever practices, he is liberated from all bonds, full of eternal bliss, becomes. Liberation (mukti) is by sadvimarśa (true reflection) — not by effort alone but by the power of true reflection.

**Consciousness-fire (v. 22).** Consciousness-fire swallows; that well-awakened is; when then that pure, to be known, consciousness-fire, all-devouring. The consciousness-fire (cidvahni) swallows the universe; when well-awakened (suprabuddha), it is pure, all-devouring (sarvabhakṣaka).

**By this power (v. 23).** By this power, united, yogi marked by liberation, state attains, pure; that soon, no here doubt. By this power (etadbalena), united, the yogi marked by liberation (nirvāṇalakṣaṇa) attains the pure state; soon, no doubt.

**Consciousness-fire on gaining power (v. 24).** Consciousness-fire on gaining power even, universe shines, consciousness-made; wave of self-bliss-nectar, this risen, outside. On gaining power (balalābhe), the consciousness-fire shines; the universe is consciousness-made (cinmaya); the wave of self-bliss-nectar (svānandāmṛtakallola) rises outside.

**Self as Maheśvara (v. 25).** That indeed quiescence gone, by forms of being is manifested; own-self, Maheśvara, god, plays, supreme lord. That (the wave) gone to quiescence (śyānatā), by forms of being (bhāvarūpa) is manifested; own-self is Maheśvara, god, playing (krīḍate), supreme lord (parameśvara).

**The liberated liberates (v. 26).** Thus whoever constantly knows, he liberated, liberates also multitude of beings in an instant from terrible saṃsāra-bondage. Whoever constantly knows this is liberated (mukta) and liberates (mocayati) the multitude of beings (sattvavrāta) in an instant (kṣaṇenaiva) from terrible saṃsāra-bondage (ghorāt saṃsārabandhanāt).

**Bliss-powered (v. 27).** Whoever, someone, bliss-powered, he established in own state; thus whose exultant-rising. Whoever is bliss-powered (ānandabala) is established in own state (svarūpapadasthita); whose exultant-rising (samullāsollāsitodaya).

**Fixed in own nature (v. 28).** His fixed in own nature, in distraction even, that is desired; this by effort-power, by people, is attained. His, fixed in own nature (svarūpaniṣṭha), in distraction (vyutthāna) even, that (state) is desired; this by effort-power (prayatnasāmārthya), by people, is attained.

**From middle-center expansion (v. 29).** From middle-center expansion, and from bliss-consciousness, shines; from its light, divine vision, of form-knowledge, is manifested. From middle-center expansion (madhyadhāmavikāsa) and from bliss-consciousness (cidānanda), shines (prakāśate); from its light (tatprakāśāt), divine vision (divyadṛṣṭi), of form-knowledge (jñānarūpā), is manifested (vibhāvyate).

**Divine vision (v. 30).** Immaculate, radiant, pure, full of eternal bliss, supreme; and net of body-cutting, knowledge-vision expands. Divine vision is immaculate (nirmalā), radiant (suprabhā), pure (śuddhā), full of eternal bliss (nityānandamayī), supreme (parā); the net of body-cutting (nirdehacchedajāla), knowledge-vision (jñānadṛṣṭi) expands (vijṛmbhate).

## bodh-p04 — Middle-center expansion; upāyas (vv. 31–40)

**Middle-center expansion (v. 31).** In middle-center expansion, but, means, powerful state; etc. of thought-decay, contraction, expansion, in many ways. In middle-center expansion (madhyadhāmavikāsa), the means (upāya) is a powerful state (balavatsthiti); thought-decay, contraction, expansion etc. in many ways (anekaśaḥ).

**From thinking of nothing (v. 32).** From thinking of nothing, indeed, of thoughts, decay may be; from consciousness-awareness, playful power, that is attached, in realm of being. From thinking of nothing (akiñciccintana), thoughts (vikalpa) decay (kṣaya); from consciousness-awareness (cidbodha), playful power (vilasacchakti) attaches in the realm of being (bhāvamaṇḍala).

**Divine stream (v. 33).** There attached, divine stream, sees, of pure nature; contraction of power, this indeed is said by brahma-teachers. There, attached, one sees the divine stream (divyaugha) of pure nature (vimalātmaka); this is called contraction of power (śaktisaṅkoca) by brahma-teachers.

**Rest in bindu/nāda (v. 34).** Etc. of rest in bindu, nāda at end, beginning; and including fear, anger, disgust etc. grasping. Rest in bindu/nāda at end and beginning; including fear, anger, disgust etc. grasping (parigraha).

**Middle-center expansion (v. 35).** Thus this said, goddess, middle-center expansion; there capable, in days, thus obtains supreme prosperity. Thus this said, goddess, middle-center expansion (madhyadhāmavikāsakam); there, capable (śakta), in days (dinaiḥ), thus obtains supreme prosperity (parasampadam).

**Inner samādhi impression (v. 36).** Who inner samādhi impression wanders, of pure radiance; he by power of emission indeed, reflecting, and again and again. Who wanders with inner samādhi impression (antaḥsamādhisaṃskāra), of pure radiance (vimalatviṣ); he, by power of emission (visargabala), reflecting (vimṛśya), again and again (punaḥ punaḥ).

**In distraction (v. 37).** In distraction even, because his own form fully manifests; of yogi fixed in awareness, of eternal bliss essence. In distraction (vyutthāna) even, because his own form (svarūpa) fully manifests (samprakāśate); of yogi fixed in awareness (bodhaniṣṭha), of eternal bliss essence (nityānandarasa).

**Full of manifest bliss (v. 38).** That full of manifest bliss, strengthened by mantra-power; consciousness of full I-ness, thus of inner exultation practitioner. That, full of manifest bliss (prakāśānandamayī), strengthened by mantra-power (mantravīryopabṛṃhitā); consciousness of full I-ness (pūrṇāhantācid), thus of inner exultation practitioner (antaḥsamullāsābhiyoginaḥ).

**The yogi as lord (v. 39).** There that sporting yogi situated in status of mantra-lord; there capable, lord, just as plays, expanded by power. There, that sporting (vilasan) yogi situated in the status of mantra-lord (mantreśvarapada); there, capable (śakta), lord (vibhu), just as plays (krīḍate), expanded by power (śaktibṛṃhita).

**Freedom (v. 40).** Thus freedom of his, all-pervading, world-lord, enjoying various differences, unbroken in wondrous state. Thus freedom (svatantratā) is his — all-pervading (vyāpaka), world-lord (jagadvibhu), enjoying various differences (nānābhedajuṣa), unbroken in wondrous state (citrapadasthitasyākhaṇḍitā).

## bodh-p05 — Closing: non-dual knowledge; the yogaśāstra (vv. 41–42)

**Non-dual knowledge (v. 41).** This non-dual thus said, nectar of knowledge unsurpassed; whoever may drink, is quenched, his fever of saṃsāra-poison. This non-dual (advaya) knowledge is unsurpassed nectar (jñānāmṛtam anuttamam); whoever drinks it is quenched (chāmyate) of the fever of saṃsāra-poison (saṃsāraviṣamūrchanam).

**The method (v. 42).** This method of bliss of awareness for attaining Śiva, of great souls; of king named Kṣema, treatise extremely pure, thus composed by Śrī Kṣemarāja, named Bodhavilāsa, yogaśāstra. This method of bliss of awareness (bodhollāsavidhā) is for attaining Śiva (śivaprāpyai), for great souls (mahātmanām). The text is the extremely pure treatise (atyantanirmalam śāstram) of King Kṣema (rājñaḥ kṣemābhidhānasya), composed by Śrī Kṣemarāja (śrīkṣemarājakṛtaṃ), named Bodhavilāsa (bodhavilāsākhyaṃ), a yogaśāstra (yogaśāstram).

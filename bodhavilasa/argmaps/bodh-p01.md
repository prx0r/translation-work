# Bodhavilāsa bodh-p01 — ARGUMENT MAP (consciousness as free cause; contraction into mind)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 bodh-p01 = vv. 1–10. Lore: Pensa 1961 (Rivista di Studi Orientali 36: 125–134, standard edition); Kṣemarāja (11th c., Abhinavagupta's disciple, author of Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya, Śivasūtravimarśinī, Spandanirṇaya). Key: citi (consciousness) is svatantra (free) and the hetu (cause) of the universe; it unfolds variety from its own freedom (svātantryarūpā); it contracts into mind (citta) and māyā-bound knowership (māyāpramātā); liberation (mukti) is at hand through recognizing that I-ness (ahaṃtā). The text is a yogaśāstra (yoga treatise) — practical, not just philosophical.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Consciousness (citi) is free (svatantra) and the cause (hetu) of the universe. It manifests as supreme self-bliss (cidānandaghana), while in its own ground it unfolds variety by will (svecchā) and difference of subject and object (grāhyagrāhakabheda). Contracted into mind (citta) and māyā-bound knowership (māyāpramātā), the same consciousness appears bound, yet liberation (mukti) remains at hand through recognizing that I-ness (ahaṃtā). The text's core move: liberation is not creating something new but recognizing what one already is — the original freedom of consciousness.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — salutation and core thesis (vv. 1–2):**
> *oṃ namaḥ netrāya namaḥ śivāya satataṃ pañcakṛtyavidhāyine | cidānandaghanasvātmaparamārthāvabhāsine*
[i.e. Oṃ, salutation to Netra, salutation to Śiva, constantly, maker of the five acts, manifester of supreme self-bliss.] Then: *citiḥ svatantrā viśvasya hetur ity abhidhīyate* — consciousness is free and the cause of the universe, thus it is called. *Svātantryarūpā jñātā ca siddhīr vā saṃprayacchate* — of the form of freedom, the knower, and bestows powers or liberation.

**Step 1 — the unfolding (v. 2 cont.):**
> *sā svabhittau svecchayaiva viśvasyonmīlanāya ca | nānānurūpavaicitryād grāhyagrāhakabhedataḥ*
[i.e. she, in her own ground, by her own will, for the unfolding of the universe; from various corresponding variety, from difference of object and subject.] Consciousness unfolds the universe from her own ground, by her own will, through the variety of objects and subjects.

**Step 2 — the variety (v. 2 cont.):**
> *viśvasya tasya vaicitryaṃ grāhyabhedena saṃsthitam | nānāvaicitryayogena cottamādhamasaṅkhyayā*
[i.e. the universe's variety is established by difference of objects; by variety of varieties and by number of high and low.] The world's variety is established by the difference of objects and the hierarchy of high and low.

**Step 3 — contraction into mind (v. 2 cont.):**
> *citisaṅkocanāc cittaṃ cetano 'pi maheśvaraḥ | vaṭadhanikāvat tatra viśvarūpaṃ ca tad viduḥ*
[i.e. from contraction of consciousness, mind; consciousness also, the great lord; like a banyan seed, there, the universal form, and that, know.] From the contraction of consciousness, mind arises; consciousness itself is the great lord; like a banyan seed containing the whole tree, the universal form is in the contracted mind.

**Step 4 — consciousness and mind (v. 2 cont.):**
> *caitanyam ātmano rūpaṃ śuddham atyantanirmalam | tad eva niyamāsaktaṃ cittaṃ cid abhidhīyate*
[i.e. consciousness is the form of the self, pure, extremely immaculate; that same, attached to rules, mind, consciousness, is called.] Consciousness is the pure form of the self; when attached to rules, it is called mind.

**Step 5 — rest and contraction (v. 2 cont.):**
> *arthonmukhatayā saṃvic citte viśrāntim āgatā | saṅkocātmapradhānatvāc citiś cittaṃ vidur budhāḥ*
[i.e. with object-directedness, awareness in mind has arrived at rest; from contraction as self-predominance, consciousness, mind, know the wise.] Awareness arrives at rest in mind; the wise know that mind is consciousness contracted into self-predominance.

**Step 6 — māyā-knower (v. 2 cont.):**
> *māyāpramātā cidrūpas tanmayatvena dehinām | vartate viṣayāsaktaḥ sadvivekavivarjitaḥ*
[i.e. māyā-knower, consciousness-formed, as identity with that, of embodied beings, exists, attached to objects, devoid of true discrimination.] The māyā-knower is consciousness-formed but attached to objects, devoid of true discrimination.

**Step 7 — the contracted self (v. 2 cont.):**
> *sadaikaḥ pratithaś cittaṃ kalāsattvaguṇādibhiḥ | saptapramatṛkatvena ṣaṭtriṃśattattvasaṅkhyayā*
[i.e. one constant, primary, mind, by kala, sattva, guṇa etc.; by seven knowerhood, by thirty-six principles.] Mind is constant and primary, conditioned by kala, sattva, guṇa etc.; through seven knowerhoods and thirty-six tattvas.

**Step 8 — the great lord (v. 2 cont.):**
> *ekaḥ sarvagataḥ śuddhaḥ sambodhātmā maheśvaraḥ | saivāhantāparijñānān muktiḥ karatale sthitā*
[i.e. one, all-pervading, pure, of the nature of supreme awareness, the great lord; from non-recognition of that I-ness, liberation is situated in the palm.] The great lord (Maheśvara) is one, all-pervading, pure, of the nature of supreme awareness. Liberation is at hand (in the palm) from non-recognition of that I-ness (ahaṃtā).

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The banyan seed analogy (vaṭadhanikāvat) — the T1 gloss renders it as "like a banyan seed, there, the universal form"; the Sanskrit is compressed. The analogy: the seed contains the whole tree; similarly, the contracted mind contains the universal form.
2. The "seven knowerhoods" (saptapramatṛkatvena) and "thirty-six principles" (ṣaṭtriṃśattattvasaṅkhyayā) — these are standard Trika cosmology; the text does not enumerate them here.
3. The text is a yogaśāstra (yoga treatise) — practical, not just philosophical. The esoteric second sense (twilight language) is present but less emphasized than in Paramārthasāra.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the passage's own movement: salutation → core thesis (citi is svatantra, hetu of viśva) → unfolding (svecchā, grāhyagrāhakabheda) → variety (grāhyabheda, ottamādhamasaṅkhyā) → contraction into mind (citisaṅkocanāc cittaṃ) → consciousness and mind (caitanyam ātmano rūpaṃ, tad eva niyamāsaktaṃ cittaṃ) → rest and contraction (saṃvic citte viśrāntim āgatā) → māyā-knower (māyāpramātā) → contracted self (sadaikaḥ pratithaś cittaṃ) → great lord (ekaḥ sarvagataḥ śuddhaḥ sambodhātmā maheśvaraḥ) → liberation at hand (muktiḥ karatale sthitā). The key move is v. 2's *saivāhantāparijñānān muktiḥ karatale sthitā*: liberation is at hand from non-recognition of that I-ness. And *citisaṅkocanāc cittaṃ*: mind is consciousness contracted. Carry both into p02 (liberation-glory proceeds). Read it as foundation laid: free cause, willed unfolding, mind contracted, palm-held release.

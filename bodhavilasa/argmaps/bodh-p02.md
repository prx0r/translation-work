# Bodhavilāsa bodh-p02 — ARGUMENT MAP (liberation-glory proceeds; contraction and expansion)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 bodh-p02 = vv. 11–20. Lore: Pensa 1961; Kṣemarāja (11th c.). Key: the limited knower (pramātṛ) is established in the unlimited knower (paramātman); liberation-glory proceeds through stages (bhūmikā); the self assumes vast powers by its own will (svecchā), like an actor; from contraction (saṅkocana) it becomes a saṃsārin, from expansion (vikāsa) it is lord of powers. The five acts (pañcakṛtya) are performed by the self even when contracted.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Liberation-glory (*mokṣalakṣmī*) proceeds through stages (*bhūmikā*). The self assumes vast powers by its own will (*svecchā*), like an actor. From contraction (*saṅkocana*) it becomes a saṃsārin, from expansion (*vikāsa*) it is lord of powers. The five acts (*pañcakṛtya*) — creation, maintenance, dissolution, concealment, grace — are performed by the self even when contracted. The text's core move: the limited knower is not separate from the unlimited knower; the same self, by contraction and expansion, appears as both.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — liberation-glory proceeds (v. 11):**
> *pramātṛsaptakatvena ṣaṭtriṃśattattvasaṅkhyayā | bhāvitaḥ sa maheśāno mokṣalakṣmīḥ pravartate*
[i.e. cultivated by seven knowerhoods and thirty-six principles, he, Maheśāna, liberation-glory proceeds.] The limited knower is established (bhāvita) in the unlimited knower (Maheśāna); liberation-glory proceeds through stages.

**Step 1 — its stages (v. 12):**
> *tadbhūmikā darśanādyāḥ sarvā eva na saṃśayaḥ | bhūmikāḥ kṛtrimā yadvan naṭo gṛhṇāti svecchayā*
[i.e. its stages, perceptions etc., all indeed not doubt; stages artificial, just as an actor assumes by own will.] The stages (bhūmikā) are artificial (kṛtrimā), like an actor assuming roles by own will — not real limitations but appearances.

**Step 2 — the self assumes vast powers (v. 13):**
> *tadvad eva cidānandasvecchocchalitabṛṃhitaḥ | ātmā gṛhṇāti vipulā darśanādyā vibhūtayaḥ*
[i.e. likewise indeed, expanded by bliss-will, the self assumes vast perceptions etc. powers.] The self, expanded by bliss-will (cidānandasvecchocchalitabṛṃhita), assumes vast powers (vibhūtayaḥ).

**Step 3 — contraction (v. 14):**
> *cidvatsvaśaktisaṅkocāt saṃsāre malināyate | apūrṇo 'smīti vividhair malaiḥ sa malinīkṛtaḥ*
[i.e. from contraction of own power, in saṃsāra, becomes impure; "I am incomplete" thus; by various malas, he is defiled.] From contraction of own power, in saṃsāra, the self becomes impure; "I am incomplete" — defiled by various malas (māyīya, āṇava, kārma).

**Step 4 — all-pervading yet contracted (v. 15):**
> *māyīyāṇavakārmākhyair vyāpako 'pi jagadvibhuḥ | vyāpī yadvan mahādevaḥ kroti pañcakṛtyatām*
[i.e. named māyīya-āṇava-kārma, all-pervading also, lord of world, all-pervading; just as great god does five acts, likewise this self also does, deluded by power.] Even all-pervading, the self, named by the three malas, is deluded by power; yet, like the great god (Maheśvara) who performs the five acts, this self also does the five acts.

**Step 5 — contraction and expansion (v. 16):**
> *śaktimohitaḥ śakto vikāsāc chaktīśo gīyate | saṅkocanāt saṅkocitaḥ saṃsārīty abhidhīyate*
[i.e. deluded by power, capable; from expansion, lord of powers is said; from contraction, contracted, as saṃsārin is called.] From expansion (vikāsa), he is lord of powers (śaktīśa); from contraction (saṅkocana), he is contracted, called saṃsārin. The same self, by expansion and contraction.

**Step 6 — māyā (v. 17):**
> *māyā rūpaṃ hi yat kiṃcid ālokayitum udyatā | sādhyasṛṣṭiṃ vijānīyād anyā śaktiḥ sthitir matā*
[i.e. māyā is form indeed; somewhat, to perceive, intent; created creation should know; other power, preservation is considered.] Māyā is form; to perceive somewhat, intent; created creation should know; other power (śakti) is preservation (sthiti).

**Step 7 — dissolution and grace (v. 18):**
> *saṃhārāt saṃhṛtā devī cidaikye 'nugraho mataḥ | tadekasārāprathanāt tirodhānābhidhīyate*
[i.e. from dissolution, withdrawn, goddess; in unity of consciousness, grace is considered; from manifestation of that essence, concealment is called.] From dissolution, withdrawn, goddess (devī); in unity of consciousness (cidaikya), grace (anugraha) is considered; from manifestation of that essence, concealment (tirodhāna) is called.

**Step 8 — the fivefold process (v. 19):**
> *itthaṃ sadā saṃnihitaḥ pañcavāhavidhiḥ kramāt | jñātaś cintāmaṇir asāv ajñānāt kāṣṭaloṣṭavat*
[i.e. thus always present, the fivefold process in order, known; the wish-jewel, that; from ignorance, like wood and clod.] Thus always present, the fivefold process (pañcavāha) in order, known; the wish-jewel (cintāmaṇi), that; from ignorance, like wood and clod.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The "seven knowerhoods" (saptapramatṛ) and "thirty-six tattvas" (ṣaṭtriṃśattattva) — standard Trika cosmology; the text does not enumerate them here.
2. The fivefold process (pañcavāha) — creation, maintenance, dissolution, concealment, grace; the text implies them (v. 18) but does not list them explicitly.
3. The T1 gloss for v. 11 says "cultivated by seven knowerhoods and thirty-six principles" — the Sanskrit *bhāvitaḥ* means "established/cultivated," implying the limited knower is established in the unlimited knower.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the passage's own movement: liberation-glory proceeds (v. 11) → its stages are artificial, like an actor (v. 12) → the self assumes vast powers (v. 13) → contraction: impure, defiled (v. 14) → all-pervading yet contracted, like the great god performing five acts (v. 15) → contraction and expansion: saṃsārin vs. lord of powers (v. 16) → māyā is form (v. 17) → dissolution, grace, concealment (v. 18) → fivefold process always present, wish-jewel (v. 19). The key move is v. 16: the same self, by expansion and contraction, appears as both lord of powers and saṃsārin. And v. 15: even all-pervading, the self performs the five acts, like the great god. Carry both into p03 (liberation by true reflection). Read it as one self in two postures: bound by contraction, lord by expansion — actor-wise.

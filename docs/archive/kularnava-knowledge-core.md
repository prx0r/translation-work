# KNOWLEDGE CORE — Kulārṇavatantra (pass-2, researched 2026-11-11)

*Scholarship-backed. Every claim below carries its source. Where editions
disagree we record the disagreement instead of inventing precision —
numbering differs between editions, so ALWAYS key to our e-text's own
markers, never to a book's chapter numbers.*

## A. BIBLIOGRAPHIC FACTS (verified)

- **Date:** genuinely uncertain. Range cited by the *Concise Oxford
  Dictionary of World Religions*: **c. 1000–1400 CE**; standard survey
  (Goudriaan & Gupta, *Hindu Tantric and Śākta Literature*, Harrassowitz
  1981) treats it as medieval and **likely composite, grown over time**.
  A minority view (Lorenzeti 1992, via Gómez 2025) argues 7th c. — record
  as unconfirmed, do not adopt.
- **Sanskrit edition:** Tārānātha Vidyāratna, *Tantrik Texts* vol. V,
  general editor **Arthur Avalon (Sir John Woodroffe), 1917**.
- **English:** M. P. Pandit, *Kularnava Tantra: Text and Readings*
  (Ganesh & Co. 1965; Motilal reprint 1999/2017) — **readings, 11
  chapters of 17**, not a complete translation. Ram Kumar Rai
  (Prachya Prakashan 1983) is the fuller English.
- **Verse count:** 2058 in the printed edition; **2068 in our Muktabodha
  witness** (differences include 21 verses at the end of ch.10 present
  only in some editions — Woodroffe's note). Never cite a bare verse
  number without edition.
- **Status:** one of the most authoritative Kaula scriptures, quoted as
  *pramāṇa* by later Śākta and Śrīvidyā authors (Avalon; standard
  reference works). Colophon claims the printed text is 1/5 of a
  125,000-verse original (lost).

## B. THE DOCTRINE (what translators must hold)

> **kula = the Goddess/Śakti, dynamic reality; akula = Śiva, the still
> transcendent ground; the Kaula path knows and worships the two as one.
> One reality (Parāśiva); jīvas are "sparks of the fire"; liberation is
> recognition of what one already is — which is why human birth is
> pressed as precious and irreplaceable.** (Avalon's introduction;
> text's own opening frame.)

Consequences for glossing:
1. **kula has two technical poles** (Goddess/Śakti-as-reality vs the
   clan/lineage of initiates) — context decides; never a blanket gloss.
2. **akula** is not "non-family" in a privative sense: it is Śiva as the
   unchanging ground. Gloss as *not-kula (the still ground)*.
3. **Liberation = recognition** — every soteriological passage inherits
   this; do not translate it as "attainment".

## C. STRUCTURE — with an honest edition warning

**WARNING (researched correction):** scholarly descriptions place the
guru-teaching around **ullāsa 13**, dīkṣā **ullāsa 14**, and the
pañca-tattva/vessel material **ullāsa 5** (per the Sanātana Rahasya
survey citing chapter loci; Pandit's 11-chapter arrangement differs;
Avalon's 1916 introduction describes 17). My earlier draft's map
(guru 8–9, dīkṣā 10) was inferred from the e-text layout and is
**superseded** — verify against `prathamollāsaḥ`-type markers in our
witness before rechunking. Record the observed marker positions in the
work's `source/` notes; do not force either numbering.

Theme sequence that is safe to rely on (any edition):
frame (Devī's question of release from saṃsāra) → greatness of Kula and
ranking of paths → guru and lineage authority → eligibility and dīkṣā →
paña-tattva ritual (wine/meat/fish/mudrā/union) → kuladravya, cakra,
assembly → avadhūta conduct and conclusion.

## D. THE PAÑCAMAKĀRA — contested interpretation (keep both)

1. **Literal-operational reading:** the five M's as actual substances/
   acts in Kaula ritual (madya, māṃsa, matsya, mudrā, maithuna).
2. **Symbolic reading:** "true wine" as the nectar of union, etc. —
   "firmly attested in the Kaula and Śrīvidyā interpretive tradition and
   in Woodroffe's expositions, and consonant with the Kularnava's own
   anti-indulgence verses" (the text's "if wine brought liberation"
   passage is the primary anchor).
3. **Critique to record, not adopt:** Gómez (2025, *Revista Científica
   Arbitrada de la Fundación MenteClara* 10) argues the Pandit/Avalon
   English **sanitised** the five M's into metaphor — i.e. the symbolic
   reading may be a colonial-Indian reception artefact, not the text's
   plain sense.
**Translator rule:** gloss the verse's own words; where symbolic readings
are tradition-attested, put them in C1/notes, NEVER silently into L2.

## E. TRAPS (scholarly)

1. **Guru absolutism.** The text establishes strict verticality — "only
   submission to the guru guarantees liberation", regardless of caste at
   entry. Translate as the text's claim; do not soften or endorse.
2. **Edition chapter-count mismatch** (11 vs 17) — the single biggest
   source of false verse/chapter claims. Cite edition + verse.
3. **Composite text**: internal inconsistency is expected — do not
   harmonise contradictory passages; record both.
4. **nitiśāstra overlap**: one line of scholarship treats it partly as
   niti (ethico-political instruction) — relevant when a passage reads
   like general moral advice rather than tantric doctrine (do not
   over-tantrify ethics).
5. **Named loci for common topics:** guru → ullāsa ~13; dīkṣā ~14;
   vessels/pañca-tattva ~5; path-ranking early ullāsas. Verify per ms.

## Stotra 1
- First song of twenty; reader has nothing presupposed. Its thesis (v.7) is the premise of every later song's devotion-talk, and its polemic (vv.16–18) sets the collection's stance toward technique.
- It moves in three acts.

## Stotra 2
- Second song, after Stotra 1's foundation: where Stotra 1 argued (devotion needs no ground), Stotra 2 trains (naming opposites until they coincide). Read aloud, in sequence.

## Stotra 3
- Love-song as metaphysics: affection (praṇaya) does the work argument cannot — demanding, dancing, and bewildered by turns — so that the hardest doctrines (third beyond being, knower-in-known) arrive as lovers' speech, not theses.
- Third song: after foundation (Stotra 1) and litany (Stotra 2), the first intimate address — the second person throughout. Read aloud as the turn from praise to love-talk.
- It alternates demand and doctrine.

## Stotra 7
- It treats the mind as both patient and agent: dropped (1), axed (2), melted and blazed (3) — all passive verbs — then fed (4), skilled (5), melted again by sun (6): cure by immersion and nourishment, never by argument. Verse 7 escalates the claim socially (praise where gods wait outside); verse 8 epistemologically (frenzy as unmaimed seeing); verse 9 closes the arc with the boldest move in the set: possessiveness itself is preserved and remarried — the mine-thought hardens around devotion's family instead of possessions.

## Stotra 11
- Verse 12's cornered metaphysics is complaint, not theodicy — do not systematize it. Verse 10's cheating-sufficiency and verse 9's pearl-mechanism are images, not casuistry. The personal names in Lakshmanjoo's telling belong to the telling, not the text.

## Stotra 12
- The close (28–29) turns ontology into benediction and grammar into worship: being itself becomes the blessing form, and the nothing/something pattern becomes the final rite.

## tantrasara-a01/c1/C1.md
- **SUMMARY.** Abhinavagupta opens with a maṅgala invoking his parents — Mother of stainless art and new creation, Father of hidden splendor with five faces — promising the Tantrasāra as the plain-words distillation of the vast Tantrāloka, then enters the teaching: knowledge, not ritual, is liberation's cause, because it opposes ignorance, the cause of bondage. Ignorance is twofold — intellectual (doubt and wrong conviction) and personal (thought-natured, contracted; saṃsāra's root). Initiation can wash the personal kind, but initiation itself requires prior intellectual determination, so determinative knowledge comes first. Scripture alone fully authorizes, absorbing lesser revelations discriminatively. Own nature is one autonomous light — the same in all things, unbounded by place, time, or form — whose contraction is bondage (the atom) and whose expansion is freedom; autonomy itself is bliss, will, consciousness, knowledge, action. Re-manifestation runs sudden or gradual, with or without means — will, knowledge, action as the three approaches (śāmbhava/śākta/āṇava), and a fourth.

## tantrasara-a01/c1/C1.md
- **EXPLANATION.** The passage moves in four steps, each building on the last. First, the epistemic diagnosis: bondage is not a thing but a not-knowing, and not-knowing has two seats — the intellect that cannot decide (doubt, wrong conviction) and the person whose awareness is pulled tight into thoughts. Initiation, which removes the personal kind, presupposes the intellectual kind's removal, so determinative knowledge is principal; practised, it finishes the other job too, since thought-practice ends in thought-free awareness. For what you are — awareness squeezed into thoughts, but light by nature — is Śiva already. So in every case, adopt knowledge that decides rightly about everything. Second, the authority claim: scripture that counts is Lord-spoken, since it takes whatever lesser books say, keeps it discriminatively, and out-argues the rest. Lesser revelations free you partway; only the Lord's word — five streams, ten, eighteen, eight and all their branches — frees altogether. Its essence runs pañcasrotas → ṣaḍardha → Mālinīvijaya, whose content no gathering can exhaust. Nobody is free, or frees, without the nature of things spelled out — only pure knowing does that. And since man's highest end stands on practised knowing, this book begins here to secure it. Third, the ontological core: own nature can only be light (what doesn't shine can't be what a thing is), and only one light (nothing alien enters light, so natures can't differ; place and time are themselves light), and autonomous (illuminedness needs another light; none exists) — hence all-pervading, eternal, every form and none. Autonomy is bliss; wonder, will; shining, consciousness; grasp, knowledge; fitting every form, action — really three powers (will, knowing, doing). Contracted, that light is the atom; free, it is Śiva. Fourth, the fourfold means previewed: sudden or gradual re-manifestation, with or without means — will, knowledge, action as the three approaches, plus a fourth.

## tantrasara-a02/c1/C1.md

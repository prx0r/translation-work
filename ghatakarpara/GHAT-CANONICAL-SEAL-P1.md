## The parable (canonical draft, ≈85s spoken)

> A woman stands at her gate while the monsoon clouds arrive —
> separated from her beloved, and her whole speech is this: release
> the foreign love, or release me; what more is there to say? The
> groves around her shine for love's sake and not their own — every
> sprout is his face — and the flowers pick on one flowering shrub
> until even the shrub is hard to laugh at; she bows. The clouds
> thunder, travelers grow longing, and her sorrow grows endless
> with them — yet this is exactly what the lovers' festival honors,
> dear friend: the arrival of the clouds. At the end she pours water
> into a small pot at the gate's foot: pour for the poet whose
> wordplay would conquer even hers. She has said it.

**Map (hinge):**
- separated, "release the foreign love, or release me" → p01 *the
  separated beloved's voice; kill me — without him; release… what
  more to say* [p01]
- groves shine for love's sake, sprouts = his face → p02 *groves
  shine for Kāma's sake, chief of groves by sprouts* (grove =
  vibhāva) [p02]
- flowers pick the shrub, she bows → p02 *Kuṭaja, why laughed at by
  flowers? I bow — hard to laugh at you* [p02]
- clouds thunder, travelers longing, sorrow endless → p03 *clouds
  thundering make travelers long; sorrow grows endless* [p03]
- the lovers' festival = cloud-arrival → p03 *they honor the
  love-festival… cloud-arrival, dear friend, they bring* [p03]
- pouring water into the small pot, the conquering poet → p03
  *may I be conquered by which poet, by yamakas — to him I pour
  water in ghaṭakarpara* (the title's own vessel; yamaka = the
  wordplay that outplays the poet) [p03]

**Limit:** the beloved never appears (viyoga is the form — only his
faces in sprouts); the pot-pour is HONOR-water for a superior
craft, not a funeral rite reading (flagged: water-pouring carries
ancestral connotations in the culture, but this verse points it at
the poet — the text's own aim, kept as stated).
**Rasa:** rati-viyoga → karuṇā-adjacent longing (p01) → grove-love
(p02) → **vīra/adbhuta at craft** (p03: camatkāra at the yamaka) —
the parable ends on wonder-conquered, not on sorrow: her last act
is honoring the better wordplay.
**Bar check:** one rendered figure; retellable; plain (vibhāva/dhvani/
yamaka decoded: shine-for-love, never-told-message, wordplay).

## The seal — statement

**The gate, the clouds, and the pot:** monsoon vertical — below the
small terracotta pot receiving her poured water (the closing
honor-rite, the title's vessel); the woman at her gate mid-frame in
a rose sash, groves sprouting green around her, one flowering shrub
under flower-tease at the rim; above, great rain-clouds crowned
with a festival-garland, tiny longing travelers beneath them — the
love-festival that IS the cloud-arrival.

## Composition laws (this seal)

ONE subject (the woman; clouds = weather, pot = object). Beloved
STAYS absent — his faces are sprouts only (budding shapes, no
faces drawn — keep sprouts plain to avoid literal faces; his
"faces" live in the parable's line, not the picture). Garland on
the cloud must read festive, not stormy-sacred. Rain stops short of
the pot (two waters, never mixed). No letters (the yamaka itself
can't be drawn — the conquering wordplay is honored by the pour).

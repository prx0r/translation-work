# Ghatakarpara — ARGMAP passage 1 (verses 1–9, the lament)

*2026-11-01. Source: GRETIL (Abhinavagupta Ghatakarpara commentary). Lore: sandeśa-kāvya precursor (Wikipedia/owiki), KSTS 67 (Abhinava's vivṛti), Abhinava's attribution to Kālidāsa (rejected by Lienhard). Rasa: śṛṅgāra (erotic love-in-separation) with vira/adbhuta coloring.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Is the beloved's lament a mere complaint, or does it perform the erotic rasa (śṛṅgāra) through the
sandeśa-kāvya device? The poem's answer: the lament IS the rasa — the separated woman's
voice, the cloud as messenger, and the beloved's absence together constitute the aesthetic
experience, not a description of it.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Kāma arises (vv.1–2):** *manmathe ca hṛdi hantum udyate* — Kāma arises in the heart, to kill.
*proṣitapramadayedam udyate* — this, the separated beloved, arises. The lover's desire and the
beloved's absence are one event, not two.

**The accusation (v.3):** *nirdayena paradeśasevinā māriṣyatha hi tena māṃ vinā* — by the cruel
one serving a foreign land, you will kill me without him. The foreign land (paradeśa) is the
rival; the husband's service abroad is the betrayal.

**The release (v.4):** *anyadeśaratir adya mucyatāṃ sāthavā tava vadhūḥ kim ucyatām* — release
foreign-land love today, or your wife — what is to be said? Either abandon the rival or face the
wife's accusation. The dilemma is the lover's, not the beloved's.

**The messenger device (vv.5–6):** *cātakaś ca tṛṣito 'mbu yācate* — the cātaka, thirsty, begs
water. *duḥkhitā pathika sā priyā ca te* — the suffering traveler, she is your beloved. The
cātaka bird (which drinks only rain) becomes the figure for the lover: thirsting for the
absent beloved, appealing to the cloud as messenger.

**The cloud as messenger (vv.7–8):** *ambudaiḥ śikhigaṇo vinādyate* — by clouds the peacock-flock
is sounded. *kā ratiḥ priya mayā vinādya te* — what delight, beloved, without me today? The
cloud arrives; the peacocks cry; the landscape responds to the lover's mood.

**The close of the lament (vv.9):** *toyadāgamakṛśā ca sādya te* — thinned by cloud-arrival, she
today. *durdhareṇa madanena sādyate* — tormented by hard Kāma. *śokasāgarajale ca pātitām* —
fallen into sorrow-ocean's water. *tvadguṇasmaraṇam eva pāti tām* — remembrance of your virtue
alone protects her. The lament closes not in despair but in the sustaining memory of the beloved.

**The meta-appeal (vv.9 cont.):** *dravati ca kaluṣaṃ jalaṃ nadīnāṃ* — the rivers' water melts
the impurity. *kim iti ca māṃ samavekṣase na dīnām* — why do you regard me, not wretched?
*gambhīramegharasitavyathitā* — pained by deep cloud-rasa. *kadāhaṃ jahyāṃ sakhi
priyaviyogajaśokadāhaṃ* — when shall I abandon, friend, the burning of separation-born sorrow?

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Whether "sādya te" (v.8) is a compression of sādyate or a separate reading — kept as-is in T1.
2. The cātaka's thirst: literal (bird drinks only rain) or figurative (lover thirsts for beloved)?
   Both are live; L2 keeps the literal image and lets the figure resonate.
3. Whether the cloud is addressed as messenger or as witness to the lover's pain — the sandeśa
   device is implicit, not stated. Abhinava's vivṛti makes it explicit; the poem leaves it open.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed. The lament is understood: Kāma arises; the beloved accuses; the messenger device
(cātaka/cloud) carries the appeal; the close sustains through memory of virtue. L2 renders
each verse in plain English, tracing to L0 ids. The śṛṅgāra rasa is named in C1, not in L2.

# Ghatakarpara — ARGMAP passage 2 (verses 10–18)

*2026-11-01. Source: GRETIL. Lore: sandeśa-kāvya (Wikipedia/owiki), Abhinava vivṛti (KSTS 67).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Does the beauty of the grove serve the rasa, or is it decoration?

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**For Kāma's sake (v.10):** *madanasya kṛte niketanānāṃ pratibhānty adya vanāni ketanānām* —
for Kāma's sake, today the groves of abodes shine. → grove exists for rasa, not rasa for grove.

**Beloved as chief (v.11):** *tvaṃ mañjarībhiḥ pravaro vanānāṃ netrotsavaś cāsi sayauvanānām* —
you are chief of groves by sprouts, eye-festival of youths. → beloved IS the grove's beauty.

**Kuṭaja mocked (v.12):** *kuṭaja kiṃ kusumair avahasyate* — kuṭaja, why laughed at by flowers?
*praṇipatāmi ca duṣprahasya te* — I bow, hard to laugh at you. → poet sides with humble tree
over showy flowers. (Sandeśa-kāvya convention: messenger is lowly but essential — cātaka,
kuṭaja, cloud vs. showy elements: flowers, peacocks.)

**Nīpa released (v.13):** *tava puṣpanirīkṣitāpadehaṃ visṛjeyaṃ sahasaiva nīpa deham* — your
flower-looked-at body, I would release, quickly, nīpa, body. → surrender, not destruction
(visṛjeyaṃ = letting-go). Compound puṣpanirīkṣitāpadeham: "flower-looked-at body" or "body
looked at by flowers" — both readings live.

**Bee and creeper (v.14):** *madhunaḥ samavekṣya kālatāṃ bhramaraś cumbati yūthikālatām* — bee
kisses yūthikā creeper. → lover's anubhāva in miniature (bhramara = lover, creeper = beloved).

**Dust obscures (v.15):** *salilair vihataṃ rajaḥ kṣitau ravicandrāv api nopalakṣitau* — by waters
scattered, dust on earth; sun and moon not seen. → rajas (passion-dust) obscures luminaries.

**Peacocks cry (v.16):** *navāmbumattāḥ śikhino nadanti meghāgame* — new-cloud-drunk, peacocks
cry at cloud-arrival. → rasa intoxicates even birds.

**Cloud roars (v.17):** *sendrayudhaś ca jalado 'dya rasann ibhānāṃ saṃrambham āvahati
bhūdharasannibhānām* — cloud resounds with Indra's weapon, bringing elephants' ardor. → cloud's
power → elephants' ardor.

**Water falls (v.18):** *paridhīraravaṃ jalaṃ darīṣu prapaty adbhutarūpasundarīṣu* — loud roar,
water in caves falls, wonderful-formed beauties. → aesthetic close, not narrative.

## 3. Open (not invented)

- "puṣpanirīkṣitāpadeham" (v.13): "flower-looked-at body" or "body looked at by flowers" —
  both readings live; L2 keeps "flower-looked-at."
- Kuṭaja mockery (v.12): affectionate or satirical? Poet bows → affectionate, but mockery is real.
- Nīpa release (v.13): surrender or destruction? visṛjeyaṃ = letting-go, not killing. Kept as release.
- Sandeśa-kāvya structure: part 1 = hero, messenger, route; part 2 = destination, heroine's grief,
  message, identification mark. Our verses 10–18 are landscape (route). The identification mark
  (private incident known to lover/beloved) is not clearly identified; Abhinava's vivṛti would say.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed. Grove is vibhāva; beloved is sprout; kuṭaja mocked and bowed to; nīpa released; bee
kisses creeper; dust obscures; peacocks cry; cloud roars; water falls. L2 renders ghat-p02 in plain English, tracing to L0 ids.

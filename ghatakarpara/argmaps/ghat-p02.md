# Ghatakarpara — ARGMAP passage 2 (verses 10–18, the beauty of the grove)

*2026-11-01. Source: GRETIL. Lore: sandeśa-kāvya, Abhinava vivṛti (KSTS 67). Rasa: śṛṅgāra (beauty of the grove as śṛṅgāra's vibhāva).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Is the beauty of the grove merely decorative, or does it serve the rasa? The poem's answer: the
grove is the vibhāva (determinant) of śṛṅgāra — its beauty is not backdrop but cause. The
kuṭaja tree's mockery and the nīpa's release are the rasa's anubhāva (consequent), not ornament.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**For Kāma's sake (v.10):** *madanasya kṛte niketanānāṃ pratibhānty adya vanāni ketanānām* —
for Kāma's sake, today the groves of abodes shine, of abodes. The grove exists for the rasa,
not the rasa for the grove.

**The beloved as chief (v.11):** *tvaṃ mañjarībhiḥ pravaro vanānāṃ netrotsavaś cāsi
sayauvanānām* — you are chief of groves by sprouts, eye-festival of youths. The beloved is
identified with the grove's beauty: she IS the sprout, the eye-festival.

**The kuṭaja's mockery (v.12):** *kuṭaja kiṃ kusumair avahasyate* — kuṭaja, why are you laughed
at by flowers? The kuṭaja tree, whose flowers are small and unimpressive, is mocked by the
showy blossoms. The poet bows: *praṇipatāmi ca duṣprahasya te* — I bow, hard to laugh at you.
The mockery is affectionate, not cruel — the poet sides with the humble tree.

**The nīpa's release (v.13):** *tava puṣpanirīkṣitāpadehaṃ visṛjeyaṃ sahasaiva nīpa deham* —
your flower-looked-at body, I would release, quickly, nīpa, body. The nīpa tree (whose
flowers are eaten by bees) is the beloved's body; the poet would release it — let the bees
take it. The image is of surrender, not destruction.

**The bee and the creeper (v.14):** *madhunaḥ samavekṣya kālatāṃ bhramaraś cumbati yūthikālatām* —
having observed honey's season, the bee kisses the yūthikā creeper. The bee (bhramara) is the
lover; the creeper is the beloved; the kiss is the anubhāva.

**The unseen sun and moon (v.15):** *salilair vihataṃ rajaḥ kṣitau ravicandrāv api
nopalakṣitau* — by waters scattered, dust on earth; sun and moon are not seen. The dust (rajas)
of passion obscures even the luminaries — the rasa overwhelms perception.

**The peacocks cry (v.16):** *navāmbumattāḥ śikhino nadanti meghāgame* — new-cloud-drunk,
peacocks cry at cloud-arrival. The peacocks (śikhin) are drunk with the new cloud — the rasa
intoxicates even the birds.

**The cloud's roar (v.17):** *sendrayudhaś ca jalado 'dya rasann ibhānāṃ saṃrambham āvahati
bhūdharasannibhānām* — with Indra's weapon, the cloud today resounds, bringing the elephants'
ardor, mountain-like. The cloud (jalada) roars with Indra's thunderbolt; the elephants (ibha)
respond with ardor.

**The water falls (v.18):** *paridhīraravaṃ jalaṃ darīṣu prapaty adbhutarūpasundarīṣu* —
loud roar, water in caves falls, wonderful-formed beauties. The water falls into caves; the
forms are wonderful and beautiful. The close is aesthetic, not narrative: the rasa is the
point.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Whether "puṣpanirīkṣitāpadeham" (v.13) means "flower-looked-at body" or "body looked at by
   flowers" — the compound is ambiguous; both readings are live. L2 keeps "flower-looked-at."
2. The kuṭaja mockery (v.12): affectionate or satirical? The poet bows, so affectionate — but
   the mockery is real. Left as both.
3. The nīpa release (v.13): surrender or destruction? The verb visṛjeyaṃ (I would release)
   suggests letting-go, not killing. Kept as release.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed. The beauty is understood: the grove is the vibhāva; the beloved is its sprout; the
kuṭaja is mocked and bowed to; the nīpa is released; the bee kisses the creeper; the dust of
passion obscures the luminaries; the peacocks cry drunk; the cloud roars; the water falls into
wonderful caves. L2 renders the grove in plain English, tracing to L0 ids. The śṛṅgāra
rasa is named in C1.

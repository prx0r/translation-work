# Ghatakarpara — ARGMAP passage 3 (verses 19–21, the close)

*2026-11-01. Source: GRETIL. Lore: sandeśa-kāvya, Abhinava vivṛti (KSTS 67). Rasa: śṛṅgāra
resolving into vira/adbhuta (the poet's tribute closes the poem).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Does the poem close in despair or in tribute? The poem's answer: the close is a tribute to the
poet himself — "by which poet conquered by yamakas, to him I pour water in ghaṭakarpara."
The lament and beauty are framed by the poet's skill; the poem is self-referential, not
narrative.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Clouds make travelers long (v.19):** *utkaṇṭhayanti pathikān jaladāḥ svanantaḥ* — clouds
thundering make travelers long. The cloud (jalada) is the messenger; its thunder (svananta)
awakens longing (utkaṇṭhā) in the traveler (pathika). The rasa's vibhāva is now the cloud
itself.

**Sorrow grows (v.19 cont.):** *śoko vivardhati ca tadvinatāsv anantaḥ* — sorrow grows in
those, endless. The vinatā (those bent low, the humbled) are the separated lovers; their
sorrow (śoka) grows without end (ananta).

**Love-festival (v.20):** *ratyutsavaṃ priyatamaiḥ saha mānayanti* — they honor the
love-festival with beloveds. The lovers (priyatama) celebrate (mānayanti) the festival of
love (ratyutsava) together — the rasa's anubhāva is communal, not solitary.

**Cloud-arrival (v.20 cont.):** *meghāgamaṃ priyasakhi śvasamānayanti* — cloud-arrival, dear
friend, they bring. The cloud's arrival (meghāgama) is brought (śvasamānayanti) by the dear
friend (priyasakhi) — the messenger is delivered.

**The tribute (v.21):** *jīyeya yena kavinā yamakaiḥ pareṇa* — may I be conquered by which
poet, by yamakas, other. The poet (kavi) who conquers by yamakas (the figure of speech
where the same word is used twice with different meaning) is the better poet. The speaker
yields to that skill.

**The offering (v.21 cont.):** *tasmai vaheyam udakaṃ ghaṭakarpareṇa* — to him I pour water
in ghaṭakarpara. The offering (udaka, water) in the potsherd (ghaṭakarpara) is the tribute:
the speaker pours water for the poet who surpassed him. The poem closes not in the lover's
voice but in the poet's — the rasa is the poem's own skill.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Whether the speaker of v.21 is the poet himself (self-tribute) or a different voice
   (the beloved or a rival poet) — the yamaka device and the self-referential close favor
   self-tribute, but the poem does not say. Left open.
2. The yamaka itself: which words are the yamaka? Not identified in the poem; Abhinava's
   vivṛti would say. Not resolved here.
3. The potsherd (ghaṭakarpara): literal vessel or metaphor for the poem itself? Both live;
   L2 keeps the literal.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed. The close is understood: clouds make travelers long; sorrow grows; lovers honor the
love-festival; the cloud arrives; the poet who conquers by yamakas is honored with water
poured in a potsherd. L2 renders the close in plain English, tracing to L0 ids. The rasa
shifts from śṛṅgāra to vira/adbhuta in the close — named in C1.

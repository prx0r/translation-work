## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk E)

1. **the cit/acid (conscious/inert) distinction** — the heart: the conscious-form is the
   self-luminous (svayaṃprakāśa); the inert is the not-self-luminous. The two are not of equal
   rank (no tulyakakṣyatā); the co-reference is only metaphorical.
2. **the dvairāśya (two-fold)** — being/non-being, conscious/inert; there's no real third class
   (the Sāṅkhya guṇas are constructions over the experienced).
3. **the grāhyagrāhaka (grasped-grasper)** — the difference-practical-activity of the known and
   the knower is māyā-made; in truth there's one knower.
4. **the Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi citation (v.21)** — "although the object-establishment is restricted
   in the breath-eight-governed jīva, even there it is established in the supreme self; by that
   self alone, how would there be the breath's restraint?" — the direct parallel to our audited
   text.
5. **the Sāṅkhya pradhāna refutation** — the principle (pradhāna) is a construction over the
   experienced pleasure-pain-delusion; its separate-ness is not-established.
6. **the memory/constructive (smṛti/vikalpa)** — the remembered, the constructed, and the
   conjecture all live off the prior experience (the vāsanā); the not-flashing has no
   determination.
7. **the self-luminosity** — "what shines by itself, not by another, is the conscious-form; the
   other is not-in-truth-conscious."
8. **the māyā-position** — in the māyā, the blue-and-the-rest are created (the conscious-unconscious
   appearance); the "I"-part is the full supreme-Śiva; the "this"-part rests in the "I."
9. **the Nareśvaraviveka** ("aṇavaś cidacidrūpāḥ") — the atomic ones, conscious-and-unconscious-form.
10. **the two Gītā citations** — 2.12 (the eternal "I was never not") and 7.10/10.37 (the sixth-case
    determination).

**The crux:** the consciousness/inert distinction and the two-fold. The self-luminous
conscious-form is the real; the inert (and the Sāṅkhya guṇas, the constructions) are not-in-truth
conscious. The māyā is the Lord's own power manifesting the conscious-unconscious appearance.
This is the epistemological-ontological core, engaging the Buddhist (memory/construction) and the
Sāṅkhya (pradhāna) in turn, and citing the Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi (our audited parallel).

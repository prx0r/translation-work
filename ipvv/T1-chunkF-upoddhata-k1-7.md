*Source: M00020 lines 3126–3700 (continuous). Base: Torella's ĪPK — the upoddhāta (preliminary)

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk F)

1. **The Buddhist opponent (Vijñānavāda).** Abhinava identifies the real pūrvapakṣa as the
   Saugata — the one who denies the self and makes the cognition-series (jñānasaṃtāna) the only
   reality. The Lokāyatikas are dismissed (they lack a position to refute with); the
   "one-self-with-hidden-lordship" and "many-non-Lord-selves" views are not the primary target.
2. **Kārikā 1 (svalakṣaṇābhāsaṃ jñānam).** The perception (nirvikalpaka, svalakṣaṇa-appearance) is
   one; the vikalpa (constructive cognition) is manifold and articulate (sābhilāpa); the two are
   not reducible. The vikalpa is word-related (śabdasaṃbandhin) — its object is the apoha, the
   general (sāmānya), not the svalakṣaṇa.
3. **The "eka" (one) word** — three readings: (a) rāśi-singularity (the non-constructive class),
   (b) own-form-internal-distinction (no inner class), (c) "some-one-thing." The ṭīkākāra fits the
   middle reading.
4. **Kārikā 2 (ahaṃpratīti as body-bound).** The I-cognition is not the constant self's
   manifestation; it terminates in the body-and-the-rest (śarīrādyavasāyinī). The prayoga: no
   self fitted with permanence, because of non-perception of what is perceptible-by-nature.
5. **The sābhilāpa (articulate) viśeṣaṇa** — the I-cognition is articulate; its articulation is
   non-deviating (abhilāpāvyabhicāra). The "ahaṃ" is not a bare inner grasp but an articulate
   cognition, which is why the avasāyinī-ending (tendency-to-end) shows its constructedness.
6. **Kārikā 3 (smṛti).** Memory proves the constant self: in the destruction of experience, the
   memory that follows the experience (tadanurodhinī) could not be, unless the experiencer is a
   stable self. The siddhāntin's own reply: the experience is destroyed outward (bahirmukha) but
   undestroyed inward (antarmukha) — self-perception (svasaṃvedana) survives.
7. **The three gatis (courses) for the sarvajña** — (a) constant awareness comprehending past-
   present-future; (b) the non-constant cognition-series with the samanantarapratyaya carrying
   the object's direct-manifestation; (c) the pure appearance of the wanted object. All three
   show memory cannot have an independent object.
8. **Kārikās 4–5 (the antargaḍu).** The siddhāntin: memory arises from the prior-experience
   impression (saṃskāra), so it lives on the experience, not on the constant self. The objector
   answers: then the "inside-stake" self (antargaḍu) has no purpose. The refutation: the outward-
   face part (the imitation, anukaraṇa) alone survives as the impression — the inward self-
   luminous awareness is not needed.
9. **The bhūrja-leaf simile** — the non-constant continuum still shows imitation (anukaraṇatā):
   the rolled-unrolled leaf, the arrow's velocity (vega), the river. The saṃskāra is essentially
   anukaraṇa (imitation), not a fixed dharma in a constant substrate. The kūṭastha-eternal is
   rejected; only saṃtāna-nityatā holds.
10. **Kārikā 6 (smartā as kalpita).** If memory is established from the impression alone, the
    rememberer (smartā) is constructed like the seer (draṣṭā) — a kalpita, not a real self. The
    I-cognition (ahaṃpratīti) does not reveal the self, only the body/continuum.
11. **The saṃskāra-dharmin debate.** The impression cannot inhere in the continuum (which is not a
    real dharmin beyond the cognitions); experience and impression are non-different by the
    support-identity (āśrayābheda). The one support is the constant self — but that very
    constancy makes it unusable in generating the memory (no viśeṣa).
12. **Kārikā 7 (the varṣātapa simile).** The sky-leather simile: as rain and heat have no fruit on
    the sky (the unchanging), so the constant self cannot produce memory. The leather-like
    (changing) is non-eternal; the sky-like (constant) is like the non-existent. The general
    principle (niyamavat): a construction is only justified if it is capable of its end.
13. **The crux.** The whole passage turns on whether memory (smṛti) requires a constant self. The
    Buddhist says no — the saṃskāra/anukaraṇa in the cognition-series suffices; the eternal self
    is a vain hypothesis (vyartha kalpanā). Abhinava (as the siddhāntin) is here giving the
    pūrvapakṣa its strongest form before refuting it later in the vimarśa.

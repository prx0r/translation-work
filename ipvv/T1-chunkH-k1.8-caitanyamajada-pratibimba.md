# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk H: the reflection (pratibimba) argument, the mirror & echo — T1

*Source: M00020 lines 4300–5077 (continuous). Base: Torella's ĪPK — the continuation of the
caitanyam ajaḍā discussion. Covers: the bhoga as the mixing of reflections in the buddhi; the
ahaṃkāra's superimposition; the saṃyoga/samavāya relation; the reflection-argument in full (the
mirror-reflection, the echo pratiśrutkā, the taste-reflection, the smell-field); the Sāṅkhya
buddhi's reflection of the puruṣa refuted; the vākyapadīya citations; the jñāna and jñātatā
(cognizedness) — whether the object's manifestness (prakaṭatā) is a real dharma of the object or
the cognition itself; the Nyāyasūtra adhikaraṇasiddhānta; the conclusion that only the cognition
(jñāna) is the cognizedness, the prakāśa. Max-effort T1.*

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk H)

1. **The bhoga (enjoyment) as reflection-mixing.** The enjoyment is the one-ness (aikya) operated in
   the buddhi-determination (adhyavasāya) between the person's reflection and the object's reflection
   — not a real property of the external object. The Yogasūtra 3.35 (sattvapuruṣayor pratyayāviśeṣo
   bhogaḥ) is the anchor.
2. **The ahaṃkāra's superimposition.** The ahaṃkāra makes the not-enjoying, faultless self appear as
   the agent-enjoyer (upakārakatva) — a figurative treatment (upacāra) that creates a new cognition-
   word-particular in the figurative-treater alone.
3. **The saṃyoga/samavāya (conjunction/inherence).** Whether the bhoga is by saṃyoga or samavāya is
   indifferent (ko virodhaḥ?) — any relation suffices; the practical activity (vyavahāra) of enjoyment
   is contact-made (saṃsargakṛta) in all views (paropādhau sarvā dhīr vyāvahārikī).
4. **The Sāṅkhya's bhoktṛ refuted.** The pradhāna transforms (pariṇamate); the jaḍa (inert) cannot be
   the enjoyer; only the cidrūpa (conscious-form) is fit for enjoyment, and its multiplicity follows
   from pratiniyama (Sāṅkhyakārikā 18). The bhoga's anāditva would block liberation — resolved by
   viveka (discrimination).
5. **The svacchatva (clearness) rule.** A clear thing reflects only a more-clear thing (as the white
   cloth reflects in the crystal, not vice versa). The buddhi is less clear than the person; the
   person's manifestation (puruṣaprakāśa) is all-sides-clear (sarvataḥ svaccha) and expects nothing
   (ananyāpekṣa). The buddhi cannot reflect the person — the cause-opposed perception
   (kāraṇaviruddhopalabdhi).
6. **The reflection-rule (anvaya-vyatireka).** A mirror reflects only the form-arrangement (rūpasaṃsthāna),
   not heaviness/touch. The pure dense form is needed; taint blocks it. The Tantrāloka 3.6 (the concealed
   lover touching the mirror) is cited.
7. **The Naiyāyika view.** Reflections are errors (bhrānti) — the repelled eye-rays grasp the own face.
   Abhinava's refutation: the own-face-as-other would make us indifferent to ornaments; the child's
   single-flavored cognition ("this") shows it; the reflection (pratibimba) is a real third class
   (tṛtīyarāśiḥ), not mere error. The vastutva (being) of the reflected is denied only by the 
   vyāpakānupalabdhi of the "this-being-that-class" (sajātīya).
8. **The pratiśrutkā (echo).** The echo is a real reflection of the sound, not a distinct primary sound:
   its faintness at a distance (the hearers' inversion), the Tantrāloka 3.29 and 3.34 (the sound-
   reflection verses), and the śruti-timing. The Naiyāyika's "sound travels" (śabdajaśabda) is rejected.
9. **The rasa (taste)-reflection.** The pungent taste of the pomegranate appears in the other's teeth-
   water (dantodaka) as a reflection (rasāveśa), proved by the avadhāna-awakening; the pomegranate-
   juice tasted-in-own-mouth objection is refuted. The reflected taste in the other's mouth is not
   experienced — because the field is interior (antardehavṛtti).
10. **The avabhāsana (manifestation) as the essence.** The reflection is essentially manifestation
    (avabhāsanasāra) — not place, form, measure, etc. (Tantrāloka 3.23, the darpaṇavidhi). It depends
    on the inner organ's operation (antaḥkaraṇavyāpāra) engaging the sense-organ.
11. **The Sautrāntika/Devala.** Even the Sautrāntika's cognition-reflection needs the primary non-inert
    conscious power (mukhyā ajaḍā citiśakti) to arrange the objects; the Devala verse (puṃsi kartṛtvam
    āropya) is cited. The reflection cannot make the inert do the arranging.
12. **The satkāryavāda (Sāṅkhyakārikā 9, asadakaraṇāt).** The self cannot be the material cause
    (upādāna) of the buddhi: the effect's being is in the cause-complex (sāmagrī), not in one material
    cause. The kaivalya (isolation) would force the person to gain/lose the effect-remainder (kāryaśeṣa),
    an impossible opposition of dharmas — hence the person is not the transforming material cause.
13. **The anubhavabala (experience-force).** Only one conscious self-luminous tattva (the experience)
    is established by experience-force; the "self-reflection" and "consciousness-shadow" of the buddhi
    are not separately given. The 'ahaṃ' is the self-luminous cognition itself, not a dharma of a
    transcendent person. The bandhamokṣa (bondage-liberation) is operated in the person by a superimposed
    relation — its karmapravacanīya force is scrutinized.
14. **The jñāna-jñātatā (the cognition is the cognizedness).** The object's manifest-ness (prakaṭatā) is
    not a real dharma of the object (else infinite regress: the manifest object would need another
    manifesting); it is the cognition itself (jñānameva jñātatā). The cognition is pratyakṣa by
    self-perception (svasaṃvedana), established by the adhikaraṇasiddhānta (Nyāyasūtra 1.1.30). The
    jñātatā is not universal (not toward all knowers), so it is the cognition, not the object-form.
15. **The hetu-grammar.** The long discussion of yasmāt/tataḥ in the anuvāda (restating) — whether the
    reason is the stated fact or its cognition. The ancient practice (cirantana) used the fifth case in
    restating (yato hi pūrvānubhavasaṃskārāt, ĪPK 2.5); the moderns live on prasiddhi only. The
    distinguished-reason (viśiṣṭahetuka) is the valid one.
16. **The svaprakāśa-jñāna as the self.** If the self-luminous cognition is established, is it the self?
    Abhinava: its place/time differences (jñeyopādhi — "here the pot", "now the pot") are cognized-made
    and body-limited (for the pleasure-cognition), so it is not the eternal-pervading-one self — but the
    cognitions as place-time-distinct flow in a continuum (saṃtati), which is the world-course. This
    sets up kārikā 9 (kriyā): the action too cannot be a real dharma of the object (the body) nor one
    single action in a sequential (kramika) one.

**The crux.** Chunk H is the deepest engagement yet with the reflection-metaphysic: whether the 
cognition can be a reflection (of the object) in a substrate (the buddhi) — the Sāṅkhya's view — or
must be the self-luminous consciousness itself (the siddhānta). The answer: reflections are real
(appearance-based), but the cognition is not such a reflection in an inert buddhi; the clear
(svaprakāśa) consciousness alone can arrange the objects (vyavasthā) and enjoy (bhoga). The
manifestation (avabhāsana) is the essence of both reflection and cognition; and the cognition is
ultimately self-luminous — the manifestness of the object is the cognition itself (jñānameva
jñātatā).

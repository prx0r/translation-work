# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk J: kārikā 11 (tena kartāpi kalpitaḥ), the relation refuted, the pūrvapakṣa's close — T1

*Source: M00020 lines 5585–5750 (continuous). Base: Torella's ĪPK — kārikā 11 (dviṣṭhasyānekarūpatvāt,
siddhasyānyānapekṣaṇāt, pāratantryādyayogācca, tena kartāpi kalpitaḥ). Covers: the consolidation of the
four-fold absence (abhāvacatuṣṭaya), the relation (saṃbandha) refuted by the bādhaka (opposer), the
apekṣā (expectation) as the supposed relation and its rejection, the pāratantrya (dependence), the
rūpaśleṣa (form-adhesion), the Bhartṛhari verses on the relation, and the conclusion that the agent
(kartā) is constructed (kalpita) — the close of the pūrvapakṣavimarśa (the second vimarśa). Max-effort T1.*

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk J)

1. **The four-fold absence (abhāvacatuṣṭaya).** The kārikā 11's reason (tena) consolidates four
   absences: (i) no other relation (nānyo'sti saṃbandhaḥ), (ii) no other action (nānyad asti — the
   kriyā is not a real separate thing), (iii) the relation's being-determination-absence, and
   (iv) its non-being-determination. These together make the agent constructed (kartāpi kalpitaḥ).
   The ṭīkā states this via the fifth case (abhāvāt).
2. **The bādhaka (opposer) for the relation.** The relation's universal absence is established not
   by the bare non-perception of the seen (which only yields "not here-now") but by the opposition
   (virodha) internal to the other's own acknowledged form — the two-form opposition. The citation
   (iṣṭaṃ viruddhakārye'pi deśakālādyapekṣaṇam) anchors the place-time-restricted non-being.
3. **The sāmānyalakṣaṇa refutation.** What stands simultaneously in two, with own-form-completion,
   is not one — like the two pots in two houses. Oneness = non-distinct-form (abhinnarūpatva); the
   two-established-ness (dviṣṭhatva) is pervaded by the distinct-form, so the one-relation is
   negated by the opposed-pervaded-perception (tadviruddhavyāptopalabdhi).
4. **The apekṣā (expectation) as relation.** The genitive ("of the king") prompts an expectation —
   the relation is said to be this expectation. But the expectation is only the helper-object
   (upakārakaviṣaya): the inert (jaḍa) cannot expect (apekṣā pāratantryaṃ ca na saṃbandho jaḍātmanoḥ);
   the non-transcendent helper would just be the cause (kāraṇa); the existing (sat) cannot expect
   (saṃś ca sarvanirāśaṃso bhāvaḥ katham apekṣate). The practical-activity (vyavahāra) is the
   construction (vikalpa) of the practical-activity-doer, not the relation-thing.
5. **The genitive and the effect-cause.** "Of the king, in the man existing" — the expectation is
   one-gone (the genitive) and the other-gone (the locative), not a mutual expectation. The
   mutual-expectation (parasparāpekṣā) and dependence (pāratantrya) are also refuted: the mutual-
   facing-ness (unmukhatā) is a mere word, not a shared relation. Even here the effect-cause-relation
   (kāryakāraṇabhāva) is what rests.
6. **The rūpaśleṣa (form-adhesion).** The "mutual form-obtainment" (paraspararūpā prāpti, like the
   fingers) fails: two forms cannot adhere; if adhesion is oneness, it is no relation
   (dvitve sati kathaṃ bhavet).
7. **The Bhartṛhari relation-verses.** The relation has no word expressive of it by its own dharma
   (nābhidhānaṃ svadharmeṇa saṃbandhasyāsti vācakam); it is followed only through its helper
   (upakārāt sa yatrāsti dharmastatrānugamyate); it is the power of powers, the quality of
   qualities. Abhinava notes this is a stotra (praise), not a nyāya-statement — hence not refuted.
8. **The kāryakāraṇabhāva not applicable here.** The self is not the effect; the cognition does not
   make the self its cause (via the yasminsati maxim — the infinite-regress); the action is nothing;
   so no relation here at all. This is the tatsamāśraya point: the verb-root says the action, the
   suffix says the agent, but no relation joins them.
9. **The jñātṛtā (knower-ness).** The experience-construction-memory-cognition-series alone exists,
   not a knower. The example: in the knower-ness there is the cognition but no other/relation; in
   the other-agent-ness there is no action, no other, no relation — so the agent is constructed
   (kalpitaḥ). The cognition-action too is refuted (as a kriyā, whether dhātvartha for the grammarian
   or pratyayavācya for the bhāvanā-Mīmāṃsaka). This is the firmest part of the debate.
10. **The close of the pūrvapakṣavimarśa.** The vṛttikṛt's phala (fruit) of the whole nanu-
    svalakṣaṇābhāsaṃ...tena kartāpi kalpitaḥ passage: "How can the self be the all-Lord?"
    (katham ātmā sarveśvaraḥ). The dharmin-unestablished (jñānasyaiva bodhātmanaḥ) and the reason-
    unestablished (kriyākāraka iti tāvanmātralakṣaṇam) are shown; the svabhāva-hetu for the lordship-
    practical-activity-establishment is absent. The Buddhist establishes the cognition-continuum-
    alone (jñānasaṃtatimātram) as the self. The pūrvapakṣavimarśa (the second vimarśa) closes here.

**The crux.** Chunk J completes the Buddhist's pūrvapakṣa: having refuted the self (dharmin), the
cognition (jñāna), the action (kriyā), and the relation (saṃbandha), the reason "from the 
knowledge-action-joining, lordship" (jñānakriyāyogād aiśvaryam) is entirely destroyed — neither the
knower-ness nor the agent-ness is established. The agent (kartā) is constructed (kalpitaḥ) like the
seer (draṣṭā) was in kārikā 6. Only the cognition-continuum (jñānasaṃtati) remains — the Buddhist
view. This is the complete pūrvapakṣa that the siddhānta will refute in the following vimarśas.

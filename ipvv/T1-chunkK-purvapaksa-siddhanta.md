# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk K: the third vimarśa, the siddhānta's reply, the self-luminous dṛk — T1

*Source: M00020 lines 5750–6300 (continuous). Base: Torella's ĪPK — the third vimarśa (atha tṛtīyo
vimarśaḥ) opens, and the siddhānta replies to the pūrvapakṣa. Covers: the maṅgala-verse of the third
vimarśa (icchāśaktisunirbharam); the reply kārikā 1 (satyaṃ kintu smṛtijñānaṃ pūrvānubhavasaṃskṛteḥ —
memory-cognition does not reveal the prior experience, only rests in the self); kārikā 2 (dṛk svābhāsaiva
nānyena vedyā — perception is self-luminous, not known by another cognition); the jaḍājaḍa (inert/non-inert)
distinction and its four-fold definition; the ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi (v.13); the pramātuḥ (knower) as
self-luminous and not inferable; the Prābhākara and Kaṇāda views refuted; the siddhānta's heart: the 
cognition-continuum needs a non-inert knower (pramātā). Max-effort T1.*

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk K)

1. **The third vimarśa opens.** The siddhānta replies to the pūrvapakṣa with the maṅgala-verse
   (icchāśaktisunirbharam — the will-power-full Bhairava). The reply is given by two adhikāras
   (jñāna and kriyā) in the seven verses (ślokasaptaka) beginning "satyaṃ kintu" and ending
   "smṛtyapohanaśaktimān."
2. **The prekṣāpūrvakāra style.** The style of the deliberate user: "not" for the wholly-to-be-
   refuted, "true" for the slightly-to-be-acknowledged, "true but" (satyaṃ kintu) for the 
   slightly-to-be-removed. The Buddhist's refutation of the Sāṅkhya/Kaṇāda cognition is agreed
   (saṃmatam); the 'kintu' asks for the more.
3. **Kārikā 1 (satyaṃ kintu smṛtijñānaṃ).** The memory-cognition, though born from the prior-
   experience impression, rests in the self (ātmaniṣṭhaṃ) — it does not reveal the first experience
   (nādyānubhavavedakam). The experience and the memory are mutually distinct (anyonyabhinnā); the
   memory shines only its own object in its own time (svakāle eva svaviṣayasyaiva), never the
   other's time or object — even in the trikāla-vikalpa ("was/is/will be"), the "this"-ness of the
   object is not actually experienced.
4. **The bāhyavāda refuted.** The external-object view ("the blue shines") makes the manifestation
   enter the object, making the manifestation non-existent (asat) like in the false glass-lump
   cognition. The vyāpaka: the object-embracing form is pervaded by the that-dharma-possessed-ness
   (taddharmāviṣṭatvam); the glass-lump-manifestation lacks it. Hence the know-ness (vedyatva) of
   the external is refuted by the pervader-opposed-pervaded-perception.
5. **The kevala svasaṃvedana.** The sensory/non-sensory division is inner-difference-made by the
   cause and own-form; where no unsādhāraṇa cause is found, the general (autsargika)
   self-perception is used. Even the four-fold-perception-speaker's cognition is self-cognizing.
6. **Kārikā 2 (dṛk svābhāsaiva).** The perception is self-luminous alone, not known by another
   cognition — like the taste-perception is not known by the form-cognition. The experience
   (anubhava), being perception-form, is not known by the memory-cognition. The impression does
   only the object-ness (saviṣayatā) by figurative use; it does not effect the knowledge of the
   prior experience (na tadgatiḥ).
7. **The four-fold rule (caturdhā).** The dṛk svābhāsaiva is explained four ways: (i) own-form-only-
   manifestation (svātmanaḥ prakāśaḥ), (ii) the not-manifesting-has-no-form (svātmā asya prakāśa),
   (iii) never-by-the-other-favor (svātmana eva prakāśate na parasya), (iv) the blue-form is not its
   form (paro'sya na prakāśaḥ). The reverse four-fold defines the inert (jaḍa).
8. **The jaḍājaḍa distinction.** The non-inert (ajaḍa) is that whose own-form is the manifestation
   (prakāśātmā), whose manifestation is rested in itself; the inert (jaḍa) is that which shines
   only by another (its manifestation rests in the knower, not in itself). The blue shines in the
   knower, not in itself — hence it is inert.
9. **The ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi (v.13).** "Thus in the self the asat-like ones are of the manifestation
   alone; the inert ones — the manifestation alone is of the own-self, with the own-other selfs."
   The inert objects are only the manifestation's — the manifestation itself is the non-inert
   knower (ajaḍaḥ pramātā), the great mirror-like unfolding (śaktivijṛmbhā) of Mahādeva.
10. **The pramātā as self-luminous, not inferred.** The knower cannot be inferred as a separate
    thing: the inference only touches the general (sāmānya), never the svalakṣaṇa; hence only the
    apoha/general-form can be the inferred "knower," not the real pramātā. The knower's own
    self-luminousness is not knocked away by the constructed (kalpita) inference — like the cow-ness
    (gotva) is not knocked away by its inference. The parapramātṛ is inferred only as the 
    body-entered manifestation (the other's operation), which actually strengthens the 
    self-luminous-ness.
11. **The Prābhākara and Kaṇāda refuted.** The Prābhākara's "the self is not inert" is reconciled:
    the self shines not as "this" (idam) but in its own way; the aparokṣatā (non-transcendence) is
    the self-luminous-ness (svaprakāśātmatā), not a third manner (which would be the non-being). The
    Kaṇāda's "the cognition is a quality born in the self; the self is inferred by the mind" is
    refuted: the cognition-arising either produces the object's manifest-ness (which is a refuted
    object-dharma) or IS that manifest-ness (the cognition = the manifesting-ness); the object
    cannot be perceptible without its own manifestation.
12. **The svaprakāśa's own inference.** Even though the cognition is non-transcendent with the
    knower, its "being inferred" (by the other's operation) is only the constructed (kalpita) —
    it does not make the supreme-object self-luminous-ness broken. The Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi 20
    (yadyapy arthasthitiḥ) anchors: the object-establishment even in the breath-governed jīva is
    in the supreme self.
13. **The close.** The pramātā is necessarily self-luminous (svaprakāśa) — otherwise there is no
    knower-ness at all (pramātṛtāyā eva ayogāt). The cognition-continuum requires the non-inert
    knower; the Vivṛti's middle passage (the jaḍājaḍa investigation) is the ground for this.

**The crux.** Chunk K is the siddhānta's heart: the reply to the Buddhist pūrvapakṣa. The cognition
is self-luminous (dṛk svābhāsaiva), not known by another cognition; the memory does not reveal the
prior experience but rests in the self. The inert/non-inert distinction grounds the whole: the 
non-inert is the self-luminous manifestation itself, the inert is what shines only in another. The
self-luminous manifestation is the ajaḍa pramātā (the non-inert knower) — the unfolding of Mahādeva's
power. This answers the Buddhist's cognition-continuum: a continuum of non-self-luminous cognitions
cannot explain the knower-ness, the memory, or the object's manifestation.

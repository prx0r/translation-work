## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-A)

1. **The smṛti as the Lord's power.** The fourth vimarśa opens by establishing the memory (smṛti) as
   the Lord's own power (smṛtiśakti) — the paramaṃ svātantryam (supreme freedom). The order (jñāna →
   smṛti → apohana) is stated, but the smṛti is taken first because it was the one "not-if" (na cet)
   established in the previous siddhānta (Vol 1) — the prasaṅga-reversal gives the self-Lord's
   establishment, and the smṛti is that atiśayam abhijñānam.
2. **Kārikā 1 (sa hi pūrvānubhūtārthopalabdhā).** The free-one (svairī) Maheśvara, being the perceiver
   of the prior-experienced object even in the other time, reflecting "that" (sa iti), remembers. The
   key: the prior-later both-times are the vimarśa's two times; the vimarśa's freedom (svairitva) is
   the paramaṃ svātantryam. The Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi, the Divākara citations, and the dhyānādibhāvaṃ
   smṛtireva verse anchor this.
3. **The kāla (time) and the kāla-śakti.** The avabhāsana (manifestation) is the creation-action; the
   kartṛśakti is the avabhāsanā. The time-order (krama) is the co-being-non-being (asahabhāva) of the
   manifestations, raised by the kālaśakti (the kālotthāpakatva). The bhūtādi-kāla-mixed
   avabhāsanā is the kriyā. The potter's making (kumbhakaraṇa) is the same — the param-Lord's cognition
   not other than the potter's cognition (2.4.21: itthaṃ tathā... icchaiva hetutā kartṛtā kriyā).
4. **The smṛtikriyā and the tamas.** In the māyā-pramātṛ, the memory is the inward-face (antarmukha)
   form's rehearsal of the prior outward-face; the remembered object is covered (tamasā avacchādita),
   and the vimarśa's clarification (sphuṭīkaraṇa) is the icchā's beginning (upakrama) — the susmūrṣā.
   The Spandakārikā 16 (natu yo'ntarmukho bhāvaḥ) and 33 (yathā icchābhyarthitaḥ) are cited.
5. **The smartṛtva of the body refuted.** The body/breath-eight's rememberer-ness is only the
   superimposed-freedom (samāropitasvātantrya); the consciousness-part is not divided from the
   Bhagavat (cidaṃśastu na bhagavato bhidyate). The jñānamāla (the Buddhist's cognition-series)
   cannot establish the memory (kāryakāraṇabhāvena bheda is refuted via dṛk svābhāsaiva 1.3.2).
6. **The māyā-pramātṛ's memory.** The māyā-knower cannot remember the fainted or the unseen without
   the rule (aniyatatva); the unseen's ruler is the consciousness-tattva-form Lord (the 
   īśvaraprerito verse from the Gītā). The param-self enters the turya/suṣupta seed-ground
   (bījabhū), then manifests the dream-ground — the "it remembers" is that manifestation.
7. **The ekatva, vibhutva, kartṛtva.** The freedom/oneness/agent-ness of the māyā-knower are ended in
   the param-Lord's form (parameśvararūpaparyavasitāni). The vibhutva is vividhaṃ kṛtvā bhavati — not
   the mere vyāpakatva (the kāla and deśa are vedyabhūmipraviṣṭa; the Divākaravatsa verse dūrānti-
   deśādigatārkacandra...). The viṣaya-bheda objection to the memory is removed.
8. **The asaṃpramoṣa-smaraṇa.** The memory is the experience's non-loss (asaṃpramoṣa) — the not-utter-
   loss of the prior manifestation; but it is not the experience itself (the ghaṭa/gau example: the
   cow is a different object). The kevalam: the ekībhāva with the blue-manifestation-part (the common
   object) across the other knowers (sarvajñakrimiprabhṛti) is what the māyā-pramātṛ enters by the 
   tādātmya-union (sāyujya) — the "ahaṃ nīlaṃ bhāsayāmi" (1.6.7). This is the creation (nirmāṇa), not
   the prior-being memory.
9. **The apohana (removal) and the niścaya.** The determination (niścaya) is apohana-form — it removes
   the not-that-form (atadrūpa) superimpositions. The kālabheda is not real (avāstava), so the
   "I-experience-now" (idam aham idānīmeva anubhavāmīti) conceit is the memory-identity (tādātmya).
   The Śaṅkaranandana/Dharmottara verses (siddhaṃ siddhatayā vastu nirūpyaṃ na tathā tataḥ) are cited.
10. **The pratyabhijñāna and the tulyakālatā.** When the two (the object and the experience) are united,
    the recognition (so'yam iti) arises. The one-rehearsal/one-prescription gives the two differences;
    the equal-rehearsal/prescription gives the two. The yogin superimposes the prior time on the
    object, not on the experience.
11. **Kārikā 2 (bhāsayecca svakāle'rthātpūrvābhāsitam āmṛśan).** The memory's object is the svalakṣaṇa
    (with the prior place-time avaccheda, the deśa-kāla-saṃkoca-core); the parāmarśa necessarily
    involves the manifestation (prakāśanāntarīyaka); the parāmarśa is the prakāśa's own nature
    (svabhāva) — the svabhāva-hetu: "of the object, the rehearsal is manifestation-self, from its
    own-nature-ness." The nirviṣayatva of the vikalpas is refuted (they are viṣayavat via the
    sāmānyalakṣaṇa); the memory's ekaviṣayatva establishes the one cognition-tattva.
12. **The liṅ (vidhi) analysis.** The long digression on the ākhyāta/liṅ: the action is the primary
    expressed (kriyaiva mukhyam abhidheyam); the vidhi is the niyoga (rule); the "juhuyāt svargakāmaḥ"
    is "he-sacrifices-alone" (juhoty eva); the liṅ's kālaviśeṣa is not expressed but the nitya-
    pravṛtta-vartamāna is shown. This is a grammatical interlude connecting to the kriyādhikāra.
13. **The present-rehearsal.** The vārtamānika-vimarśa, being, does not give the vastu's that-time-ness
    but only rests in the non-removal of the prior manifestation-rehearsal. The pūrvaprakāśitavimṛṣṭa
    is the asaṃpramoṣa; the pūrvaprakāśitatva is why the manifestation is avaccheda-ed by the place
    etc. (the yesterday-water/friend/pot example).

**The crux.** Chunk V2-A establishes the smṛti as the Lord's own power (smṛtiśakti = paramaṃ 
svātantryam): the free-one (svairī) Maheśvara, who has the aśeṣakālānugata (all-time-following) 
awareness, reflects "that" (sa iti) and remembers. The memory's two times are the vimarśa's; the
manifestation (avabhāsana) is the creation-action, and the time-order is the kālaśakti's raising.
The māyā-pramātṛ's memory is the contracted manifestation of the one freedom; the svalakṣaṇa's
rehearsal necessarily manifests, since the parāmarśa is the prakāśa's own nature. This is the 
fourth vimarśa's opening argument, continuing the siddhānta's positive account of the Lord's powers.

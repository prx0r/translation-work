# IPVV Vol 2 — Chunk V2-B: the sahajavimarśa, the prakāśa/vimarśa, kārikās 3–5 — T1

*Source: M00021 lines 700–1400 (continuous). Base: Torella's ĪPK — the fourth vimarśa continued.
Covers: the māyāpade memory; the ābhāsa/anābhāsa order; the yogi's perception and the param-
pramātṛ; the svalakṣaṇa/sāmānya (the general and the particular) and the deśa-kāla avaccheda;

experience does not shine separately in the memory); kārikā 5 (yoginām api bhāsante — the yogins'
cognitions also shine, not known by another); the svasaṃvida (self-cognition) as the jñāna's
characteristic; the grāhyagrāhaka. Max-effort T1.*

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-B)

1. **The māyāpade memory.** In the māyā-position the memory-practical-activity is the limitor-form
   contraction (aupādhikarūpasaṃkoca) of the manifestation. The 'tena' (by that) gives the reason
   for the memory's establishment: the naśe/sadbhāve (the destruction has no fault, the being has no
   support) — hence the "not-if" (na cet) fall is fit as a prasaṅga-reversal.
2. **The ābhāsa/anābhāsa order and time.** The krama (order) is the ābhāsānābhāsātmā time; the body's
   per-instant difference bestows the utter time-difference; the body-knower-conceiting ones (whose
   cognitions depend on the external sense-organs) have no fitness to see past/future. But with the
   prasaṃkhyāna (reflection) teaching, the body-conceit is thinned, and the ātyantika-kālabheda
   falls away — the memory of the prior-experienced objects then is.
3. **The yogi's perception and the param-pramātṛ.** The Mālinīvijayavārtika 4.4 (yogam ekatvam
   icchanti) is cited: the yogin's objects are in his own time-less self as "I" or "I-this"; the
   question of the some-only manifestation and prior-time-distinction is answered via the
   svātantrya. The māyā-knower's memory in the puryaṣṭaka/prāṇa/śūnya is only the aupādhika-
   difference.
4. **The smṛti's svarūpa.** The memory is the cognition-particular on the avacchinna-object of the
   past experience — but if the prior manifestation does not open (anunmeṣe) and there is no prior-
   manifestation-rehearsal, it is just a new experience (abhinava eva anubhavaḥ), not memory. The
   'pūrvānubhava' is the distinguishing feature.
5. **The svatantravikalpa and the "father-king" example.** The distracted (vyākṣepa-born) vikalpa,
   even if it flashes the false "father is king," is not memory (no pūrvānubhavollekha). The memory
   requires the ekībhāva with the prior-knowers' cognition-manifestation; otherwise the
   tadānīntana/other-knowers' attachment fails — the smṛtitva is negated in the two examples.
6. **The ghaṭābhāsamātra and the arthitva.** The desire (arthitva) determines the extent of the
   memory: the water-carrying desirer remembers the golden pot only by the wide-bottom-belly-form;
   the scholar remembers only the brāhmaṇa-word's correctness. The svalakṣaṇa-form-ness of the
   general (sāmānya) is spread by the deśa-kāla alone.
7. **The sāmānya/svalakṣaṇa.** The general (sāmānya) is not the Buddhist's svalakṣaṇa-contracted-
   self; the bhāva is vikasitasvabhāva (expanded-own-nature), and the saṃkoca (contraction) is by
   the deśa-kāla manifestation-generals mixed (the ṣaḍrasa-drink analogy). The mutual-avaccheda
   (anyonyāvaccheda) — "sat dravyaṃ, sat guṇa" — with the paryāya (alternation) and the yugapad
   (simultaneity). The niyatiśakti (rule-power) of the Bhagavat governs the ābhāsa-mixing.
8. **The sāmānyatā and the Vākyapadīya.** The 'sāmānya' word is unetymologized (avyutpanna,
   sādhāraṇārtha), not the bhāva-suffix-ending. The Vākyapadīya 3.14/3.9/2.11 are cited (the jāti
   of jātis, the anupravṛttidharma). The svalakṣaṇa = the contracted general (the vyāpakatva/
   nityatva removed by the deśa-kāla); "here-this-is" (atredaṃ sad) — the "sad"-locus.
9. **The pratyakṣīkāra and the Patañjali smṛti.** The present-viśeṣaṇa enters the memory, so the
   remembered appears as if directly perceived (pratyakṣīkāra); Patañjali's "svapno bhāvitaḥ
   smartavyā smṛtiḥ" is cited. The Yogasūtra 1.43 (śabdasaṃketasmṛtipariśuddhau... nirvitarkā) is
   cited; the sahajavimarśa (natural-reflection) is the "sa iti" vimarśa.
10. **Kārikā 3 (naca yuktaṃ smṛterbhede).** The remembered's manifestation is not the now-one but
    the prior-one; in the memory the self-part manifestation is time-unlimited, the object-part
    is the prior-one with the necessary rehearsal. The māyā-pramātṛ's separate-like manifestation
    (māyāśaktyā pṛthagbhūtam iva) is only in the experience-cognition, not in the memory. The
    ekaviṣayatva establishes the ekapramātṛ (the adhikaraṇasiddhānta).
11. **The ekatva and the ekātmā.** The oneness (aikyam), the tracing-form (anusandhānarūpaṃ), the
    freedom (svātantryaṃ) — the self's distinguishing feature. "Eka ātmā" is said with the bhāva-
    suffix (taddharmaprādhānya) like "caitanyam ātmā." The Ārya verse (dharmāntarapratikṣepā-
    pratikṣepau) and the Śivasūtra are cited. "Saḥ" for the hidden-form, "eṣa" for the clear-form —
    the pratyabhijñā (recognition) is indicated.
12. **Kārikā 4 (naiva hyanubhavo bhāti smṛtau).** The experience does not shine separately in the
    memory like the prior object; the "prāg aham anvabhūvam" is the ātmārohaṇa-manifestation. The
    agent-lagging-ness of the experience (kartṛlagnatā) even in the object-lagging-ness
    (karmalagnatāyāṃ); the māyā-pramātṛ's dharmas (sukhitvādayo) are the known-ones, their vedyatā
    is figurative (upacaritā). The pratyagātmatā — the pratyagātma-class is bhedaprāṇa from the
    known-ones; the one self-cognition-knower is "anekakāla" figuratively. The pūrvapakṣa (1.2.6
    smartā draṣṭeva kalpitaḥ) is refuted.
13. **Kārikā 5 (yoginām api bhāsante).** The yogins' cognitions shine, not known by another
    perception, being self-cognition (svasaṃvidaikamānāḥ), even in the known-position. The three-
    fold other-cognition (the "this-one-knows," the "this-is-my-past-experience," the "remembered
    by the other") are all the other-knower-gone perceptions (pramātrantaragatā upalambhāḥ) — the
    dṛśaḥ-plural in the sūtra. But in the memory (prakṛte) there is no such "I-experienced" separate
    manifestation — the memory's experience is asmadartha-śarīrībhūta (our-meaning-bodied).
14. **The jñāna not known by another, the svasaṃvidmātra.** The Buddhist/Sāṅkhya/Kaṇāda/Jaiminīya
    views are each refuted: none can explain the yogin's knowledge of the other's cognition. The
    cognition's characteristic (svabhāva) is the self-cognition-alone-form-ness (svasaṃvinmātrarūpatva),
    the vyāvartaka from the inert (jaḍa). The four-fold niyama: svaiva saṃvit / parāpi saṃvit /
    svasya saṃvideva / asaṃvid api — the caturvidhaniyamaniyantritarūpāvabhāsa. The jñāna's being
    known by another would fail this four-fold rule.

**The crux.** Chunk V2-B develops the smṛti's svarūpa and its ekaviṣayatva with the experience: the
memory manifests the object by the prior-manifestation path (the object-part is the prior one), not
as a separate now-manifestation (which would be a new experience or a creation). The svalakṣaṇa is
the contracted general; the parāmarśa is the prakāśa's own nature (1.5.11). The ekaviṣayatva
establishes the one cognition-tattva (ekapramātṛ), and the cognitions' ekātmatā (one-self-ness) is
the self. The jñāna cannot be known by another cognition (it is svasaṃvidmātra, the four-fold rule),
which grounds both the memory's validity and the yogin's knowledge of the other's cognition.

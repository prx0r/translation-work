## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-I)

1. **The chunk's scope.** V2-I = M00021 lines 6000–6800, the jñānaśakti-vimarśa's positive thesis on the powers. It closes kārikā 13 (the aiśvarya = icchānuvartitva; the ānanda = pūrṇatā; the parā-vāk and the paśyantī); states kārikā 14 (*sā sphurattā mahāsattā*) — the throbbing as the great being, the heart (hṛdaya) of the param-Lord, with the sphurattā/spanda/ūrmi synonyms and the mahāsattā/nityatā analysis; kārikās 15–16 (*ātmānam ata evāyaṃ jñeyīkuryāt / svātantryād amuktam ātmānaṃ*) — the self makes itself the known, and the free Lord constructs and deals with himself through the īśa-etc. constructs; kārikā 17 (*nāhantādiparāmarśabhedād asyānyatātmanaḥ*) — the division of the ahantā/idantā reflexive-awarenesses does not make the self other; kārikā 18 (*māyāśaktyā vibhoḥ saiva bhinnasaṃvedyagocarā*) — the jñāna/saṃkalpa/adhyavasāya powers are the one vimarśaśakti contracted by the māyāśakti; kārikā 19 (*sākṣātkārakṣaṇe'py asti vimarśaḥ*) — the vimarśa is present even in the direct-manifestation instant, else running etc. could not happen without anusandhāna.

2. **The term-ledger LOCK carried from V2-E/F/G/H.** vimarśa = reflexive-awareness; parāmarśa = the (self-)reflexive-awareness; pratyavamarśa = the re-reflexive-awareness; prakāśa = manifestation; sphurattā = throbbing (kept as sphurattā); spanda = the throbbing-motion (kept as spanda); ūrmi = wave; saṃvit = consciousness; jñāna = cognition; icchā = will; māyāśakti = the māyā-power; jñeyīkaraṇa = the making-into-the-known; sākṣātkāra = direct manifestation; adhyavasāya = determination; saṃkalpa = construction; smṛti = memory; ahantā/idantā = I-ness/this-ness. The older "rehearsal/reflexion/light" variants remain retired.

3. **Kārikā 14 (sā sphurattā mahāsattā ... hṛdayaṃ parameṣṭhinaḥ).** The reflexive-awareness-power is the sphurattā, the mahāsattā, the deśa-kāla-non-particularizing being — the heart of the param-Lord. The hṛdaya is not a mere metaphor (upacarita) but the primary (mukhya): the Gītā 14.27 (*brahmaṇo hi pratiṣṭhāham*) confirms that the reflexive-awareness "I" is the resting-place of the Bliss-Brahmā. The sphurattā is the "I"-attached flashing that is transferred onto the object ("to me the jar flashes") — i.e., the being (bhāva) flashes only by entering the cognition (bodhāveśa); the sphurattā-analysis is the bridge to the recognition.

4. **Kārikās 15–16 (jñeyīkaraṇa and the īśa-constructs).** The self makes itself the known (jñeyīkuryāt), not by facing an external known (which would break its freedom into dependence); the potter's case is metaphorical (upacarita) — only the param-Lord is the real agent. In kārikā 16 the free Lord, without losing his freedom, constructs himself as īśa/Śiva etc. and deals (vyavahārayet) with himself through teaching/worship/meditation. The Gītā 10.20/15.15 confirm the "I-as-the-self-in-all-hearts" is the cognition-self, not the body.

5. **Kārikā 17 (nāhantādiparāmarśabhedāt).** The apparent division of the reflexive-awarenesses (the "I" of the establishing, the "this" of the established) is no ground for the self's otherness: the root-reflexive-awareness "I" (mūlaparāmarśa) never abandons, and it penetrates (anuvedhaka) even the "this"-reflexive-awareness. The "like the tiṅ-said action" (tiṅvācyakarmavat) — the action-word's action is the reflexive-awareness itself (the Vākyapadīya 3.6.1 *dik sādhanaṃ kriyā kāla iti*), so the number/time etc. attach only to the substance-becoming appearances, not to the reflexive-awareness.

6. **Kārikā 18 (māyāśaktyā vibhoḥ saiva ... jñānasaṃkalpādhyavasāyādināmabhiḥ).** The jñāna/saṃkalpa/adhyavasāya/smṛti powers are not new tattvas beyond the vimarśaśakti; they are the one vimarśaśakti contracted by the māyāśakti (the māyāya-known-tinge, māyīyavedyoparāga). The variety of the powers and of their objects is from the māyā-tinge, not from any real division of the cognition (jñānādinām abhedaḥ). This is how the "Lord manifests" (bhāsyate) is a metaphor (upacāra) for the self-manifestation.

7. **Kārikā 19 (sākṣātkārakṣaṇe'py asti vimarśaḥ).** Even in the perception-instant the vimarśa is present — the "how-otherwise" (katham anyathā): without it, running and the other purposive actions, destitute of anusandhāna, could not happen. The vimarśa is present in the construction and in the direct manifestation alike (the object is constructed only as manifested; the not-manifested is never constructed). The cross-references: ĪPK 1.3.1–1.3.2 (*dṛk svābhāsaiva*), the Pramāṇavārttika 3.174 (*saṃketasmaraṇopāyam*), and the Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi 20.

**The crux.** V2-I is the positive culmination of the jñānaśakti-vimarśa's account of the powers. The one vimarśaśakti — the Lord's own reflexive-awareness — is everything: the sphurattā-mahāsattā that is the heart of the param-Lord (kārikā 14); the freedom by which the self makes itself the known and, without losing freedom, constructs and deals with itself as īśa/Śiva (kārikās 15–16); the root-"I"-reflexive-awareness that penetrates even the "this"-reflexive-awareness, so the apparent division is no otherness (kārikā 17); and the single power that, contracted by the māyāśakti, appears as the jñāna/saṃkalpa/adhyavasāya/smṛti powers (kārikā 18). Kārikā 19 secures the vimarśa even in the perception-instant. The whole is the ground of the recognition: the knowing self that deals, constructs, remembers, and perceives is the one Lord whose own nature is the reflexive-awareness "I."

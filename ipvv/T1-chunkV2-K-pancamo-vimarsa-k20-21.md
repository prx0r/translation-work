## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-K)

1. **The chunk's scope and the ||5|| close.** V2-K = M00021 lines 7600–8405, the close of the jñānaśaktivimarśa (pañcamo vimarśa). It completes kārikā 20 (the vācaka's own-form reflexive-awareness; the svaprakāśatva like the lamp; the Vākyapadīya 1.50/1.55; the svarūpavācakatā; the niścaya/saṃśaya and svasaṃvedana; the Bhaṭṭa and Dharmottara; the adhyāsa of the vācaka's form on the vācya — the saṃketa and the vyavahāra, the daṇḍī/siṃha examples, the prāiṣa, the madhyamā-vāk, the saṃsāra-tree, the Vaikharī) and states kārikā 21 (*kevalaṃ bhinnasaṃvedyadeśakālānurodhataḥ jñānasmṛtyavasāyādi sakramaṃ pratibhāsate* — the powers appear with order only by following the place-and-time of the different cognized; in themselves they are one and order-less, resting in the I). It closes with the colophon of the jñānaśaktivimarśa ||5||, after which the sixth vimarśa (V2-L) begins.

2. **The term-ledger LOCK carried from V2-E through V2-J.** vimarśa = reflexive-awareness; parāmarśa = the (self-)reflexive-awareness; pratyavamarśa = the re-reflexive-awareness; anuvyavasāya = the re-observation; prakāśa = manifestation; vācya/vācaka = the denoted/denoting; svarūpavācakatā = the own-form-denoting; sphoṭa = the sphoṭa; adhyāsa = superimposition; saṃketa = convention; vyutpatti = derivation; vyavahāra = dealing; prāiṣa = the command; paśyantī/madhyamā/vaikharī/parā = kept transliterated; saṃsaraṇa = the rolling (the saṃsāra-tree); saṃskāra = impression; manaskāra = mind-attention; niścaya = determination; saṃśaya = doubt; saṃvedana = cognition; svasaṃvedana = own-cognition. The older "rehearsal/reflexion/light" variants remain retired.

3. **Kārikā 20's vācaka-section.** The word's own form (svarūpa) is reflexively aware (svanāmnā parāmarśa) because the reflexive-awareness is the manifestation's nature — the word, like the lamp, manifests itself and the object. The Vākyapadīya 1.50/1.55 (*ātmarūpaṃ yathā jñāne / grāhyatvaṃ grahaṇatvaṃ ca*) anchor this. The Bhaṭṭa and Dharmottara passages defend the svaprakāśatva of the determination against the adhyavasāyāpekṣa-prāmāṇya view. The adhyāsa of the vācaka's form on the vācya (and vice-versa) is what makes saṃketa and vyavahāra possible; the daṇḍī and siṃha examples show it is a true superimposition (on a non-divided ground), not a mere metaphor.

4. **The prāiṣa and the madhyamā-vāk.** The command ("bring the jar") shows the reflexive-awareness's mutual pervasion (parasparavyāpti) in the dealing — the speaker's and hearer's reflexive-awarenesses exchange agent/action roles (the image-image reversal). The madhyamā-vāk (the inner speech in the prāṇa) is where the grasped-grasper phenomena are constructed and where the saṃsāra-tree (saṃsaraṇa) has its root — watered by the rasa of attachment. The four-fold vāk (parā/paśyantī/madhyamā/vaikharī; Ṛgveda 1.164.45) and the Spandakārikā 48 give the ontological map; the Vaikharī is where the word appears as ear-grasped, yet in truth only the word-pervasion extends there (Śivadṛṣṭi 2.8).

5. **Kārikā 21 (kevalaṃ bhinnasaṃvedya...).** The jñāna/smṛti/avasāya powers appear "with order" (sakrama) only by following the place-and-time of the different cognized (bhinnasaṃvedya); in themselves — resting in the I-reflexive-awareness — they are one and order-less (the aham-viśrānti is akhaṇḍita; the sakramatva appears only on its back, tatpṛṭha). The deśa-kāla are the marks of the cognized (vedyadharma); the māyāśakti (1.5.18) produces the difference of the cognized, so the powers are atirikta-natirikta (transcending-not-transcending) — the param-Lord's own power (1.2.7). The colophon closes the jñānaśaktivimarśa.

**The crux.** V2-K completes the jñānaśaktivimarśa. Kārikā 20 shows the word's own form is reflexively aware (like the lamp), and that the adhyāsa of the vācaka's form on the vācya is the ground of all saṃketa and vyavahāra — the whole of language is the reflexive-awareness superimposing its own form on the object and the object's form on itself. The prāiṣa and the madhyamā-vāk locate this in the knower, where the saṃsāra-tree is rooted in the restless construction watered by attachment. Kārikā 21 then seals the positive thesis: the powers (jñāna, smṛti, avasāya) appear with order only by following the cognized's place-and-time; in themselves, resting in the I, they are the one order-less reflexive-awareness — the param-Lord's own power, atirikta-natirikta. This is the culmination of the fifth vimarśa, the full account of the jñānaśakti as the manifestation of the one consciousness.

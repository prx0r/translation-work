## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-L)

1. **The chunk's scope.** V2-L = M00021 lines 8405–9200, the ṣaṣṭho vimarśa — the apohanaśakti (the removal power), which governs smṛti (memory) and the whole construction (vikalpa) process. It states the 11-verse introduction; kārikā 1 (*ahaṃpratyavamarśo yaḥ prakāśātmāpi vāgvapuḥ nāsau vikalpaḥ sahyukto dvayākṣepī viniścayaḥ*) — the reflexive-awareness "I," though it is word-bodied (vāgvapus, the parā-vāk), is not a construction but a determination that excludes the twofold; kārikā 2 (*bhinnayoravabhāso hi syād ghaṭāghaṭayordvayoḥ prakāśasyeva nānyasya bhedinastvavabhāsanam*) — the counterpart (pratiyogi) is possible in the object (the māyā-word), not in the manifestation; kārikā 3 (*tadatatpratibhāabhājā mātraivātadvyapohanāt tanniścayanamukto hi vikalpo ghaṭa ityayam*) — the construction "this is a jar" is the determination that removes the not-jar; kārikās 4–5 (*cittattvaṃ māyayā hitvā bhinna evāvabhāti yaḥ dehe buddhāvatha prāṇe kalpite nabhasīva vā / pramātṛtvenāhamiti vimarśo'nyavyapohanāt vikalpa eva sa parapratiyogyavabhāsajaḥ*) — the "I" that descends into the body, mind, breath, or the conceived void is a vikalpa, born of the other-counterpart's manifestation.

2. **The term-ledger LOCK carried from V2-E through V2-K.** vimarśa = reflexive-awareness; parāmarśa/pratyavamarśa = the (re-)reflexive-awareness; prakāśa = manifestation; ābhāsa = appearance; saṃvit = consciousness; jñāna = cognition; smṛti = memory; apohana = removal (the apohanaśakti = the removal power); vikalpa = construction; niścaya = determination; saṃśaya = doubt; pratiyogi = counterpart; virodha = opposition; vācya/vācaka = denoted/denoting; saṃskāra = impression; vāsanā = impression/vāsanā; icchā = will; kriyā = action; sakrama/akrama = with/without order; māyāśakti = the māyā-power; śūnya = the void; prāṇa = the breath; puryaṣṭaka = the eight-fold inner body. 3. **Kārikā 1 (ahaṃpratyavamarśo... vāgvapuḥ nāsau vikalpaḥ sahyukto dvayākṣepī viniścayaḥ).** The reflexive-awareness "I" is the pure manifestation's own form — the parā-vāk, word-bodied — yet it is not a vikalpa: a vikalpa always operates with the twofold (the that-form and the that-form-less, the affirmed and the negated, joined, divided, suspected), whereas the pure "I"-reflexive-awareness has no counterpart (pratiyogi). It is a determination (niścaya) that invokes/excludes the twofold (dvayākṣepī) — i.e., it is the apohana-operation itself, but on the pure ground where no counterpart is possible. The Bhaṭṭa and Dharmottara passages defend the svaprakāśatva of the determination; the vācaka/sphoṭa analysis (the word-body) is developed.

4. **Kārikā 2 (bhinnayoravabhāso hi... prakāśasyeva nānyasya bhedinas).** The counterpart is possible where two divided things (jar and not-jar) both manifest (the māyā-word); but in the manifestation (prakāśa) no not-manifestation counterpart is possible, since to manifest is precisely to remove the not-manifestation. The virodha (opposition) is defined as the mutual-removal standing (anyonyaparihāra) — the saṃsṛjyante-na-bhidyante maxim, the ahinakula example, and the deśa/kāla of the virodha are all resolved to show that the pure manifestation has no possible counterpart.

5. **Kārikās 4–5 (the aham in the body/mind/breath/void).** The "I" that descends into the body ("the white I"), the mind ("the happy I"), the breath ("the hungry I"), or the conceived void ("the all-forms-free I") is a vikalpa — born of the manifestation of the other-counterpart (parapratiyogyavabhāsajaḥ). The pure cittattva, by the māyāśakti (not by mere avidyā — the Gītā's "anityāśuciduḥkhānātmasu" is read as māyā, not ignorance-alone), is divided and descends; the prāṇa/puryaṣṭaka and the śūnya analysis show these are the contracted knower-states, not the pure aham. The Gītā 6.26 and the Spandakārikā 12 refute the abhāva-Brahmā view: the aham-manifestation is always-shining, not void.

**The crux.** V2-L establishes the apohanaśakti (removal power) as the operation of the reflexive-awareness in the māyā-word. The pure "I"-reflexive-awareness is not a construction — it is the word-bodied manifestation itself, a determination that excludes the twofold, with no possible counterpart (kārikās 1–2). The construction (vikalpa) is precisely the determination that operates with the counterpart — "this is a jar" removes the not-jar (kārikā 3). When the "I" descends into the body, mind, breath, or the void, it is a vikalpa born of the other-counterpart's manifestation (kārikās 4–5); only the pure cittattva, free of the māyā-contraction, is the un-constructed reflexive-awareness. This is the smṛti/apohana complement to the jñānaśakti: memory and construction are the one reflexive-awareness operating by removal in the māyā-word, and the recognition is their return to the pure aham.

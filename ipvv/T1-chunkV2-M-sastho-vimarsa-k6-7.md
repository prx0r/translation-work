## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-M)

1. **The chunk's scope.** V2-M = M00021 lines 9200–10000, the ṣaṣṭho vimarśa (the apohanaśakti,
   continued). It opens at the close of kārikā 5's commentary (the prāṇa/puryaṣṭaka, the
   suṣupta/turya, the māyā/anava/kārma malas; the ahaṃpratyaya as pure and impure, anubhava and
   anusandhāna; the Śivadṛṣṭi/Gītā/Spandakārikā citations; the Sāṃkhyakārikā 29 and Yogasūtra
   1.40); it then gives **kārikā 6** (*kādācitkāvabhāse yā pūrvābhāsādiyojanā saṃskārāt kalpanā
   proktā sāpi bhinnāvabhāsini* — the joining of the "this" from the prior appearance, by the
   impression, is the construction, resting in the different manifestations), with the aikyayojanā/
   pratyabhijñā (the shadow-made recognition) analysis, the cidadvayavāda (Vasugupta), the
   bālyayauvana and the body-states continuity; and **kārikā 7** (*tadevaṃ vyavahāre'pi prabhur
   dehādimāviśan bhāntam evāntararthaugham icchayā bhāsayet bahiḥ* — even in dealing, the Lord,
   entering the body etc., manifests the inner object-mass without, by the will), with the
   kṛtyapañcaka (sṛṣṭi/sthiti/saṃhāra/tirodhāna/anugraha), the parameśvara as the self-manifesting
   knower, the two pratītis (the "I" and the "I-this"), the pure/mixed/unpure states, the māyāśakti
   as the source of the divided manifestation, and the kalā/vidyā (the action-range and the
   cognition-range of the divided knower). The chunk closes at the objection about the
   mantreśvaras and liberation (10000), which V2-N (10000–10385, the close of the ṣaṣṭho vimarśa
   \|\|6\|\|) answers.

2. **The term-ledger LOCK carried from V2-E through V2-L.** vimarśa = reflexive-awareness;
   parāmarśa/pratyavamarśa = the (re-)reflexive-awareness; prakāśa = manifestation; ābhāsa =
   appearance; saṃvit = consciousness; jñāna = cognition; smṛti = memory; apohana = removal (the
   apohanaśakti = the removal power); vikalpa = construction; niścaya = determination; saṃśaya =
   doubt; pratiyogi = counterpart; anusandhāna = continuity; anubhava = experience; kāla = time;
   māyāśakti = the māyā-power; kalā/vidyā = (action-)range/(cognition-)range; suṣupta/turya =
   deep sleep/fourth state; puryaṣṭaka = the eight-fold inner body; icchā = will; kriyā = action;
   sṛṣṭi/sthiti/saṃhāra/tirodhāna/anugraha = creation/maintenance/destruction/concealment/grace.
   The older "rehearsal/reflexion/light" variants remain retired.

3. **Kārikā 6 (kādācitkāvabhāse...).** The "this" of the construction is not the pure ahaṃ: it is
   the joining (yojanā) of a "this" from a prior appearance (the child-body with the youth-body,
   the name "Caitra" with the present "I", the hungry/thirsty/weak breath-states, the happy/
   unhappy mind-states, the limited/unlimited void-states), done by the impression (saṃskāra). The
   recognition (pratyabhijñā) that "this very is I" across the changing body-states is thus a
   construction resting in the different manifestations (bhinnāvabhāsini) — the counterpart
   (pratiyogi) is possible because the manifestations differ. The cidadvayavāda (Vasugupta) is
   invoked for the identity of consciousness across states; the pūrvānubhava/uddrikta and the
   sākṣātkāra-vs-smṛti distinction keep the pure ahaṃpratyaya free of the vikalpa.

4. **Kārikā 7 (tadevaṃ vyavahāre'pi...).** The Lord, whose param-essence is manifestation
   (prakāśaparamārthaḥ), by his lordship enters (āviśati) the body etc. as knower even in the
   māyā-word, makes them his own, and manifests the inner (antaḥ) object-mass without (bahiḥ), by
   the will (icchayā) alone — the inner-tool/outer-tool known-ground (antaḥkaraṇabahiṣkaraṇavedyatā-
   bhūmi) receiving the idam in order, clear then unclear. The kṛtyapañcaka: the same Lord, always
   free, in the famous creation/maintenance/destruction/concealment/grace times AND in the
   unending dealing-times, enters the distinct knowers, builds the unity, maintains the state,
   destroys the prior knower-unity, shows the dissolution by enclosing his own dense form, and
   manifests the grace-portion (anugrahakalā) by the unity-entry. This is the "five-fold deed" as
   the apohanaśakti's self-unfolding; the "na asaṃbhāvyam etat" and the liṅ in the reason show the
   inconceivability is only apparent.

5. **The pure/mixed/unpure states and the two pratītis.** The pure state is the Śiva-ground
   (śuddhaśivabhūmi) where the ahaṃ (the anubhava-svabhāva) is un-constructed; the Sadāśiva-ground
   is the manobhūmi where "I-this" (aham idam) flashes un-divided; the māyā-ground is where the
   prakāśa-part is closed (nimīlita) and the beings appear as the divided. Abhinava reads the
   Sadāśiva/Īśvara states as the reflexive-awareness-primary and the manifestation-primary daśās;
   the distinction (bheda) between them is not a real object-distinction (the "nīlam asphuṭaṃ pītaṃ
   sphuṭam" example, the cviprayoga) — there is no excess or lack in the Lord. The time-distinction
   is only from the māyā-impression; in the Lord "with-which-form-not-does-it-shine" — the 
   "now-shines/previously-shone/will-shine" is all pure manifestation (śuddhabhāsanamātraparamārtha),
   like the mirror's clarity-unfolding (svacchatāvijṛmbhita). This grounds the viruddhavyāptopalabdhi
   (opposed-pervasion-apprehension) of ĪPK 1.3.2 (dṛk svābhāsaiva): the distinction-dealing is
   pervaded by the un-distinction-dealing.

6. **The kalā/vidyā and the māyāśakti.** The māyāśakti gives the self-ness (ātmatā) in the not-self
   and contracts the knower-ness; but by that much alone the contracted knower does not become the
   knower of the divided (nīlasukha): a pervader-non-apprehension (vyāpakānupalabdhi) shows that
   the mere knower-ness (pramātṛtāmātra) does not entail the divided-object consciousness — else
   the deep-sleep/fourth states would not be conceived. Hence something beyond the māyā is to be
   expected: the kalā (which, by the general operation, moves the divided action — the lame's
   walking-effort — and, specifically, the action-organs complete it) and the vidyā (which, by the
   general operation, grasps the divided object — the blind's tasting the juice — and, specifically,
   the cognition-organs complete it). The sva. 11.98 (kalodbalitacaitanyo vidyādarśitagocaraḥ) is
   the āgama anchor; the rāga/kāla (passion/time) are the upādhis (adjuncts), not the raisers, of
   jñatvakartṛtva (the knowing/acting). The kārikā 7 commentary here completes the transition: the
   "śuddhacidātmana" and "viśvanirmāṇecchoḥ" glosses fold the pure/mixed/unpure creation into the
   one manifesting Lord.

**The crux.** V2-M establishes the apohanaśakti's self-unfolding as the Lord's kṛtyapañcaka — the
same recognition-power by which the "this" is joined to the "I" across the changing body-states
(kārikā 6) is the power by which the free Lord, entering the distinct knowers as their
self-manifesting essence, builds, maintains, destroys, conceals, and graces the world by the will
alone (kārikā 7). The pure "I"-reflexive-awareness is never a construction; the construction is
always the joining of a prior appearance in the divided manifestations. When the Lord's manifestation
enters the māyā-ground the appearance is contracted and divided, and the kalā/vidyā (the action- and
cognition-ranges) are what make the divided dealing possible; but even there the object is only the
consciousness (saṃvideva viśvarūpā, na ghaṭaḥ kaścit svatantraḥ) — no free jar outside the
manifestation. The recognition is the return to the pure ahaṃ, and the whole creation/dealing is
the one prakāśa unfolding its own clarity.

3. **Kārikā 5 (smṛtau yaiva...).** In memory, the prior experience does not appear separately as a
   known (it is past, its object gone); it appears only as the un-divided self-consciousness that
   announces the memory's own being. Neither the experience's own consciousness nor the memory (by
   itself, or as object) is the valid means of the prior-experience's being: only the one
   self-consciousness (svasaṃvedana), un-broken across time, is — and that is the knower-essence
   (pramātṛtattva). The memory, being past-time-stained (gṛhītagrāhitva), is non-valid; the
   self-consciousness, being time-cut-free (antarmukhe kālāvacchedavirahāt), is valid. The
   "mayā ghaṭo'nubhūtaḥ" cognition is not denied but confirmed; the middle side (that the proof is
   non-distinct from the memory's self-consciousness) is established.

4. **Kārikā 6 (bādhyabādhakabhāvo'pi...).** Even the refuted-refuter relation between cognitions
   rests on the one knower. The Buddhist's opposition (virodha) by co-absence, by destruction, by
   other-room, by other-valid-means — each fails or is only apparent. The shell/silver example: the
   cognition "this is shell, not silver" destroys the prior "this silver is true and object-action-
   capable" cognition's result-continuation, and rests — only because both rest in the one free
   knower (pramātā svatantraḥ). The bādhyatā of the to-be-abandoned and the bādhakatā of the
   abandonee follow by the svabhāvahetu and the viruddhavyāptopalabdhi. The Bhaṭṭa view (that the
   shell-support alone produces the silver-consciousness) is dismissed: without the one knower the
   one-support is unknowable. The pramāṇalakṣaṇa sūtra (2.3.2, so'ntastathāvimarśātmā...) is
   invoked.

5. **Kārikās 7–11 (the bādha as the abhāva; viviktabhūtala; kintvāloka; piśāca).** The main
   argument: the bādha (refutation) of the silver-cognition is not a separate cognition of a
   distinct non-being (vyatiriktābhāva) — it is the same cognition "this is shell" whose conformity
   (yathārthatā) is itself the announcement of the silver-cognition's non-validity. The ground/jar
   example is analogized: the pure-ground cognition is the jar-non-being cognition because the
   jar's non-being is nothing other than the pure ground's own manifestation (tādātmya), not a
   distinct object. Kārikā 9 (viviktaṃ bhūtalaṃ śaśvad) fixes the ground's clarity as its
   own-self-resting (svātmaniṣṭhatā) — it is always the same, so it cannot prove the non-being of a
   distinct (bhinnasya) jar except by identity. Kārikā 10 (kintvālokacayo...) gives the positive
   account: the light-mass / the warm-soft-touch IS the ground's own form, and it is that (not a
   distinct non-being) which the own-cognition proves to be jar-less (aghaṭātmatā) — the
   vyatiriktābhāva is the light/touch itself, dealt by the tādātmya. Kārikā 11 (piśācaḥ...) answers
   the objection that then any absence (the goblin's) would be the light's: the goblin is not a
   part of the light's own form (un-light, extra-sensory), so its non-being cannot be established by
   the light's identity; the sarvathā (in all ways) is the niyama (rule) restricting the absence to
   the known own-form.

**The crux.** V2-P completes the argument that the bādha (refutation/opposition) and the abhāva
(non-being) are not separate realities but the operations of the one free knower's self-consciousness.
Memory is valid only by the un-broken self-consciousness that is the knower-essence (kārikā 5); the
refuted-refuter relation between cognitions rests only in that one knower (kārikā 6); the bādha of
the silver-cognition is not a distinct non-being but the very conformity of the shell-cognition
(kārikā 7); the pure ground never proves a distinct non-being except by identity (kārikā 8–9); the
positive absence is the own-form (light/touch) dealt by identity (kārikā 10); and only what is part
of the own-form can be negated, the goblin being excluded by the niyama (kārikā 11). The whole
ṣaṣṭho/saptamo vimarśa argument converges: the dealing, the relation, the effect-cause-being, the
memory, the bādha, and the abhāva are all the one pramātṛ's freedom — the pramāṇa is prāpaka only
as the knower's operation, and the recognition (pratyabhijñā) of the Lord as that one knower is the
śāstra's purpose.

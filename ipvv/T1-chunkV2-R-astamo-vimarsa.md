## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-R)

1. **The chunk's scope.** V2-R = M00021 lines 12423–13200, the opening of the aṣṭamo vimarśa (the
   māheśvarya, the great-Lordship), the last vimarśa of the jñānādhikāra. It contains the maṅgala
   (pūrvaṃ yad anuttaram amṛtabhūmim āsādya saptamīṃ kalanāṃ...), the 11-verse plan (of which this
   chunk gives the first nine), and the jñānaviṣayaṃ māheśvaryaṃ: kārikā 1 (tātkālikākṣasāmakṣya-
   sāpekṣāḥ kevalaṃ kvacit ābhāsā anyathānyatra tv andhāndhatamasādiṣu — the appearances, dependent
   on the at-that-time eye co-presence, only somewhere, otherwise elsewhere in the blind/utter-dark);
   kārikā 2 (viśeṣo'rthāvabhāsasya sattāyāṃ na punaḥ kvacit vikalpeṣu bhaved bhāvibhavadbhūtārthagāmiṣu —
   the distinction of the object-manifestation in being-ness, not anywhere again, would be in the
   constructions going to the future/present/past objects); kārikā 3 (sukhādiṣu ca saukhyādihetuṣv
   api ca vastuṣu...); kārikā 4 (gāḍham ullikhyamāne tu vikalpena sukhādike...); kārikā 5
   (bhāvābhāvāvabhāsānāṃ bāhyatopādhiriṣyate nātmā sattā tatasteṣām āntarāṇāṃ satāṃ sadā — for the
   being-non-being manifestations, the external-ness is declared an adjunct, not the self-being-ness,
   hence of them, the inner ones, the being is always); kārikā 6 (āntaratvāt pramātraikye naiṣāṃ
   bhedanibandhanā arthakriyāpi bāhyatve sā bhinnābhāsabhedataḥ); kārikā 7 (cinmayatve'vabhāsānām
   antareva sthitiḥ sadā māyayābhāsamānānāṃ bāhyatvād bahir api asau); kārikā 8 (vikalpe yo'yam

   construction, this uplifting, it too is external, the separate-manifestation; the knower-unity-
   inner-ness, hence the distinction too is the external-ness); and kārikā 9 (ullekhasya sukhādeś ca
   prakāśo bahirātmanā icchāto bharturadhyakṣarūpo'kṣyādibhuvāṃ yathā — of the uplifting and the

3. **The māheśvarya (great-Lordship) and the 11-verse plan.** The aṣṭamo vimarśa determines the
   maheśvara: the māheśvarya is twofold — jñānaviṣaya (knowledge-object) and kriyāviṣaya (action-
   object); the former is determined here in the jñānādhikāra. The 11-verse plan (per the
   sūtrārthāvatāra): verse 1 says the appearance sometimes depends on the eye's co-presence,
   sometimes not; verse 2 that it is still one appearance; verses 3–4 the arthakriyā-appearance too
   is appearance-fixed and where; verse 5 the appearance-other-variety (the wall-conception);
   verses 6–7 the external-ness by own-form and by distinction; verse 8 the upasaṃhāra of the
   knowledge-power-support; and the summarizing verse introducing the future action-section. This
   chunk covers kārikās 1–9.

4. **Kārikā 1 (tātkālikākṣa...).** The appearances that depend on the at-that-time eye co-presence
   (the perception and the immediately-following construction) occur only somewhere; elsewhere (in
   the blind, the utter-dark) they do not depend on the present eye but on the long-happened
   impression (ciravṛttasaṃskāra). The "kevalam" (only) marks the sūtrārthāvatāra's addition: the
   dealing in the Lord is all, and only here the to-be-said-other. The atoms (aṇu), the recognized
   selves (pratyabhijñātātman, the mahāmantreśvaras) whose appearances always shine "I", are fixed by
   the niyatiśakti.

5. **Kārikā 2 (viśeṣo'rthāvabhāsasya...).** In being-ness (sattā), the object-manifestation has no
   distinction anywhere: whether the construction goes to the future, present, or past object, the
   manifestation's existing-ness is the one always-shining (sadātana prakāśa), grasped by the
   "I"-reflexive-awareness (ahamity āmarśa). The "nīlaṃ paśyāmi / utprekṣe / smarāmi" are one blue-
   appearance; the variety in the māyā-knowers is only the joining/unjoining of the appearances. The
   saṃskāra-named appearance (the impression-form pratibhāsa) is the beings' "I"-ness teaching, but
   it is not the pure ahaṃpada (it is varaṇamayatā, covering-natured); the bahiṣkārya (external-
   making) is the "this"-manifestation of the I-one-self's covering.

6. **Kārikās 3–4 (sukhādiṣu; gāḍham).** In the pleasure-and-the-rest and their causes, the
   manifestation, though existing, is past (atīta), hence the standing differs. The pleasure-
   appearances are present with the I-ness but not in it as the present; the past-ness alone fits
   them (they always have occurred, even in the prior birth). The present-ness is the external-
   resting. Kārikā 4: when the construction intensely uplifts the (past) pleasure, the standing at
   the end is the utter-pleasure-form — because it is then presented as present (pratyupasthāpita),
   not remembered (the memory is asphuṭa). The meditation (dhyāna) of the nectar-stream, the kanda-
   suṣumṇā entry, the attachment (āsaṅga), the adṛṣṭa, are the "kutaścit" (from-something) reasons.
   The kāryakāri (object-action-doing) of the pleasure is full even if its past object was unreal:
   the pṛthivīkṛtsnā meditation, the bhāvitā, even the asatya, still accomplish the arthakriyā.

7. **Kārikā 5 (bhāvābhāvāvabhāsānāṃ...).** The external-ness is not the self (ātmā/own-form) of the
   being or non-being manifestations — it is an adjunct (upādhi), the other-appearance-nature, the
   stain-qualifier (uparāganimitta viśeṣaṇa). Their being-ness is always-inner (āntarāṇāṃ sadā
   sattā). The non-being (abhāva) is not a nothing: the jar's non-being is the jar-itself-thus
   (ghaṭa eva tathābhūtaḥ), the tucchatā (emptiness) belongs to the jar, and the world's
   distaste at losing wealth shows the abhāva is a real object. Even the yogi-cognition of the
   past/future (the prāgabhāva, pradhvaṃsābhāva) is real. In our view the prakāśanamātrasāratva (the
   manifestation-mere-essence) is the beings' form: the abhāvātmaka form too manifests. The
   śaśaviṣāṇa (hare's-horn) is the atyantābhāva ("the hare's-horn does not become"), not a mere
   conceptual (sāṅkalpika) like jar-cloth. The "te tu" (they however) = the abhāvas shine by the
   re-reflexive-awareness-niścaya (pratyavamarśasāraniścayātmanā).

8. **Kārikās 6–7 (āntaratvāt; cinmayatve).** In the knower-unity (pramātraikya) the inner-ness means
   no bhedanibandhanā arthakriyā (no distinction-grounded object-action): the object-action is
   appearance-fixed (ābhāsaniyata), and in the inner state there is no bheda. Kārikā 7: since the
   manifestations are consciousness-made (cinmaya), their standing is inner-alone always; only by the
   māyā (the freedom-marker māyāśakti) is the external-ness manifested, and then the standing is
   both outside and inside — but the inner-manifestation of the external-manifestation-cognition
   (the wall, bhittikalpa) is not opposed (like the blue-ness vs the momentariness; the kalpitānyonya-
   parihāra is not pāramārthika). The anubhava is the saṃskārātmā withdrawn, inner, boddhṛmayā. The
   ābhāsana, cut (vicchidyate), is the jñāna; the siddhatā-bhāga is the bhedābhāsa. The Saugata
   sārūpya and the Jaiminīya prakaṭatā (object-dharma) are invoked. The tātkālikādi example and the
   sphuṭāvabhāsa establish the distinction even in the mind-objects.

9. **Kārikā 8 (vikalpe yo'yam ullekhaḥ...).** In the construction (free or experience-supporting),
   the uplifting (ullekha) is external too, not only the external-organ-known: it shines distinct
   from the knower-form (pramātṛrūpād vyatirikta eva bhāti, "ayam iti" idantayā). The inner-ness
   (āntarya) is the unity with the knower; the external-ness is its opposite. The body (the "I am
   white, I see the jar") and the mind (the buddhi) are dealt both as inner and external. The
   śaktitraya (jñāna/kriyā/icchā) are not divided from the power-possessor; their limitedness
   (pārimitya) is the viṣayakṛta contraction — the prāṇāpāna, the deśa (wall/mountain) are the
   saṅkocakopadhāna, and disregarding them the recognition-restored knower fills the viśvāvabhāsatā
   (the Vijñānabhairava 60/107: the tree-less, mountain-less place, abandoning the body-expectation,
   becomes pervading in days). The ghaṭābhāsabhitter ekatā (one-ness of the jar-appearance-wall)
   admits the twofold external-ness by the ābhāsāntaravyāmiśraṇa.

10. **Kārikā 9 (ullekhasya sukhādeś ca...).** The manifestation of the uplifting and the pleasure-
    and-the-rest with the external self is from the will (icchā) of the Lord, like the eye-form of
    the eye-and-the-rest grounds: the internal reflection of the blue/pleasure/sthāyi-vyabhicāri
    mind-operations is the Lord's external-making freedom (bahiṣkaraṇasvātantrya), and even the
    potter's clay/stick/wheel appearances are the Īśvara-will (parā eva saṃvit; the kumbhakāro'sau
    2.4.9). Wherever the bound-one's will is connected, even there the Lord's freedom is the
    support (nimittāntarābhāvāt). The lajjā is a cittavṛttiviśeṣa, not sukha/duḥkha. The
    bauddha/mānasa manifestation is resolved (the niścaya ends the prakāśa, so the "bauddha" is
    non-other). The Naiyāyika bhāṣya-ṭīkā distinction (pratyakṣa/mānasa/yogi/prātibha/aindriya) and
    the Nyāyasūtra 1.1.4 (indriyārthasannikarṣotpannam) are noted. The memory: the
    pūrvagṛhītatva — the memory-self-consciousness is the pramāṇa (1.7.5) — is re-joined at the end.

**The crux.** V2-R opens the aṣṭamo vimarśa (the māheśvarya): the great Lord (maheśvara), the one
knower, is the support (āśraya) of the knowledge-power, and the māheśvarya jñānaviṣaya is
determined here. The appearances, though divided and external-seeming, are inner (āntarāḥ), their
being-ness always-shining (kārikās 1–2, 5, 7); the external-ness is an adjunct, not the own-form
(kārikā 5); the object-action is appearance-fixed and absent in the inner knower-unity (kārikā 6);
the uplifting in the free construction is external too, but only because it shines distinct from the
one knower (kārikā 8); and all of this — the pleasure, the uplifting, even the potter's dealing —
is the manifestation of the Lord's will, the external-making freedom (kārikā 9). This completes the
jñānaviṣaya māheśvarya; V2-S (13200–13405) closes the aṣṭamo vimarśa (the aikya, the
bhāvikriyādhikāra introduction) and the whole jñānādhikāra.

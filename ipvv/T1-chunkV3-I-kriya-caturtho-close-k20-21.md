## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V3-I)

1. **The chunk's scope.** V3-I = M00022 lines 7200–7662, the close of the kriyādhikāra caturtho
   vimarśaḥ (the kāryakāraṇabhāvavimarśa). It completes kārikā 19's commentary (the pratibimba /
   ādarśa, the nīla-citsattva, the bāhyārthavāda/Dharmottara refutation), translates kārikā 20
   (*vāstave'pi cidekatve na syādābhāsabhinnayoḥ / cikīrṣālakṣaṇaikatvaparāmarśaṃ vinā kriyā*) and
   kārikā 21 (*itthaṃ tathā ghaṭapaṭādyābhāsajagadātmanā / tiṣṭhāsor evamicchaiva hetutā kartṛtā
   kriyā*), and closes with the ||4|| colophon (source 7661: kriyādhikāre kāryakāraṇabhāvavimarśaś
   caturthaḥ || 4 ||). The āgamādhikāraḥ (teaching-section) heading follows at source 7662 — the
   start of V3-J. The source is clean, dense Sanskrit throughout.

2. **The term-ledger LOCK (carried into Vol 3, unchanged).** kriyā = action; kriyāśakti = the
   action-power; jñānaśakti = the knowledge-power; icchā = will; vimarśa = reflexive-awareness;
   parāmarśa = re-recollection; prakāśa = manifestation; saṃvit = consciousness; pramātṛ = the
   knower; ābhāsa = appearance; pratibimba = reflection; ādarśa = mirror; nirmeya = measure;
   nirmātṛ = the measurer; hetutā = causality; kartṛtā = agent-ness; avidyā = ignorance; māyāśakti =
   the māyā-power; māyāpramātṛ = the māyā-knower; satyapramātṛ = the true-knower; kāryakāraṇabhāva =
   effect-cause-being; śaktivibhāga = the power-distinction; camatkāra = wonder; rāga/kāma =
   attachment/desire; kāraka = actor; upacāra = metaphor. The older "rehearsal/reflexion/light"
   variants remain retired.

3. **The pratibimba (reflection) and the citsattva.** The mirror (ādarśa) shows the distinct form
   without the form's miXing into it (the elephant in the mirror is not the elephant itself, nor
   another elephant, nor unreal — it is the mirror's own clear manifestation of that distinct form).
   The blue (nīla) is not separate from the consciousness-form: the cognition alone, manifesting
   with the self and with the non-self, is the blue's manifestation; the un-deniable
   meaning-manifestation (arthāvabhāsana) and the beginningless nature of the cognition-grounding
   (jñānalakṣaṇa) establish that "the cognition-manifestation alone is the blue-manifestation." The
   bāhyārthavāda (externalist, Dharmottara/Dharmakīrti) is refuted: no form of the jar can enter the
   dense mirror; the yellow-conch (pītaśaṅkha) delusion differs from the mirror-mountain because in
   the latter both appear fully (ubhayapūrṇaprakhyā), so there is no akhyāti (non-manifestation) in
   the mirror case.

4. **The kriyāśakti in the Lord.** Abhinava resolves the apparent āgama-distinction of the powers
   (icchā in the Lord, jñānaśakti in Sadāśiva, kriyāśakti in Īśvara): the action-power is the
   will, and the will is the reflexive-awareness (vimarśa/parāmarśa) — the camatkāra (wonder) that
   rests in the self. The icchā/rāga/kāma spectrum and the sautsukya (eagerness) follow from the
   degrees of self-resting (svātmaviśrānti). The free consciousness alone is the agent (kartṛ);
   the inert has no svātantrya, hence no action — the "Himālaya" and the "logs burn" are mere
   upacāra (metaphor), as the Grammarians' kāraka and the pāka example show.

5. **Kārikā 20 and the cidekatva.** Even accepting the real consciousness-unity with the
   distinction as avidyā, the action is impossible without the one wish-marker re-recollection
   (cikīrṣālakṣaṇaikatvaparāmarśa) joining the appearance-distinct jar and consciousness. The
   Vedānta anirvācyā avidyā is dismantled (of what? for whom?), the pratītinirūḍhi (cognition-
   firmness) favors the distinction's truth, and the kriyā/karaṇa requires the joining knower.
   Kārikā 21 crowns it: the desiring-to-stand (tiṣṭhāsor) Lord, with the jar-cloth-and-the-rest
   appearance-world-self, has the will alone as causality, agent-ness, and action.

**The crux.** V3-I closes the kriyādhikāra caturtho vimarśa: the reflection-manifestation grounds
the citsattva (all appearances are the one consciousness's manifestation, kārikā 19 close); the
action requires the one knower's wish-re-recollection joining the appearances (kārikā 20); and the
will alone is the causality, agent-ness, and action of the desiring-to-stand Lord (kārikā 21). The
||4|| colophon closes the caturtho vimarśa and the whole kriyādhikāra; the āgamādhikāra begins at
source 7662.

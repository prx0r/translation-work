## L1_READABLE — continuous English (one segment per block)

### S1 — root kārikā (ĪPK 2.2.1)
The cognitions of action, relation, universal, substance, direction and time — whose substrate is
both unitary and plural — are to be regarded as true, because of [their] stability and [their]
efficacy.

### S2 — the cognition of action as grounded elsewhere than in consciousness
Whatever cognition of action arises apart from the very essence of consciousness — an appearance of
action that is of a form both one and many, cut off by the power of māyā, and which takes the essence
of action as its object — that cognition is grounded in the true alone. For it is stable (not
uprooted by any refuter) and it carries agreement [with subsequent experience]: in the intended
[case], the attainment of the village is the very operation it helps to bring about. For a real
entity, in bringing about its effect, accepts both difference and non-difference, and it is the
carrier of agreement. In that [case], that which is the own-form of it, un-deviable, is precisely the
"form" (mūrti); and what, by the force of its appearance, makes even a non-own-form become its
own-form — that, clinging to the form, is the "action."

### S3 — the "Caitra is walking" example
Thus: the body of Caitra [and] Maitra, though divided by this and that place, time and shape,
shines without ever abandoning its oneness in the form "this is the very same [body]." That very
[unity] is the action, in the form "Caitra is walking." And in an action that has thus become
[real], the grasping (vimarśa) "Caitra is going" is experienced as continuing, as not uprooted by
anything; and it is the helper, in the intended attainment of the village, of that [action].

### S4 — quotation (Vīrāvalī-Bhairava 106)
"… in the connection [there is] the attentiveness (sāvadhānatā)."

### S5 — being-manifest alone is the form, not the causal efficacy
Holding in the heart that it is the being-manifested-alone which is the form of things — not the
doing of an effect (arthakriyākāritva) — [Abhinavagupta] examines that doing-of-an-effect, saying
"here," and the rest, down to "and it will be said," with a rich context. There he investigates
whether the doing-of-an-effect [is], of the being, by identity and by un-deviating-dharma-ness, or
by deviating-dharma-ness, the own-form, or the marker (lakṣaṇa), or — as operating near a marker —
the upalakṣaṇa, or whatever. There, "own-form-ness" he declares to be metaphorical (aupacārika),
saying "from the metaphor." For [just as] "having branches" alone is the tree-ness — a mere
exclusion by synonym-form-ness [of the word] — so it is not [said] in the world, "the unreal does
the effect," [because of] the non-known-ness by synonym-ness. And it is not [the case that it is]
marker-ness, he shows, saying "and it is marked."

### S6 — quotation
"even in the external meaning, thence, the distinction of the two, the manifesting meaning and its
[cognition]…"

### S7 — causal efficacy as sattva; the objection and reply
And there, with the making-cognition of that [thing], the doing-of-an-effect of a cause is
established as its being-ness (sattva) for anyone; but not [the doing-of-an-effect] of what is
manifest at the same time as that cognition, of the blue. [Objection:] "Let even what is simultaneous
with the manifestation be the cause." Here this very point must first be said: "of two of equal time
there is the effect-cause relation" — but this is not said [as the answer]; rather, for the
arrangement of the other known object, it is a further refutation. Here he says: "equal," for it is
the being-manifested-alone that, at that time, is the cause in the dealing of cause-ness; and that
is undifferentiated in the two-moon too — such is the sense. Now: "of the two, the blue, that which
is not manifesting while the anvaya and vyatireka both exist, simultaneous with the manifestation,
that very one is the cognition-cause; the two [anvaya and vyatireka] are present in the blue; that,
from the cognition, existed before alone; but not in the two-moon" — this he says [with] "thus."

### S8 — the true-word, the compound
He shows "the relation," and the rest, "forward," citing "the self-resting (svātmaniṣṭhā), the
distinct-manifestation…" (2.2.4) and the rest of the sūtras. Of the word "true" he shows the
exclusion, saying "from the being-manifested-ness alone." [Objection:] "Truth-ness is to be said of
the object, not of the appearances, of the object-bearers; how then the karmadhāraya?" — "Not [so]:
'the true appearances' is a ṣaṣṭhī-compound, fitting in a single sentence, of the relation and the
rest, by the primary/metaphorical distinction, from the simultaneous appearances, because of the
joining of difference and non-difference." This he removes, saying "with the appearance-truth-ness
alone"; this metaphor is not [merely] a metaphor; for with respect to the māyā-word this is a
metaphor — as is said:

> "from the Lord-superintended-ness the Lord alone is everything."

Thus, then, here there is no distinction at all between the appearance and what is manifested. Then
he says "from the that-form-ness"; thus even in the māyā-word, the param-essence-word not being
abandoned, [it is] explained with the karmadhāraya. Now, taking the bahuvrīhi-side, supporting the
māyā-word alone, he explains, "or (athavā)." "Of these, though, of the relation and the rest" —
"he even (so'pi), the use"; "thence alone, with our established rules, that which is the
object-action-use; thence-from-that-reason, with the different reference, the 'tas' fifth [cases]
are to be joined; otherwise the two 'eva'-words would be simply disconnected." "These (ete)," the
relation and the rest. This very [point] he fits, saying "convention." "With what speech are they,
[the relation and the rest], separately declared, from the sensory, and from the mental, from the
delusion?" He says: "thus, and." For if the distinction-ness of the conventional-being were wished
[to hold] by the delusion-form, then that would not be fitting: from the delusion-word-said, from
the mental-indeed, from the delusion, from the timira-marked, from the sensory, this separate
declaration would not be fitting. "From the delusion alone, from the spread, thus the declaration,
from some specific trace" — the ancient authoritative explanation he states for the refutation,
"that-again." With this, from his own explanation, the specific is indicated. This he does not
tolerate: "thus indeed." The 'khalu'-word indicates the ridicule. With this he says: "from the
other delusion no specific trace is here obtained in the explanation, by which the separate
declaration-worthiness would be."

### S9 — the convention-mind and the delusion
[Objection:] "The convention-mind, thus, becomes a coverer, if only the mind-raised [is the seen];
never, even at any time, is the seen — he — attributed to the general and the rest; in the
attribution, even-equal, with this object, of the conventional, the separate taking." This he
refutes, "not-even"; "of the moon the twoness — the seen-ness alone, if this; of the many-grasped-
common, even of the one, the seen-ness alone" — the rule is equal. Thus, with this much, of the
delusion-ness no one has any more, any specific, having established [which], even the
delusion-specific-ness is not agreed by the text-author alone, declaring with the dvandva; he says
"having-superimposed, and the rest." In the simultaneous-support-ness [there is] the dvandva; and
she is of the equal-own-form-two alone. The general, not swallowing all the specifics, operating,
possessing even the other specific, wishing the specific, crooking [it], not separately obtained,
the word-entry alone makes. "Directly," with the world-cognition, clearly. For with the
'dāntava'-'līvarda'-and-the-rest words alone the Ārya-worldly [people] declare the bulls; but with
the 'cow'-word the female-cows alone — thus alone the cognition. "In 'the cow-possessing this city',
when said, indeed, the curd-milk-prosperity alone manifests." "The conventional-ness": "of the
conventional the marker is this-much alone" — even by the other, not-wishing, this much alone is
to be accepted. That marker, with the marked-word, he indicates, "that-which (yattathā)." And in
this marker, with the manifestation-use two removed, in the non-possibility, our victory; with the
non-possibility, again, the scattered [being] in the use of the other — with this intent:

> "… with that reason (tena hetoḥ), the establishment, the un-refuted (abādhitā)"

and the rest. Otherwise, of the atoms the doing-of-effect-reason would be un-established. This is
the verse, the 1.

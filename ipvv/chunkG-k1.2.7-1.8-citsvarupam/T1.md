# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk G: the jñāna/cit refutation, kārikās 7–8 (the consciousness-form), the Sāṅkhya buddhi — T1

*Source: M00020 lines 3700–4300 (continuous). Base: Torella's ĪPK — the upoddhāta continuation.
Covers: the conclusion of kārikā 6 (the yasminsati verse), the refutation of the jñāna-reason
(kārikā 7: jñānaṃ ca citsvarūpaṃ cettadanityaṃ kimātmavat), the cit/acid (conscious/inert)
distinction, the Sāṅkhya's buddhi-as-vṛtti (kārikā 8: caitanyam ajaḍā), the ābhāsa/vyavasthā
(appearance/establishment), the bhoga (enjoyment), and the puruṣa/buddhi relation. The heart of
the passage: the jñāna-cognition cannot be the self's action (dharma) without inheriting the
self's own eternity/pervasion/oneness, and the inert (jaḍa) cannot manifest the object.
Max-effort T1.*

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk G)

1. **Kārikā 6's conclusion.** The yasminsati-verse establishes the pervasion of the reason
   (vyāpaka): a construction is justified only as the cause of its effect (taddhetutvena), else
   there is infinite regress of reasons (hetūnām anavasthitiḥ). The constant self is such a
   groundless construction for the Buddhist.
2. **Kārikā 7 (jñānaṃ ca citsvarūpaṃ cettadanityaṃ kimātmavat).** The core refutation of the
   jñāna-reason: if the cognition (jñāna) is of the consciousness-form (citsvarūpa) like the
   self, then it is eternal like the self (and the dharmi-hetu relation collapses — a dharma
   cannot differ from its dharmin in both being and non-being); if it is inert (jaḍa), it cannot
   manifest the object (nārthaprakāśatā).
3. **The svaprakāśa (self-luminous).** The consciousness-form's characteristic is self-luminous-
   ness; this is opposed (viruddha) to being-known (vedyarūpatva), which is the other-manifested
   (paraprakāśyatā). Freedom and dependence (svātantrya/pāratantrya) are opposed. So the
   cognition cannot be both conscious and known — hence non-eternal.
4. **The dṛṣṭānta (example) logic.** The "ātmavat" example is used by sādharmya (similarity), not
   vaidharmya (difference). The gloss on the negation: "kim... na anityam" — "why not non-eternal,"
   i.e. "not non-eternal," a prasajyapratiṣedha.
5. **The Jaiminīya and Sāṅkhya positions.** The Jaiminīyas take the person as cognition-form
   (saṃvidrūpa) with distinct states; the Sāṅkhyas as pure self-perception (svasaṃvedana), non-
   transforming (apariṇāmin). Both fall under the same prasaṅga.
6. **The deśa-kāla (place-time) restriction (saṃkoca).** The place-time are the known (vedya) that
   would contract (saṃkocaka) the cognition. But the awareness (bodha) is of a different rank
   (bhinnakakṣya) from place-time: it shines as "I" (aham) with freedom, while place-time shine
   as "this" (idam) dependent on the other. A different-rank thing cannot contract one of a
   higher rank (as the sky cannot be contracted by the underworld). Cited: ĪPK 1.52
   (kevalaṃ bhinnasaṃvedyadeśakālānurodhataḥ).
7. **The Sāṅkhya buddhi.** The buddhi-tattva is the "instrument aggregate" (karaṇaskandha), the
   first transformation (mahān) of the pradhāna; inert (jaḍa) by itself but appearing-cognitive
   (cetanavat) through the person's reflection (puruṣapratibimba). This is figurative awareness
   (upacaritabodha).
8. **Kārikā 8 (caitanyam ajaḍā).** If the cognition were neither wholly conscious nor wholly
   inert, the Sāṅkhya proposes the buddhi that takes on the object's form (rūpa) and the self's
   consciousness (caitanya) by reflection. Abhinava's refutation: the inert cannot arrange the
   objects (vyavasthā), and the conscious cannot both be and not-be non-inert — the two (cid/acid)
   are utterly opposed in one substance.
9. **The viśrānti (rest).** The object-cognitions rest (viśrānti) in the experience-determination-
   form consciousness; the unconscious object cannot establish (vyavasthāpayet) the distinct
   states (vyavasthā) of blue/yellow. This is the "establishedness" that needs a non-inert ground.
10. **The vṛtti (operation) as transformation.** The Sāṅkhya's vṛtti is a transformation-particular
    (pariṇāmaviśeṣa): the buddhi's operation is the cognition. The three types (kārya, dharma,
    vṛtti pariṇāma) are distinguished; the cognition is the vṛtti-transformation.
11. **The sāmānādhikaraṇya (co-reference).** "Buddhi = jñāna" is not a genuine identity but the
    aggregate-well-knownness (samudāyaprasiddhi) over the parts — the weak action-meaning is
    absorbed. So the co-reference removes the bodhatā-doubt.
12. **The phala (result).** The cognition-action's result is the sattva-part (sattvāṃśa), the
    light/manifesting guṇa. The sattva is pure (śuddha), the rajas dispels the tamas-cloud; only
    then does it manifest the blue-and-the-rest and the pleasure-and-the-rest. The fruit is the
    consciousness-shadow (saṃvicchāyā) — the undifferentiated personal citta-vṛtti-awareness.
13. **The bhoktṛ-bhogya (enjoyer-enjoyed).** The enjoyment (bhoga) is the mixing (melana) of the
    self- and object-reflections in the buddhi — not a property of the external object
    (prakaṭatā). The self as direct seer (sākṣāt draṣṭā) does not produce the manifest-ness; the
    ahaṃkāra mediates the superimposition (upakārakatva) that makes the self appear as agent.

**The crux.** The whole passage is the pūrvapakṣa's assault on the jñāna-reason for lordship: the
self's knowledge cannot be a real dharma (else eternal/pervading/one like the self) nor an inert
thing (else no manifestation). The Sāṅkhya's buddhi is the most sophisticated answer (reflection-
based cognition), and it is refuted in detail — the inert cannot arrange, the conscious cannot
contract, the enjoyment needs the non-inert consciousness as its ground. The jñānādhikāra's core
(svaprakāśa consciousness, viśrānti, svātantrya) is here exercised against the Sāṅkhya.

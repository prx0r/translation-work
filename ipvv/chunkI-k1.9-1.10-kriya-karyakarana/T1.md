# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk I: the kriyā (action) and the kāryakāraṇabhāva (cause-effect) — T1

*Source: M00020 lines 5077–5585 (continuous). Base: Torella's ĪPK — kārikā 9 (kriyāpy arthasya
kāyādes) on the action as not a separate real thing, and kārikā 10 (tatra tatra sthite) on the
cause-effect relation. Covers: the definition of kriyā as the sequential-parts (pūrvāparībhūta-
bhāga) operation, the grammarian Bhartṛhari citations (the āśritakrama verses), the ātman's
jñāna/icchā/kriyā, the relation (saṃbandha) refuted as a real third thing, and the
kāryakāraṇabhāva as the prior/posterior-being (pūrvatva/paratva) of the two relata. Max-effort T1.*

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk I)

1. **Kārikā 9 (kriyāpy arthasya kāyādes).** The action is not a real separate thing; the 
   body-and-the-rest (kāyādi) only has "the birth of the new-new in place-place" 
   (tattaddeśādijātatā). The action is not other (nānyā) — because of non-perception (adṛṣṭeḥ);
   nor is it one and sequential (na sāpyekā kramikā) — because the sequential cannot be one.
2. **The kriyā-characteristic.** The action (kriyā) is the operation (vyāpāra) related to the
   operator (kāraka), its parts (bhāgāḥ) being prior-later-opposed (pūrvāparībhūtāḥ) and sequence-
   operating (kramavṛttayaḥ). The Bhartṛhari citations (āśritakramarūpatvāt tatkriyeti) anchor this.
3. **The īśvara's action.** For the Lord-self-speakers, the self does have the action: "desires,
   knows, strives" (icchati jānāti yatate) — cited from ĪPK 1.3, 1.6, 1.24 (tadecchā tāvatī,
   evaṃ na jātu cittasya, ghaṭādigrahakāle tu ghaṭaṃ jānāti sā kriyā).
4. **The bhāgas (parts).** The parts of the "knows"-action (intention, lamp, etc.) are not themselves
   prior or later (apūrvāpare) but only prior-later-opposed by mutual expectation. The objection
   "let them just be the action by their own form" is rejected — that would destroy the 
   āśritakramatva (the sequential-form-ness) that is sought.
5. **The avayavī/aggregate refuted.** The action cannot be a whole (avayavī) — the sequential cannot
   be begun by non-sequential parts; nor an aggregate (samudāya) — because of non-simultaneity 
   (ekakālatvābhāvāt), unlike the army (senā) whose members are simultaneous. Nor even the parts
   alone — that would yield only difference (bheda), but the prior-later-being requires a 
   non-difference-touch (abhedasparśa).
6. **The one-action by cognition.** The oneness of the action (ekā kriyā) is cognition-determined
   (buddhyā vikalparūpayā adhyavaseyam) — the parts' prior-later-being is a construction over them.
   This is not rootless: it follows from the one-operator (ekakāraka) — the agent-object oneness
   (kartṛkarmaikya) and the one-fruit (ekaphaloddeśa).
7. **The ākhyāta's number.** The number (saṃkhyā) in "cooks" is the agent-object-gone (kartṛkarmagata),
   not the action's own; the 'eka' word means only difference-absence (bhedābhāvamātra). The
   action is ayutā (united) with the substance — by inherence or identity, not separate.
8. **The pratyabhijñā of the substance.** The one operator-substance is established by recognition
   (aikyapratyabhijñānāt — "sa eva ayaṃ caitra"). The action is inferred as the kārakātirikta
   kramika vastu: if the substance alone produced the fruit it would be before; if the other, then
   not expected; so there is a sequential distinct thing — the action. But this inference (from
   pratyabhijñāta substance + fruit) is itself refuted by the opposer (bādhaka): the fruit is not
   established from the recognized one substance.
9. **The bhāvanā (Mīmāṃsaka).** The action can also be the bhāvanā (the generated-operation, 
   jananātmā vyāpāraḥ) of the person in the to-be-effected heaven — the kriyāpy arthasya reading 
   via arthāpatti. The ṭīkākāra uses the Buddhist view to refute the Kaṇāda/Sāṅkhya/Mīmāṃsaka 
   cognition-views after the relation-refutation sūtra.
10. **The āśraya (support) refuted.** The sequential action cannot inhere in one support: the prior
    and later parts differ both in own-form and time, which forces the one support to have
    opposite dharmas (viruddhadharmādhyāsa) in four ways (caturdhā). The time-difference is
    universal (sārvatrika) and ends in own-form-difference; the place-difference is not universal.
11. **The samūha (aggregate) and āśritakrama.** The "sequence-possessing-like non-sequential ones"
    (āhitottaraśaktitvāt... kramavanta ivākramāḥ) is refuted — the 'iva' shows the unreality of the
    sequence; the later instants receive no power from the prior. The action is the mere new-being
    (apūrvajanmamātra) of the cognition-form in each time.
12. **The pariṇāma (transformation).** The action gathers under "transformation" (pariṇāma): both
    the place-difference-ones (growth/decline) and the time-sequence-ones (standing, sitting, 
    knowing, remembering, desiring). "Goes" = place-difference; "stands the day" = time-difference;
    "it blues/whitens" = own-form-difference. So the place-time-own-form-other-ness IS the action.
13. **Kārikā 10 (tatra tatra sthite).** There-there standing, "this becomes that" alone is seen;
    there is no other relation (nānyo'sti saṃbandhaḥ) besides the effect-cause-relation
    (kāryakāraṇabhāva). The relation is not a third thing beyond the two relata.
14. **The kāryakāraṇabhāva.** The cause-effect relation is just the prior-ness/later-ness
    (pūrvatva/paratva) of the two own-forms: the one whose being precedes (prayojakasattākaḥ) is
    prior (the cause), the one whose being follows (prayojyasattākaḥ) is later (the effect). The
    two are seen simultaneously in one perception (sakṛtpravṛttapratyakṣa) — "smoke-being when 
    fire, not when no fire" — the anvaya-vyatireka. Cited the two śloka on the kāryabuddhi.
15. **The relation as case-meaning.** Both the seventh case (tatra) and the genitive (rājñaḥ) and
    the operator-cases (kārakavibhakti) mean only the effect-cause-relation — there is no other
    relation-thing. The relation is not one-time (naikakālikam): in the fire's being there is no
    smoke, in the smoke's time the fire-instant is gone; so the "one relation" is a construction
    (vikalpa), not a real thing. Cited: ityamiśrāḥ svayaṃ bhāvāstānyojayati kalpanā.
16. **The sattāniṣedha.** The refutation of the relation's being (sattā) is by the opposer-pramāṇa
    (bādhaka) — dviṣṭhasya (kārikā 11) up to pāratantryādyayogāt — combined with kārikā 10's
    "nānyo'sti." So "there is no other relation" is established by the two sūtra-parts together; 
    the future sūtra's pañcamī (tena) will then state the reason.

**The crux.** Chunk I refutes the action (kriyā) and the relation (saṃbandha) as real separate
things, to destroy the jñānakriyāyogāt-reason for the self's lordship. The action is only the
place/time/own-form-difference (the new-being) in the substance, gathered by cognition into a
"one" sequential thing; the relation is only the effect-cause prior/later-ness of the two relata.
Both are constructions (kalpanā), not pāramārthika — exactly parallel to the jñāna's rejection in
Chunk H. This completes the destruction of the second part (kriyā) of the jñāna-kriyā-yoga reason,
setting up kārikā 11 (tena kartāpi kalpitaḥ) in Chunk J.

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk L)

1. **The yogi-perception and the other's cognition.** The other's cognition is known by the yogi-
   perception (Yogasūtra 3.20: pratyayasya paracittajñānaṃ saṃyamāt), not by inference: by the 
   dhyāna-absorption the yogin cultivates non-difference with the other's citta, and (since the 
   bheda-abhimāna is māyā-made) the other-citta shines clearly — it is not inferred. This removes 
   the doubt that the self's manifestation-by-inference is possible.
2. **The ekapramātṛ and the smṛti-dvaya.** "This one experienced" vs "I experienced" — the two
   memories are different-characteristic (vilakṣaṇa). The self-resting (ātmaviśrānta), other-not-
   expecting memory ("I") is the our-meaning (asmadartha); the other-established one is different.
   The manifestation is the self-resting, other-not-expecting (ananyāpekṣā); being known by memory
   is other-expecting — a contradiction.
3. **The anaupādhika characteristic.** The cognition's self-luminous-ness is unconditional 
   (anaupādhikaṃ lakṣaṇam); it does not become otherwise by the object-difference. The otherwise-
   becoming is pervaded by the conditional-ness (aupādhikatva) — which the self-luminous-ness lacks,
   so its otherwise-becoming is negated. Hence the cognition's other-manifested-ness is opposed 
   (viruddham) — jñānasvarūpam anyasaṃvedyaṃ na (the future sūtra).
4. **The atyantaviruddha.** The utterly-opposed is fitted by the reason: the memory-known 
   (smṛtivedya) time — the self-resting I-form (ātmaviśrāntirūpo'ham) would not shine; but the 
   self-luminous/other-manifested are opposed. The dṛk svābhāsaiva is re-established via the 
   svasaṃvedanaikarūpā.
5. **The anyavedyatā via the cause.** The experience is not the memory's object even by cause-ness: 
   if it were, the taste-cognition would be the object of the form-cognition (by the immediately-
   preceding-condition). The distinction is the one-knower-inherent (ekajñātṛsamaveta) cognition-
   ness — the one-object-inherence (ekārthasamavāya) for the Kaṇāda. Without it, Vāsuki's cognition
   would be the object of ours — everyone-all-knower (sarvaḥ sarvajñaḥ), the great turmoil.
6. **The indriya-niyama (sense-organ rule).** The mutual-revealer-ness of the cognitions is rejected:
   the three-fold sense-organ rule (from the organ's force, the organ's fitness, and the number of
   organs) would be lost. The rasa-taste is manifested only with its limitor (upādhi) — the pure 
   taste (kevala) is not manifested by the eye alone (else the ghost-like unseen limitor).
7. **The cakṣurjanitā.** The eye-born cognition does not manifest the taste-cognition (which is 
   non-existent in its own form — like the past experience in memory); it manifests only the 
   error-cognition (sabhramajñāna) with its limitor and the limitor's (the taste). So the reason 
   is not unestablished. The sense-organ-rule-non-being would cause the blind-too-not-blind, etc.
8. **The sākāravāda and the yaugapadya.** In the simultaneous (yaugapadya) mutual-revealer-ness: 
   in the dancer's-seeing, each spectator's cognition would be revealed by the others' — the 
   infinite-dancer's-woman-manifestation (anantanartakyavabhāsa) instead of the one. The lotus-
   mistake (kamalādoṣa) example shows the mixed (vyāmiśra) appearance. So neither order nor 
   simultaneity permits mutual-revealer-ness.
9. **The saṃskāra as niyāmaka refuted.** The impression-born-ness cannot rule (the saṃskāra gives 
   only the similarity, sādṛśyamātra); the graspable/grasper parts of the prior experience are not 
   manifested in the memory. The destroyed (naṣṭa) cannot enter a relation with the memory — the 
   cause-opposed-perception (kāraṇaviruddhopalabdhi) of the destroyed-ness.
10. **The ekaviṣayatva (one-object-ness).** Even the Vaiśeṣika (accepting the constant self) cannot 
    establish the memory-experience one-object-ness: there is no third cognition to grasp their 
    similarity — the cause-non-perception (kāraṇānupalabdhi). The figurative use (upacāra) is not 
    the real-vastu-establishment.
11. **Kārikā 3 (memory as error).** The memory does not have the seen-object as a real support; it 
    rests on the object only by error (bhrāntyā dṛṣṭālambanatā). The past father is destroyed — 
    how would he shine? The smārta-father-appearance is place-time-unrestricted and cannot unite 
    with the experiential appearance. The memory is the seen-object by error — this is unfitting 
    (asamañjasam). The Śaṅkaranandana citation (artho na bhāsate bhrāntismṛtyorayogāt) denies the 
    object-dharma-ness of the manifested.
12. **The asad-ātmākhyāti.** The memory supports the not-existing (asad), or (in the ātmākhyāti 
    view) manifests the vāsanā-constructed cognition-ākāra; but never the prior experience or its 
    object — both are non-existent at the memory time. It only determines by the conceit-core 
    (abhimānasāra) — "that was experienced" is the experience-time-touching (anubhavakālasparśa). 
    The three false/self-cognitions (vitathā/asad/ātmākhyāti) are indicated.
13. **Kārikā 4 (smṛtitaiva).** The memory's error-ness, impression-born-ness, and object-establishing- 
    ness are all refuted (sarvatomukham). The memory's primary form (smṛter mukhyaṃ rūpam) — the 
    non-loss (asaṃpramoṣa) of the experienced — is uprooted: the prior experience and its object are 
    wholly hidden (muṣita). The anumāna's error does not establish the object like the memory's 
    (the anumāna has the succession, pāraṃparya; the memory does not). The smṛti is not a vikalpa 
    that is necessarily erroneous.
14. **Kārikā 5 (bhrāntitve cāvasāyasya).** If the determination were erroneous, the object would not 
    be established from the inert (na jaḍād viṣayasthitiḥ); hence in the non-inert, the object-
    establishment rests in the own-rising (nijollekhaniṣṭhānā). The determination (adhyavasāya) is 
    the one that errs (bhramarūpa), not the self-cognition (svasaṃvit) or the non-constructive 
    manifestation.
15. **The dvicandra and the satkhyāti.** The two-moon non-constructive perception is NOT error — the 
    appearance IS the being (pratibhāsa eva sattā); the not-existing cannot shine (asacca khyāti is 
    contradicted). The error is only the determination "this external two-moon is common" 
    (bāhyatāvasāya), which the opposer uproots. The Bhaṭṭa verses (asatyasyābahirbhāvo bādhaḥ 
    sattvam ato dvidhā; ābhāsabhede tv arthaḥ kastatrābhedo bhramo vapuḥ) anchor this. The good 
    Śaṅkaranandana's vijñānaśūnyatā-nīti (bāhyārthaśūnyatā) is justified: the manifestation of the 
    blue is not another's dharma, so its manifestation is self-established (vyāpakānupalabdhi).

**The crux.** Chunk L completes the siddhānta's refutation of the memory-as-error thesis. The 
memory does not reveal the prior experience (only rests in the self — kārikā 1) nor is it error 
(kārikā 3) nor does its determination establish the object by being erroneous (kārikā 5). The 
Śaṅkaranandana's position — that the manifested is not the object's dharma and that the memory 
supports only the not-existing — is refuted by the satkhyāti/pratibhāsa-is-being insight: the 
appearance is the being, so the two-moon non-constructive perception is not error; only the 
external-ness-determination (bāhyatāvasāya) errs. The determination is the error-maker, not the 
self-cognition. This preserves the memory's validity (against the Buddhist's reduction of memory 
to error) and the self-luminous cognition's priority.

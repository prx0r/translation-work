# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk M: the jñānādhikāra reflexion core, the anusandhāna, kārikās 6–7 — T1

*Source: M00020 lines 6900–7600 (continuous). Base: Torella's ĪPK — the culmination of the 
jñānādhikāra. Covers: the close of kārikā 5 (the bāhyatāvasāya — external-ness-conclusion as the 
error); kārikā 6 (evam anyonyabhinnānām aparasparavedinām jñānānām anusandhānajanmā naśyen 
janasthitiḥ — the mutual non-knowing cognitions would destroy the tracing-born world-course); the 
anusandhāna (tracing/connecting) as the essence of the knower; kārikā 7 (na ced antaḥkṛtānantaviśvarūpo 
maheśvaraḥ syād ekaścid vapur jñānasmṛtyapohanaśaktimān — unless there be a Maheśvara who has 
interiorized the infinite universe-forms, a single embodiment, powerful in cognition-memory-removal); 
the ekātmatā (one-self-ness) of the cognitions; the vyavahāra (worldly course) requiring it; the 
maheśvara as the one knower. This is the siddhānta's conclusion. Max-effort T1.*

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk M)

1. **The bāhyatāvasāya as the error.** The error in the magic/eye-defect is only the external-ness-
   conclusion (bāhyatāvasāya) — the appearance itself is the being (pratibhāsa eva sattā). The
   child who sees the shore-trees "running" from the boat is told: it is only the appearance-dharma,
   not a real motion. The non-constructive self-cognition is not error; only the "this external is
   common" determination errs.
2. **The vyavasthā as pramātṛ-dharma, not viṣaya-dharma.** The establishment (vyavasthā) of the
   objects is the knower's dharma (pramātṛdharma), not the object's (viṣayadharma) — because it is
   per-knower-distinct (pratipramātṛ bhidyate), like pleasure. The object-dharma is not per-knower-
   distinct (the white cloth is white for all). Hence the pervader-opposed-pervaded-perception:
   the establishment's being the object-dharma is pervaded by the non-per-knower-distinction.
3. **The saṃvidātmanā.** The determination's operation is the consciousness itself (saṃvid) — not a
   separate thing; the "full manifestation" (pūrṇaḥ prakāśaḥ) is what it is. Where there is no full
   manifestation, there is no error either (like the pleasure's self-cognition) — the 
   vyāpakānupalabdhi. The error is only the memory-determination on the not-object (atīrthe'rthe).
4. **The jaḍājaḍa arrangement (suvyavasthitaḥ).** The inert/non-inert characteristics are well-arranged:
   what is "this"-ness (idantā) — manifested by another, distinct from the manifestation — is inert;
   what is "I"-ness (ahantā) — self-luminous, the manifestation itself — is non-inert. The two are
   mutually opposed and well-divided (pravibhaktaṃ rūpam).
5. **Kārikā 6 (the anusandhāna).** The mutually-distinct, non-mutual-knowing cognitions would destroy
   the tracing-born person-standing (anusandhānajanmā janasthitiḥ). The cognition cannot accept the
   other-cognition's object (unless via the other-cognition-known-ness, which is refuted). The
   desire (arthitā) requires the determination of the object as the pleasure-means — which requires
   the object-mixing (viṣayamelana). Without the tracing, the desire/operation/obtainment/removal-
   taking world-course would perish.
6. **The ekapramātṛ.** The tracing (anusandhāna) is the essence of the knower (pramātā) — the "I"
   cognition (aham idaṃ jānāmi) is I-ness-concluded (ahantāparyavasitam) and prior-later-self-form-
   rehearsal-rested. Without it, the person would be faint-like (mūrcchitaprāyī).
7. **The viṣayamelana refuted for the Buddhist.** The object-mixing cannot be established by the 
   external-object view: the seen-ness (dṛṣṭatā) is not the object's dharma (it is the prior
   cognition-form), nor the cognitions' own-form identity, nor the mutual-revealer-ness. The 
   ālayavijñāna is momentariness-incapable. The sākṣiṇī cognition ("known is this object") is
   refuted via the jñānāntaravedyatā.
8. **The jñānāntaravedyatā (the cognition-known-by-another-cognition) refuted in detail.** The 
   cognition cannot be known by another cognition: the two-manifestation, the pot-example (the
   cognition's manifestation is not the object's dharma), the self-manifestation-necessary-other-
   manifestation (ātmaprakāśananāntarīyaka-paraprakāśana), the aham as the I-manifestation with 
   freedom (ananyamukhaprekṣitva), the Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi 22 (prakāśasyātmaviśrāntirahaṃbhāvaḥ),
   the Naiyāyika's transcendent self refuted, the infinite-regress, and the vyāpakaviruddha.
9. **The lamp-example refuted.** The lamp-lighting-another-lamp analogy fails: the cognition is not 
   the inert (it manifests itself); its manifested-ness (prakāśyatā) does not become its own form
   (like the pot), so it does not become inert. The grāhakasya jñānasya na kācit khaṇḍanā.
10. **The ghaṭasyāpi and the virodha.** Even the pot's being-manifested is not its own dharma; the 
    pervader-opposed-pervaded-perception shows the cognition's own-manifestation (svasaṃvidrūpatva)
    negates its other-cognized-ness (parasaṃvedyatva): the "this"-ness (idantā) is opposed to the 
    "I"-ness (ahantā), which is the consciousness's characteristic. The svabhāva-hetu / 
    viruddhopalabdhi-hetu forms are shown (the śiṃśapā examples).
11. **The naśyet and the ekātmakatā.** If the cognitions were many-selfed (anekātmatā), the world-
    course would necessarily fall (vyāpikā prasajyate). By remainder (pāriśeṣya) the cognitions'
    one inward-face consciousness-tattva form (ekā antarmukha-saṃvittattvasvarūpatā) is established —
    the one self (ekātmatā) of the cognitions.
12. **The bhaṭṭa verses.** vyāpṛttiḥ pratyayasyārthe tatra kiṃcidakurvataḥ — the cognition's operation
    in the object does nothing to it; so the object-dharma-ness of the cognition is not fit, and the
    known-ness of the cognitions is removed. The vyavahāra is pervaded by the one-object-ness 
    (ekaviṣayatvena vyāptaḥ).
13. **Kārikā 7 (na ced antaḥkṛtānantaviśvarūpo maheśvaraḥ).** Unless there is a Maheśvara who has
    interiorized the infinite universe-forms (antaḥkṛtānantaviśvarūpaḥ), a single body 
    (ekaścid vapuḥ), powerful in cognition-memory-removal (jñānasmṛtyapohanaśaktimān) — the 
    establishment fails. The one-self-ness (ekātmatā) of the cognitions is established by the 
    not-deniable practical-activity (vyavahārabalāt).
14. **The śaktayaḥ and the māheśvaryam.** The powers (jñāna/icchā/kriyā, the unmeṣa-nimeṣa) are the
    Lord's supported (tadāśritāḥ), not independent, not separate from his own form — the power-
    joining is his Maheśvar-ness. The consciousness-tattva is the manifestation-form, the wall of all
    cognitions (sakalapramābhittibhūtam), self-established (svayaṃ siddha).
15. **The jaḍa as not-designated-by-the-manifestation.** "The inert shines" is refuted: what is not
    the manifestation's own form cannot be co-referential with it in truth (like the vermilion with
    the blue). The "the inert shines" statement makes the inert the manifestation's form — the
    svabhāva-hetu via the kāraṇānupalabdhi. The Nareśvaraviveka (svayaṃ prāsādo na śveto) anchors.
16. **The bhinnāvabhāsinām.** The distinct-manifesting ones (the objects), though appearing distinct
    by the conceit (abhimāna), are in truth the "I"-manifestation-alone form (ahaṃprakāśamātrasvarūpāḥ)
    — the daṇḍāpūpīya-maxim shows the one knower behind the many manifestations.

**The crux.** Chunk M is the jñānādhikāra's conclusion: the many mutually-non-knowing cognitions 
would destroy the tracing-born world-course (kārikā 6); hence they must share one self (ekātmatā) —
and that one self is the Maheśvara who has interiorized the infinite universe-forms (kārikā 7), the
consciousness-tattva as the manifestation-form wall of all cognitions. The cognition cannot be known
by another cognition (it is self-luminous, its "I"-ness opposed to the "this"-ness of the inert);
the establishment of the objects is the knower's dharma; and the distinct appearances are in truth
the one self-luminous manifestation. This is the siddhānta's positive culmination, answering the 
Buddhist pūrvapakṣa completely.

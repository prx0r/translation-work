## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk N)

1. **The tādrūpya (that-form-ness).** The cognitions, manifesting with the I-part excess
   (ahamaṃśātirekeṇa), have the supreme-real manifestation-self-I-ness-that-form-ness
   (prakāśātmakāhantātādrūpyam) unbroken even in the māyā-state. The excess/non-pervasion/
   non-eternality are the object-facing-limitor-made (viṣayaunmukhyopādhikṛta); the māyā (the
   Lord's power) does not cover the own-form (śaktyā śaktimatāmanāvaraṇāt).
2. **The ekavākya of naśyet / na cet.** The vṛttikṛt joins "would-perish the person-standing"
   (naśyet janasthitiḥ) and "not-if the Maheśvara" (na cet) into one statement via the 
   hetumallīṅā. The tracing (anusandhāna) of all the objects' cognitions is the consciousness-
   tattva itself (aśeṣapadārthajñānānāmanyonyānusandhānaṃ cittattvam) — the one inward-face
   consciousness, not a separate thing.
3. **The kevala-pradeśa (the alone-place).** The alone-place cognition (kevalapradeśajñānaṃ, the 
   "no pot here" place) is the key test: the pot's absence is not established by the separate 
   non-perception (which would need an infinite regress), but by the pervader-opposed-pervaded-
   perception. The aghaṭa is a paryudāsa (the other-vastu manifestation), not a prasajyapratiṣedha.
4. **The Dharmakīrti verses.** ekopalambhānubhavād idaṃ nopalabhe iti... — the non-perception is 
   established by perception alone; otherwise the non-being-ness of the object would be known from 
   the non-perception and the non-being-ness of the perception by another — the infinite regress. 
   The anupalabdhi of the absent second cognition is not a separate pramāṇa.
5. **The rūpa-sparśa and the vikalpa/smṛti.** The touch-cognition and the form-cognition (different 
   sense-organs) cannot be co-cognized: the vikalpa cannot refute the prior form-cognition (it is 
   not the not-that-form vikalpa); the memory only imitates the prior experience, it does not 
   manifest it as object (jñānānāṃ parasaṃvedyatvasya nirākṛtatvāt). The smṛti's anukaraṇa means it 
   cannot reveal the co-being (sahabhāva) of the two cognitions.
6. **The ekasvasaṃvedana-viśrānta.** When there is one inward-face consciousness-tattva (not 
   separate self-cognitions), the two cognitions, if simultaneous, would rest in the one self-
   cognition and manifest as one — which does not happen (the darkness-time "I do not see the 
   form"). Hence the one self-cognition is established (svātmani rūpajñānaṃ nāstīti niścinvan).
7. **The ananyāpekṣa and the ekam antarmukham.** "Who are you whose pot-cognition?" — the other-
   cognition-known-ness leads to the infinite regress and the loss of the own-other division. Hence 
   the other-not-expecting self-luminous "I" (ananyāpekṣaprakāśam aham) is the one inward-face 
   consciousness-tattva, which removes all the confusions (samastaviplavāvakāśābhāvaḥ).
8. **The smṛtyādi and the svācchandya.** The many cognitions are traced by the one inward-face 
   (which manifests as-much-as it wills — svecchāvaśāt yāvaj jighṛkṣati tāvat pratyavabhāsate), so 
   there is no simultaneity-all-tracing-fall. The niḥstimita (non-flashing) would be the inert-ness; 
   the vimarśa (reflexive-awareness) is the Bhagavat's power-particular (sphurattāspandādi).
9. **The jñāna-smṛti-apohana śaktis.** The Bhagavat's powers are jñāna (manifestation), smṛti 
   (the inward-face form's rehearsal of the prior outward-face — the memory that enables the 
   vyavahāra of the momentary appearances), and apohana (the vikalpa's removal of the suspected 
   opponents). The phalabheda (result-difference) is why the one power is spoken of in the plural — 
   the fire is burner/cooker/illuminator; the one power is the bhāva's own form, not separate 
   (the Mālinīvijayavārttika 3.5, Mālinītantra 4.5 verses).
10. **The viśvarūpatā and the māyā-pramātṛ.** The Bhagavat, from his freedom, separates a being from 
    the manifestation and manifests it; the māyā-pramātṛ is the contracted manifestation. The 
    māyā-pramātṛ's outward-face form is the new-new cognition; the aiśvara svātantrya is the 
    jñānaśakti. The Gītā 11 (the viśvarūpa-darśana) is cited: Arjuna sees the universe in the 
    body — the paramātman's own. The anyonyātmatā (mutual-self-ness) of the beings is the 
    īśvarāṅgatā (the Lord's limb-ness).
11. **The apoha and the svalakṣaṇa.** The vikalpa is the apohanaśakti: it removes the suspected 
    not-that-doers (like the pot suspected of the many-opponents by the object-action). The 
    anyonyātmatā makes the "not-that-form" (atadrūpatā) possible. The ācāryapāda verses 
    (yāvatyo'rthakriyāstāvatsaṃkhyo'tatkāriṇāṃ gaṇaḥ; ekapratyavamarśo yastatkāriṣu bahuṣvapi) 
    anchor this. The svalakṣaṇa's being-manifested has no opposite to be suspected (the 
    Bhaṭṭa's apoha is refuted; the niḥsvabhāvatva would make the blue/yellow vikalpas identical).
12. **The māyīyatā and the paramārtha.** The māyā-position (the pramātṛ-prameya bheda) is the 
    not-rehearsed (avimṛśyamāna) one-form-entry of all in the manifestation — the real is the 
    tādātmya (identity), the ekarūpāveśa (one-form-entry). The māyā does not remove it (else no 
    manifestation); it only withholds the rehearsal (the neti-parāmarśa). This is the 
    viśvātmatva (universe-self-ness) that is not unmanifested.
13. **The Bhagavat's vikalpavyāpāra.** The Bhagavat himself does not have the new-manifestation/
    memory/construction dharmas (they arise in the māyā-pramātṛs from his freedom); his freedom 
    IS the jñānādi-śaktis. But in the Īśvara-state he accepts the "not" (neti) rehearsal — the 
    apohanaśakti is present even there (the "idam aham" co-reference needs it). The contraction 
    (saṃkoca) of the sarvajñatā means his māyā-ness is not for him.
14. **The mattaḥ and the fifth-case.** The Gītā's "from me" (mattaḥ) — the cause-ness/support-ness 
    of the own-self — is the pañcamī; the lyab-elision in hetau (matsāmarthyādetat). The 
    jñāna/smṛti/apohana are the same cognition-power variously used; the two new powers are not 
    separate from the jñānaśakti (tānyeva ca śaktir iti).
15. **The colophon.** The third vimarśa (paradarśanānupapattivimarśaḥ) in the jñānādhikāra closes 
    — and with it Vol 1 of the Īśvarapratyabhijñāvivṛtivimarśinī.

**The crux.** Chunk N completes the volume: the maheśvara of kārikā 7 is the one consciousness-
tattva whose manifestation-freedom is the cognitions' tracing, memory, and removal (the apoha); the 
universe-forms are interiorized in him (antaḥkṛtānantaviśvarūpaḥ); the māyā-pramātṛs are the 
contracted manifestations of the one freedom; and the paramārtha is the one-form-entry (ekarūpāveśa) 
of all in the manifestation, with the bheda being only the withheld rehearsal. The self-luminous 
cognition, the ajaḍa-pramātṛ, and the maheśvara are one — this is the siddhānta's final, complete 
statement, closing the third vimarśa and the volume.

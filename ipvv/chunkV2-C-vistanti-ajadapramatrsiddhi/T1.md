## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-C)

1. **The grāhakagrāhya and the sārūpya.** The object-ness (viṣayitā) of the cognition is its that-
   ākāra-imitation-ness (the sārūpya); but the cognitions cannot have the object-object relation
   among themselves, because their difference is not supreme-real (only the jñeya-part, not the
   bodha-part, can be the object). The two-mirrors analogy is rejected. The inference: no supreme-
   real difference → no object-object relation (vyāpakānupalabdhi). Dharmakīrti's pervader-chain
   investigation is noted as not-made.
2. **The māyā and the bodha's svarūpa.** The māyā does not cover the awareness's own form (like the
   fire's burning-power); the covering is itself the producing of a prior-state-distinct form. In
   the one-ending object-being, the cognition cannot be known by another — the own-form-manifestation
   alone is the viṣayīkṛtatva (the oyster-shell/conch example; the vyāpakānupalabdhi).
3. **The yogi's knowledge of the other's cognition.** The three kinds (this-one-knows; my-past-
   experience-in-the-other-body; the-other's-prior-seen-now-remembered) are all the other-knower-
   gone perceptions (pramātrantaragatā upalambhāḥ). The tattvam = paramātmavedakatva; the sarvajña.
   The oneness (aikyam) with the other would make the yogin a partaker of the other's dharmas — the
   pitiable saṃsāri-state (the saffron-water analogy); but the sukhādi rests only in the grāhya-part
   (not in the pure knower).
4. **The anugrahāpaghāta (favor/harm).** The real-knower (vāstava pramātā), attained by the firm
   determination, does not conceive "I am happy" — hence no favor/harm (Gītā 2.58, 12.15). But in
   the vyutthāna (arising) state, the body-I-being-impression-firmness makes the burning-pain arise
   in the buddhi (the traveler analogy). Those who have loosened the body-I-ness do not get the
   pleasure-pain even in the sandal-touch (the Spandakārikā 8 on the icchā).
5. **The first kind → memory.** The paracittajñāna (knowing the other's cognition) is reduced to
   memory (smṛti) via the asaṃpramoṣa — the jātismara (birth-remembrance); the not-own-experienced
   past-births are new-cognitions from the impression-direct-cognition. The third kind: the pot
   seen by another is remembered by the yogin via the pūrvadṛṣṭatā (prior-seen-ness), not as new —
   the tatpūrvaprakāśa identity. The Manu-and-the-rest's dharma-manifestation is the param-Lord's
   beginningless manifestation made into memory.
6. **The smṛtyanubhava not vedyavedaka.** Even in the memory-experience, there is no known-revealer
   relation: the pure (śuddha) awareness-form (sarvārtharūpatā śuddhir jñānasya), the citiśakti, the
   prabhāsvaram cittaṃ (Yogadarśana). The māyā-power cannot split the awareness (the Spandakārikā 2:
   tasyānāvṛtarūpatvāt); the Nareśvaraviveka; the turya-word with the crow-repetition.
7. **Kārikā 6 (smaryate yaddṛgāsīn me).** The memory's root (maulika) rehearsal is "by me seen" (mayā
   dṛṣṭam) — the Vākyapadīya 3.1.105 (ghaṭajñānam iti jñānaṃ ghaṭajñānavilakṣaṇam). The difference in
   the memory is only guṇa (quality), not grown (aprarūḍha); the root-cognition is augmented
   (upavṛṃhyate), not refuted, by the explanation-cognition.
8. **Kārikā 7 (yā ca paśyāmy aham imaṃ ghaṭo'yam iti vāvasā).** The determination (avasā) "I see this"
   reflects the seeing (darśanam) in the grāhakāṃśa; it thinks (from the impression) and directly-
   cognizes the experience. The "this" (ayam) word rests not on the pot-form alone but on the
   perceptibility-rehearsal — the perceptibility is the I-conceit-object (ahaṃkāragocara), the
   knower-made (pramātṛmaya). The Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi 15 (idamityasya vicchinnavimarśasya kṛtārthatā)
   is cited; the infinite-regress of the "my-seeing" is noted.
9. **Kārikā 8 (tanmayā dṛśyate dṛṣṭo'yaṃ sa ity āmṛśaty api).** The grāhyagrāhakatābhinnau arthau
   (the two objects — grasped and grasper — non-distinct in their being-ness) shine in the one
   pure-cognition-form knower. The "seen"/"seen-by-me" and "this"/"that" rehearsals mark the
   experience-memory; the "this"-ness-rehearsal makes the grasper "I" and the graspable "this"
   discontinuous (vicchinnaprathau) — but they are tanmayau (made of that), not separate from the
   pure-knower-supreme-object. The Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi 20 (yadyapyarthasthitiḥ) is the anchor.
10. **The māyāśakti and the viśvātmatā.** The beings are of the paramārthaprakāśa (the conscious-
    only-supreme-object); the māyā-power cannot divide them from the manifestation (else the 
    aprakāśana). The vedyīkaraṇam of the objects is the māyā-fault (3.2.9: bodhānām api kartṛtvajuṣāṃ
    kārmamala); but the bodha's manifestation-form-ness toward all would make everyone all-knowing —
    so the contraction (saṃkoca) is the aṇu-mala (anyastvahaṃbhāvo vedyeṣvapyāṇavo malaḥ). The
    difference-manifestation (bhedena prathā) in the not-all-knowing-ness is thus established.
11. **The paśulakṣaṇa and the aṇutva.** The māyā-knower's pratyagātma-cognition, being the 
    prāṇādisaṃkoca-partaking, is the aṇu (atomic); the Kiraṇa-verse (paśur nityo hy amūrto jño...)
    defines the paśu (bound one). The pure revealer-ness (vedakatā) is distinct from the īśvara's;
    the "iva" (like-the-Lord) is fit because the idam-aham parts in the māyā-knower are distinct
    from the īśvara's form.
12. **The śūnya-vedaka and the param-Śiva.** The breath's revealer is the buddhi, its revealer the
    void (śūnya) — the "śūnyātiśūnya" (the final knower-form) is the vedyarāśi-removed pure-
    consciousness. The verse (tathāhi vedyate yadyat tanmadhye'haṃ na kaścana...) anchors; the
    ākāśasamāpatti (sky-attainment) and the mahāvidehā (great-discarnate). The yogin's direct
    cognition is "I-this" (aham idam), not the separate-known — the construction (vikalpa) gives
    the vedyatā. The vyāpakaviruddhopalabdhi.
13. **The upadeśa (teaching).** When the param-Lord teaches "I am your self," he teaches the
    created-many-dharma-face form (atadrūpavyāvṛttyā), not the pure-non-divided cognition directly
    — the "this-Īśvara-my-form-your-self" (the vaidharmya-example with the body's teaching). The
    siddha-pādas verse (atītaḥ panthānaṃ tava sa mahimā vāṅmanasayoḥ...). The ābhāsa (appearance)
    is the param-Lord's own form (tadeva parameśvarasvarūpameva, na adhikam) — 1.5.16.
14. **The grāhyagrāhakayugalaka and the colophon.** The graspable-grasper pair, infinite-inner-
    difference-divided, shines in the param-manifestation-form Bhagavat — like the great-mirror
    holding the reflection (the svabhāvahetu: tadrūpeṇa prakāśamānatva). The fourth vimarśa
    (smṛtiśaktivimarśaḥ) closes at ||4||.

**The crux.** Chunk V2-C completes the caturtho vimarśa (the smṛtiśakti): the memory's root is the
grasper's "by me seen" rehearsal; the grāhyagrāhaka (graspable-grasper) are the two objects that,
though distinct in their manifestations, are both tanmayau (made of the one pure cognition) — the
"this"-ness-rehearsal makes them discontinuous, but they rest in the one pure knower (the 
ahantā-viśrānti, the prakāśasyātmaviśrāntirahaṃbhāvaḥ). The māyā-power does not split the awareness;
the paśu (bound one) is the contracted cognition (aṇu); the final knower is the śūnyātiśūnya (the
param-Śiva). The whole is grounded in the Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi and the mirror-metaphor of the 
param-manifestation.

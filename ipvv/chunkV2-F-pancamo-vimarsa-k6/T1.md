## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-F)

1. **The chunk's scope.** V2-F = M00021 lines 3600–4400, the close of the pañcamo vimarśa's bāhyārthavāda and the transition to the īśvarādvaya. Its spine: (i) the refinement of the other-minds (parasantāna) inference — the candana/agni and ṣṭhīvana (spitting) examples prove the samīhitā-niyama (only the *aimed* action is the wish's effect), so the other-stream can never be inferred from a merely-uttered appearance; (ii) the bāla-rudana and āvaraṇa/upasaṃhāra arguments against the vijñānavādin; (iii) the īśvarādvayavādin's entry — caitra, created by the param-Lord in one-ness (ābhāsāntaraikya), knows all like maitra; the stotra and Śivadṛṣṭi citations; (iv) kārikā 6 (*syādetadavabhāseṣu*) — the appearances alone suffice for dealing; the external object is needless; (v) the full refutation of the external object as whole (avayavin — the Nyāya-sūtra 2.1.34 ground) and as atoms (paramāṇu — the Vaibhāṣika position), establishing that only the ābhāsa is real and is the object of arthakriyā.

2. **The term-ledger LOCK is carried from V2-E** (vimarśa = reflexive-awareness; prakāśa = manifestation; ābhāsa = appearance; saṃvit = consciousness; jñāna = cognition; bodha = awareness/awakening; samīhā = wish; vyāhāra = utterance; pramātrantara = other knower; parasantāna = other stream; upasarpaṇapratyaya = approaching-cognition; samanantarapratyaya = immediately-preceding-condition). The "rehearsal"/"reflexion"/"light" variants are retired.

3. **samīhitā/samīhitatva.** I render samīhitā as "the aimed (state)" / samīhitatva as "the being-aimed," to keep the niyama argument legible: only the *aimed* action can be the effect of a wish — abandoning samīhitā-tva is impossible. This is the crux that defeats the other-minds inference and grounds the "the wish is established only by one's own cognition (svasaṃvitsiddha)."

4. **The kārikā 6 reading.** "teṣvevāvasite sati vyavahāre kim anyena bāhyenānupapattinā" — "if the dealing is determined in those very appearances, what [need] of the other, the external, with its impossibility?" The "anupapatti" (impossibility) of the external is the whole preceding refutation. The deśa/kāla niyama verse and the "pramāṇyaṃ vastuviṣayaṃ" and "bhrāntitve cāvasāyasya" (1.3.5) citations are the epistemic core.

5. **The avayavin/paramāṇu refutation.** Following the Nyāyasūtra 2.1.34 (*sarvāprasiddhir avayavyasiddheḥ*), the whole collapses; then the atom is refuted by the six-part-contact and the ekāneka form-contradiction. The [X]-flagged crux is the exact force of "jñānamekaṃ gṛhṇadeva aparaṃ gṛhṇātīty etāvatā vaitatyābhāsaḥ" — the two-ness appearance is admitted but the configuration-appearance is not; I keep both the given gloss and note the kha-readings.

6. **The īśvarādvaya turn.** The stotra (*muhurmuhur aviśrānta*), Śivadṛṣṭi 1.3/1.6, the Śatarudriya, and the Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi 23 (*prakāśasyātmaviśrāntir ahaṃbhāvaḥ*) all converge on: the Lord's freedom manifests the whole appearance-variety within himself; the sādhāraṇī (Lord-made) sṛṣṭi is where the knowers' difference melts; only by the "living-cognition ladder" (jīvadbodhasopāna) is the advaya reachable. The ahaṃ-grasping of "ahaṃ ghaṭo bhāse" is the seed of the recognition.

**The crux.** V2-F completes the refutation of the external object. The other-minds inference fails because the other's wish is never established in the inferer's own cognition (svasaṃvitsiddha), and only the aimed action is the wish's effect — so the first/third-person split cannot be bridged, and if it were, it would fit a demon or a worm equally. Abhinava then turns the whole debate: the appearances (ābhāsa) are the only reality the dealing needs; the external object (whether the Nyāya whole or the Vaibhāṣika atom) is refuted, and what remains is the manifesting cognition alone. This is the ground on which the īśvarādvayavādin stands: caitra is created by the param-Lord in unity, knows all like maitra, and the one Lord-freedom manifests the varied appearance within himself — the jñānaśakti's conclusion and the bridge to the sixth vimarśa (smṛti/apohana).

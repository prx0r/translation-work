## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-G)

1. **The chunk's scope.** V2-G = M00021 lines 4400–5200, the jñānaśakti-vimarśa's īśvarādvaya turn. It closes the external-object refutation (the avayavin/paramāṇu shadow-sun and six-part arguments) and states the positive thesis: kārikā 7 (*cidātmaiva*) — the Lord, who is consciousness, manifests the whole object-mass from within himself, like a yogin, with no external cause; the caturvidhā sṛṣṭi (parā, sūkṣma, vyūha, vibhava) with the tanmātra and pratyayasarga; kārikā 8 (*anumānamanābhātapūrve*) — inference never concerns the unmanifested, since it is construction (vikalpa) and construction needs a prior experience; the kārya-hetu/svabhāva-hetu and pakṣadharmatā analysis; kārikā 9 (*ābhāsaḥ punarābhāsād bāhyasyāsīt kathañcana*) — the external is never established by any means; kārikā 10 (*svāminaścātmasaṃsthasya*) — the Lord's self-standing being manifests the beings; hence the will-reflexive-awareness proceeds (the aham-prakāśa, the sahaja prakāśa, the sarva-ābhāsa resting in the self).

2. **The term-ledger LOCK carried from V2-E/F.** vimarśa = reflexive-awareness; prakāśa = manifestation; ābhāsa = appearance; saṃvit = consciousness; jñāna = cognition; bodha = awareness/awakening; icchā = will; samīhā = wish; vyāhāra = utterance; kriyā = action; pramātrantara = other knower; upasarpaṇapratyaya = approaching-cognition; niyatiśakti = the necessity-power; tanmātra = subtle element (kept as tanmātra); sṛṣṭi = creation; sādhāraṇī/asādhāraṇī = common/uncommon. The "rehearsal/reflexion/light" variants remain retired.

3. **cidātmaiva (kārikā 7).** I render the compound as "the consciousness-self-alone is the Lord" — the deva who is cid-ātman. The point: the Lord's lordship (aiśvarya) is the freedom to join and disjoin appearances; hence he can manifest the whole object-mass from within himself, no external upādāna. The yogin-example is the crux: the yogin's creation is real (arthakriyākāri) though not caused by the famous clay-potter chain; so "nirupādānam" means "without the external famous-cause material," not "without any cause." The [X]-flagged reading: whether the yogin still uses the assembled atoms (saṃghaṭitāḥ paramāṇavaḥ) or the pure will — I follow the text's own turn ("with a grass-bit overstepping the fame" → the pure will; and the Bhaṭṭa-Divākara verse confirming the dream's own-creation).

4. **The caturvidhā sṛṣṭi.** The fourfold creation (parā/sūkṣma/vyūha/vibhava) is introduced via the āgama verse *parasūkṣmavyūhayogād vibhavācca caturvidhā*. I keep the structural mapping in the apparatus: paratva in the three higher, sūkṣmatva in the two, vyūha in one; the niyatiśakti supplies the upādāna-appearance (tanmātra etc.) while in truth everything is nirupādāna in the Lord. The pañcabrahma and the pratyayasarga (buddhi-tattva-attribute) are the being-creation that interpenetrates the tattvas.

5. **anumānamanābhātapūrve (kārikā 8).** The seed: inference is vikalpa; vikalpa needs a prior experience; so inference never concerns the never-manifested. The seed→sprout, clay→jar, stick-wheel→jar examples establish kāraṇatva as the universal "seen-prior" by pratyakṣa+anupalambha. This is the ground for denying that the external (never experienced) can be inferred. The kārya-hetu (apūrva, "new," inferred) vs svabhāva-hetu (identity-based, established) distinction is the epistemic core; the pakṣadharmatā and the "sāmānyato-dṛṣṭa" are handled via the dharmapratyakṣa argument.

6. **The anumāna/vyāpti and the indriya.** The sense-organ itself is never perceived directly (nityaparokṣa), yet it is inferred only as the "seen-form-light-more some cause" — i.e., by the universal. So the inference never reaches an unmanifested particular. The pratyābhāsa (per-appearance) operation of the pramāṇa (cf. 2.3.2 *ekābhidhānaviṣaye mitiḥ*) is the bridge to the later adhikāra.

**The crux.** V2-G completes the īśvarādvaya-vāda's positive turn. The external object being refuted (both the whole and the atom), the only source of the ordered appearance-variety is the Lord's own freedom: consciousness alone (saṃvid eva) is the all-world-cause, and its aiśvarya is precisely the freedom to join and disjoin appearances. Kārikā 7 states this via the yogin-example; the caturvidhā sṛṣṭi gives the ontological map (parā/sūkṣma/vyūha/vibhava with the tanmātra and pratyayasarga) — all resting, in truth, in the Lord (ananyopādāna). Kārikā 8 then shuts the door on ever inferring the external: inference is construction needing prior experience, so the never-manifested can never be reached — the bāhya has no epistemic purchase. The ahaṃ-prakāśa (svāminaś cātmasaṃsthasya, kārikā 10) is the seed of the recognition: the being that manifests itself as "I" is what manifests all beings within itself.

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-G, continuation)

7. **The anumāna/vyāpti (kārikā 8 continued).** The kārya-kāraṇa relation is established by pratyakṣa + anupalambha alone (the smoke-fire, clay-jar, stick-wheel-jar examples); the cause-ness is a universal grasped "as-seen-prior." The svabhāva-hetu establishes only the already-established (vyāvahāryate, not newly inferred); the kārya-hetu alone infers the "new." Inference never reaches the never-manifested external. The pakṣadharmatā is a third member, itself grasped by present perception, so the pervasion-construction does not make the inference touch an unmanifested particular.

8. **The sense-organ (indriya).** It is never perceived directly (nityaparokṣa), yet it is inferred only as "the seen form-light-more some cause" — i.e., by the universal of cause-ness; and the "seen" designation is fit because the cause-ness-universal is seen. The apoha aside (saugata's prativyāvṛtty-adhyavasāya) and the pratyābhāsa operation of the pramāṇa (2.3.2 *ekābhidhānaviṣaye mitiḥ*) are the cross-reference.

9. **Kārikā 9 (ābhāsaḥ punarābhāsād bāhyasyāsīt kathañcana).** The external can be neither perceived nor inferred: the not-manifested cannot be the object of any pramāṇa, since every pramāṇa is cognition-form and only the manifested is cognized. The "somehow" (kathañcana) is the residue that even a concession leaves — the inference, being saṃvedana-rūpa, stays in the own-cognition; the distinct is never reached. This closes the bāhya question.

10. **Kārikā 10 (svāminaścātmasaṃsthasya).** The Lord's self-standing being manifests the being-mass; the manifestation is not apart from the will-reflexive-awareness — the icchā is the parāmarśa-particular, and the parāmarśa is the prakāśa's own nature (so there is no infinite regress of the will wanting to will). The "eka"-word and the *nareśvaraviveka* verses establish that the to-be-created must be non-separate-from-first (apṛthakpratham) with the creation-will. The sahaja (natural) manifestation is the aham-prakāśa; the this-ness is the bāhyatā-appearance that joins it. This is the seed of the whole recognition: the being that is self-standing as "I" manifests all beings in itself, and the will-reflexive-awareness is its own nature.

**The crux (continuation).** The epistemic closure of the bāhya is complete: no pramāṇa — perception or inference — reaches the external, because every pramāṇa is cognition-form and only the manifested is cognized (kārikā 9). The positive thesis then stands alone: the Lord, who is consciousness, is self-standing as "I" and manifests the whole being-mass within himself; the will-reflexive-awareness that would make a cloth or a jar is nothing but the manifestation's own nature (kārikā 10). The sahaja aham-prakāśa and the sarva-ābhāsa resting in the self are the ground of the recognition (pratyabhijñā) that is the whole jñānaśakti's culmination.

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-H)

1. **The chunk's scope.** V2-H = M00021 lines 5200–6000, the deepest vimarśa-section of the jñānaśakti-vimarśa. It completes kārikā 10 (the icchā as the prathama-tuṭi; the smaraṇa; the niyatiśakti; the kṣaṇabhaṅga), states kārikā 11 (*svabhāvam avabhāsasya vimarśaṃ vidur*) — the reflexive-awareness is the manifestation's own nature, otherwise it would be inert like crystal — and develops it through the sphaṭika/ādarśa and the sārūpya, the grāhakagrāhya, the camatkāra/ānanda and the rasa-theory (Abhinavabhāratī), the ātman/anātmā prakāśana; then kārikā 12 (*ātmāta eva caitanyaṃ citkriyā citikartṛtā*), and kārikā 13 (*citiḥ pratyavamarśātmā parā vāk*) with the whole parā/madhyamā/vaikharī vāk and the Śivadṛṣṭi refutation of the grammarian's paśyantī.

2. **The term-ledger LOCK carried from V2-E/F/G.** vimarśa = reflexive-awareness; parāmarśa = the (self-)reflexive-awareness / the rehearsal-of-the-own-form (kept as "reflexive-awareness"); prakāśa = manifestation; ābhāsa = appearance; saṃvit = consciousness; jñāna = cognition; bodha = awareness/cognition; icchā = will; camatkāra = rapture (kept as camatkāra); ānanda = bliss; vāk = word/speech (parā vāk = the highest word); paśyantī/madhyamā/vaikharī = kept transliterated; sphoṭa = the sphoṭa; kriyā = action; kartṛtā = agency; niyatiśakti = the necessity-power. The older "rehearsal/reflexion/light" variants remain retired.

3. **Kārikā 11 (svabhāvam avabhāsasya vimarśaṃ vidur anyathā prakāśo'rthoparakto'pi sphaṭikādijaḍopamaḥ).** The reflexive-awareness ("I") is the manifestation's own nature; if it were not, the manifestation, even tinged with the object, would be like the crystal or the inert — i.e., no different from jaḍa. The whole kārikā-11 commentary turns on showing that the mere object-tingeing (like the mirror reflecting the jar) is not enough; only the will-entry (icchā-anupraveśa), the svātantrya, makes the manifestation alive. This is the heart of the ajaḍa-pramātṛ thesis.

4. **The camatkāra/rasa passage.** Abhinava here gives one of his most famous statements of the rasa theory: the camatkāra is the reflexive-awareness (parāmarśa) resting in the knower (pramātṛ-viśrānti), the paramānanda, the nirvṛti; the sahṛdaya is one whose heart (hṛdaya = parāmarśa) takes the rasa, disregarding the standing manifestation-part that rests in the object. He explicitly cross-references the Abhinavabhāratī (*rasā nava* = the nine rasas, the obstacle-free taste-form mind-operations). The [X]-flag: the exact sentence on the vīta-vighnatva of the śṛṅgāra-rasa is dense; I keep the reading that the rasa-taste rests wholly in the knower, transcending the object's obstacle.

5. **Kārikā 12 (ātmāta eva caitanyaṃ citkriyā citikartṛtā).** The same-substratum-ness (sāmānādhikaraṇya) of the self, consciousness, the consciousness-action, and the consciousness-agency is the vimarśa-prādhānya. The Śivasūtra and Kallaṭa are the anchors; the grammatical analysis (citi/kip, caitanya/ṣyañ, cetanakriyā) shows the kriyā is reflexive-awareness-form and the relation is knower-rested. The substance (dravya) discussion ties the self to the universal-as-padārtha.

6. **Kārikā 13 (citiḥ pratyavamarśātmā parā vāk svarasoditā).** The consciousness-action is the reflexive-awareness, the parā-vāk; this is the Lord's freedom and lordship — not the paśu's action-organ word. The vāk-śakti enters the (inert) organ-class and makes it act freely (Spandakārikā 1.6). The paśyantī/madhyamā/vaikharī analysis, the sphoṭa (Vākyapadīya 1.85), and the svarasoditā-as-nityoditā confirm the parā-vāk as the pure ahaṃ that has no deśa-kāla-ākāra contraction; and the Śivadṛṣṭi (2.20, 2.80) refutes the grammarian's claim that paśyantī is the param — the paśyantī is still contracted (viṣayārpitātmanā), so only the parā-vāk (the pure ahaṃ, sadāśivarūpatā) is the param.

**The crux.** V2-H is the philosophical core of the jñānaśakti-vimarśa. It establishes that the reflexive-awareness ("I") is the manifestation's own nature (kārikā 11): a mere mirror-like object-tingeing would leave the manifestation inert; only the will-entry, the svātantrya, vivifies it. The camatkāra is the reflexive-awareness resting in the knower — the paramānanda that no mere object-pleasure (with its vyatiriktākāṅkṣā) can give; this is the ground of the rasa theory. Kārikā 12 ties the self's own-form to consciousness, action, and agency (the same-substratum vimarśa). Kārikā 13 identifies the consciousness-action with the parā-vāk, the Lord's freedom and lordship: the pure ahaṃ that manifests the whole world within itself, in which the knower-object difference is not yet threaded (bhedāsūtraṇarūpa); the paśyantī/madhyamā/vaikharī are its contracted derivatives, and the parā-vāk (sadāśivarūpatā) is the param. This is the seed of the recognition and of the whole māheśvarya.

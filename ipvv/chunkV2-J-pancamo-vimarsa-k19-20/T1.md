## T1 apparatus — the key decisions (Chunk V2-J)

1. **The chunk's scope.** V2-J = M00021 lines 6800–7600, the close of the jñānaśakti-vimarśa's vimarśa-section. It completes kārikā 19 (the saṃskāra-ādhāna/prabodhana — the reflexive-awareness is what deposits and awakens the memory-impression; the staimitya argument — even the absorbed perception is vimarśa-form, else the "again-constructing" and the later remembrance could not happen; the manaskāra/avadhāna and the anuvyavasāya) and states kārikā 20 (*ghaṭo'yam ityadhyavasā nāmarūpātirekiṇī pareśaśaktir ātmeva bhāsate na tvidantayā* — the determination "this jar" is beyond name-and-form; it is the param-Lord's power manifesting like the self, not by the this-ness), with the vācyavācaka and sphoṭa analysis, the sāmānādhikaraṇya of the guṇa/dravya, and the refutation of the vācaka's idantā.

2. **The term-ledger LOCK carried from V2-E through V2-I.** vimarśa = reflexive-awareness; parāmarśa = the (self-)reflexive-awareness; pratyavamarśa = the re-reflexive-awareness; anuvyavasāya = the re-observation; prakāśa = manifestation; saṃskāra = impression; vāsanā = vāsanā/impression; bhāvanā = meditation; manaskāra = mind-attention; avadhāna = attention; adhyavasāya = determination; saṃkalpa = construction; vikalpa = construction; smṛti = memory; svalakṣaṇa = own-marker; sāmānya = universal; sphoṭa = the sphoṭa; vācya/vācaka = the denoted/denoting; icchā = will. The older "rehearsal/reflexion/light" variants remain retired.

3. **Kārikā 19's saṃskāra-section.** The core: only the vimarśa-form cognition (the reflexive-awareness-resting pramātṛ) can deposit (ādhāna) and awaken (prabodhana) the saṃskāra; a reflexion-empty perception could do neither. The child's first perception is not vimarśa-empty (the opponent's claim), because convention-grasping (saṃketagrahaṇa) requires the reflexive-awareness; and the "all-cognition/quieted-state" (staimitya) argument shows the absorbed perception too is vimarśa-form — else the "again-constructing" would differ, and the later remembrance could not arise. The Bhaṭṭa and abhidharma citations (Abhidharmakośa 2.50) are the anchors.

4. **Kārikā 20 (ghaṭo'yam ityadhyavasā nāmarūpātirekiṇī).** The determination "this jar" is beyond the name ("jar") and the form (the shape); it is the param-Lord's own power (pareśaśakti), manifesting like the self (ātmeva — i.e., as "I"), not by the this-ness (na tvidantayā). This answers three objections: (i) the coarse word-tinged vimarśa is still the manifestation's life (not a separate inert thing); (ii) the Sāṃkhya's claim that adhyavasāya is a buddhi-attribute (jaḍa) is wrong — it is the citi's power, the paśu's determiner; (iii) the vimarśa has no form other than the vācya/vācaka — the third reflexive-awareness is the vimarśa itself. The vācyavācaka/sphoṭa analysis (Yogasūtra 3.17, Ślokavārttika 4.182) and the sāmānādhikaraṇya of the guṇa/dravya establish that the this-ness does not attach to the vācaka.

**The crux.** V2-J completes the vimarśa-section's proof that the reflexive-awareness is the life of the manifestation. Kārikā 19 shows it is the vimarśa that deposits and awakens the memory-impression (so memory and construction presuppose it), and that even the absorbed perception (staimitya) is vimarśa-form — for otherwise neither the re-construction nor the later remembrance could occur. Kārikā 20 states the positive result: the determination "this jar" is beyond name-and-form; it is the param-Lord's power, manifesting like the self as "I," not by the this-ness. The paśu's determinations, memories, and constructions are all the one vimarśaśakti contracted by the māyāśakti; and in the recognition they return to the pure aham. This is the seed of the whole recognition that the jñānaśakti-vimarśa has been preparing.

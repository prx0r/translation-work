3. **Kārikā 10 (tadaikyena vinā...).** The world-path (lokapaddhati) of the consciousnesses — all
   dealing (vyavahāra) — would not be without the unity (aikya) of the cognitions; and since it is,
   the unity is of-necessity. It is not hard to establish: the param-essence of the consciousnesses
   is the manifestation (prakāśa eva hi saṃvidāṃ paramārthaḥ), and it is one, from the non-being of
   distinction; the one is, in-truth, the knower (pramātā). The vṛtti's "paramātman" = the param-
   essence-knower-ness (paramārthapramātṛtā); the Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi 20 is invoked. The Sāṃkhya-
   Jaiminīya svaprakāśā-saṃvid, the ancient Vaiśeṣika citsvabhāva-ātmā (1.2.7), and the Buddhist
   grāhikā-buddhi all confirm. The māyāśakti's bheda is not real: the distinction is from the other
   (the body, the known), not from the manifestation; the grāhyagrāhaka (the māyīya body and the
   paramārthapramātṛ) — the tanmayā dṛśyate (1.4.8) — makes the bheda a pratibimbakalpa (reflection-
   like). The bhrānti (the shell-silver) is real only with respect to the māyāpramātṛ: the
   saṃvāda/visaṃvāda are his arthakriyā objects.

4. **Kārikā 11 (sa eva vimṛśattvena...).** The great Lord is he alone whose reflexive-awareness-
   ness (vimṛśattva) is fixed (niyata, un-broken): the manifestation-form never appears un-reflected
   (avimṛṣṭa), like the galvarka-gem. It is the "I"-camatkāra, the bliss-form, the other-facing
   (ananyonmukhatātmanā). The pure knowledge-action of the God is the vimarśa itself. The
   prakṛti/pratyaya analysis (the jñāna/kriyā words): the nature means saṃvedana-jñāna, the
   pratyaya (kvi) denotes the action whose kriyā is the siddhatā-pratyavamarśa-fitness — the
   re-reflexive-awareness (pratyavamarśa) resting in the freedom (svātantrya). The ahaṃvimarśana
   (the I-reflexive-awareness): pure in the śuddha-daśā (the paramātman, the ahaṃ-bhāva eternal),
   un-pure when the māyāśakti's idantā conceals it (the saṃsāra-goers' state). The un-pure ahaṃvimarśa
   arises from the bhinnam idaṃbhāga (the mind/breath body) — its svarūpa is fallen (pracyuta) by the
   guṇatraya (the jñāna = sattva, the kriyā = rajas, the niyāmaka tamaḥ); the puruṣa is not other —
   it is the contracted-consciousness, the non-recognition of the own lordship (the sa
   svarūpāparijñānamayo 'nekaḥ pumān, 4.1.3). The ahaṃvimarśana is echoed as bhittikalpa (wall-like),
   not as the vyāpaka (the "yatra yatra dhūmaḥ" rule). The tri-daśā (śuddha/miśra/aśuddha) and the
   āgamasiddha daśāntaratritaya (paravyāmiśrāpara) are both kept (the etā eva ca; the atrāparatvaṃ
   bhāvānāṃ, 3.1.5).

5. **The jñānādhikāra is COMPLETE.** The aṣṭamo vimarśa closes at the ||8|| colophon (the
   śaktivargaikāśrayagata-māheśvarya-vimarśa), and the whole first adhikāra closes at the colophon
   "samāptaś ca ayaṃ jñānādhikāraḥ prathamaḥ" (source 13401). Vol 2 (M00021) — vimarśas 4–8 of the
   jñānādhikāra — is fully translated. The next target is Vol 3 (M00022), the kriyā-, āgama-, and
   tattva-ādhikāras.

**The crux.** V2-S closes the aṣṭamo vimarśa and the whole jñānādhikāra: the consciousnesses are one
in the manifestation, and that one is the knower — the world-path of all dealing rests on this unity
(kārikā 10); and the great Lord (maheśvara) is he whose reflexive-awareness (vimṛśattva) is fixed
and un-broken — the pure knowledge-action of the God is the vimarśa itself, the "I"-camatkāra, the
freedom (kārikā 11). The un-pure ahaṃvimarśa of the bound one is only the māyā-concealed, guṇatraya-
stained, contracted form of the same pure ahaṃ; the recognition (pratyabhijñā) of the Lord as the
one knower — whose reflexive-awareness, knowledge, and action are the manifestation's own freedom —
is the whole purpose of the jñānādhikāra, now complete.

   bhedābhedavatārthena tena na bhrāntir īdṛśī* — thus indeed the object-attainment would be, of the

3. **The satya/asatya/saṃvṛtisatya distinction.** Kārikā 1 establishes the action/relation/
   universal/substance/direction/time cognitions as true (satyāḥ): they are so by the stability
   (sthairyam, the not-uprooted reflexive-awareness) and the use (upayoga, the arthakriyā). The
   satya (e.g. the jar-cognition, whose "this jar" vimarśa is un-uprooted and object-action-
   serving), the asatya (e.g. the double-moon, whose vimarśa is uprooted and whose arthakriyā fails),
   and the saṃvṛtisatya (the conventional-true, the sāmānya: the sarvatrika/sadātanī cognition that
   does not cease) are distinguished. The sāṃvṛta is a bhrāntiviśeṣa (a delusion-specific), not a
   separate truth-class. The arthakriyākāritva (object-action-doing-ness) is not the own-form
   (svarūpa, that would be paryāya) nor the marker (lakṣaṇa, that would over/under-extend) but the
   upalakṣaṇa (the incidental marker) whose upalakṣita is the svātantrya.

4. **Kārikās 2–3 (the ekatva/anekatva; the anuvyavasāya).** The one inner essence (āntaraṃ tattvaṃ,
   the saṃvinmātra) is un-computable (kalanāśūnya); the same one, attaining the external-organ-
   known-ness, goes into the many-ness from the place-time-own-nature — without opposition, since
   the cognition is un-broken (like the cause/non-cause). Kārikā 3: the mind-re-determining
   (mano'nuvyavasāya), being, makes the action-and-the-rest conceptions supporting the two (the
   one-many). The sāmānya and the other universals gain their "life" (jīvita) only in the
   construction (the sāmānyoparañjakatā, the sambandhibhedodreka, the kṣaṇabhedaikya, the
   avayavarāśīkaraṇa). The māyāpramātṛ's anuvyavasāya is the occasion of the kriyādi-vyavahāra; the
   śivatādaśā and the sadāśivādibhūmi are not the locus.

5. **Kārikās 4–6 (the sambandha, the jāti/dravya, the kāraka).** Kārikā 4: the relation is the
   mutual-form-clinging (paraspararūpaśleṣa), the bhedābhedakoṭidvaya-swing (dolāyantra), resting in
   the one knower. Kārikā 5: the class/substance manifestations are one-form outside too — the
   one-cow-ness with the individual-distinction (the "imā gāvaḥ", the abheda-qualifier/bheda-
   qualified); the jar is the niḥsandhibandhana-one with the extended-many-parts (the vaitatya, the
   part-mass). The māyā-fit (māyocita) pramātṛ, the sambandhamukhaprekṣi, confirms. Kārikā 6: the
   kāraka-conjunction (samanvaya) is the kriyāvimarśa-object; the action's distinction (by the
   adjuncts) and its un-distinction (by the one-agent-rest) are the relation-life. The
   dig/kāla (direction/time) cognitions are the limit-limitable, the bhedāvamarśana-middle-
   abheda-ending (melayantī) — the relation-form. The samavāya (inherence) of the Kāṇādas is
   rejected: its one-ness yields the sarvapadārthasaṅkara, its bheda yields the anavasthā, and its
   "tādātmyena iva" is only the sādṛśya/utprekṣā; the samavāya's svātantrya is the parameśvara or
   his śakti — our view.

6. **Kārikā 7 (the arthasiddhi, the bhrānti).** The object-attainment (arthasidhi) of the object-
   action-desiring world is only through the bhedābhedavat artha (the distinction-un-distinction
   meaning) — the relation-general-and-the-rest. Without it no arthakriyā; where it is not cognized
   it still flashes within (antaḥsphurann eva). If this were untrue, nothing would be true; so
   whoever deals with it as delusion (bhrāntivyavahāra) is himself the deluded one (sa eva paraṃ
   bhrāntaḥ). The sthairyopayogau (the stability and the use) of the grasping-sūtra are fit; the
   stability = the bādhakābhāva, the use = the saṃvāda (in one explanation), or the stability =
   the saṃvāda and the use = the arthakriyākāritva (in the other).

**The crux.** V3-B establishes the dvitīyo vimarśa of the kriyādhikāra — the bhedābhedavimarśa. The
action, relation, universal, substance, direction, time cognitions are true (kārikā 1) — their
truth is the un-uprooted manifestation (prakāśamānatā) and the arthakriyā, not a separate real
feature. The one inner essence (saṃvinmātra) enters the many-ness through the place-time (kārikā 2);
the mind-re-determination (anuvyavasāya), by the free knower's operation, makes the kriyādi-
conceptions (kārikā 3). The relation is the mutual-form-clinging in the one knower (kārikā 4); the
class and substance manifestations are one-form outside too, with the individual-part-distinction
(kārikā 5); and the kāraka-conjunction is the action-reflexive-awareness-object, the direction/time
being the limit-limitable relation (kārikā 6). Finally, the object-attainment is only through the
bhedābhedavat artha, and its "delusion" is the delusion of the deluder (kārikā 7). This completes the
second vimarśa of the kriyādhikāra.

**The question:** How can the action, relation, universal, substance, direction, and time-cognitions be true (satya) rather than mere delusion, given that the external view (bāhyavāda) cannot account for them?

## 2. The argument, reconstructed

Abhinavagupta opens the dvitīyo vimarśaḥ with a maṅgala and a seven-verse plan: the Lord's un-thwarted freedom (apratihata svātantrya) in the form of action-power (kriyāśakti) is to be established, since the externally-refuted action-relation and related categories, which fail to fit in the external view, fit fully here. The first kārikā declares that the action, relation, universal, substance, direction, and time-cognitions are true (satyāḥ), by stability (sthairya) and by use (upayoga), as one-many-support (ekānekāśrayā).

The commentary explains: the action-cognition (kriyābuddhi) arising elsewhere than from consciousness-essence (saṃvittattva) is mere action-appearance (kriyābhāsa), cut off by māyā-power; but the action-essence (kriyātattva) as object is true (satya eva), because the reflexive awareness (vimarśa) resting on it is stable (sthira), not uprooted by the refuter (bādhaka), and agrees (saṃvādavān) with the intended action (grāmaprāpti). The being (bhāva), in performing object-action (arthakriyā), accepts distinction-non-distinction (bhedābheda); its un-deviable own-form (avyabhicaraṇīya svarūpa) is the form (mūrti), while what, by appearance-strength, makes the non-own-form into own-form, is the action (kriyā). Thus Caitra's body, though divided by place-time-form, manifests as "that very, this" (tad eva idam), and "Caitra goes" is the action; the reflexive awareness continuing in this is never uprooted, and the village-attainment is unbroken object-action. In double-moon consciousness, by contrast, the reflexive awareness is uprooted, and the object is untrue (asatya) by deficiency of object-action use, even if it causes delight or excitement.

The satya/asatya/saṃvṛtisatya distinction is then drawn: the conventional-true (saṃvṛtisatya) is nothing of the general (sāmānya) from its truth-ness, while the untrue (asatya) is the double-moon and the like. The external action too is explained by action-power alone (kriyāśaktyaiva), so the external view's action is already accounted for. The stability (sthairyam) is the always-helper (sarvadopayogin), and the object-action use (arthakriyopayoga) is the object-ness (arthavattva). The sūtra-meaning is brought in by joining the prior section with the other section, with the action taken as example (dṛṣṭānta).

The commentary then examines whether object-action-doing-ness (arthakriyākāritva) is the being-ness's (sattva) own-form (svarūpa), marker (lakṣaṇa), or near-marker (upalakṣaṇa). Own-form-ness is metaphorical (aupacārika): branch-possessing alone is tree-ness, a mere exclusion, and the unreal does not do object-action, so it is not known in the world as synonym. Marker-ness is shown not to hold: the marker is the un-deviability (avyabhicāraṇīyatā), mutually; otherwise over-extension and under-extension would result. Although direct-manifestation-cognition (sākṣātkārijñāna) and construction-less consciousness (kalpanāśūnya vedana) are not synonyms, being distinct exclusions, they are marker and marked by mutual non-deviation. The others (anyeṣām, the knowns) are then taken up.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain

1. The text breaks off mid-sentence ("a...") at the end of the supplied slice; the continuation of the argument on the others (anyeṣām) and the later kārikās is not present.
2. The exact scope of "satya/asatya/saṃvṛtisatya" as applied to the six cognitions (kriyā, saṃbandha, sāmānya, dravya, dik, kāla) is asserted but not fully worked out in the slice.
3. The citation "(vi. bhai. 106)" and the verse "(2.2.4)" are referenced without full text in the slice; their exact contribution is not recoverable here.
4. Whether arthakriyākāritva is finally settled as upalakṣaṇa (near-marker) or as the being-ness itself is left in progress in the slice.

## 4. What is established

The passage establishes that the six cognitions (action, relation, universal, substance, direction, time) are true (satya) by stability and use, as one-many-support, because the reflexive awareness resting on the action-essence is stable and agrees with the intended action, unlike the double-moon case. It establishes the satya/asatya/saṃvṛtisatya distinction and the mūrti/kriyā distinction (un-deviable own-form vs. appearance-strength making non-own-form into own-form). It establishes that the external action is explained by action-power alone. It does not, in this slice, settle the final status of arthakriyākāritva (own-form, marker, or near-marker); that inquiry is opened but not closed.

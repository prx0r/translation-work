## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** This is the very opening of the IPVV. The maṅgala (the prostration to Śiva) has been
made; now Abhinavagupta expounds the first kārikā — the *spontaneity* (svācchandya) of the Lord. His
thesis: the Lord is not a bare, featureless light that reveals a world outside itself; he is the
self-aware manifestation, the very being of which is to shine forth as the world and to be aware of
shining. The whole of his doctrine (the svātantryavāda) is here in seed.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The spontaneity as the self-aware manifestation (lines 200–210):**
The blue and the other things, without abandoning their blue-ness, shine forth and are one with the
manifestation (prakāśābhedamayāḥ). This is the Lord's spontaneity (svācchandya): being of the nature of
consciousness (saṃvidātmaka), he shines forth as the varied knowers-and-knowns, with a body that
appears as more than the consciousness-form even though it is not more, and he is aware of (vimṛśati)
this. The shining of the not-more as if it were more is the "clear" (svaccha) nature, like a mirror: the
mirror reflects the wall, the house, the elephant, the horse, the person, the pot, the potter — all the
distinctions of parts and part-holders, support and supported, cause and effect — not being more than
itself, yet appearing as if it were. Its not being aware of this (tathāvimarśābhāvād) is its
un-spontaneity, its inertia (jaḍatva). But the param-eśvara, shining thus, *is aware* of himself as
shining — for the consciousness culminates in the reflexive-awareness (saṃvido vimarśaparyantatvāt).

**The time-objection (lines 210–228):**
The objection: "then it would always shine so." — The "always" (śaśvat): if "sometimes" were a real,
independent time, it might be objected; but there is no such time. "At this time" — the word's meaning
is nothing other than the sun's movement (the shining sun). So the question "why does the world shine
with the sun's movement?" is like asking "why does not the blue shine as yellow?" And if it did not,
the sun's movement would not shine either. On the day of dissolution, that very day would be today, not
the present day. So there is nothing un-experiential here. The consciousness's non-excess is seen even
in the dream, the construction, and the making of a śāstra — so the fabrication beyond the experience
is the un-experiential. Hence: "śaśvat" = the impossibility of time-division; "acchanna" = the
non-excess of the own-form; "svaccha" = the possibility of the excess-appearance even in the
non-excess; "svacchanda" = the union with the freedom of the reflexive-awareness.

**The prostration (lines 228–249):**
The vṛtti-word shows the identity-manifestation of these forms in the Lord, the powerful Śiva: śaśvat
(un-cut by time), acchanna (not partaking of the other-ness of the own-form in the least), svacchā
(bearing the shining of the more), svacchandā (free of the other-rest-dependence, the self-resting
spanda). He, Śiva, the param-eśvara, is the param-essence of the supreme-and-lower good (the bliss-juice
enjoyment and liberation), who removes the saṃsāric delusion of the a-śiva form — to him the śāstra-kṛt
makes the prostration. The prostration is the samāveśa (the entry): the abandoning of the
māyā-knower's conceit, the subordination of the "I"-conceit, and the rising of the consciousness-form
own-nature. The "namaḥ" is a mere indicator (like the other-nipāta), not the offering of a word-form;
by the Gītā-rule, the "namaḥ" heard pleases the Lord. The svāhā and the rest, in the offering, have
only the indicated meaning (the increase of the flame, the gift to the deity) — not a bare word-form.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The four terms (śaśvat, acchanna, svaccha, svacchanda) and their precise glosses — preserved as
   given; the "svacchandā" as the pāryantika-pratiṣṭhāsthāna-vimarśāśūnyatā.
2. The "a-śiva-rūpa-sāṃsārika-mohāpahastana" (the removal of the saṃsāric delusion of the
   non-Śiva-form) — the soteriological frame; kept as Abhinava's.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the Lord's spontaneity is the self-aware manifestation — the
consciousness that shines as the world and is aware of shining (like a mirror, but aware). The four
epithets (un-cut by time, un-other, clear, spontaneous) are unpacked. The prostration is the entry into
this self-aware Śiva. The whole doctrine is here in seed.

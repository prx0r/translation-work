# IPVV — Vol 1, Chunk B: ARGUMENT MAP (the prostration, the disciple's eligibility, the Gītā-bhaktas)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00020 lines 600–900
(the continuation of the kathaṃcit āsādya sūtra's commentary — the prostration's meaning, the
disciple's eligibility, the Gītā-bhaktas). Works from BOTH the L0 records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The opening of the IPVV has made the prostration to Śiva. Now Abhinavagupta must
explain what that prostration actually is and means — and who is eligible to make it. The commentary
on the word "kathaṃcit āsādya" (having attained, in some way) opens up: the attaining of the Lord is
the attainment of *dāsya* (the servant-ness), and the servant-ness is the very heart of the eligibility
and the devotion. The whole is framed against the Gītā's account of the four kinds of devotees.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The right teacher and the prostration's form (lines 600–618):**
The right teacher (samyagvyutpādakaḥ śāstrakāraḥ) is he who does not abandon the view; the Buddhists and
the rest are its makers-and-haters (this is set out in the Kathāmukhatilaka). The ṭīkā-kāra, seeing an
un-precedented form of the prostration, makes the "jaya-namaskāra" and the rest, for the ones who
support it (the ādi includes the vandana, the pradhyāna). The form that rests in the two [the Lord and
the devotee] must, being conveyed through one of them, culminate in the rest of both: "the jar shines,
the jar sees" — the jar, being manifested, manifests [its own manifestation] — this is the
object-understanding. The prostration is two-resting. The "jaya/jayasi/jayeti" words, though moving by
the param-eśvara's excess, must have the self-subordinating as the support (else the excess fails); the
"namaḥ/namāmi/vande" words, though moving by the self-subordinating re-recollection, must rest in the
Lord's excess. The two are one thing, two-aspect, made manifest by the word for the whole audience.

**The dāsya (servant-ness) and the eligibility (lines 618–646):**
"Kathaṃcit" (in some way) says that the dāsya is extremely hard to get — so the more is the
self-subordination stated. "Mahāphala" (great-fruit): the Lord's excess is attained, being the servant,
in the Lord; the "great-fruit" is the cause of the attainment of all the fortunes (samastasampat) — the
word is the pratyabhijñā's distinction and the dāsya's distinction (the Vṛtti: "dāsyalakṣmī" — the
lakṣmīs born of the dāsya, all the fortunes, attained from the param-eśvara's grace; else the
lakṣmī-word would be meaningless). "Vācāpi vā" (even by speech): by the 'api', also by the body. The
prostration and the excess, resting in the mental experience (the mind-awareness) and the word-essence,
not in the inert body; so the devotee, offering the undivided mind-speech (the consciousness-power),
offers the whole world to the Lord. (The āgama: "and he should offer his own self.")

**The Gītā-bhaktas and the good-person (lines 646–900):**
The prostration by the word is the upacāra (the metaphor), the mark by which the mind-offering is
inferred; not the primary [sense]. The "satyam" (true): the removal of obstacles is indeed attained,
but the greater purpose is that of the verse-fragment bound to the prostration. The "adyāpi" (even now):
un-brokenly, by the entry into the book, the whole heart is crossed; the offering is in that, not in the
self. The "vyākhyātṛ" (the explainer) is honored; in the pratyabhijñā's establishment, the Lord's
favor is inferred, preceded by the Lord's favor. The objection: "the eligibility suits the explainer,
but how the listeners?" — the listener does not establish the recognition, nor the dāsya, apart; the
re-recollection is one. [The four Gītā-bhaktas — the distressed, the seeker of knowledge, the seeker of
wealth, the knower — are all eligible, in their gradations, for the dāsya and the recognition.]

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the prostration is the two-resting homage — self-subordinating and
Lord-exalting, made in the mind and speech, not the inert body; the dāsya (servant-ness) is the heart
of the eligibility, attained by the Lord's grace and fruitful of all fortunes; and the devotees — the
four Gītā-kinds — are all eligible, in their gradations, for the recognition.

# IPVV — Vol 1, Chunk C: ARGUMENT MAP (the svātmā: the Lord as the self of all, and the jñātṛ-kartṛ)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00020 lines 1200–1800
(the commentary on kārikā 1.1 — the svātmā, the māyā, the Upaniṣadic citations, the jñātṛ-kartṛ).
Works from BOTH the L0 records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The kārikā says "the own-self alone" (svātmā eva) is the Lord. But what does
"own-self" (svātmā) mean? Does it include everything — the inert things, the void, the whole world? The
objector presses: if the maheśvara is the self of the blue, then he is equally the self of the yellow,
and so how is "this is the blue, this is the yellow" possible — how is the world divided? Abhinavagupta
answers by explaining that "own-self" means the *consciousness* that is the self of all, and that the
division of the world is the division of what appears *within* that consciousness, not a division of the
self itself.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The "own-self" (svātmā) — what it includes (lines 1200–1236):**
The "sva" (own) in "svātmā" attracts a relation; being un-said, it is not restricted — so the whole
being-class, inert and conscious, is perceived [as related]. For the conscious, he is the one self, of
the nature of consciousness. But for the inert — the separately-cut-off forms — how can they have one
self, since separate-natured things cannot have one nature (the contradiction)? The siddhānta's answer:
here "svātmā" (the own-self) is the own (sva), the self's own — the non-other-dependent, un-conditioned
relation (svasāmānyalakṣaṇa, arthakriyākāritva, the non-other-expecting). For the inert, in their
inert-ness, even being/non-being is hard in the self — how much less the blue/yellow/pleasure/pain
difference. If what is in itself, were not, what would suffer? If it did the object-action, what then?
If it shone, what is its own glory? "It shines for me, for Caitra" — how does that fit? If the
perception is that, then what is it to the thing? "By that it shines" — that is a new, un-precedented
reason (else the white would make the yellow white). If it is the thing's own, then by that it does not
happen elsewhere (like the blue's blue-ness in the yellow) — so the manifestation-form is the knower's
own; how, then, in the blue? The identity of the being [with the consciousness] is the pervader; else
the blue-would-be-yellow and the rest would be the un-fit, opposed to the rule (dṛśyatā-rūpya-pervader-
niyama). The identity's other-ness is opposed; and that is the other-ness. But without the blue's
being-manifested there is no own-life; so, by the un-removable manifestation, the identity of the being
with the knower's manifestation must be accepted. Hence the blue and the rest are the awareness-forms
(bodhātmānaḥ), not another body — so their own-self too is the maheśvara. This is the desired.

**The void and the division (lines 1228–1249):**
"Let the lump (loṣṭa) and the rest be" — for the lump, clearly manifesting, who disputes its
awareness-ness? Even the void (abhāva) — the mere "nothing" — as long as it is so, does not shine; then
it is other than being. If it is cognized, then the maheśvara is its own-self too; if it is
un-cognized, then it is nothing, so whose self is it? The "cognized" word (budhyamāna-padam) answers
the objection "even the blue's non-being has the maheśvara as its self": the acceptance of the
being-cognized is the answer — else the objection is without support. The nanu: "if the maheśvara is the
blue's self and the yellow's self, how is 'this is the blue, this is the yellow' divided?" Answer: all
the known is the body (śarīra) of the consciousness-self — as the body, though known, is "I" by the
un-metaphorical support (aham-avaṣṭambha), so the whole world is "I" to the consciousness. The
I-ness of the body is not the upacāra (as of a tool, like the blanket), even for the separately-
self-conceited. Hence the division is not accepted by us: the consciousness is world-form (viśvarūpā),
and the blue-yellow series too. (The Upaniṣadic citation: as the space-of-the-jar and the
space-of-the-cloth are the one space, so the whole world is the one consciousness.)

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The Upaniṣadic/vedānta citation (the ghaṭākāśa/paṭākāśa — the space-of-the-jar) — cited to
   confirm the one-space/one-consciousness; kept as given.
2. The "dṛśyatā-rūpya-vyāpaka-niyama" (the rule of the seen-ness-and-silverness-pervader) — the
   technical rule; kept close to the text.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: "svātmā" is the own-self, the consciousness, which is the self of
all — including the inert and the void (in their being-cognized). The division of blue and yellow is
not a division of the self; the whole known world is the body of the one consciousness, "I" as the body
is "I". The blue and the yellow are the awareness-forms, whose own-self is the maheśvara. This is the
svātmā, and the jñātṛ-kartṛ (the knower-doer) is this one Lord.

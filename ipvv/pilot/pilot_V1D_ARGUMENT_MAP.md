# IPVV — Vol 1, Chunk D: ARGUMENT MAP (memory, the eternal knower, the pramāṇa)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00020 lines 1800–2400
(the commentary — memory, the eternal knower, the pramāṇa/karaṇa). Works from BOTH the L0 records and
the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** How can the self be the *eternal* knower, if memory reaches only what one has
experienced — and the Rāmāyaṇa was composed by Vālmīki long ago, whom "I" was not? The objection: "I
was not at the time of the Rāmāyaṇa." Abhinavagupta answers that the *param-essence knower* — the
consciousness-self — is un-cut by all three times; the "I" that was not at that time is only the
present body, not the self. Memory itself, he shows, presupposes the eternal knower.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The Rāmāyaṇa objection (lines 1800–1816):**
If memory reaches only the experienced, then "I was not at the time of the Rāmāyaṇa" — for that time
was not experienced by me. If it is said "from the āgama we know that time," then the āgama too
presupposes the experiencer's experience. But Vālmīki was the experiencer then, not "I." The question
is apt; but the *aham-vimarśa* (the "I"-recollection) of the consciousness-nature is established as
eternal, to this extent. And he is the one param-essence knower; if the other-breaths-minds-
eight-fold-city-bodies are distinct, the knower-distinction is made by their difference. So the "this
body was not at that time" is the truth here [not "I was not"]. At the dissolution and the beginning of
creation, "we were not" — else the special-time-reference would not be. In the "we were not" cognition,
the past-time-appearance, brought into the liṭ (perfect) ending, does flash. This is a cognition; and
it is not the experience-form (else, like the yogin, the present would flash the past-object).
Hence it is memory; and memory presupposes the prior experience. The utprekṣā (conjecture) too
presupposes memory or experience at the root. But the pure consciousness-mere, being free of the
time-tint and incapable of appearing as a thing beyond its own form, cannot [alone] bear the memory —
so the memory is [of] the body-and-the-rest-tinted. The prior-body-tint is distinct from this-body-
tint; so the memory-ness is not clear, like the young man who was not a child (the "not-yet-child"
state).

**The eternal knower (lines 1822–1849):**
Where the conceit of the experiencer is firm in the body, there the memory-ness is clear. Enough: it is
established that the self's manifestation is not un-precedented (apūrva). The apūrvatva is by being;
the apūrva-prakāśatva by the attainment. The self's non-being-before, nor the experience-of-the-
existent — in truth, the being-ness rests in the manifestation, so the being-priority rests in the
manifestation's un-precedented-ness — hence the sāmānādhikaraṇya (co-reference) is fit. The "cakāra" is
not used. The knower's apūrva-prakāśatva-dealing is not without the prior knower (it needs the
memory of the prior time); without it, the being's pervader (the non-dependence on that form) would
fail — like the cloth without the clay-lump. So the knower's apūrva-prakāśatva presupposes the
eternal knower. But the param-essence knower is never apūrva-prakāśa; and all the knowers are
un-divided from him — so in truth there is no apūrva-prakāśatā. (The Gītā 2.12: "for truly I was
never not… nor these lords; nor shall we not be" — the self, the param-essence knower, is un-cut by
the three times; by its un-dividedness, so are the others.) In the māyā-knower, the apūrva-prakāśatā
may be, but it rests in the eternal param-essence knower. The pramāṇa/karaṇa is thus grounded in the
eternal knower.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The Gītā 2.12 citation (the "I was never not") — the load-bearing confirmation; cited, the exact
   textual frame preserved.
2. The "atiśiśutā-padānabhūta iva yūnaḥ" (the young man who was not yet a child) — the memory-
   clarity example; kept as Abhinava's.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: memory presupposes the prior experience, and hence the eternal
knower; the "I was not at the Rāmāyaṇa" refers only to the present body, not the self; the
param-essence knower is un-cut by all three times (the Gītā 2.12), and all the knowers are un-divided
from him — so in truth there is no un-precedented manifestation. The pramāṇa is grounded in the
eternal knower.

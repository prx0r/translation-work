# IPVV — Vol 1, Chunk F: ARGUMENT MAP (the upoddhāta: the Buddhist no-self and the self's lordship, kārikās 1–7)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00020 lines 3126–3700
(the upoddhāta's dvitīyo vimarśa — the Buddhist pūrvapakṣa against the self, and the establishment of
the self's lordship, kārikās 1–7). Works from BOTH the L0 records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The upoddhāta (the preliminary) has two vimarśas. In this second, Abhinavagupta faces
the opponents who deny the self's lordship. Who are the true opponents? The Cārvāka (materialist)
cannot even frame a refutation (having no means of proof); the Sāṃkhya (the many selfs, or one
self with hidden lordship) is not wholly far. The real pūrvapakṣin is the **Buddhist (Sautrāntika/
Vijñānavādin)**, who denies the self outright. Against him Abhinavagupta must establish: the self is
real (the perception/self-awareness), and it is the Lord (the knowledge-action relation is its
lordship).

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The opponents (lines 3135–3162):**
Those who dispute the self's being — how could they attain its lordship? They are the root-opponents.
The Cārvāka, lacking a stance of their own, cannot refute the other side (without entering the means-of-
proof conventions; entering them, they lose their Cārvāka-ness). So the Sautrāntika (the Buddhist) is
the pūrvapakṣin here. Those who accept the self — the many (Sāṃkhya) or the one with hidden lordship —
are not wholly far; the self's own-mark and the not-accepted dharma can be brought in. So it is the
Buddhist no-self that is the real opponent. The 11 verses (from "nanu svalakṣaṇābhāsaṃ" to "tena kartāpi
kalpitaḥ") set out this pūrvapakṣa.

**The plan (lines 3153–3170):**
Two verses reject the perception-self-holder by the non-perception of the seen (dṛśyānupalabdhi). Three
reject the inferred-self-holder, saying the memory/synthesis and the rest are otherwise-established.
One says the qualities (the cognitions) being qualities, the quality-holder is established. Thus, the
self (dharmin) being rejected, the reason for the knowledge-action-relation (the lordship-dealing) is
to be rejected: two verses show the cognition's own form as un-established in the other's view; three
show the action-relation is nowhere established (no supporter, refuted). The siddhāntin's view (the
self, the reason, the to-be-established as the dealing-object) is briefly echoed by the pūrvapakṣin
for refutation. The "ātmaiva tu" — the pūrvapakṣin's own view, in a half-verse, shows the self alone [is
the Lord]. The 'tu' shows the siddhāntin's view is to be refuted. The siddhāntin, saying the
non-perception is un-established, is answered by the "ahaṃpratyayenāpi" (even by the I-cognition): the
self is established even by the "I"-cognition, the ādi including the sense/breath/knowledge-stream. The
'nanu' indicates the objection.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the Buddhist no-self is the real opponent (the Cārvāka cannot
argue, the Sāṃkhya is near); the 11 verses reject the perception-self and the inferred-self, and then
establish the self's lordship; the self is established even by the "I"-cognition (the ahaṃpratyaya),
and the knowledge-action relation is its lordship. This is the upoddhāta's defense of the self-as-Lord.

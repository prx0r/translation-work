## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The Buddhist has denied the self (the dharmin) outright. But even granting the
self, the opponent offered a reason for its lordship: "by the union of knowledge and action"
(jñānakriyāyogāt). To refute this, Abhinavagupta must show that *knowledge itself* (jñāna), as the
opponent conceives it, is impossible — whether it is taken as of the consciousness-form (then it is
eternal, like the self, and cannot be a momentary quality) or as inert (then it cannot reveal any
object). This is the kārikā 7 dilemma, which leads into the Sāṃkhya buddhi.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The framing (lines 3705–3718):**
The self (dharmin) being rejected, no lordship could be established by the reasons. To refute the
"jñānakriyāyogāt" reason, the cognition itself is shown to be un-established in the other's view —
whether inert or conscious — by the prasaṅga (the undesired consequence). For by the same reason by
which the self is eternal, the cognition too would be eternal; but then "its being so, how is it a
quality?" — so the dharmin/hetu/svarūpa mutually contradict (the support-supported relation fails). The
twofold dilemma (inert/conscious) is shown generally.

**Kārikā 7 (lines 3719–3744) — the dilemma:**
> If the cognition is of the consciousness-form, then why is it not eternal, like the self? Or, if it
> is inert, then how is there the object-manifestation of it?

The 'ca' collects the further un-established-ness. If the cognition has the consciousness-form
naturally, like the self, then it is not momentary (not eternal like the self alone). The
consciousness-nature, whose marker is self-manifestation, is opposed to the object-form, the
being-manifested-by-another — for freedom and dependence are opposed; and by the being-an-object, the
place-time-union is pervaded — so, by the perception of the self-manifestation (the
vyāpaka-viruddha), the place-time-union (the vyāpaka-opposed) is negated. And the place-time-union is
the momentariness. What is fixed, being in no way not, is eternal; what is somewhere and sometimes,
and not elsewhere and otherwise, is not fixed. Even in the union of all places and times, in truth
there is no union — if that place or time does not enter the own-form, how "there"/"then"? If it is
covered (āvṛta), then that which is covered all around — how is it held by another? The covered is
supported; how could it bear another's support? So the "one part-holder in many parts" and all the rest
are un-established. So if the cognition is the consciousness-form, it is eternal-omnipresent-form, and
then the other-self — the cognition-cause — is of un-established own-form. If it is inert, then how is
its manifestation-form toward the object? — the sūtra-meaning. The objection: "'cit' is called the
cognition; and for the Vaiśeṣikas the self's own form is not that — the cognition is its quality; so
how 'like the self's consciousness-form, the cognition would be eternal'?" — answered: "from the
non-being of the being-manifested-by-another." Here the cognition is the blue-and-the-rest
manifestation; it is the self's occasional dharma, made by the mind-union and the rest; when its cause
is absent, it is not, and then the self is not blue-manifestation-flashed.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the cognition cannot be a separate momentary quality, for if it
is consciousness-form it is eternal (self-manifestation is opposed to being-an-object); if inert, it
cannot manifest. The Sāṃkhya buddhi (the intellect as the locus of the cognitions) is next.

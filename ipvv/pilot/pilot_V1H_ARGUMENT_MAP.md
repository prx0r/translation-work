# IPVV — Vol 1, Chunk H: ARGUMENT MAP (the reflection argument: enjoyment as the union of reflections, and the samavāya)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00020 lines 4300–5077
(the continuation — the pratibimba (reflection) argument, the mirror and the echo, and the Vaiśeṣika
samavāya). Works from BOTH the L0 records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** What is *enjoyment* (bhoga) — the experiencing of objects? Abhinavagupta argues that
it is the *union of reflections* in the consciousness: the reflection of consciousness (cit-pratibimba)
and the reflection of the object (viṣaya-pratibimba) meet in the buddhi (the intellect), joined by the
ahaṅkāra (the ego) into the single "I know this." Against the Vaiśeṣika, who makes the cognition a
quality of the self joined by inherence (samavāya), Abhinavagupta shows that the union (saṃyoga) is
enough, and that the whole dealing is *relation-made* (saṃsargakṛta).

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Enjoyment as the reflection-union (lines 4301–4323):**
The objection: "let the transformation-action not be real in the self; but why is not the enjoyment
(bhoktṛtā) param-real?" Answer: the self is consciousness-mere; it is not the enjoyer of objects. The
aham-kāra, the ego, makes the self — which is not in itself an enjoyer — dealt with as an enjoyer. For
in the metaphor (upacāra), there is no real change in the thing-metaphored; the dealing belongs to the
metaphorer, who gains the special word. Else, by the infinite division of the knowers, the one body
dealt with simultaneously as fire and water (the contradictory) would take on infinite contradictory
forms in a single moment. The ego, by the reflection-union of consciousness and the object-reflection
in the buddhi-tattva, produces the conceit "I know this" — the union of the two reflections, the
conscious and the inert. That conceit, made an object by the buddhi's operation, joined with the
consciousness-reflection, is the enjoyment. In the Vaiśeṣika too, the cognition-quality is other than
the self; how is its enjoyment? "By inherence" — then the mere relation is the cause of the
enjoyment-form. Here too the relation is shown: the consciousness-reflection, not-different-by-
determination, joined with the object-reflection, mixed by the ego, determined by that buddhi — the
union of the one-buddhi-support — this is the enjoyment-dealing, everywhere relation-made.

**The union (saṃyoga) vs. the inherence (samavāya) (lines 4327–4344):**
The relation is the attainment-marker (prāpti-lakṣaṇa) saṃyoga, or the samavāya, by the other
doctrine. Or, if the saṃyoga's priority is not determined in the prior dissolutions and the great
dissolutions, the saṃyoga, being beginningless, is not distinct from the samavāya. And all this is the
buddhi-tattva's dharma; the dharmin-dharma relation may be called saṃyoga, or tādātmya, or samavāya —
once the thing is obtained, what does it matter which word? Where the consciousness-reflection is in
the buddhi (the cid-aviśiṣṭatā), there the blue-reflection is too — by the support-supported
non-separate-establishment, even the samavāya-ness may be said; no contradiction. In all views, the
enjoyment-dealing is relation-made (saṃsargakṛta). (The verse: "all the cognition in the other-upādhi
is vyāvahārika, ayathārtha…"). The 'vā' (or) indicates this. The ego-tattva's operation — "I do, I
know" — is the dealing; the self, fit for the enjoyment-dealing (the knowledge-nomination), whose own
form is the buddhi-knowledge transferred to the buddhi, the object-reflection and the
consciousness-reflection…

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The "paropādhau sarvā dhīr vyāvahārikī / ayathārthā" verse — cited; the exact source/force kept.
2. The saṃyoga/samavāya equivalence (the beginningless saṃyoga) — Abhinava's own move; preserved.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: enjoyment is the union of the consciousness-reflection and the
object-reflection in the buddhi, mixed by the ego into "I know this"; the self is consciousness-mere,
not really an enjoyer; and the relation (saṃyoga or samavāya) is enough — the whole enjoyment-dealing is
relation-made. The Vaiśeṣika's samavāya adds nothing; the union is beginningless and non-distinct.

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The Buddhist has been refuted on the self. But the opponent's reason for the self's
lordship was "jñānakriyāyogāt" — by the union of knowledge and action. Having shown that the cognition
(jñāna) is impossible in the opponent's view (V1-G), Abhinavagupta now shows that *action* (kriyā) is
impossible in the opponent's view too. Kārikā 9: the action of a body is the coming-to-be of this and
that place-and-the-rest; it is not other, from the unseen (adṛṣṭa); nor is it one — for the ordered of
the one is not fit. The action, like the cognition, cannot be a separate momentary thing.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Kārikā 9 (lines 5077–5093):**
> The action of the body-and-the-rest, of the meaning, is the birth-of-this-and-that-place-and-the-
> rest; not other, from the unseen; nor is it one — for the ordered of the one is not fit.

The "adṛṣṭeḥ" (from the unseen) means, by the pramāṇa, the non-perception (anupalabdhi). The
perception, to the determination (avasāya-paryanta) — the pramāṇa-operation — the "dṛṣṭi" (the
seeing) culminates in the determination; the "avasāya" word, by its own-form and by the
being-connected-with-another, is to be connected in both the pramāṇa's non-determination and the
reason's being-refuted. By the reason, the pramāṇa-operation's being-refuted is said in the own-form
("nor is it one — for the ordered of the one"); by the being-connected-with-another, "how would the
ordered be of the one?" The jñāna's un-fitness, obtained by the prasaṅga (V1-G), is here collected
with the 'api' (also) for the kriyā: "not only [the cognition], but also the action is not fit."

**The nature of action (lines 5094–5121):**
The action is not, in the world, the movement-form (calanarūpa), since the word-knowers do not take a
movement of the body-less self; they establish the self's agent-ness only in the union of knowledge,
desire, and effort (jñāna-cikīrṣā-yatna-yoga), and the knowledge-and-the-rest are qualities, and their
inherence is not an action. So an action fit for the self must be stated. Its marker (lakṣaṇa): the
operation (vyāpāra) of the kāraka, the cause of the fruit-attainment, the means (nimitta), which is
related to the doer, or inhering in it, or its own-self — whose own-form (svaṃ rūpam) is such that its
parts are prior-and-later, of the order-form. (The five ślokas of the guru: "as long as the
established-or-un-established is spoken as the to-be-established, being of the resting-order-form, it
is perceived as that action"; "by the quality-parts, the mass of the order-born, the unity-constructed
by the mind, is called action"; "white is whitened, this, being manifested by whiteness, the
resting-order-form, the declaration proceeds"; "by the cause-effect-relation it sounds, the
resting-order; the sound, in the order's cessation, is called just 'sound'.") Such an action is in the
self — "he desires, he knows, he strives" — the perception of the this-and-this-prior-later-dharmas;
and it is accepted by the īśvara-ātmavādins. (The guru: "such is the will, such the knowledge, such the
action.")

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The five śloka quotations (the guru's kriyā-definition) — cited at length; their exact source kept.
2. The "adṛṣṭeḥ / anupalabdheḥ" (the unseen/non-perception) — the Buddhist's hidden cause; preserved.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the action, like the cognition, cannot be a separate momentary
thing in the opponent's view. Its real nature is the ordered operation — the prior-later forms of the
agent's willing, knowing, striving — which is one, in the self, though its parts are ordered. This is
the action whose union with knowledge is the self's lordship.

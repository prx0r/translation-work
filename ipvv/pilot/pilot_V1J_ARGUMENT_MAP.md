# IPVV — Vol 1, Chunk J: ARGUMENT MAP (kārikā 11 — "thence the agent too is constructed")

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00020 lines 5585–5750
(kārikā 11 — the close of the pūrvapakṣa: the relation refuted, so the agent too is constructed).
Works from BOTH the L0 records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The Buddhist's pūrvapakṣa has denied the self (V1-F), the cognition (V1-G), and the
action (V1-I). Now it completes itself: since the *relation* (between the cognition and its object, or
the agent and the action) is also impossible, "thence the agent too is constructed (kalpita)." This is
the close of the no-self attack — the agent, like everything else, is only a construction. (It will be
Abhinavagupta's task, in the siddhānta, to show the opposite — that the agent is the real, free self.)

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The fourfold absence (lines 5587–5602):**
The kārikā's "thence" (tena) gathers a fourfold absence (abhāva-catuṣṭaya) as the reason: (i) the
absence of the action (established in the prior sūtra: no supporter, refuted); (ii) the absence of the
other [the object]; (iii) the absence of the relation; and (iv) the absence of the determination of the
being [of these]. The relation's absence: the relation is not — for the two-resting thing cannot be one
(dviṣṭhasyānekatvāt), the established does not expect another (siddhasyānyānapekṣaṇāt), and the
dependence (pāratantrya) is unfitting. This fourfold absence, combined, is the reason for "the agent
too is constructed." In the ṭīkā, the 'from the absence' (abhāvāt, the fifth case) summarizes it; the
'ca' connects with the prior sūtra.

**The perception/refutation (lines 5602–5624):**
The objection: "the relation is refuted by the non-perception of the seen." But the refutation, in
truth, is not a smashing (like the hammer breaking the pot) but the announcement of the determined
non-being; and it is shown by the non-perception by the pramāṇa. True — but the non-perception gives
the non-being only with the restriction of place and time: "here, now, there is no jar." (The verse:
"the non-being-certainty is not of the far-ones.") Hence, to announce the universal (sārvātrika)
non-being of the relation, one must state a contradiction grounded in the prameya, producing the
impossibility of the opponent's own form: "what you yourself said of its form is contradicted by
another form you said" — so, by the establishment of the two-form contradiction, the refuter is
successful; and there is no dharmin-etc. objection. (This is set out in the prasaṅga-text.) Then the
common-marker refuter is stated: "what stands simultaneously with the own-form-completion of two — that
is not one," like the two jars in the two houses [not one]. The relation, if it stood in both, would
not be one; so the agent/relation is constructed.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The "dviṣṭhasyānekarūpatvāt" (the two-resting's many-form) — the relation's crux; preserved.
2. The "sārvātrikī asattā" (the universal non-being) refutation via the two-form contradiction —
   Abhinava's own move; kept close.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the Buddhist's pūrvapakṣa closes by refuting the relation — the
two-resting cannot be one — so the agent too is only constructed. The refutation is the announcement of
the determined non-being (with the place-time restriction, not a bare non-perception). This completes
the no-self attack, which the siddhānta will reverse: the agent is the real, free self.

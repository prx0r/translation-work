## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The Buddhist's pūrvapakṣa (V1-F..J) has denied the self, the cognition, the action,
and the relation. Now, in the third vimarśa, the siddhānta replies. Abhinavagupta grants the Buddhist
much — that the cognition is not a separate thing in the Sāṃkhya/Kāṇāda views — but he turns it to his
own end: the cognition is *self-cognition* (svasaṃvid), and this self-cognition is precisely the self
that the Buddhist denied. The "I"-seeing (dṛk) is self-luminous, and it is the Lord.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Maṅgala + the plan (lines 5758–5794):**
"Bowing to the will-power-full, nectar-shining, capable of generating endless worlds; the Bhairava-joy,
thickened by the waves of the own-power…" The siddhānta is established in two adhikāras. In seven
verses (from "satyaṃ kintu" to "smṛtyapohanaśaktimān"), Abhinava examines and refutes the other view
(paradarśanānupapatti). The jñāna-vicāra is this adhikāra (the action-relation in the second). The plan:
two verses show the cognition is self-cognition (in memory, the experience does not shine, even in the
impression-raised); two reject the memory's error-ness (with a doubt), and by the third the
construction's error-ness is rejected by the prasaṅga (all the determinations); one shows that even
with the impression, in the other view, memory is unfitting (the dealing-lapse); one establishes it in
our view. The "na" is used where the pūrvapakṣa is wholly to be stopped; the "satyaṃ kintu" where
partly to be accepted and partly rejected. This is the style of the prekṣāvat (the reflective) ones.
Here "satyaṃ" (true) grants much of the pūrvapakṣa: the "in the Sāṃkhya/Kāṇāda view the cognition is
not established" — that is agreed. But the "kintu" (but) shows the further distinction: what the Śākya
meant is to be sharpened.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The "satyaṃ kintu" concessive style (the prekṣāvat) — Abhinava's rhetorical method; preserved.
2. The "smṛtyapohanaśaktimān" (the one possessed of the memory-removal-power) — the siddhānta's
   description of the self; kept close.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the siddhānta grants the Buddhist that the cognition is not a
separate thing, but turns it around — the cognition is self-cognition, the self-luminous "I"-seeing,
which is the very self the Buddhist denied. This is the self-luminous dṛk, the Lord. The jñāna-vicāra
begins the reply.

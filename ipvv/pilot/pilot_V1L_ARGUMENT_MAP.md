## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The siddhānta holds that the cognition is self-cognition (svasaṃvid), and the self
is self-luminous. But two objections arise. First: if the self is self-luminous and not manifest as an
object, how does the *yogin* perceive *another's* cognition clearly (the yogipratyakṣa)? Second: if the
cognition is self-luminous only in its own stream, could it not be manifested by another cognition in
another stream? Abhinavagupta answers both — the yogin's knowledge of another is the yogipratyakṣa (a
direct seeing, by the union with the other), not an inference; and the cognition is self-luminous
throughout, never the object of another. This is the Śaṅkaranandana territory (the memory-error theory).

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The yogin's perception of another (lines 6303–6333):**
The objection: "if the self is not directly manifest as another's, how does the knowledge of another's
cognition shine clearly — since the other-self is not perceived, and the inference does not give the
clear-form?" Answer: this is the yogipratyakṣa (the yogin's perception), not an inference. By the
rule (yo. sū. 3.20: "of another's cognition, the knowledge, from the saṃyama"), by the power of
dhāraṇā-dhyāna-samādhi, when the yogin meditates the non-difference with the other, that meditation,
following the thing, and in truth the unity, the difference-conceit being māyā-made, the other's
cognition shines clearly — not inferred. The objection "let it be by inference then" — no: the
yogipratyakṣa is known. The "paraprakāśyatva" (the being-manifested-by-another) of the self is to be
removed. In the yogipratyakṣa, the rāga-and-the-rest (the passions) are manifested; but with their
experience, the yogin's unity [is attained], so the experience shines as self-luminous, not as an
object. The 'eva' is used after the compound, not "the knower-manifestation" here (the
abheda-bhāvanā cannot establish what is always shining). So the other's cognition is a
sākṣātkāra (a direct seeing), not a mere inference-determination. Hence the other knower too has no
being-manifested-by-another. The Buddhist's other-mind-establishment is refuted at length in the
vāsanāprabodha-sūtra.

**The dṛk as self-luminous (lines 6338–6344):**
The "dṛk svābhāsaiva" (the seeing is self-manifest alone) is the present prameya, established by
another route. The objection: "the cognition is self-luminous in its own stream; but in another stream,
with respect to another knower, it could be manifested by another cognition, by the difference of
object, since the contradictory-dharma-overlay does nothing (the same woman gives pleasure to the lover
and pain to the rival, with no contradiction)." Answer: "by the being-rested-in-the-one-knower, and"
(ekapramātṛniṣṭhatvena): "he experienced, I have experienced" — the unity of the knower. The
self-cognition rests in the one knower; it is not manifested by another. [This is the memory-error
(Śaṅkaranandana) refutation: the cognition's self-luminousness and the knower's unity.]

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the yogin's knowledge of another is the yogipratyakṣa (a direct
seeing by the union, not an inference); the cognition is self-luminous throughout, never the object of
another (it rests in the one knower, as "he experienced, I have experienced"); and the Buddhist's
other-mind is refuted. The dṛk is self-manifest alone — the self-luminous seeing that is the self.

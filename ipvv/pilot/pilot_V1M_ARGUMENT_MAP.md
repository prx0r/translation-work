# IPVV — Vol 1, Chunk M: ARGUMENT MAP (the jñānādhikāra core: the determination and the self-luminous, kārikās 6–7)

(the culmination of the jñānādhikāra — the determination (adhyavasāya) cannot establish the external,

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The siddhānta holds that the cognition is self-luminous and the self is one. The
Buddhist's last resort is the *determination* (adhyavasāya) — the act by which the cognition is taken
to establish an external thing. Abhinavagupta now shows that the determination cannot do this: it is
itself only the manifestation-form in the self, and the "external" it seems to posit is not
established by it. And the memory-cognition is not separate from the cognition — both are the
self-luminous awareness.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The external and the determination (lines 6900–6932):**
The external-object-made (bāhyārthakṛta) is only by the strength of the firm-practice-impression
(dṛḍhābhyāsavāsanāmahimnā); from the very first, the appearance, shining in the consciousness and in
the determination, is drawn-to-the-external only for the slow-minded (in the illusion, the timira, and
the rest) — so the error-ness is said from the very first. But he who knows the timira-and-the-rest as
the cause of the kakṣā-and-the-rest appearance does not determine the external-ness. So the child who,
astonished, cries "the trees on the far bank rush against the current!" is told: "it is not so — the
trees do not rush; it is only seen so." This says: the manifestation-dharma alone is this. So, by the
non-construction (nirvikalpaka) accepting the external-ness, the error-ness is tolerable in the self;
but in the shell-silver error, since the first non-construction does not accept the silver, it is not
tolerable — the other-intent. The determination's "third part" (the adhyavasāyāṃśa), in the
world-famous error (the "even of others, in the hair-and-the-rest, the erroneous outward-facing
cognition" of Nareśvaraviveka), shows the error-form in every determination. So the external, which was
to be established by the error-form determination, has no standing in that part — for its inertness.
And in the part where there is the non-inert, the non-external, there the establishment is not to be
made (the memory-cognition is so). Hence the determination's object-establishing-ness is not fit, like
the present memory-cognition. The memory is the upalakṣaṇa. The part that is not erroneous — the
non-construction self-experience — does not establish the object (it shines only its own form and
self-appearance); so it is un-divided from the memory-cognition, not external-natured (jñānād
avyatiriktam).

**The determination's failure (lines 6934–6944):**
The determination, of the construction-operation form, though it seems to have descended from its own
part and is begun to be taken as the object-establisher, does not carry through — the "yāvatā" (insofar)
indicates the non-carrying of the begun-conception. The objection: "let it be both erroneous and
establishing — what is the harm?" — answered: "even of the erroneous" (bhrāntasyāpi), by the reason
"from the inertness" (jaḍatvāt). The objection: "the object-establisher — what does it establish? As
the fire burns the wood though inert, so the determination establishes the object — why the
inert/conscious question?" — answered: "the establishing is, indeed" (vyavasthāpanaṃ hi): in the self,
the experience, the manifestation-and-the-rest foremost…

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The Nareśvaraviveka citation ("even of others, in the hair-and-the-rest, the erroneous
   outward-facing cognition") — cited; kept as given.
2. The "jñānād avyatiriktam" (the un-divided from the cognition) rule — the load-bearing point;
   preserved.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the determination cannot establish an external thing — it is only
the manifestation-form in the self, and the "external" is drawn-to only by the impression, not
established; the error-form is in every determination; the non-erroneous part (the non-construction
self-experience) is un-divided from the memory-cognition, not external. The determination's failure
rests on the inertness of the external. The cognition is self-luminous, one, and never establishes a
thing outside itself.

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** This closes Vol 1. The cognitions (jñāna) have been shown to be self-luminous and one
with the "I" (ahaṃ). The final points: (1) how the cognitions, though appearing in their object-parts,
are un-divided from the "I"-part; (2) the māyā is the Lord's power, which does not cover his own form;
(3) the anusandhāna (the synthesis) is the consciousness alone — the maheśvara's śaktis (knowledge,
memory, apoha) are his; and the colophon closes the volume.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The cognitions un-divided from the "I" (lines 7600–7628):**
The cognitions are of the manifestation-mere own-form. How, then, do they not take the form of the "I"
when they shine beyond the "I"-part? Their excess over the "I"-part — the non-pervasion, the
non-eternity — shining as the object's own-form-place-time-following, contracted — that is made by the
object-facing upādhi. But if the object were manifestation-natured, even that excess-conception would
be uprooted. The "tathāpi" (still): by the "I"-part-un-divided way. The difference of the object, made
by the māyā-power, is only a conceit (abhimāna-mātra); the difference, constructed by the freedom
(svāchandya), is of the cognitions — and even in the māyā-state, the param-essence manifestation-form
"I"-un-divided-ness is un-broken. For the māyā is the Lord's power; it does not cover his own-form (the
power does not cover the power-holder). Hence, by the establishment of the present prameya, the other
to-be-established (the vyavahāra) also is established — the self-contained consciousness-non-duality
(svatantra-cidadvaita). The "antaḥkṛtānantaviśvarūpaścidvapurjñānādiśaktimān" (the one whose body is
consciousness, with the inner-made-infinite-world-form, possessed of the knowledge-and-the-rest
powers) — the sūtra-part. The inner-made, the outer-form, though external, by the knower-form-own-self-
one-form-ness, un-lost in the least, made by the freedom-power, is accomplished; and the inner, resting
in the one-knower-own-ness, made by the manifestation of the outer-form-ness, the infinite
(counted as the four/six/sixteen/twenty-four/thirty-six and more) world-form.

**The māyā and the anusandhāna (lines 7632–7644):**
The "na cet" (if not) — the vṛtti-kṛt had explained it before, "how?" — the two sūtra-parts ("naśyet"
and "na cet") are one sentence, by the hetumallīṅga. The objection: "the arising of the memory was said
to be the world's standing (janasthiti); here it is to be said, 'if the synthesis were not' — so how is
'consciousness-form' said?" Answer: "the synthesis of all the padārtha-knowledges, mutually, is the
consciousness-tattva." By the co-reference (sāmānādhikaraṇya), by the following-ness (anugāmitvena), the
synthesis — the one-becoming of even the seemingly-separate cognitions — is the consciousness alone,
inward; not anything else. The "dadāti" (gives) is in the karma-kartṛ. The synthesis by itself, like
the cloth-thread-fragments. If the synthesis is the mixing, then the mixer is not other than the
consciousness-tattva; the kriyā-kartṛ are applied with the non-difference (their unity in the param-
essence). Hence: "from the synthesis is the world's standing" — true; but that synthesis, of the
one-object-ness-marker, is not possible by another way — so, by the remainder, it is dependent on the
consciousness-form alone. The maheśvara's śaktis (knowledge, memory, apoha) are his; the apoha; and the
colophon closes Vol 1.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the cognitions are un-divided from the "I"; the māyā is the Lord's
power, which does not cover his own form; the synthesis (anusandhāna) is the consciousness alone —
the one-becoming of the cognitions — dependent on the consciousness-form; the maheśvara's powers are
his; and this closes Vol 1. The self-contained consciousness-non-duality is established.

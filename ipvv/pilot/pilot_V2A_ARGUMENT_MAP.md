# IPVV — V2-A ARGUMENT MAP (the jñānādhikāra caturtho vimarśa: memory as the Lord's power, the aham)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00021 lines 29–700
(the jñānādhikāra, caturtho vimarśa — on memory (smṛti) as the Lord's power, and the aham). Works from
BOTH the L0 records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** Having established knowledge (jñāna), the jñānādhikāra now takes up memory (smṛti)
and the power of removal (apohanaśakti). The prior view (pūrvapakṣa) held that memory is merely the
impression's revival after the experience is gone ("in the destruction of the experience, memory…").
Abhinavagupta's siddhānta: memory is itself a *knowledge* ("true, but the memory-knowledge…"), and the
whole concern — the aham, the self — is established by memory. His thesis: **memory is the Lord's own
power**, the un-broken manifestation that ties the past and the present into the one "I."

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Maṅgala + the 8-verse plan (lines 31–73):**
"Bowing to the Lord, the removers of all woe, the nectar-fluid, the un-thwarted-freedom-unfolding-rest,
the nectar-handed-crowned…" The order is jñāna, then smṛti, then the apohanaśakti (the removal-power)
within the two. The prior view ("in the destruction of the experience, memory") vs. the siddhānta
("true, but the memory-knowledge"). The plan (8 verses): one on memory's establishment; one on memory's
power to manifest the experienced object; one on the entry into unity with the experience and its
object; one that in memory the experience does not appear separately as object (even the yogin's
knowledge of another does not appear as a separate object); one that memory's separation of the
cognition is only constructed (kālpanika), not real; one on the construction's identity with
experience; one (the last) that the remembered, the memory, and the rememberer — and, by the way, the
seen, the seeing, and the seer — rest in the one consciousness-essence.

**Kārikā 1 — the remembered object's apprehension (lines 48–164):**
"Since he is the apprehender of the previously-experienced object…" The memory's object appears in the
knower (arthau bhātaḥ pramātari). The refutation of the prior view (the memory cannot be the mere
impression's revival): the memory is the un-broken manifestation of the experienced object; the
experience and its object enter into unity; the experience does not appear separately in memory.

**The aham — inward and outward (lines 165–204):**
The "I" (aham) shines both inward and outward: as "the blue-seer" it is inward; as "I, thus" it is also
outward. The experience is the outward-natured blue-knowledge. By the freedom of the awareness
(bodhasvātantryāt), "I would see the blue" — an inner construing precedes the external blue-
manifestation. The place and time of the object, tinting the object, enter the knower and the
experience; "I saw the blue then" — the three (I, blue, then) appear entered into that. The object-ness
of the experience is the manifestation-ness; it is not a real separate thing (the "arthākāra," the
object-form, as against the māyā's prostitute-like seduction). The time-dealing (kālavyavahāra) — "the
jar stands, will stand, stood" — is the un-brokenness of the manifestation; the experience is the gate;
as long as it is not touched by time, there is no time-dealing.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The "veśyā iva māyāprabhāveṇa" (the prostitute-like māyā-power) image (line 181) — the māyā
   seducing the self into the object-form; kept as Abhinava's image.
2. The grammatical point (the tṛc/samāsa in "arthaviṣaya") — the kriyā-vṛddhi; kept close to the text.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: memory is not the mere revival of an impression — it is the
Lord's power, the un-broken manifestation that brings the experienced object back into the knower. The
"aham" shines both inward and outward; by the awareness's freedom one construes "I would see the blue"
and then the blue appears; the place-time tint the object and enter the knower; and time itself is the
un-brokenness of the manifestation. The one who remembers is the one who saw — the one self, tied by
memory into the un-broken "I."

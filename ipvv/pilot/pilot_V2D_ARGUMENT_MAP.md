# IPVV — V2-D ARGUMENT MAP (the pañcamo vimarśa: the knowledge-power, the manifestation as the object's form, kārikās 1–6)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00021 lines 2082–2800
(the jñānādhikāra, pañcamo vimarśa — the jñānaśakti (knowledge-power), kārikās 1–6). Works from BOTH
the L0 records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** Having examined memory (smṛti) and the construction (vikalpa), Abhinavagupta now
examines the knowledge-power (jñānaśakti) that both presuppose. The central claim: **the manifestation
(prakāśa) is the very form of the objects** — the objects are not something outside the light that the
light reveals; their being is their being-manifested. Against the Vijñānavādin (Buddhist idealist), who
denies the external object, Abhinavagupta defends a qualified realism — but grounds the object in the
manifestation. The plan (21 verses): one states the thesis; two that manifestation is the object's
form; two answer the Vijñānavādin; one that the external object is not needed; one rejects perception
of an external thing; two on the inference; and so on to the close (the reflexive-awareness as the life
of cognition).

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Maṅgala + the plan (lines 2084–2110):**
"Praising the Lord's abode — full, from the un-surpassed awakening, the unfolding-bliss-will, the
rising-nectar-beautiful…" The jñānaśakti is examined in 21 verses: the thesis (k1); manifestation as
the object's form (k2–3); the Vijñānavādin answered (k4–5); the external object not needed (k6); the
rejection of perception of the external (k7); the inference (k8–9); the objects' necessary inherence
in the consciousness-self by the re-recollection (k10); the reflexive-awareness as the essence of the
manifestation (k11–14); the knowledge-re-recollection as the known (k15–17); the jñāna/jñātṛ division
even in the one manifestation (k18); the reflexive-awareness as the life even in the un-constructed
(k19–20); the establishment of the jñātṛ-jñāna distinction (k21).

**Kārikā 1 (lines 2112–2121) — the present appearances:**
> Of the present-manifesting beings, the manifestation, of the inner-standing-alone, is fitting as
> the outer-self (bahirātmanā).

The present beings, as they manifest, have their manifestation "as the outer-self" — the inner
standing-alone, manifesting as outer. The memory and the construction do not have a common
(sarvapramātṛsādhāraṇa) object by difference; even in the perception, the object does not exceed the
experience; and in the memory that depends on it, how much less. The 'api' and the 'yāvat' are read as
the sūtra's remainder. The sūtra-meaning: by the present-ness, the manifestation of the beings is their
being-inner-standing-as-outer.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The "bahirātmanā" (as the outer-self) — the inner standing manifesting as outer; the precise
   reading is preserved.
2. The Dharmakīrti/Dharmottara and the bāhya-anumāna (the inference of the external object from the
   reflection) — dense; the map keeps the doctrinal sense (the cognition's inner blue-form expects an
   identical arpaka, like a mirror-reflection).

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the jñānaśakti is examined; the manifestation is the form of the
objects (the inner standing manifesting as outer); the Vijñānavādin's denial of the external is
answered (the object is not a thing apart from the light, yet it is not a mere fiction — the cognition's
form expects an identical ground, like a reflection); and the reflexive-awareness is the life of the
cognition. The knowledge-power is the manifestation that is the very being of the objects.

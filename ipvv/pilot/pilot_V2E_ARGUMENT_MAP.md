(the pañcamo vimarśa, kārikās 5–6 — the inference (anumāna) of the external object, the sārūpya, the

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** Having shown that the cognition is self-luminous (V2-D), Abhinavagupta now examines
the *inference* of the external object. The Vijñānavādin says: the cognition's inner blue-form is a
reflection (pratibimba), and a reflection requires an external ground (the arpaka) — so the external
object is inferred. But how, since the external object is never perceived (so no pervasion can be
grasped)? Abhinavagupta answers by showing how the inference works (like the sense-organ, inferred
from the perception it causes), and how the sasaṃvāda (the agreement) makes the determination valid —
so the external object, perceived and confirmed, is real.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The sense-organ analogy (lines 2800–2816):**
The objection: "then, like the sense-organ, it would be merely a cause." The "kevalam" (merely)
answers: the determination (adhyavasāya) "I see" is of the cognition-form-object; it is not established
by the mere-cause-ness, for the object is the "this." To show the distinction from the mere-cause
inference, "nahi": the senses are the cause of the sense-born cognitions, but the object is not — for
the "I see the sense by the cognition" determination does not arise. The sense is the object of the
inference alone. Here, the special: "the un-grasped, how determined as seen?" — by the sārūpya: whatever
place-time-accompanied own-form-variety of the inner blue, all of it, un-lost, of its arpaka (its
ground) — by the sāmānābhihāramelana-rule, the determination arises, of the cognition-form-object, from
the sārūpya-made error: "this object-form, un-divided from the cognition, fit for the 'this,' I
determine."

**The sasaṃvāda (lines 2819–2844):**
And this determination, though error-born, is not (like the shell-silver determination in the grasped
shell) a bare error; by the thing's bond (arthapratibandhāt), determining the cause-able-to-endow-the-
cognition-form, even the error is, by the rule "the relation is the pramā" (bhrāntirapi saṃbandhaḥ
pramā), a pramāṇa-operation in the self. (The verse: "in the non-possibility of the object, from the
non-being, even in the perception the pramāṇa-ness; of the bond-natured, in its cause-ness, the two
are equal.") This pramāṇa-operation, by the seeing-strength, taking the "I see" determination-form, uses
the perception as the object's helper (amātya iva paravaśīkṛtaṃ rājānam) — so the external object is
perceived (pratyakṣagṛhīta). Then the inference's operation: "with the agreement (sasaṃvāda), the
root-cause, the arpaka of the forms, is the object similar to that form" — this much is the inference's
operation. If the inference refuted the external (like the sattvahetu refutes the stability), then the
external-determination would be rootless, a non-pramāṇa-fruit (like the mirage-water); if it did not
determine (like the sattvahetu's momentariness), then the practical dealing would fail. The "natu
akṣaṇikatvavat" (not like the non-momentariness) — the distinction. The "indriyopayogena" — the sense
is used in this cognition, with the determination-accepted-external-object-ness.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The "sāmānābhihāramelana" rule — the technical grammar/logic rule; kept close.
2. The "bhrāntirapi saṃbandhaḥ pramā" — the striking claim that even the error, being a bond, is a
   pramāṇa; the crux, preserved.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the external object is inferred (like the sense-organ, from the
cognition it causes, by the sārūpya); the determination, though error-born, is by the thing's bond a
pramāṇa-operation; the perception is the object's helper; and the inference's operation is the
sasaṃvāda (the agreement with the cause). The external object, perceived and confirmed, is real.

# IPVV — V2-F ARGUMENT MAP (the pañcamo vimarśa: the other-minds inference, and the refutation of the external, kārikā 6)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00021 lines 3600–4400
(the close of the pañcamo vimarśa's bāhyārthavāda — the other-minds (parasantāna) inference, the
bāla-rudana, the īśvarādvaya, and the refutation of the external object). Works from BOTH the L0
records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** Can one infer *another's* mind (paracitta) from its uttered appearances — e.g. infer
Maitra's intent from his speech or action? The Buddhist (Vijñānavādin) thinks the other-stream can be
inferred. Abhinavagupta shows it cannot: only the *aimed* (samīhita) action is the wish's effect, and
the other-stream, being other, cannot be established by the mere utterance (which could belong to
anything). This leads into kārikā 6 — the appearances alone suffice for dealing, and the external object
(whole or atom) is refuted.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The candana/agni analogy (lines 3600–3644):**
As, when one wishes to infer the fire in the sandalwood, if the sandalwood's fragrance of the smoke is
set aside, only the fire in general is established, not the sandalwood-fire: the distinction (the
sandalwood-fragrance-ness of the smoke) must be accepted — for without it the desired (the
sandalwood-fire) is not established. But the other-stream is not established without the "being-gone-to-
the-other" (the paragatatva, the first-person-related distinction) — which cannot be inferred. The
"being-gone-to-the-other" is not the non-establishedness-of-the-desired's-cause (the kāraṇa); it is the
opposed (the kāraṇa-viruddha-upalabdhi). As the sandalwood-fragrance is not a further distinction of the
sandalwood (not resting apart from it), so the specific is that which rests apart from the other. Else
the "cow" and the "speckled" would both be specifics (the sāmānya-viśeṣa-dealing would fall; the two
would both be specifics, and their co-reference would fail — yet "the cow is the speckled" holds). So
the "being-gone-to-the-other" of the appearance, resting apart from the mere utterance, is the opposed.
In the present case: the "Maitra utters" — the being-gone-to-the-other, of the maitra-made operation-
utterance, in the other-knower (the parasantāna, caitrātma) — is the distinction that cannot be inferred
from the mere utterance. As the sandalwood-fire needs the fragrance-perception (the
vyāpti-grasping, in the palace), so here — but the other-stream cannot be established, for only the
samīhitā (the aimed) is the wish's effect; else even the piśāca-like aimed action in the caitra would be
established.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The "sāmānyaviśeṣavyavahāra" (the universal-particular dealing) and the cow/speckled co-reference —
   the grammar; preserved.
2. The "piśācaprāya" (the piśāca-like) example — the aimed action of the other; kept close.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the other-stream cannot be inferred from the mere utterance,
because only the aimed action is the wish's effect, and the "being-gone-to-the-other" is not
inferable (like the sandalwood-fragrance — the further distinction). Hence the appearances alone
suffice for dealing (kārikā 6), and the external object — whole or atom — is refuted. Only the ābhāsa
is real.

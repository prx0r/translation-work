# IPVV — V2-G ARGUMENT MAP (the pañcamo vimarśa: the īśvarādvaya — the Lord manifests from within, kārikā 7)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00021 lines 4400–5200
(the transition to the īśvarādvaya-vāda's positive thesis — kārikā 7: the Lord manifests the whole
object-mass from within, like a yogin, without an external cause). Works from BOTH the L0 records and
the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The Buddhist's external object (whole or atom) has been refuted (V2-F, the
shadow/sun, the avayavin, the paramāṇu). But the objector asks: "you established the external by the
appearance-order-variety as the cause; so how is it un-established now?" Abhinavagupta answers with the
positive īśvarādvaya thesis: the external is not needed, because the Lord manifests the whole
object-mass from within himself, by his will, like a yogin, without any external material cause.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The avayavin/paramāṇu refutation (lines 4400–4443):**
The whole (avayavin) is refuted by the shadow/sun argument: the jar has shadow on one side, sun on the
other, the middle covered, the two sides uncovered — so the part-holder body, resting on such
contradictory parts, cannot simultaneously bear the shadow and be sun-joined and covered and not-covered.
The raktādi (the colored) are marked by the covering: in a variegated thread, one part red, another
not-red, another black, another yellow. Hence the real-thing's pervader — the being-free-of-
contradictory-dharma-connection — its opposite (the contradictory-dharma-connection) being perceived,
the whole's real-ness is negated. The same for the atom (the paramāṇu): the middle atom, with two atoms,
would have the sun-contact on one side and not on the other, and the middle's covering by both — but the
middle, being part-less, cannot have two sides. So the shadow/sun-distinction, the covered/uncovered,
the red/not-red, is established only in the atom-collection view (the Vaibhāṣika) — and that too fails
(the part-less cannot have sides; the two-part atom is the avayavin; the infinite-regress of the parts).
The ālambana-parīkṣā and the Vijñaptimātratā etc. (the Buddhist texts) are cited. The upshot: the
external object, having no pramāṇa, cannot be established.

**Kārikā 7 (lines 4445–4484) — the Lord manifests from within:**
> The consciousness-self alone, indeed, the God, the inner-standing, by the will, outward — like a
> yogin, without the material-cause, the object-mass, he would manifest.

The consciousness-form is the locus of the object-establishment (accepted by all); by the synthesis-
power it is one; its lordship (aiśvarya) — the God-ness — is the freedom of joining-and-separating the
appearances (established). The manifestation-identity of the objects is said at length. Hence the
inner-containing-world-form (antargatavaiśvarūpya) is from that lordship. With it, there must be the
will (icchā), of the un-thwarted-freedom-marker. By the will, the creation is seen in the dream and the
construction (the jar without the clay-cause-lineage). The objection: "then the jar is unreal" — let it
be so; still the yogin creates the army and the rest by the will alone. If the atoms, by the will, are
joined (the upādāna), then if the whole jar-making-cause-complement (the karma, the dharma, the
stick-and-wheel helpers, the clay) were accepted, the yogin would be a potter; but with even a straw,
transcending the known, the dream-construction shows the consciousness's power to create the
being-appearance. (The Bhaṭṭadivākavatsa: "no other cause of the dream's object-variety is seen than
your manifestation-power; being seen-powerful, it alone is the one cause of the whole world's
manifestation.") The yogin's consciousness, though tinted by body and breath, is, by its purity, the
creator; and the consciousness-tattva alone, everywhere, is the creator (the cittattva). The example is
not separately explained (the dārṣṭāntika form explains it; the grantha, by the repetition, speaks in
both). Hence the God, the consciousness-self, manifests the object-mass from within, by the will, like a
yogin — without an external cause.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The Bhaṭṭadivākavatsa verse — cited; the exact application preserved.
2. The "cittattvaiva sarvatra nirmātṛ" (the consciousness-tattva alone, everywhere, is the creator) —
   the crux; preserved.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the external (whole or atom) is refuted by the shadow/sun and the
part-less-atom arguments; so the Lord manifests the object-mass from within, by his will, like a yogin,
without an external material cause (as in the dream); the consciousness-tattva alone, everywhere, is the
creator. This is the īśvarādvaya positive thesis.

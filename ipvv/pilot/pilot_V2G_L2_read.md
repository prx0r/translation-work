**The Lord creates from within, like a yogin**

The external object has been demolished. The whole (avayavin) cannot stand: a jar has shadow on one
side and sun on the other, its middle covered and its sides uncovered — so the one whole that was to
bear all these contradictory states is impossible. And the atom (paramāṇu) fares no better: the middle
atom, being part-less, cannot have two sides, one sun-lit and one shaded; the two-part atom collapses
back into the whole it was meant to replace; and the parts run on to infinity. Whether the Buddhist
posits a whole or atoms, the external thing has no valid means behind it. It cannot be established.

But then the objector asks a fair question: "You yourself established the external by the variety of
the appearance-order, as its cause. So how is it un-established now?" And this is where Abhinavagupta
turns from destruction to construction. The external is not needed, because the Lord manifests the
whole world from within himself, by his own will, without any external material cause — exactly as a
yogin does.

Consider what has been granted on all sides. The consciousness-form is the locus in which objects are
established; and by its power of synthesis it is one. Its lordship — its very God-ness — is the freedom
to join and separate the appearances. And the objects are, in their being, the manifestation of this
consciousness. So the whole world is, from the first, *within* the consciousness — the inner-containing-
world-form. And with this freedom there must be the will: the un-thwarted will, whose very nature is
freedom. And that the will creates — without any external cause — is already seen in the dream. In the
dream, the jar appears, and the whole clay-wheel-stick-lineage of causes is absent. The dream proves
that the consciousness alone can create an object-mass, by its will, without a material cause.

The objection: "then the jar is unreal." Let it be so, Abhinavagupta answers — and still the point
holds. For the yogin creates an army, a city, by the mere will. If someone says "the atoms are joined by
the will, as the material cause," then if the whole known cause-complement (the clay, the wheel, the
helpers) were accepted, the yogin would be just a potter. But when he transcends the known — creates
with even a straw — the dream and the construction show that the consciousness's power to bring about
the being-appearance needs no external cause at all. As the verse has it: "no other cause of the
dream's object-variety is seen than your manifestation-power; being seen to have that power, it alone
is the one cause of the whole world's manifestation."

And even the yogin — whose consciousness is tinted by body and breath — is, by its purity, a creator;
so how much more the pure consciousness-tattva, which everywhere is the creator. The God, the
consciousness-self, manifests the whole object-mass from within himself, by his will, like a yogin,
without any external material cause. This is the īśvarādvaya — the non-dual lordship. The world is not
made from a stuff outside the Lord; it is his own inner being, manifested outward by his will. And this
is why the external object was never needed: not because the world is unreal, but because its reality
is the Lord's own manifestation, arising from within, by the freedom of his will.

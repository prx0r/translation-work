# IPVV — V2-I ARGUMENT MAP (the pañcamo vimarśa: the powers as the vimarśa, kārikās 14–19)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00021 lines 6000–6800
(the positive account of the powers — the close of kārikā 13, and kārikās 14–19: the sphurattā, the
jñeyīkaraṇa, the powers as the vimarśa). Works from BOTH the L0 records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** Having established the reflexive-awareness (vimarśa) as the manifestation's nature
and as the parā-vāk (V2-H), Abhinavagupta now gives the positive account of the powers. He must: (1)
show that the paśyantī-speech, the word, is not a separate param (the grammarian's paśyantī-as-param is
refuted); (2) state the sphurattā (the throbbing) as the great being, the heart of the Lord (k14); (3)
show how the self makes itself the known (jñeyīkaraṇa, k15–16); (4) show the ahantā/idantā division is
no ground for the self's otherness (k17); (5) show the jñāna/saṃkalpa/adhyavasāya powers are the
vimarśaśakti contracted by māyā (k18); and (6) show the vimarśa is present even in the moment of direct
manifestation (k19).

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The speech-power not-param (lines 6000–6044):**
The speech (vāk) and the mind (manas) — their motion — are only the ahaṅkāra-vṛtti (the ego-operation),
like the hand; so how could they be the param? The paśyantī-speech's param-ness is refuted (the
Śivadṛṣṭi). Here the param-eśvara is the word-mass (śabdarāśi); his power is the varied distinct-and-
non-distinct; the mātṛkā-devatā-group, the eight-fold Rudra-power, the fifty phonemes, the fifty
Rudra-powers. The "pañcamantramahāmūrtir bhagavān" — the Lord's word-one-body-ness, of the
reflexive-awareness, the manifestation-form, is accepted. But the māyīya phonemes (the word-forms) are
like the jar; the raising-knowledge (the avikalpa-savikalpa, the paśyantī-madhyamā-form) is like another
cognition; the speech-organ (vāgindriya) is like the hand, of the duality-nature, placed in the māyā-
side. So how could it be the param? It is of the object-cognition-organ-form. But where the own-action-
part-success is seen even there, that is made by the param-essence-power-touch (the spa. kā. 8: "from
the self-power-touch…"). (The mā. vi. 3.28: "they, being touched by them, become the givers of the
fruit of all the desires — for the lord of the sādhakas, not otherwise.") What is contracted by time,
place, form, action — that is not the param (like the jar). So the paśu-form (the word, the cognition,
the organ) — the param-ness is the fullness (pūrṇatva); its opposite, the contraction, is the
svabhāva-viruddha-upalabdhi. (The Śivadṛṣṭi 2.17: "like the hand-organ, this is not the Brahman, the
speech-organ"; 2.19: "by the reflexive-awareness-experience, she, the speech, is first reached.")

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The Śivadṛṣṭi 2.17/2.19 and the mā. vi. 3.28 citations — cited; the applications preserved.
2. The "pañcamantramahāmūrti" (the fifth-mantra great-form) — the Lord's word-body; kept close.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the speech and the mind are only the ego-operation, not the param;
the param-eśvara is the word-mass, of the reflexive-awareness; the paśyantī-speech is not the param
(only the vimarśa is); and the powers (the jñāna/saṃkalpa/adhyavasāya) are the vimarśaśakti contracted
by māyā. The sphurattā is the great being, the heart of the Lord. This is the positive account of the
powers.

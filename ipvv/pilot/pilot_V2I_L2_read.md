**The powers of the Lord are his reflexive-awareness**

The reflexive-awareness is the heart of the doctrine. Now Abhinavagupta must show what this means for
the powers — and, first, what it does *not* mean. The grammarian would make speech, the paśyantī, a
separate supreme principle. But speech and the mind, Abhinavagupta insists, are only the operation of
the ego — like the hand. They are not the param. The param-eśvara is the word-mass (śabdarāśi), whose
power is the varied, the distinct-and-non-distinct; he is the great form of the fifth mantra, the
reflexive-awareness that is the manifestation-form. But the māyīya phonemes — the words we actually
speak — are like the jar; the knowledge that raises them (the paśyantī-madhyamā) is like another
cognition; and the speech-organ is like the hand, placed in the side of māyā. None of these is the
supreme. They are of the object-cognition-organ form.

And yet — and this is the delicate point — where the word does its work, where it succeeds in its own
part of the action, that success is not its own: it is made by the touch of the supreme power. As the
teaching says, "from the self-power-touch…" the things become givers of the fruit of all desires. The
power that makes the word effective is the reflexive-awareness of the Lord, flowing through it. What
is contracted by time, place, form, and action is not the param — like the jar. And the param-ness is
fullness (pūrṇatva); its opposite, the contraction, is what the experience refutes. The paśu-form — the
word, the cognition, the organ — is contracted; the param is the full, which the vimarśa is.

And this leads to the positive account of the powers. The throbbing (sphurattā) — the very pulsing of
the manifestation — is the great being (mahāsattā), undivided by place and time, the heart of the
param-Lord (kārikā 14). It is the same as the spanda, the wave (ūrmi), the eternal being. And the
powers by which the Lord acts — the making of the known (jñeyīkaraṇa), the constructing and dealing
with himself (k15–16) — these are all the reflexive-awareness. The division of the "I"-awareness and
the "this"-awareness (ahantā/idantā) is no ground for the self's otherness (k17): the self remains one
in both. And the powers of cognition, of conception, of determination (jñāna, saṃkalpa, adhyavasāya)
are, in truth, the one vimarśaśakti, contracted by the māyā-power into the many forms (k18). And even
in the very moment of direct manifestation — the sākṣātkāra — the vimarśa is present (k19): else the
running and the rest could not happen without the anusandhāna, the synthesis that joins.

So the whole positive account is one thought. The Lord is the word-mass, the reflexive-awareness; his
powers — the throbbing, the making-known, the determining — are all the one vimarśa, seen contracted by
māyā. The speech and the mind are not separate supremes; they are the ego's operation, quickened by the
Lord's power-touch. And the vimarśa is present everywhere — in the sudden surge, in the direct
manifestation, in the determination — because it is the very nature of the light, the heart of the
param-Lord.

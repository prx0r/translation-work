## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The vimarśa-section closes. Two final points: (1) the word (vācaka) and its
determination — the "this" of the jar is not a separate thing the word pastes on; the word's own form
is the reflexive-awareness, self-luminous like a lamp (the sphoṭa analysis); and (2) kārikā 21 — the
cognition/memory/determination appear ordered (sakrama) only by following the place-and-time of the
different cognized; in themselves, the reflexive-awareness-resting cognitions are one and order-less.
This closes the jñānaśakti vimarśa (||5||).

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The vācaka and the sphoṭa (lines 7600–7644):**
The substance-ness (dravyatva) is by the primary-ness, the object-action-use, the being-differentiated
by the other-dharmas (the kriyāvatva). The whole, of the other-appearance-form, flashes with the
primary-re-recollection ("this," as in "rājapuruṣa," the king-man — the king and the rest, without the
primary-re-recollection, would not raise the substance-ness). In the present case, "this jar": the
"jar"-word, going to the secondary-place in the meaning, the "this"-re-recollection is with the
non-difference; else the word, not superimposed on the meaning, would be recalled as free-standing
(the "from this fire, ḍhak" rule), not as the meaning-speaker. The "śuklaḥ paṭaḥ" (the white cloth): the
white-quality, previously manifested-and-recalled, at the time of the distinction, is not again
manifested-recalled (in the many-cognition-object-ness view). The saṃketa (the convention) — the cloth-
word, after the convention, does not shine by the mere experience (like the white-quality), but is
superimposed on the vācya (the meaning) — so here it is not the distinction/qualified but the adhyāsa
(the superimposition). Hence, without the separate "this"-re-recollection, the name's being-recalled-as-
distinct-from-the-knower-essence is doubtful. The answer ("svanāmnā tu" — by the own-name): where the
manifested is not re-recollected, the being-manifested itself is hard (shown). As the external blue,
manifested, is re-recollected as "blue," so the vācaka (the word), the manifestation, necessarily has
the vimarśa. Nor is there, beyond its own-form, another word (general or specific) by which it would be
recalled as the blue-speaker; nor by another similar word (the anavasthā would destroy the
blue-re-recollection and so the blue-manifestation). Hence the own-form-re-recollection-nature alone is
the meaning's. Since the "blue"-word is the re-recollector, its self-and-other-re-recollection-ness is
its self-luminousness (svaprakāśatva). The parāmarśa is the manifestation's true nature; the lamp's
shining (its not-bearing the shadow/covering dharma) is in the self, and in the jar too — so the
manifestation, by the same being-shining, reveals the object and reveals itself. The vimarśa-dīpana
(the reflexive-awareness-kindling) is the manifestation's fruit-ground and seed-ground. The cognition,
shining by itself, by that same shining makes the object shine — clearly the self-and-other-manifesting.
So the lamp is self-and-other-light; the word [is self-and-other-re-recollection].

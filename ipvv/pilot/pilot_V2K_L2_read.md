**The word is self-luminous, and the order is only in the objects**

We have said that the reflexive-awareness is the very nature of the manifestation. Now the vimarśa on
the knowledge-power closes by drawing out two consequences — one about words, one about order.

First, the word. When we say "this jar," the "this" seems to be a thing that the word "jar" is pasted
onto. But Abhinavagupta shows this is a mistake. The "jar"-word, in the determination, does not stand
apart as a free thing that the "this" points at; it is superimposed on the meaning, and the
"this"-re-recollection is made with the non-difference. If the word were not superimposed, it would be
recalled as a free-standing sound — "from this fire, ḍhak" — not as the speaker of a meaning. The
conventional word (like the "cloth"-word after the convention) does not shine by a bare experience, as
a quality like "white" does; it shines by being superimposed on the meaning. So the relation of word and
meaning is not a distinction of two things but a superimposition (adhyāsa).

And this brings us to the word's own nature. If the manifested were not re-recollected, its being-
manifested itself would be impossible. As the external blue, being manifested, is re-recollected as
"blue," so the word — which is itself a manifestation — necessarily has the reflexive-awareness. There
is no further word, general or specific, by which it would be recalled as the blue-speaker; and to call
it by another similar word would run into the infinite regress, destroying the blue-re-recollection and
so the blue-manifestation itself. Hence the own-form-re-recollection is the very nature of the meaning.
And because the "blue"-word is the re-collector, its being able to re-collect itself and the other is
its self-luminousness (svaprakāśatva).

The lamp makes this clear. The lamp shines, revealing the objects and itself — not because it bears the
covering shadow, but because its being-shining is its own. The same is true of the word and of the
manifestation: by the same act of shining, it reveals the object and reveals itself. The
reflexive-awareness-kindling is both the fruit-ground and the seed-ground of the manifestation. The
cognition, shining by itself, by that very shining makes the object shine — it is clearly
self-and-other-manifesting. So the lamp is self-and-other-light; the word is self-and-other-
re-recollection.

Second, the order. We see cognition, memory, determination following one another, ordered in time. But
this order, kārikā 21 declares, is only by the place-and-time of the *different cognized* objects. The
cognition of the blue, then the memory of it, then the determination — these seem to be a sequence. But
in themselves, the reflexive-awareness-resting cognitions are one and order-less (akrama). The order
belongs to the objects, to the places and times that the cognized things carry; the awareness that
knows them all is one, without sequence, resting in itself. Just as the Lord is free of order though he
shows order, so the single reflexive-awareness is order-less, appearing ordered only through the
objects' place-and-time.

With this, the jñānaśakti vimarśa — the reflexive-awareness of the knowledge-power — comes to its
close. The word is self-luminous, the manifestation reveals itself and the other, and the order of our
cognitions is only in the objects, while the awareness itself is one and order-less. The knowledge-power
is, in the end, the single self-luminous reflexive-awareness, free of order, revealing itself and the
world.

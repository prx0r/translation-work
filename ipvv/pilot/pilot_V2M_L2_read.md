**The Lord descends through the states, and deals with the world**

The consciousness does not remain aloof. It descends, step by step, into the forms of the world — and
this descent is the story of the bound knower. Abhinavagupta traces it through the states. The
consciousness, entering the known-form, takes up the void, the breath, the intellect, the body, in
order. Even space, the most permissive of forms, is not wholly inert; the wind, with its power of
movement, less so; the light, with its form and brilliance, still less; the water, which receives what
enters it; and finally the coarse, dense earth, the most inert of all. And with each descent, the
self-conceit sinks further — as the elephant sinks into the mud-bank — until the consciousness comes to
identify itself with the coarse body. For the world, the first of these is the breath (prāṇa): the
touch-form, the moving, that takes up the void-following and, with the fire-connection, presides over
the rest. The consciousness-tattva is what presides over the prāṇa, which presides over the intellect,
the puryaṣṭaka (the inner city), and the body.

So the Lord, the consciousness-form, presides over the body *through* the prāṇa and the puryaṣṭaka —
not directly, by a bare experience, but as the inner light that animates them. As the Gītā says, "whatever
body the Lord attains, and whatever he departs, taking these, he goes, as the wind takes the fragrances
from the flower-bed." The īśvara, the consciousness-form, goes from body to body through the
prāṇa-puryaṣṭaka, as the wind carries the scents. And the Spandakārikā's "with the inner wheel" is
this same prāṇa, by which the organ-group obtains its proceeding, standing, and withdrawal.

And because the consciousness, through the prāṇa, presides over the states, the enjoyment continues even
after the body's death. As in the dream — the state immersed in sleep, the delusion — the
prāṇa-embraced puryaṣṭaka, being the same, passes on, and the enjoyment in the other world, by the
presiding over another body, is not impossible. The yogins are seen, remaining in their own body and
their own house, to be elsewhere — proving the consciousness's power to preside beyond the present body.

This leads to the kārikās. The construction (vikalpa, kārikā 6) is the joining of the "this" from the
prior appearance, by the impression — it rests in the different manifestations, and it is a
construction. But the *continuity* (anusandhāna) — the self that runs through the body, mind, breath,
through all the states, from life to life — is not a construction; it is the one consciousness,
un-divided. And in the dealing (kārikā 7), the Lord, entering the body, manifests the inner
object-mass without, by the will. The world we deal with is the Lord's own inner mass, manifested
outward by his will, through the very states through which he descends.

And this is the kṛtyapañcaka — the fivefold work. The Lord creates, causes to stand, withdraws,
conceals, and graces — and all five are his dealing, his manifestation of the inner mass through the
states. The consciousness that descends through the void, the breath, the intellect, and the body is
the same Lord who creates and deals with the whole world. The bound knower's descent is the Lord's
descent; the dealing is the Lord's dealing; and the fivefold work is the Lord's own manifestation,
through the prāṇa and the puryaṣṭaka, of the inner world without.

**The inner appearance stands firm — for every living one**

The ṣaṣṭho vimarśa, on the power of removal, comes to its close; and its final claim is one of
unfailing constancy. The inner appearance — the self-grasp, the reflexive-awareness — stands firm in
memory, in construction, and in cognition alike (kārikā 8). It is not something that flickers in and
out, present in cognition and absent in memory. It is the one light that runs through them all. In the
cognition whose form is the external manifestation, this inner appearance — the prior-experience-form —
is innate (kārikā 9); and it stands, too, in memory and the rest. In the freely-wandering construction,
the inner appearance is innate indeed (kārikā 10): the construction may wander at will, forming this or
that shape, but the self-grasp that shapes it is always there, in the mind's range. And hence — from the
manifestation of the uplifting as wished — knowledge and action are clear, established for every living
one (kārikā 11).

And this constancy extends even to the freed. Even in liberation, the freed souls (muktāṇava) are
distinct — yet their manifestation, by the śiva-ness, is the un-broken all-knowing. The three-time
manifestation may be established in the māyīya knower, but it does not fail to flash for them either,
from their all-knowing. And the mantreśvaras — even in liberation, by the exhaustion of their authority,
in the place of the other — by the word-obtaining (the dīkṣā, the vow, the japa, the tapas, the
concentration, the conduct), transmigrating, are all-knowing; the knower-ness is not de-valued. The
animal-atoms are distinct only in their animal-ness, generally. The categories, the worlds, the
elements, the kalā, the mantra, the word, the phoneme — the jars and the rest, with respect to their
own class, are not utterly unprecedented.

The objection "how, in the dealing-time, is the creation?" is met. The creation is by the unity — at
that time, per-knower, the jar-appearance and the body-appearance are distinct, but the consciousness-
self alone [creates them]; and the knower-distinction is not destroyed, for it stands in the
prāṇa-puryaṣṭaka and the like. And the appearance is the cognition: the jar-form appearance, though
known, is of the knower-operation-form, of the knower-standing-ness — it is the cognition itself. The
final objection — "then by the knower's will, per-moment the creation and withdrawal of objects and
knowers — how does the maheśvara shine?" — is answered: the order-word's pure-form-sequence is not the
whole; the maheśvara, the Lord, is the one who shines through it all.

So the vimarśa closes with the constancy of the self-grasp. It is innate in memory, in cognition, in
construction; it stands firm for the bound and for the freed, for the animal-atom and the mantreśvara;
and from its manifestation — the uplifting as wished — knowledge and action are clear for every living
one. The inner appearance, the reflexive-awareness, never fails: it is the very nature of the light,
and it is what makes every living one a knower and a doer. The apohanaśaktivimarśa — the reflexion on
the power of removal — here comes to its close.

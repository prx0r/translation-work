**The valid means is the knower's own operation**

The saptamo vimarśa is establishing the one support of the powers. And its deepest point is that the
valid means (pramāṇa) is not a separate instrument standing over against the knower. It is the
knower's own operation (pramātṛ-vyāpāra) — the act by which the many cognitions are gathered into the
one knower. The five objects of perception and non-perception, thrown into the one consciousness-ocean,
are joined by the freedom-wave, becoming mutually dependent (jar and cloth, as it were); they shine
with their distinct appearances and are re-reflected. The one appearance, made by the
manifestation-operation of the five, is the cause-and-effect appearance; and the re-recollection is
the knower's operation. This — the joining — is the whole logic of the valid means. Without it, even
the pramāṇas are useless. As the Mukuta-saṃhitā says, the consciousness-operation alone is the one
reason, established everywhere, in enjoyment or in liberation. The joiner alone is the means.

Now the objection presses on memory. "By perception and non-perception," the objector says, "what was
made is to be unified; this much is the acceptance of the knower. But what is this? In the
unification, the memory alone is engaged." And Abhinavagupta answers with kārikā 5: in memory, that
very self-consciousness is the valid means in the self's birth; in the prior-experience-being, it is
the proof — that alone, not another.

Consider what happens when we remember. The memory seems to bring back a prior object. But it does not
bring a *new* thing, a fresh appearance supplied by the memory-time — for memory is of the
grasped-grasping nature; it re-grasps what was already grasped. What shines in the memory is the prior
experience itself. And it does not now stand as a separate known object — for if it did, we would have
the cognition of a known cognition (jñātajñāna), which is not memory at all; and no cognition is
known-by-another (as we have shown at length). So, if the prior experience were a separate
self-luminous thing, we would have two unconnected experiences, not a memory. The only way the memory
is possible is that, at the memory-time, the prior experience itself — being the inner self-consciousness
— is what shines. The memory is the prior experience, self-conscious, remembered.

And this is the point. The memory is not a second cognition standing over against a first; it is the
same self-consciousness, the same knower, continuing. The valid means in memory — and in every
cognition — is the self-consciousness of the one knower. It is the knower's own operation, the act by
which he knows and re-knows himself. And this is why the one knower is the support of all the powers:
knowledge, memory, and removal are all his own self-conscious operation. The valid means is not a thing
the knower uses; it is the knower himself, the one who remembers, the same self-consciousness in the
self's birth and in the memory — that alone, not another.

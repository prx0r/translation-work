## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The saptamo vimarśa closes with the question of the *omniscient* (sarvajña) — the
supreme knower who is the support of all the powers. The Buddhist objector presses: if the piśāca (the
goblin) is not perceived even by the omniscient, how can he deny it? The analysis turns on whether the
omniscient's non-perception of the piśāca establishes its non-being, and how the ordinary knower
(arvāgdarśana) differs. This grounds the omniscient as the one who knows all — the support of the
powers.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The piśāca and the āloka-atoms (lines 12000–12044):**
Even if the piśāca is not seen by us, the form-and-the-rest atoms are there — not seen by us, but seen
by the omniscient. The atoms have no un-precedented variety. The "na tathā" — the obstructing ones
also, not utterly. The "dūrataram" — the shadow, made by the darkness, with the interval, the
distinguished, the same-class-devoid interval; the middle-darkness or the middle-void. In their own
place, they withdraw; by the unable-moment-production-order, they are withdrawn. Hence the
piśāca-atoms are, for the omniscient, equal to the darkness-and-the-rest-other-being-atoms. But if the
piśāca is absent, then where the piśāca had occupied the interval, the āloka-atoms alone, being seen,
are the object of the piśāca's-absence-dealing for the omniscient. The objection: "seeing the other
objects, how does the omniscient deny the distinct piśāca?" — in the piśāca's being-or-not-being, the
own-form-standing of these is not lost; the distinct-piśāca's-absence is, by this way, hard to
establish even by the omniscient. The "athavā" (or): whatever is, is to be seen by him; then, there, if
he does not see the piśāca, it is not — and there, everything is the piśāca-included thing, known by
another cognition distinct from its being; or that very cognition is the object of the
piśāca's-absence-dealing — so the interval-atom-tracing does nothing. The objection "why is it not so
for us too?" — answered (arvāk): "he who sees the limited, for him there is no known" — this
cognition arises, by which the cognition is the cause of the being-included-cognition-marker
non-being-dealing. But where the certainty arises (like the smelling, the tasting, the attending, the
touching — the necessary-conceiving of the smell-cognition), there the smell-cognition's removal is
the cause of the non-being-dealing (the breath-entering-cognition-marker, the un-divided-from-the-
smell-taste-sound-touch-absence). Hence, though the piśāca's-absence is grasped by him who grasps the
āloka or the place (by the piśāca's otherness), still it is the limited, grasped as if un-grasped (no
cause of the removal of the conceiving of the distinct in the place or the āloka). The objection "if
the piśāca's non-negatability's cause is the invisibility, then the jar's negatability's cause would
be the visibility (1.7.10, the 'svajñāna-maghaṭātmatā')" — answered (siddham): "every negation
culminates in the identity-negation; and there the visibility is un-deviating (the hetubindu: the
distinction is to be said)."

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The 1.7.10 ("svajñāna-maghaṭātmatā") and the hetubindu references — cited; the applications
   preserved.
2. The "arvāk" (the limited-seeing) vs. the sarvajña distinction — the crux; kept close.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the omniscient, seeing all the atoms, knows the piśāca's absence
in its place (the āloka-atoms alone); the limited seer (arvāk) cannot — his non-perception is only the
limited, not the established non-being; and every negation culminates in the identity-negation, where
the visibility is un-deviating. The omniscient is the support of all the powers — the one who knows all,
whose non-perception establishes, whose perception is whole. The ||7|| colophon closes the saptamo
vimarśa.

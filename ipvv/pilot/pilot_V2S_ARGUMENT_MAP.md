# IPVV — V2-S ARGUMENT MAP (the close of the jñānādhikāra: the knowers' unity and the great-Lordship, kārikās 10–11)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00021 lines 13200–13405
(the close of the aṣṭamo vimarśa and the whole jñānādhikāra — the aikya (the knowers' unity), the
māheśvarya summary, and the ||8|| + jñānādhikāra colophons). Works from BOTH the L0 records and the
raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The jñānādhikāra — the whole first adhikāra of the IPVV — closes. Its final point:
the *unity of the knowers* (aikya). It was said "the one maheśvara." Kārikā 10 establishes the unity:
without it, the world-path of the consciousnesses would not be; from the manifestation-unity, that
one-ness; that one alone is the knower, it is established. Kārikā 11 summarizes the great-Lordship: he
alone, by the fixed reflexive-awareness-ness, is the great Lord; the reflexive-awareness alone, of the
God, in the pure knowledge-action, since. The jñānādhikāra is COMPLETE (the ||8|| colophon and the
samāpta-jñānādhikāra).

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Kārikā 10 — the unity of the knowers (lines 13203–13244):**
It was said "the one maheśvara." The unity is concluded: "Without that unity, the world-path of the
consciousnesses would not be; from the manifestation-unity, that one-ness, that one alone is the
knower, it is established." Hence: without the unity of the cognitions, the whole dealing (the
world-path) would not be; and it is — so the unity of the consciousnesses is necessary. Nor is it hard:
the manifestation (prakāśa) is the consciousnesses' param-essence, and it is one (no difference); and
that one is, in truth, the knower. The vṛtti's "paramātma" says the param-essence knower-ness. The
Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi 20 ("yadyapy arthasthitiḥ…"). The 'ākārataḥ' (by the form) — the chief difference
does not exist (the own-form-grasping). The reason: the consciousness-mere-form-ness. The space-time
difference too: "deśa" — though the varied difference is the dealing, by the pervasiveness the
all-grasping taught-taught-relation is here; it is in the self too (doubt, reasoning, decision) and in
the other (teaching, buying-selling); without it, even the dealing would be un-dealt-with. The
objection: "the cognitions, in their self-experience, have the joining-rest; what is new here?" — the
"yadica": the consciousnesses, whose self is the known (the blue and the rest); the blue's
param-essence is the manifestation; hence the real unity is fit. The objection: "how is the tint made
by the blue, so that 'the tinted by the this-and-this-known' is said?" — the māyāśakti-made difference:
the known-marker part, made distinct from the manifestation as if, hence distinct, mutually distinct,
is the manifestation's own part — by it they are made distinct. The Sāṃkhya/Jaiminīya's
self-luminous consciousness is established; the ancient Vaiśeṣika's self is the consciousness-nature
(1.2.7).

**Kārikā 11 — the great-Lordship summary (lines 13370–13401):**
The Sāṃkhya's objection: "the puruṣa's sattva-rajas-operation-form knowledge-action, in the
buddhi-tattva, are dealt as 'I'" — what new procedure is this? None: the puruṣa is not another; it is
the un-recognition of the own-lordship, the contracted-consciousness-distinct; its knowledge-action,
that "I"-recollection. (This is expanded in 4.1.3.) The vṛtti's "anūditam" (re-stated) — as the
wall-like (the bhitti), not as the "where there is smoke" rule. Hence the ahaṃ-vimarśana is the pervader
of the pure/mixed/un-pure states, as the wall-like. The 'eva' (like "the fire, the smoky"). The
threefold ahaṃ-vimarśana (by the pure-etc. difference) — the three other states (the āgama-established,
the para-vyāmiśra, the apara) are not abandoned but gathered ("etā eva ca"). (The 3.1.5 will be said.)
The ||8|| colophon: "the śaktivargaikāśrayagatamāheśvaryavimarśo 'ṣṭamaḥ." And the jñānādhikāra
COMPLETE: "samāptaś ca ayaṃ jñānādhikāraḥ prathamaḥ" — the end of the whole first adhikāra.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi 20 and 1.2.7 citations — cited; the applications preserved.
2. The "bhittikalpa" (the wall-like) vs. the "where there is smoke" — the anuvāda distinction; kept.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the knowers are one — the manifestation is one, and the one
manifestation is the knower; without the unity no dealing would be. The puruṣa is not another — it is
the un-recognized own-lordship. And kārikā 11: he alone, by the fixed reflexive-awareness-ness, is the
great Lord; the reflexive-awareness is the God's pure knowledge-action. The ||8|| and the
jñānādhikāra-COMPLETE colophons close the whole first adhikāra of the IPVV.

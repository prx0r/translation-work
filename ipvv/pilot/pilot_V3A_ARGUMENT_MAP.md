# IPVV — V3-A ARGUMENT MAP (the kriyādhikāra opening: kriyā, kāla, krama)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00022 lines 57–156
(the maṅgala + kārikās 1–2, the opening of the kriyādhikāra, prathamo vimarśa).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** Is action (kriyā) — and in particular the *Lord's* action — ordered or
sequence-bound (sakrama)? And what is time (kāla) and order (krama) in relation to it?

The whole vimarśa (kārikās 1–9) is announced at the start. Abhinavagupta is now leaving the
knowledge-power (jñānaśakti) and turning to the **action-power (kriyāśakti)**. The thread: just as
consciousness's power to *know* (manifest objects) was established, so now its power to *act* must be.
But there's a paradox: the Lord is said to be beyond time and sequence (akrama), yet the world
manifests *as a sequence* (krama). How can the timeless act in time?

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Maṅgala (lines 61–62):**
> I bow to Śiva, who — though free of sequence — manifests manifold sequences; the nectar-bodied one,
> enjoying the third part of the fourfold nectar-moment (amṛtakalā).

This states the whole thesis in one image: Śiva is *without* order yet *shows* order. The problem is
set from the first line.

**The plan (lines 64–77):** Having established the form of the knowledge-power, Abhinava now
establishes the form of the action-power, in eight verses: one to reject the prior view (kārikā 1),
one to distinguish ordered/un-ordered (kārikā 2), three on the *nature* of order (kārikās 3–5), two on
the division of the sphere of ordered/un-ordered (kārikās 6–7), and one (kārikā 8) concluding that
even after dividing the sphere, both truly rest in the one alone. Then kārikā 9 on the creation-power.

**Kārikā 1 — the action of the one is not ordered; rejected (lines 82–108):**
> "Hence, although it was said that the action of the one is not ordered — 'the one' and the rest —
> [that] is rejected, by the establishing of that one [as the agent]."

**The prior view (pūrvapakṣa) being rejected (lines 87–90):** The Buddhist holds that only
momentary cognitions flash forth, in a stream, with no underlying owner (āśraya); memory is just
impression; and if cognition is inert (jaḍa) it cannot reveal an object, but if it is conscious
(ajaḍa) it has no real temporal distinctions. So "how is the self anything other than this?"

**Abhinava's reply (lines 90–108), the heart of the kārikā:** (a) distinct cognitions cannot serve
ordinary dealing; (b) memory is not from mere impression; (c) cognition, of the un-contracted
consciousness, is its own-power-built contraction by the known-object's tint — this is what "power"
and "dharma" mean. **The key image (lines 98–103):** the one can be of many natures without
contradiction, *like a mirror* (darpaṇa) that bears many reflections without losing its own oneness.
So a thing whose essence is appearance (ābhāsasāra), established as one by unimpeded
recognition-perception, can take up seemingly contradictory forms in itself and — without itself
being divided — manifest those ordered, distinct appearances. This is the action-support (kriyāśraya).
The same reasoning establishes the relation (sambandha) too. So, by establishing the one agent, all
the objections about the action-power are answered.

**Kārikā 2 — only the *worldly* action is ordered, from the time-power (lines 115–156):**
> "The ordered-ness of the worldly action is from the time-power; it obtains, but not of the eternal
> [action] of the Lord, as of the Lord [himself]."

**Objection (lines 112–113):** "Sequentiality is what action is; and how could sequence be in the
Lord, who is not cut by time?" 

**Reply:** Consider the raising and lowering of a hand. Its earlier and later moments are ordered. The
single action, the cause of the division-and-conjunction [of the hand's positions], is inferred —
even if eternal — as ordered *by the force of its ordered effect*. So the *worldly* action, resting in
distinctions, is rightly ordered — by the **time-power (kālaśakti)**, which is the Lord's freedom that
raises the appearance-distinctions, acting as the cause. But the *Lord's own* action — since he is not
cut by time — is itself un-cut-by-time, like the Lord, eternal. **The gist (lines 125–133):** in the
manifestation of known-and-knower that underlies worldly knowledge and dealing, the knowledge-power is
the capacity; and *there* the reflexive-awareness-power (vimarśaśakti), whose self is freedom in
joining and the rest, is in truth the action. Though in reality without sequence, it *appears*
ordered, because it takes up the delimited [forms] for the sake of revealing the shadow of the known
(vedyacchāyā). The Vṛtti and Vivṛti are clear.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The opening is now understood: the Lord is timeless yet manifests sequence (the mirror that
bears reflections without division); the worldly action is ordered by the time-power, which is
nothing but the Lord's freedom raising appearance-distinctions; the Lord's own action is eternal and
un-cut-by-time, appearing ordered only to reveal the known. The mirror image is the load-bearing
figure. kārikās 3–9 (the nature of time, place-order, the creation-power) continue this.

## Completion — kārikās 3–9 (from M00022 lines 166–837)

### Kārikā 3 — what time is (lines 173–203)
> Time is the movement of the sun and the rest, or the birth of this and that flower; or heat and
> cold; its mark is that — but in truth, it is order (krama) itself.

The objector had asked: how can the limited cognitions (the fast/slow ideas) be caused unless time
cuts everything? Abhinava's answer: those "upādhis" (limiting conditions) may stand as mere labels —
what makes the difference is that time is really **the Lord's power manifesting order (krama)**. And
what is manifest is *order itself*. So: time = the fixed difference of nature that order involves,
appearing as the measured sequence. The Vaiśeṣika's time-substance, the Sāṃkhya's rajas, the
grammarians' eternal, the Buddhist's momentary stream — all are, in truth, just this order (krama)
projected as "external time." It is like a dream or a magician's show: the sequence of appearances is
fixed by the conscious power itself. Wherever we call a "cause" time (e.g. "born at midday"), the
actual cause-cognition is of the *sun's sequence*, not of any eternal time-substance. Conclusion: time
is order entering into appearance — the Lord's power of manifesting and not-manifesting.

### Kārikā 4 — order rests on the appearance's being/non-being (lines 314–343)
> Order rests on difference; and difference too is from the appearance's being/non-being-ness; but the
> appearance's being/non-being is made by the Lord, the maker of varied appearances.

If time were just a difference of nature, then the fingers (which differ) would have temporal
difference too. So it is not mere difference. It is the *being and not-being of appearances* — what
is manifest and what is not — that grounds order. And this manifesting/un-manifesting is the Lord's
own *freedom* (svātantrya): he manifests appearances with varied relation (jar on the firm iron, jar
and cloth as distinct, or as one juice), and only when he manifests spring's appearance as empty of
summer's does order "blink" into being. This power to manifest-and-not-manifest is the **time-power
(kālaśakti)**. Conclusion: the order that becomes the upādhi of beings is time. (Cites vā. pa. 2.22;
the "stage-director of the world-machine" verse.)

### Kārikā 5 — from the variety of forms, place-order; from action-variety, time-order (lines 387–406)
> From the variety of the formed (mūrti), the Lord manifests place-order; from the shining of the
> variety of actions, the time-order, too, the īśvara.

The variety of the formed beings (house, courtyard, shop, shrine, garden, forest) → **place-order**
(vaitatya/extension). But the same hand, by one recognition, is one substance — yet "Caitra goes, the
fruit ripens" shows a *variety of actions* → from this the Lord manifests **time-order**. Key: place-
order is in *many* things (house-courtyard); time-order is in the *one* (the ripening fruit). Time
resides in the *known*, not in consciousness; in cognition, only in the known-part, not in the
self-awareness-part.

### Kārikā 6 — the finite knower, not the Lord, has time-order (lines 603–620)
> Everywhere even the appearance-difference would be the source of time-order; but of the
> un-continuous-appearing (vicchinna-bhāsa), of the mother (the finite knower) — of the manifested,
> not of the once-shown (sakṛt).

The 'api' includes the known-object. The variety of appearing/not-appearing that would ground time-
order belongs to the *made-knower* (the one whose marker is the void-thought, the breath, the body) —
because he has *discontinuous appearance* (vicchinna-bhāsa): his shining is not his own form (he is
jaḍa-like), it is only consciousness flashing *as* him. But the **param-essence knower**, the one
"once-shown" (sakṛt) — the continuous, un-broken manifestation (the "once-seen" of the Brahma-knower's
speech) — has never the being/non-being of appearance; so no time touches him, nor (by reference to
him) the beings either.

### Kārikā 7 — place-order, too, only for the measured, not the immeasurable (lines 667–684)
> Even place-order shines in the beings of the measured-self (mitātman); as the self by itself is
> full, so the beings shine of the immeasurable one (amita).

"Of the measured-self" is the reason (genitive). Because the beings of the immeasurable knower — whose
self is mere manifestation — shine as full by their own self, they shine like the self: un-measured,
free of place-order, *in the self*. With respect to the pure self, the Lord has no place-order; nor
(with reference to him) do the beings. For a jar, its own form IS its "place"; so bodies, breaths,
voids stand mutually excluding each other in place — that is the finite knower's seeing of
place-difference. The immeasurable has none.

### Kārikā 8 — the upasaṃhāra-śloka (the closing, line 726): the world is the consciousness-dense
self-filled; time/order, already stated, are summed up.

### Kārikā 9 — but the creation-power (nirmāṇaśakti) (line 734)

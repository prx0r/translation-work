# IPVV — V3-B K6 ARGUMENT MAP (the Kāṇāda measures: parimāṇa / pramāṇa / unmāna, aṇu/mahat)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00022 lines 1905–1951
(IPK 2.2.6 commentary, the number/measure/samkhyā section). This is the passage that read as
"the measure is the cut-other-object-expecting; with the twenty-four-digit-ness, the famous, he who
is the hand-manifestation…"*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Abhinavagupta is defending the thesis of kārikā 1 — the cognitions of action, relation, *substance*,
direction, etc. are true (by stability + efficacy). In kārikā 6 he has been showing that the
*conjunction* (samanvaya) of the grammatical adjuncts (kāraka) is the action-reflexive-awareness
object. Now, along the way, he must deal with the *substance* (dravya) cognition, which the Vaiśeṣika
grounds in **number and measure** (saṃkhyā, parimāṇa). So the whole point here is: are number and
measure *real features of things* (as the Vaiśeṣika says) or *relational, conventional phenomena*
(as Abhinava argues)? This is the same bhedābheda / saṃvṛti battle as before, now applied to
quantity.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — the maxim (line 1905):**
> "No real entity (sattvabhūto'rthaḥ) is expressed without a number." — i.e. every thing we speak of
comes numbered (one, two, …). So number seems to be essential to reality.

**Step 1 — number is relational, not a free-standing thing (1907–1911):**
In that [numbered statement], the number-*appearance* (saṃkhyābhāsa) of the nominal-meaning takes the
subordinate position (upasarjana). For it is the very nature of the case-ending-meaning (vibhaktyartha)
to be a *quality* (guṇībhāva) in the nominal-meaning. Hence, with the words "one," "two," etc., what
is grasped as primary is the *mutual-cut-appearance* (parasparāvacchedābhāsa) — the appearance of each
thing as delimiting/being-delimited-by the others. And by that, the *object-form alone* is the
number-with-its-two-folds [the one and the many]. "Number" itself, then, is just *the counting of
distinctions* (bhedagaṇanātmikā) — it is not an extra real thing added to the object.

**Step 2 — measure (parimāṇa) is likewise relational (1912–1940):**
[Abhinava continues:] "And measure, too [is such]":
> the atom, the great, the short, the long, and —

this is the measure the Kāṇādas (Vaiśeṣikas) are famous for. Take first the short-and-long:
> the length, though, would be the measure… —

so it [length] fits the definition of "measure" (pramāṇa-lakṣaṇa). Now: "measure" (pramāṇa) is
*relative to a delimited other object* (paricchinnavastvantarāpekṣa). It is *the appearance of
comparison* (samīkaraṇābhāsa): the famous hand-appearance of twenty-four digits (a cubit), with which
one compares — and this is *not* a mere external conjunction (saṃyoga). Hence he says: "measure too,
as if by an inner-unity-part, of the to-be-measured cloth, measured by the hand — from the attainment
of the conjunction [samanvaya]." The taking up of the measuring-rod (yaṣṭyupādāna) is *for the sake of
raising the short/long distinction*. [i.e. measure = the appearance of comparison against a standard,
not a real intrinsic magnitude.]

**Step 3 — atom-ness and great-ness are NOT part-less-ness (1928–1933):**
But what is said — "atom, great"? If atom-ness (aṇutva) were *mere part-less-ness* (abhāgatāmātra),
then it would belong to the part-less qualities and actions (guṇakarmādīnāṃ abhāgānām). If, on the
other hand, atom-ness were the substance's being-part-less (abhāvatva), then it would belong to space
(ākāśa) too. Both fail. So: atom-ness = *the fitness to be delimited from all sides*, by the
world-famous very-subtle [thing] — the lightest of the mustard-seed, the pod, the small box, the
little box (sarṣapaka-vāṭapiṭikapeṭaka). That alone is atom-ness; the other [extreme] is great-ness
(mahattva). [i.e. atom/mahat are relational measures of delimitation, not absolute partless/
parted natures.]

**Step 4 — the three measures: parimāṇa, pramāṇa, unmāna (1935–1949):**
The verse "the measure, though, from all sides" (parimāṇaṃ tu sarvataḥ) is what grasps this. Then:
"the prastha-kudava" — of the to-be-measured sesame-and-the-rest, measured by the wood-cup-like, the
measure whose mark is the *comparison-appearance* — not a mere conjunction; that too [is] "as if by
an inner-unity-part." And the "unmāna," the name, is marked as the "upward-measure"
(ūrdhvamāna = weight). The Kāṇādas say unmāna = heaviness (gurutva) causing the falling-action. But
that is not so: in mere heaviness there is no measuring-dealing (mānavyavahāra). Rather, the *famous*
heaviness — as by the balance-weights (prativartaka) — and its *comparison* alone is the unmāna (so
the grammarians say). Thus "measure-ness" (mānatā) is the *same* in the three [parimāṇa, pramāṇa,
unmāna]: the difference is only that the measuring is "extended," "around," or "upward," in measure-
form. And this is fit — he says "the māṣaka-rakitikā": of the to-be-measured gold, measured by the
balance-weight, the mark is the *heaviness-comparison-appearance*. So measure is used, as if by an
inner-unity-part, in the dealing (vyavahāra) of buying and selling (krayavikraya), by the
greater-and-lesser (prakarṣāpakarṣa) alone. Two examples are written down, joined by the connection.

**Step 5 — separateness too (1950–1951):**
Separateness (pṛthaktva), likewise, is preceded by the grasping of distinctions: it is the
distinction-appearance whose middle is the un-difference that is internally conjectured (śaṅkyamāna).
[And it runs] from the number-delimited "one," "separate," up to the "separate" of the far end.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The exact Vaiśeṣika definitions (parimāṇa vs. pramāṇa vs. unmāna) — the text presupposes the
   Vaiśeṣika-sūtra's three measures; my reconstruction (parimāṇa = all-sides/volume, pramāṇa = length,
   unmāna = weight/upward) follows the text's own glosses but should be checked against the Vaiśeṣika
   and Kāśikā.
2. The "twenty-four-digit" cubit and the "prastha-kudava" (volume measures), "māṣaka-rakitikā" (gold
   weights), "sarṣapaka-vāṭapiṭikapeṭaka" (mustard-pod/small-box) — realia whose exact magnitudes are
   technical; the *argument* (measure = comparison) is clear, the magnitudes are not.
3. "diṣṭya (yaṣṭy)upādānaṃ" — the reading/correction of the measuring-rod; textual.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The point is now understood: number and measure are *not* real intrinsic features — they are
*relational appearances of comparison and counting* (against a standard: the cubit, the balance, the
cup). Atom/great, short/long, heavy/light are all ways of *delimiting* a thing relative to others —
the very bhedābheda/saṃvṛti doctrine applied to quantity. The Vaiśeṣika's reification of measure is
rejected; the realia (magnitudes) are logged, not resolved.

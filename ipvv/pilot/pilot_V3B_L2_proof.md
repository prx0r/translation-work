## Pass A — S7: the anvaya-vyatireka inference block (chunk line 37)

### L1 (controlled; existing pilot output, condensed)
> "of the two, the blue, that which is not manifesting while the anvaya and vyatireka both exist,
> simultaneous with the manifestation, that very one is the cognition-cause; the two [anvaya and
> vyatireka] are present in the blue; that, from the cognition, existed before alone; but not in the
> two-moon — this he says [with] 'thus.'"

### L2 (READ prose)
Abhinavagupta now takes the case of the blue. Of the blue's presence and absence [anvaya and
vyatireka], that which is *not* manifest at the moment of the perception, but is simultaneous with
the manifesting, is what produces the cognition. Both the presence and the absence are in fact
present in the blue: the blue is what is cognized, and so it existed even before the cognition arose.
The same is not true of the two-moon: with the double-moon the presence and absence do not track the
cognition in this way. This, he says, is what the word "thus" (tathā) points to.

**L1→L2 link:** L37 T(anvayavyatirekayor)…T(tathā iti). The compressed inference (anvaya-vyatireka,
jñānakāraṇa, pūrvam abhūt eva) is unpacked into readable causal prose.

**A1 delta (SUPPLIED):** the phrase "produces the cognition" unpacks the compressed "cognition-cause"
(jñānakāraṇa); "both the presence and the absence are in fact present in the blue" makes explicit the
anvaya-vyatireka point that the compressed L1 states. Neither adds new doctrine — both are recoverable
from the source line. Status: SUPPLIED / CONSTRAINED.

**AQ delta:** this paragraph is an `INFERENCE_BLOCK` — dense formal-inference prose. Logged so a
reviewer double-checks the anvaya-vyatireka logic, but no lexical crux.

## Pass B — S9: the dāntava/līvarda / cow-bull passage (chunk line 51)

### L1 (controlled; existing pilot output, condensed)
> "For with the 'dāntava'-'līvarda'-and-the-rest words alone the Ārya-worldly [people] declare the
> bulls; but with the 'cow'-word the female-cows alone — thus alone the cognition. In 'the cow-
> possessing this city', when said, indeed, the curd-milk-prosperity alone manifests."

### L2 (READ prose)
The point is about how the world actually uses words. In ordinary Ārya usage, the words of the
"dāntava"/"līvarda" kind — whatever exactly they denote — are what the world uses to speak of bulls
[the male of the class]; whereas the single word "cow" (go) refers to the female of the class. This
is how the cognition actually runs. So too, when someone says "this city has cows" (gomad idaṃ
nagaram), what flashes in the mind is simply the city's abundance of milk and curds — not any claim
about the sex of the animals.

**L1→L2 link:** L51 T(dāntavalīvardādiśabdaireva)…T(nirbhāti).

**A1 delta (SUPPLIED):** "[the male of the class]" is supplied from the contrast with "female-cows"
(strī-gavīḥ) — recoverable from the same source line; the point is the cow-word/bull-words contrast.
Status: SUPPLIED / CONSTRAINED.

**AQ delta:** the lexemes **dāntava** and **līvarda** are `TECHNICAL_MEASURE_OR_REALIA` /
`UNRESOLVED_QUOTATION`: the *argument* (some words pick out the male, "cow" the female) is preserved,
but the specific referents presuppose an agrarian/lexicographical knowledge system. **L2 does not
invent their meaning** — it says "whatever exactly they denote" and logs the gap. This is the correct
behavior.

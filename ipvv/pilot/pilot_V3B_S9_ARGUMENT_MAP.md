# IPVV — V3-B S9 ARGUMENT MAP (the "dāntava/līvarda" cow-bull passage)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00022 lines 1139–1159
(IPK 2.2.1 commentary, the saṃvṛtisatya / sāmānya section). This is the passage that read as
"'dāntava'-'līvarda'-and-the-rest words alone the Ārya-worldly declare the bulls."*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Abhinavagupta has been distinguishing the *conventionally-true* (saṃvṛtisatya) from the *untrue*
(asatya). The objector (Buddhist/Naiyāyika) tried to collapse the conventional into the merely
fictional — like the double-moon — by saying: "the convention-mind covers [reality], and what it
covers is only what the mind itself raises; never is the actually-seen thing attributed to the
universal." Abhinava refuted that. Now he must say what *positively* the conventional-true consists
in — and in particular, what role the universal (sāmānya) plays in it. The cow/bull business is his
example of how the universal is a *conventional* (saṃvṛti) entity, not a fiction.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — recap (from prior lines 1139–1149):**
The objector: "the convention-mind is a *coverer*: it attributes to an object what was never actually
seen, such as the universal." Abhinava's reply: "not even so" (nāpi). For the double-moon's twoness is
*seen* (dṛṣṭatā) — and yet the same *seen-ness* belongs equally to any one of many objects. So by this
much, delusion-ness has no extra specific character. And then: even *delusion-specific-ness* is not
accepted by the text-author — he declares [the pair] with a **dvandva** (coordinate compound). [The
point: "convention-and-delusion" are named as a coordinate pair, implying they are *equal-form* (tulya-
svarūpa) — i.e. the conventional is not a sub-species of delusion but something coordinate with it.]
In the simultaneous-support-ness [of two equal things] there is a dvandva; and a dvandva is of the
equal-own-form two alone. The universal (sāmānya), without swallowing all the specifics, operating,
possessing even another specific, wishing the intended specific, crooking [it] toward itself — not
separately obtaining a word-entry — [this is what the universal is].

**Step 1 — the world's actual usage (the cow/bull point, lines 1150–1153):**
The proof is *direct* (āñjasyena), by the clear world-cognition. **Here is the example:** In the
Ārya-world's actual usage, the words "dāntava," "līvarda," and the like are what ordinary people use
to *point at bulls* (puṅgavān) — the male of the class. But the single word "cow" (go) refers, in the
same usage, to the female-cows (strī-gavīḥ) alone. So it is with the cognition. [i.e. the word
"cow"/"go" conventionally picks out a *specific* sub-class — the females — while the "bull"-words pick
out the males; neither is a bare universal floating free of specifics.] And when someone says "this
city has cows" (gomad idaṃ nagaram), what flashes in the mind is simply the *abundance of milk and
curds* — not any claim about the sex or a free-floating "cow-ness."

**Step 2 — the marker of the conventional (lines 1156–1158):**
"The conventional-ness": the marker of the conventional is *this much and no more* (etāvanmātram) —
and even the opponent, unwilling, must accept this much. That marker, along with the marked word, he
indicates with "that-which" (yattathā).

**Step 3 — why this wins (lines 1158–1161):**
In this marker, with the *manifestation* and the *use* [the two criteria of the earlier discussion]
removed, in the non-possibility, **our victory** (asmākaṃ jayaḥ). If, again, in the non-possibility,
the *use* is scattered in the other [i.e. the opponent's sense of use fails], then — with this
intent — he first states the *other's expectation* (parāpekṣāṃ), "objection (nanu)."

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. **dāntava / līvarda** — the lexemes. Argument: they're male-class (bull) words in contrast to
   "cow" (female). Exact referents/etymology unknown (likely regionally-marked agrarian terms for
   castrated/male bovines). NOT resolved; the *argument* (some words pick the male, "cow" the female)
   is clear and preserved.
2. The exact force of the dvandva point: "convention-and-delusion" as coordinate (tulyasvarūpa) vs.
   the objector's subordination — interpreted here as Abhinava denying the conventional is a
   sub-species of delusion. Worth a second check against the actual dvandva in the text.
3. "gomad idaṃ nagaram" — is this Abhinava's example or a quoted usage? Read as his own example of
   the word's practical referent (milk-and-curds), i.e. the conventional's *arthakriyā*.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The point is now understood: the universal/cow is a *conventional* entity whose whole reality
is "this much" — the practical, world-bound tracking of a sub-class — not a fiction and not a bare
Platonic universal. The world's own usage (bull-words vs. cow-word) proves the universal is always
anchored in specifics. The cow-possessing-city example shows the conventional's "meaning" is its
practical upshot (milk, curds). Unresolved lexemes logged, not resolved.

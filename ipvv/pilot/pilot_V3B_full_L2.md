# V3-B — the second reflexive-awareness: distinction-and-non-distinction

## Kārikā 1 — the action-cognitions are true, by stability and efficacy

The cognitions of action, relation, universal, substance, direction and time have a substrate that is
at once one and many, and they are to be accepted as true — true by their stability and true by their
efficacy.

Abhinavagupta begins by considering the cognition of action in particular. A cognition of action
that arises apart from the very essence of consciousness is an appearance of action: it is of a form
both one and many, it is cut off by the power of māyā, and it takes the essence of action as its
object. Such a cognition is grounded in the true alone. For it is stable — no refutation uproots it —
and it agrees with what subsequently follows: in the case intended, the attainment of the village is
precisely the end that this cognition helps bring about. A real entity, in producing its effect,
takes on both difference and non-difference, and it is the bearer of this agreement with what follows.

In that situation, what is the "form" of the thing? The form is that which is the thing's own
nature, unswerving from it. And the "action" is what, by the force of its appearance, makes even what
is not its own nature take on its own nature — that which clings to the form and shapes it.

Here is the example Abhinavagupta gives. The body of Caitra — and of Maitra — although it differs
according to this or that place, time and shape, nevertheless shines forth without ever abandoning
its oneness, in the recognition "this is the very same body." That very unity is the action, in the
judgment "Caitra is walking." And in an action that has thus become established, the act of grasping
"there goes Caitra" is experienced as continuing — nothing uproots it — and it serves that action in
the intended attainment of the village.

Abhinavagupta then draws the decisive line. He holds firmly that it is being-manifest, and nothing
else, that constitutes the form of things — not the doing of an effect. It is the doing of an effect
that he now examines, at length, in what follows. He asks whether this doing-of-an-effect is to be
identified with the thing's own nature — whether by identity and by the absence of deviation, or by
deviation from a common dharma; whether it is the thing's very form, or its defining mark, or a mere
incidental indicator standing near a mark. He settles: "own-form" is spoken here only metaphorically.
For just as "having branches" is what we point to when we speak of tree-ness — a mere exclusion,
since the words are synonyms — so in the world it is not said that the unreal does the effect; that
would not be known, because of the synonymy. And it is not the defining mark either, as he shows by
adding "and it is marked."

## Kārikā 2 — the one inner essence enters the many

An objector interjects. "Still," he says, "your reason is not established: the specific condition of
'not being refuted' is impossible for it. For you have declared that the substances and the rest are
one-and-many in nature — the very same thing is both one and many. How is that a refutation? This is
in fact your own objection, raised to be refuted."

To this Abhinavagupta replies:

> There, the one — the inner essence — attaining the organ-known-ness,
> goes into the many-ness, by its place-time-own-nature.

Consider the action, and the rest. Once their truth is established, the one-ness and many-ness spoken
of — even when subordinated in the Vṛtti — are to be established here as the primary thing to be
known. Reflecting on this, Abhinavagupta makes the determination. Between the one-ness and the
many-ness, he takes the one-ness: "this way it is established; and the many-ness, this way." Of the
blue and the other beings, the inner essence is the supreme essence, marked by consciousness alone.
There is no counting there, because of its dense non-difference — not even of its one-ness, since
that one-ness, there, has no separate being. But what is inner — unmixed with other appearances, going
within — and inner, known by the inner organ alone — that is the one essence. That one, again, without
losing its one-ness, attains its being-known by the external organ, and by the distinction of place
and the rest — "here and there, now and then, thin and not-thin" — it goes into the many-ness. There
is no contradiction in this, because the cognition is not broken. It is like the relation of cause and
non-cause: the distinction of objects can be spoken of only insofar as we are dealing with their mere
essential nature. Hence there is no contradiction at all between the one-ness and the many-ness;
"Caitra moves," in the action; and so in the remaining cases.
## Kārikā 3 — the mind-re-determination makes the conceptions

Another objection. "From the distinction of objects you say 'one, many.' By what is this to be
grasped? Take the case of the eye. With the eye-experience, first of all, Caitra manifests as divided
by the distinction of place alone; this cognition has no capacity to mix the appearance-distinctions
together, because it does not investigate. So they say. But with the construction — the mental
operation — the object is not truly touched at all."

To this the answer is given in the sūtra:

> These cognitions, supporting the two [one and many], the mind re-determining, being,
> make the conceptions of action and the rest — conceptions made of the knower's operation.

Here, although even in the un-constructed perception — in the mere manifestation of "it is," or of
"it is a jar" — the universal and the rest have their appearance, in the extended grasping, and even,
for the yogin, the action-manifestation and the time-manifestation could be spoken of; still, in that
state, the universal and the rest do not attain their own life. For the "life" of the appearances of
the universal, the relation, the action, the substance and the direction is this: the staining by the
universal; the rising of the distinction among the things related; the grasping of the unity of the
streaming moments; the making of the part-mass; the manifestation of the limited in the limit. And
this life is clearly manifested only in the construction. And the construction of the mind is
manifested in the re-determination — at the occasion of the māyā-bound knower. By that mind, acting as
the agent, making the re-determination — the reflective awareness that follows the direct
manifestation of an object — the action, the relation and the rest become its object.

As will be said, in the one-utterance-object, the cognition is such-and-such. The prefix "anu-"
("re-"), Abhinavagupta explains, means "after" (paścāt): "the mind alone makes the conceptions."
This is the connection. The sentence runs: "of that, its own form is specifically marked, 'the
instrument,' to the end; the two participial endings, however, carry the seed of a reason, in the
making of the conception." Between which two does the mind stand, in the Vṛtti? Having doubted, he
shows: "of the grasped and the grasper," and "external and the rest," "of that, to the end," with the
sentence. "With the knower together, the distinction and the relation rest in their absence from one
another, and cannot be dealt with — of which two." "Of that," of the knower. Here, the reason, he
gives: "of the knower." The knower — determining the object, the external and the inner, the
to-be-constructed, with varied joinings — that very object is said to be the action, the universal
and the rest. "He deals" — he makes the joining of the Vṛtti, "the mental-ness, and the rest." "The
reason" — the remainder of the three sentences.

An objection: "Who, then, is another knower…" — answered, as will be said, by the verse "if not, the
inner-made…" (1.3.7), and the rest. "The impression-born": for the impression indeed culminates in
the unity of the knower, as is said. Hence, as the Buddhists think of the experience-impression-born
cognition, so here it does not become the case — this is to be thought. "And in the eye and the rest,"
to the end, "the resting-ness, to the end" — the intention has been explained at the very beginning of
the sūtra-explanation. And here, with the grasping of the "universal," everything, the relation and
the rest, is marked by it. "The universal-form even": the un-clear-more, is the meaning. "The
universal is said": not "the universal," they are dealt with. Here, to give room to the reason — "of
them, and the rest" — and to the to-be-said, for the manifestation of the universal's own form, he
says: "of the specific-ones, following, to the end." Another objection: "Here, at this occasion, he —
the one-alone-appearance — what is to be said? Not, first, 'the universal,' which you alone
declared." Answer: "thus alone, the being-jar…" (2.3.5), in this.

Now, how is it said that "in the mixing with other appearances alone is the object-action"? Here, the
answer is: "place, and the rest." Here, now: "the 'is,' i.e. the being-manifestation, the mere
thus-ness, has become the own-marker" — with the appearances of form, blue, and the rest, which are
the external limb, and which in the own-marker-ness are no helpers, what deed is there? Without the
two appearances of place and time, not even with a thousand appearances, in the mixing, does the
own-marker-ness come to an end: "the blue, the cloth, the extended, the cotton, the soft, the thin,"
and so on, from the flashing of the mere universal-form. But "this-that" — the mixing of the
appearances of place and time — removes the universal-form. Hence that alone is the inner limb, in the
coming-to-be of own-marker-ness. This very point he establishes: "of the two alone," of the two, place
and time, in their form of east and west, and the rest. The place is marked by the far and the near;
the variety of the prior and the later…

The point about usage. With the camasa cup they bring the rice; he sprinkles the ṣoḍaśin; he takes
it — and the rest. Even so, the word is not able to make known the specific, the present-
manifestation. The meaning that is perceived from the word, without the help of a pointing finger and
the rest, is perceived as the place-time-un-cut alone — this is the meaning that is established
without effort. And this will be so in the valid-means-marker. With this much, the
re-determination-operation alone revives the essence of the universal, the action, and the rest. That
which is current, that he determines and summarizes: "thus, and." "In this," where the own-marker and
universal-form are fit for reflection, such a case. "Not then," at the time of the mental
determination. "The universal," in this very word — the cut. Here, the reason: "place and the rest";
the connection even, "then"; the action even, "then" — the connection. The other sentence is of the
form of a reason. "The said," "the mind-re-determining," "the conceptions," and — "will be said":
"… the constructions support the two" (2.2.5).

This is the third verse. Thus, with this much, in the convention, in the construction-mind, of the
flashing universal and the rest: the conventional being-ness, the name — the being-ness-specific
alone — stands; not, as in the case of the double-moon and the rest, the un-truth — this is
established.

Here is how the matter is brought into order. Of consciousness — marked by the reflective awareness of
freedom, and the unfolded effect of the action-power — that which is the relation, the universal, and
the rest. There even, the relation-form alone — marked by the many-one form — is, everywhere, the
essential form. There, though, with the joining of the un-common other form, there is the other name.
"Of the many-individual-mass, he who is the unity-own, the relation — there, 'cows,' with the
tolerance of the other designation." There, the universal-speech, in the flow of many moments, from
the strength of its support, becomes one; "moves," with the strength of the designation, is the
action-dealing; "of the threads-one-ness, 'this is the cloth,'" the substance-dealing; "of the
childhood-and-the-rest-states-one-ness," the state-possessor-dealing. Thus elsewhere.

> the relation whose self is distinction-and-non-distinction, established in all objects;
> the world-path… (ślo. 1)

This is said in the establishment of the relation. Even by the holders of divided universals, of the
inherence, of the relation, "it helps" — "the relation," by the rule, with the relation, or, of the
helped, from the acceptance. And the relation, made of the one-many, having the freedom of the knower
as its self, by the glory of the action-power. Hence the relation alone is the root in the
establishment of the unity of the knower:

> "… in the relation, the attentiveness" (vi. bhai. 106)

of the statement. That very point, to investigate, Abhinavagupta states the sūtra:

## Kārikā 4 — the relation is the mutual form-clinging in the one knower

> The beings, resting in the self, distinct-manifestation, in the one knower,
> joined by a unity that is the form of mutual relation — these are the word of the
> relation-cognition.

"The king" and "the person," because they are distinct, are manifested as external. Hence, when they
partake of unity, in the one knower, in the completion, in that very form, in the own self alone. And
that unity is not a bare unity — not the melting of the own-form — because the relation would then be
impossible; but it is the mutual clinging of forms, the simultaneous drowning and emerging, the two
phases of distinction-and-non-distinction, of the form of a swinging machine — this is the mark of the
relation. And that too is not exclusively with the knower; but mutually — "of the king, the person,"
or, of that, "the king." Merely, in the attainment of that-being, the touch of consciousness of these
is accepted as a mere means, like the fire that, in the mingling of the gold-drop, does not at all
break its own form. The two, being joined with a unity of that sort — the two beings — are the word,
and the object, of the relation-cognition "of the king, the person," and so on. Everywhere, the
relation is two-resting-alone:

> "with that self alone, of that, would be — how — with the breath, the control" (ślo. 21)

This is it. Thence, even in the māyā-bound standing, in the distinction, by the glory of
manifestation, the non-distinction does not deviate. "One-one": he alone is the operation of the
māyā-bound knower, by which that being-two, of the relation-form, is created, and manifested, and
reflected. "The distinct": from the prior, of the relation-less state, the distinct-marker — the
meaning. Thence alone, with the operation of the construction, connected with the knower, from its
being unveiled, with the known-ness by the inner organ, from its un-common-ness, with the distinction
of knowers, of the being, in the variety of the relation, the relation-state, and, becoming, is not
flawed. "Somewhere, in the appearance-part indeed, the param-Lord, sometimes, mixes the knowers, and
he unjoins the others, and the rest" — this is said many times.

An objection: "The mere made-ness of distinction-and-non-distinction is the mark of the relation,
with the primary-and-secondary quality, of the two related…"
## Kārikā 5 — the class and substance manifestations are one-form outside too

Continuing the investigation of the relation, Abhinavagupta now turns to the class and substance
appearances. Even granted that the relation is a mixture of unity and non-unity, the class-
appearances have their own distinctive character; and to state the further distinction he introduces
the sūtra:

> Of the class-substance-appearances, the one-form obtains outside as well;
> and the constructions also grasp the distinction among individuals and their parts.

Consider the class-appearances — the appearances, that is, whose essence is appearance itself, the
classes. The constructions that grasp them and reflect on them — the construction-minds — do not
merely take up the unity within: they also set up the mere non-unity outside. Insofar as, outside as
well, by the one-cow-ness running through them, the unity — that which grounds the use of the single
nominal word and helps the use of the plural word — and the distinction, which belongs to the
individual and necessarily supports the grasping of the individual: these constructions grasp,
manifest and reflect all this in the form "these cows," facing outward, taking the non-difference as
the qualifier and the difference as the qualified, in the very act of cognition. So too with the
substance-appearances: the constructions, outside as well, take up the one-form and the distinction of
parts — "jar," as the un-knotted one, with its extended-ness, of the many-part…

> "of the other self, that which is the self, is manifested as that-self-like" (vā. pa. 3.3.9)

This is it. "Thence alone" — that is, because the one-many form is not manifested in the eye-seen in
this way — is said at length; "thence from the reason," is the sense. "Would be," with the reason,
is conceivable: first, from the present-object-appearance of the eye-seen, of the universal and the
part-possessor and the rest, with its all-common-ness and clarity, even then from its presence. When
the sage Kaṇāda says:

> "from the inherence in form-substance, the eye-seen, the universals" (4.1.11)

This is it. "From the un-manifestation," taking the distinction-own-form-marker as primary, from the
resting of the cognition — the sense. "Caitra." Although even the jar and the rest are agreed to be
the part-possessor, and thus he will say "from the other sesame, and the rest"; still, "this-I, this
much, I go, I stand" — this was said to show that the recollection of oneness has no deviation, in any
manner, in the living body. "The jar indeed, like the house, the chariot, the village, the army and
the rest, even being of an aggregate nature, could be the doer of a single effect" — this is the
conceivability. "The childhood and the other states": not here is the continuity of the states
intended, in the part-possessor-mind, since that would be no help; but "this-much-I, this now, from
the prior form, with this much, the distinct-extended, the un-knotted" — here is the intention.
Thence alone, with the divided, the part-p[ossessor]…

Then, with the 'ḍatar'-ending reading and the 'ḍa' substitution, "one-of-them." "The cognizer": the
utterer and the cognizer are both intended here. "The doing of the cognition, in the utterer; the
teaching of the cognition, in the cognizer; the bringing of the cognition." This is what is said: "by
the utterer, beginning with the black [cow], when the cow is present in the heart and the word is
applied, then, even with the white cow in view, if the cognizer brings the cow, or even the kapila-
colored one — both count as makers." If that un-fixedness did not hold, how would this work? And from
the un-fixedness, the cognition of all the own-markers is established without effort. "Where," in the
chariot-appearance. "Immediately alone," by the rule of the king-and-person relation. "With the
conjunction," with the non-inherence-cause-ness, being manifested, with…

> "from the far, the one-dense, the forest"

like-that. Of the many, in the simultaneous grasping, with the interior not grasped, this-way alone
the appearance fits, from fitness, "the coarse, the one-dense"; not-in-truth, otherwise. Thence, in
the appearance, of the external, this is the dharma, manifesting; or, of the appearance alone, in the
with-form-consciousness view, of the cognition-form-marker, this is the dharma; not of the external —
this is it. This thus-appearance, "the part-possessor-appearance," is not fit to say. Thus, the
perception — the valid-means — which the other side showed, in the part-possessor-establishment:

> "the all-non-grasping, from the part-possessor-establishment" (nyā. 2.1.34)

with the sūtra, that is rejected. Now:

> "from the holding-drawing-fitness, and too" (nyā. 2.1.35)

This is it; that which was said by inference, that too he rejects, making the reason otherwise-
established: "the atom-aggregate, and the rest." "From the variety of natures," of the jar and the
rest, those which are the atoms, of them, with the strength of their own cause, with the that-being-
following, from the birth; and of the jar and the rest, those which are the enjoyers, their actions,
there, the superintending-condition. The 'vā'-word, in the aggregation. And this, even by the part-
possessor-holder, of the conjunctions, of the non-inherence-causes, for the protection, in the wind-
conjunction-and-the-rest, is of-necessity-accepted as the cause, in the non-production of the rise-
division-beginning-action. Thus, the establishing valid-means having been rejected, he shows the
refuting [means] in the part-possessor: "one, and the rest." This is the own-form-come, the refuter.
Even through the holding-quality-prior-starting-dharma-face, he addresses him: "the two-atom, and the
rest." Thus alone, the thread-gone…

With the holding, with the excess of the balance, in the cloth, the more-more of the balance, the
bending would be. Here, in the prior view, the counter-measure is said. There, in brief, first, all
this he refutes: "would-be, and the rest," "the objects, to the end." "This is the flaw" — the
establishing-non-being, the refuting-being, the form. Now, this very point he makes clear: "thus,
and," in this brief, the standing — the meaning. "And by the other, 'of the atoms, the aggregate
alone, this-way, shines'" — that which is said; there, in the aggregate-appearance, the famous, that
which is the divided-appearance-ness, the interior-non-grasping-raised-conjunction, the name, from the
co-helper alone, from the pervasion-ness, the one-juice-distinction-emerging, the pervading; that he
shows: "the mass." That non-apprehension, in the current, he fits: "this-which-is-the-Caitra-
appearance, and the rest." Objection: "if, the one alone…"

## Kārikā 6 — the kāraka-conjunction is the action-reflexive-awareness-object

Even in the action-adjunct relation, the distinction and the non-distinction are reflected upon, with
the sūtra; and, along with that, the own-form of the direction and the rest:

> The conjunction of the adjuncts has the action-reflexive-awareness as its object;
> the direction-and-the-rest cognition is supported by the relation of the limited to the limit.

"The not-elsewhere-being-marker, the cause-ness" — here, this is the object-meaning. In the Vṛtti-
sentence too, thus. "The adjunct": the substance, and the power. "This is the joining: of the power-
supporting, of the substances, that which is the conjunction — he, with the joining-glue-place, is the
cause of the action-recollection, as, of the valid-means-known two, the cognition-cause; and the
cognition, the knower-rest-ness" — this is it, in the agent, in the consciousness-form, that which is
the un-distinction-attainment, of the divided even, of the adjuncts, he alone is the conjunction.
Thus, of the wood, the pot and the rest, in the cooking-agent, is to be said. Thus, of the adjunct-
powers even, the conjunction, ending in the agent-rest-alone, with the action-face, from its
unfolding; and of the action, in the agent, from the rest. Thus, of the action, with the own-marker-
expectation and with the first-adjunct-expectation, that which is the distinction; and that which is,
with the one-agent-rest, with the result-un…

> "the action-relation…" (2.2.1)

and the rest. There, of the action alone, first, establishing the distinction and the non-distinction
two, with the adjunct-face, he states the distinction, first: "of the adjuncts, and the rest." "With
the that-form-stain-entry, the action is manifested" — this is it, the distinction-form-ness, of this.
Thus, he states, in the adjunct-vārtika:

> "the divided, of the cooking, the own-form, with the adjunct-distinction, of that-mind"

This is it, of the action-mind. Now, the non-distinction even, he states: "and 'cooks,'" with the
'ca'-word, aggregating the non-distinction together with the distinction. "'Cooks,'" there, in the
recollection: of the action-mind, the un-distinction-object-ness; since, this-form, the cooking-
freedom-made, he who is the agent, with the that-one-possessed-ness, she, the action, with that mind,
is objectified. And of that, of the agent, the one-form-ness, is the param-essence. This is what is
said: "with the woods, with the pot, the rice-gruel, 'Caitra cooks'" — the sentence-known meaning;
she, in-truth, is the action. Thence-alone, "the verb-word, the sentence," is said, by this honorable-
one. And there, the wood-gone burning-and-the-rest; the pot-gone heating-and-the-rest; the rice-grain-
gone splitting-and-the-rest — that which is, that, "the cooking-form, thus," with the distinction, in
the recollection, flashes even; in the Caitra-consciousness-freedom-rest, the un-distinction alone
ends. This known-object he summarizes: "the action-mind even." Not alone the relation-mind is the
many-object — the 'api'. Objection: "previously-alone…"

> "the with-order-ness, and, of the worldly, of the action…" (2.1.2)

This is it. "The true; this, though, otherwise, of the distinction, the determination." Then he says:
"not-alone." "From the agent-unity, and," — not alone, from the result-unity, the meaning. Thus, the
action, the one-many-form, having established, of the Vṛtti-sentence, "the mind, to the end," of the
that-explained, of the verse-half, the current meaning, with the intention, he explains: "there," in
the Vṛtti-sentence, in the verse-half. "The adjunct-ness-mind even, that which is, she, of the divided
even, of the adjunct-substances, becoming, the one-object even, the many-object" — "in the adjunct-
ness, the mind," this, here, is said. Of the action-adjunct-ness-non-being-cognition, and of the
remainder-relation-cognition, in the one-many-object-ness, in the equal, the distinction-ness, he
states: "of the king," "the cognition, the meaning." Objection: "in the remainder-relation even, the
action-adjunct-prior-ness is said," having doubted…

> "not, without the number, anyone, the being-become, the meaning, is declared"

This is it. There, though, of the nominal-meaning, the number-appearance attains the adjunct-being.
For of the case-ending-meaning, this is the own-nature: that which is, in the nominal-meaning, the
quality-becoming. Thence, with the 'one'-and-the-rest words, the mutual-cut-appearance, with the
primary-ness, is recollected. And by that, the object-form alone, the number-and-its-distinction-own.
The number, though, the distinction-counting-own, is said. "The measure even":

> "the atom, the great, the short, the long, and"

This is it, that which is the measure, of the Kāṇādas, the famous; there, the short-long, this first:

> "the length, though, the measure, would be…"

the measure-marker-attained. And the measure, the name, is the cut-other-object-expecting; with the
twenty-four-digit-ness, the famous, he who is the hand-appearance, with him, he who is the
comparison-appearance, not, outside, the conjunction-mere; that. Thence-alone, he says: "the measure
even," with the inner-unity-part, of the to-be-measured, of the cloth, with the expecting, with the
hand, from the conjunction-attainment — the yard-stick-taking, for the raising of the short-long-
distinction. "The atom-great, however," — that which is said; there, the atom-ness, if the part-less-
ness-mere, that, of the quality-action-and-the-rest, of the part-less, would be. Now, of the
substance, the part-less-ness — the atom-ness; then, of the space even, would be. Thence, with the
very-subtle-ness, with the famous, from the mustard-pod-gram-box-small-box, the lightest-alone, that
which is, from all sides, the cut-fit-ness, that very is the atom-ness; the other, the great-ness…

> "… the measure, though, from all sides"

with this, it is grasped. Then he says: "the prastha-kudava," of the to-be-measured, of the sesame-
and-the-rest, with the expecting, with the wood-cup-like, everywhere, the measure-comparison-
appearance-marker, that which is the measure; not-in-truth, the conjunction-mere; that even, with the
inner-unity-part. And that which is the unmāna, the name, "the upward-measure," is marked; that, by
the Kāṇādas, the weight-alone, the falling-action-maker, is said; not-in-truth, thus, not, it becomes,
in the weight-mere, the measure-dealing-non-being; but, the famous, that which is, with the moment-
and-the-rest, the weight; that-comparison alone, the unmāna, is fit — the grammarians. And thus, the
measure-ness, the name, in the three, is the same. "The extended, the around, with the upward-ness,
or," the measure, here, with the measure-form-ness, the specific. This very, and, is fit he says:
"the māṣaka-rakitikā." Of the to-be-measured, of the gold…

## Kārikā 7 — the object-attainment only through the distinction-un-distinction meaning

Here, "the being-ness-relation, the being-ness," is struck by the under-extension, the regress, the
purposelessness; "the object-action, however," is the different-locus; "the that-making-ness" is not
the always; with the cognition-birth, "the thus-ness, the always, even," is not fit, like the sprout,
of the seed, in the own-cognition-born, the un-wished, with the anvaya-vyatireka-grasping-non-being,
from the cause-ness-non-determination; "the object-action-making-fit-ness, in the true-false two, is
hard-to-determine; with the other object-action, the regress; and of all, of this, in the
non-manifestation, the hare's-horn-similarity" — this is it. "The being-manifested-ness alone, the
un-uprooted-reflexive-awareness, is the being-ness. And she, of the relation-and-the-rest, the
un-alarming-alone." In the standing even, if anyone would say, "from the hard-to-grasp: the world,
first, desires the object-action; she, what is, of the relation-and-the-rest?" — of that objector,
the heart, he comforts:

> Thus indeed the object-attainment would be, of the object-action-desiring mother;
> with the distinction-un-distinction-possessing meaning, by that, not the delusion, this-form.

"If the true you perceive: that, with the distinction-un-distinction-made, with the relation, without,
not any, in the dealing-ground, is the object-action. Where even he is not cognized, there even, of-
necessity, the inner-flashing alone sits." For: the pleasure-and-the-rest, desiring, this way he
would desire, if he would know that; and thus he would know, if that-very, that, would be; and still,
the un-desirable would be, of that, from the accomplishment. Thence, this is the fallen, the
un-crossable: "that which is, of the that-not-that-form two, the distinction-un-distinction-made-ness,
the relation/universal-and-the-rest-form." And this, if the untrue; what-now, the true, is said? By
that, whoever here does the delusion-dealing, he alone is the other, the deluded. "That," in the
grasping-sūtra two, the stability-use two, are fit-alone — the sūtra-meaning.

Īśvarapratyabhijñāvivṛtivimarśinī, in the action-section, the distinction-un-distinction-reflexive-

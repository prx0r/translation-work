# IPVV — V3-C ARGUMENT MAP (the pramāṇavimarśa: what the valid-means is, kārikās 1–2)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00022 lines 2055–2149
(the kriyādhikāra, tṛtīyo vimarśaḥ = the pramāṇavimarśa, the valid-means reflexive-awareness).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** What is a valid means of cognition (pramāṇa), and what is its result (pramiti /
phala)? The whole vimarśa (17 sūtras) is devoted to the *relation* (sambandha) between the known and
the knower. Abhinava opens it by asking what counts as a pramāṇa and what it produces.

The frame (lines 2062–2086): the relation is of two kinds — (a) the known/known-relation
(jñāpya-jñāpaka), resting in the inert-and-conscious, and (b) the cause/effect relation, resting in
the inert. The first is the māna/neya (measure/measured) relation; all establishment of things depends
on the pramāṇa. So this vimarśa determines the pramāṇa/prameya dealing, in 17 sūtras: two (kārikās 1–2)
on the nature of the pramāṇa and its fruit; ten on the pramāṇa's operation per-appearance (not resting
in the own-marker thing alone); one on erroneous cognition; one on the prameya in the īśvara-view;
three on the pramāṇa/prameya/fruit division and the vyavahāra.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Kārikā 1 (lines 2094–2095):**
> The object, through whose force "this, of-this-form, thus" it stands — that is the pramāṇa; and it,
> too, is self-appearance (svābhāsa), a new rise (abhinavodaya).

**Kārikā 2 (lines 2096–2097):**
> It, the inner-thus-reflexive-awareness-own (antaḥ-stathā-vimarśātmā), in the place-time-and-the-rest
> un-divided — in the one-utterance-object, the cognition (miti) is the un-broken (abādhitā) cognition
> of the thing.

**The argument (lines 2099–2149):**
1. **What a pramāṇa is, in the world's ordinary sense:** the external object (the blue) and the inner
   (pleasure), *by whose force* the cognition stands firm within its fixed limit — as "this" (by its
   own form) and "of-this-form" (by some other qualifying dharma, e.g. eternality). That is the
   pramāṇa, as commonly said.
2. **But then the discriminator reflects:** the object does not stand by its own power alone — for
   then the blue would not appear *to me* rather than *to Caitra*. So it stands by *another's* power.
3. **That "other" must be conscious:** if it were inert, it would be like a blind man showing the way
   to a blind man. So the other is of the nature of consciousness (saṃvidrūpa).
4. **But mere consciousness is not enough:** an un-differentiated consciousness could not set apart
   blue from yellow. So it is the consciousness *tinted by the blue*, turned toward the blue, shining
   as the blue — that appearance (ābhāsa) is what establishes the blue.
5. **The appearance is of a *distinct* blue:** if the blue were not distinct from consciousness, then
   the same consciousness would have to be yellow too (since all is identical with consciousness).
   So the appearance is the shining of a *distinct* blue; and though in param-essence the blue is not
   distinct from the great light (prakāśa), the distinction *appears*. 
6. **The whole creation is one power:** the arising of this is that *the contraction by the
   void-and-the-rest upādhis (śūnyādyupādhi-saṅkoca)* of the light (prakāśa) — *that alone, by the one
   creation-power, is the illumination of knower, pramāṇa, and known* (pramātṛ-pramāṇa-prameya).
   (Cites siddhapāda: "the one light rises, born of pramāṇa, object and knower.")
7. **The pramāṇa = the appearance, in the finite knower:** in the māyā-knower — contracted by the
   body-and-the-rest upādhis, with the inner-facing experience — that appearance, made distinct as it
   were, turned to the external known, *as long as it operates*, is tinted by the place-and-time of
   the known, changing moment to moment, a new-new rise; but in the *same* one, the one word-said, the
   same one that spans infinite time, related to the knower, my blue-appearance — that alone is the
   pramāṇa (since that appearance is operative in producing the cognition).
8. **What is the cognition (pramiti)?** "What, then, is the pramā?" — Answer: *that very appearance,
   of the nature of awareness (bodhātmā), is the cognition (miti), the fruit of the pramāṇa.* These
   are not synonyms (paryāya): the pramāṇa is the appearance turned outward, of the nature of light;
   the fruit is the same appearance turned inward, of the nature of reflexive-awareness (vimarśa),
   contracted by the tint of the object, in the form of the word. In the one thing, the cause and the
   fruit are both determined by the discriminator; so too in the blue-light-and-its-reflexive-
   awareness. (Cites pā. vā. 3.308: "by its force, from its standing…")
9. **Why the fruit is the reflexive-awareness:** the operation (vyāpāra) is indeed the fruit, since
   the instrument's first object is the operation; and the operation is non-different from the agent
   or instrument being operated. So in the primary sense, pramāṇa and fruit are *non-different*; the
   *further* fruit (the purpose, the activity, the attainment) may be other. Hence **the reflexive-
   awareness (vimarśa) IS the fruit**; and, by its force, the *awareness* (bodha) is the pramāṇa. The
   reflexive-awareness needs the word (śabda) and rests in the general-form-appearance, unmarked by
   the place-time-distinction-appearances, as its "life." Even "this" (ayam) says only "what is
   present, and before." Enough: "Caitra stands here now" — so far as it is within the word's reach,
   this is the path. But by the force of the speaker's own-perception-like own-marker-ness of the
   thing, there is resting in the specific. So the pramāṇa's operation is *on the appearance merely*
   (ābhāsamātre).

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The pā. vā. 3.308 and siddhapāda quotes anchor the pramāṇa/fruit relation; their exact force is
   cited, not re-derived here.
2. The "śūnyādyupādhi-saṅkoca" — the contraction by the void-and-the-rest limiting conditions — is
   the load-bearing mechanism; rendered as such, but the full list of upādhis (beyond śūnya/deha/prāṇa)
   is not enumerated in this excerpt.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the pramāṇa is the appearance of a distinct object in
consciousness, standing firm by its own (conscious) power; its fruit is the reflexive-awareness of
that same appearance, turned inward — pramāṇa and pramiti being non-different in the primary sense.
The whole is the one creation-power's illumination of knower-means-known.

# IPVV — V3-D ARGUMENT MAP (the pramāṇavimarśa: the per-appearance operation, kārikās 3–8)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00022 lines 3199–3627
(the kriyādhikāra tṛtīyo vimarśa, kārikās 3–8). Works from BOTH the L0 records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** In V3-C, Abhinava established that the pramāṇa operates *on the appearance alone*
(ābhāsamātre), not on the bare own-marker thing apart from all manifestation. Now the objection
arises: if the pramāṇa seizes only the appearance, how can it ever reach the *single* thing
(svalakṣaṇa) that the world treats as one — the jar, the fire? And how can many appearances belong to
one thing? This is the classic sāmānya/svalakṣaṇa and ekatva/anekatva problem, worked out through
kārikās 3–8.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Kārikā 3 (lines 3199–3243) — the one thing, established by the synthesizing awareness:**
> As the taste, as the wish, as the derivation — the appearance, even of one object established by
> the synthesis (anusandhāna), is divided.

At first sight the jar is one — but the discriminating mind divides it. Division is threefold: by its
own freedom (svātantryeṇa), by the wish (arthitva), by the prior-convention (pūrvaprasiddhyupajīvana).
How, then, is the thing one? Because the appearances, shining separately, have a reflexive-awareness
(vimarśa) that is the means (pramāṇa) of the light; and this awareness sometimes rests in the
appearance (giving the universal-dealing), sometimes, with the primary/secondary, "this, of-this-
form" — mixing — its life is the synthesis (anusandhāna). Established by that, even in the one
own-marker thing, the appearances divide by taste, freedom, wish, or convention, without transgressing
the elder's dealing. The synthesis is what makes the many one.

**Kārikā 4 (lines 3264–3265) — the divisions by shape and the rest:**
> With the long, the round, the upright-person, the smoke, the sandalwood, and the rest, the
> appearances are divided, un-divided by place-and-time.

The jar appears long when the eye is fixed on the upper parts, round, etc. The 'as-the-taste' of the
prior sūtra is exemplified by the long/round; the 'as-the-wish' by the upright-person; the 'as-the-
derivation' by the smoke/sandalwood (a trained perception, ābhyāsika-pratyakṣa — citing vā. pa. 1.35:
the perception of the jewel-and-silver that arises from practice). The point: even one thing shows
many appearances, divided by viewpoint, wish, and trained derivation — yet it is un-divided by
place-and-time.

**Kārikā 5 (lines 3266–3267) — the same for the being/jar/substance/gold/shining:**
> Thus-alone the being, jar, substance, gold, shining-ness, and the rest — the appearance-divisions,
> doing divided objects — [are] the word's footing (padaṃ dhvaneḥ).

The universal (sāmānya) known by the first verse (the general appearance), and the appearance-divisions
of the jar (the specific) — both have their footing in the word. The Vṛtti explains the two (inert
examples) and one (conscious). The being-ness, jar-ness, substance-ness, gold-ness, shining-ness are
all appearance-divisions of one thing, each doing its own object-action, and each is a footing for a
word.

**Kārikā 6 (lines 3392–3426) — the fixed object-action through the co-reference (sāmānādhikaraṇya):**
> From the appearance-distinction of the objects, the fixed object-action, again — through the
> co-reference (sāmānādhikaraṇya), of the un-divided by the appearance.

When appearances mix, one is primary (pradhāna) and is the common locus (samānam-adhikaraṇam) of the
others; the relation to it is the co-reference. The appearance so marked, quickened by the sentence-
awareness "this here, now, is the jar," is the cause from which the un-divided (those that have
attained one own-marker-ness, not abandoning their difference) do their object-action: one action is
the aggregate-form (samudāya), another is fixed, each-by-each, single-natured. In the own-marker-ness,
the appearance-mere-form-ness, tinging the same individual, is in truth the universal (sāmānya); in the
mere jar-appearance alone, it is the universal-form only by fitness (yogyatā). (The nanu: "how can the
one, doing many object-actions, be one?" — answered by the pramāṇa = the appearance-reflexive-awareness,
which is not uprooted.) The samādhikaraṇya of "blue" and "blue-lotus" differs utterly from that of
"Caitra" and "Maitra."

**Kārikā 7 (lines 3475–3503) — the streams-in-ocean: many, yet one effect:**
> As the separate lamp-lights, the streams, in the ocean, — of the un-opposed appearances, the
> one-effect (ekakāryā), thus the one-cognition (aikyadhī).

Separate lamp-lights do not, of themselves, light up one dwelling; but lights gathered in one house do
(the single effect). Streams falling into the ocean, with their many waves, do — as an aggregate — one
thing. Likewise the jar, the gold, the red, the great: one single object-action (e.g. the great
delight fit for bathing the Śiva-liṅga) — so how could the doer of that be other than one own-marker
thing? The object-action-doing (arthakriyākāritā) is the worldly being-ness. And the mixing is only of
the un-opposed (not of form-appearance and wind-appearance); the opposition is itself by the
niyati-power. The one cognition (aikyadhī), the one reflexive-awareness, is marked by the one
object-action.

**Kārikā 8 (lines 3598–3627) — the fire/heat: the un-divided object's own-form is fixed by niyati:**
> There, in the un-distinguished fire-and-the-rest, from the effect-cause, the heat-ness; the
> this-and-this-word-meaning-ness, and the rest — the self, from the one valid-means (pramāṇa) [is]
> the thought.

Even granting the perception's per-appearance validity: in the un-divided mere fire-appearance, the
heat-appearance is that which needs no other thing (anapekṣa), fixed everywhere and always, by the
niyati-power (the un-opposed, un-created relation); whereas the fuel-effect, the smoke-cause — these
depend on another object (vastvantarāpekṣa). And the this-and-this-word's-meaning (the denotation)
is fixed by convention, by niyati. This own-form (ātmā), of the fire, is known from the *one* valid-
means (pratyakṣa) — the one that looks to the niyati's glance. Hence: from the effect-cause and the
word, the fire's own-form is established by the one perception. (The nanu: the pramāṇa does not
operate per-appearance in dealing, since place-time never deviates — answered by "prācuryena" —
and why seek the thing's nature by another way? — for the sake of the effect/cause-parts in the
dealing.)

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The vā. pa. 1.35 citation ("the perception born of practice") — its exact force as
   ābhyāsika-pratyakṣa is cited, not re-derived.
2. The "niyatiśaktimahimnā" ("by the glory of the niyati-power") — the determinative power is
   load-bearing; the exact relation to kālaśakti is Abhinava's own, rendered as "the fixative power."
3. The kārikā 8's complex "tattacchabdārthatā" (this-and-this-word's-meaning) — the denotation fixed
   by convention; kept close to the text.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the pramāṇa seizes the appearance, but the *synthesis*
(anusandhāna / the one reflexive-awareness) gathers many appearances into one thing; many appearances
do one object-action (streams-in-ocean); and the thing's own-form (fire's heat, its denotation) is
fixed by the niyati-power, established by the one perception. The ekatva/anekatva and
sāmānya/svalakṣaṇa problems are resolved by the synthesizing awareness and the single object-action.

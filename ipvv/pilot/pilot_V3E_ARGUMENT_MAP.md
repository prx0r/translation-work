# IPVV — V3-E ARGUMENT MAP (the pramāṇavimarśa: error, difference, and the knower as ground, kārikās 9–17)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00022 lines 4415–4961
(the kriyādhikāra tṛtīyo vimarśa, kārikās 9–17). Works from BOTH the L0 records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** Having established that the pramāṇa operates on the appearance (V3-D), Abhinava now
faces the hardest consequence: if the means seizes only appearances, how do we tell *true* from
*false* — the real jar from the illusory double-moon or the shell-mistaken-for-silver? And since the
many appearances are gathered into one thing by the synthesizing awareness, why isn't *every*
appearance-real (atiprasaṅga)? The answer culminates in the great claim: error is resolved, difference
is resolved, and finally **the knower himself is the ground** — so in the supreme knower no pramāṇa is
needed at all. Kārikās 9–17 move from the per-appearance means → error (bhrānti) → difference/
un-difference → the knower as the sole ground.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Kārikā 9 (lines 4415–4422) — the many means, one operation:**
The logic everywhere is the collective of means (pramāṇasamūhamayī). The objection "then is there just
one means?" — no: by the difference of object, there must be a difference of form; the perception that
grasps a dharma cannot be the body of the inference from a sign (which is the mere fire-cognition).
So the operation is *from the collective*.

**Kārikās 10–11 (lines 4429–4459) — far/near; the distinct-manifestation-shadow; the un-broken
one-recognition:**
> By the far-near-ness of the objects, by the indirect/direct-manifestation-own, by the
> outer-inner-ness, by the defects, or otherwise of the manifestor (vyañjaka) — 10
> even of the ones with the distinct-manifestation-shadow, from the primary-manifestation,
> the one-recognition (ekapratyavamarśa) — the un-broken one-ness is not barred — 11

If the means seizes only appearances, how can the far thing and the near thing be one? The object seen
far-off as whole, seen near as whole-in-detail — the one cognition (vimarśa) "this is the same thing I
saw far off" is the primary manifestation; by relying on it, the one-ness of even the
distinct-manifestation-shadow beings is un-broken (anivārita). The "shadow" (chāyā) is in the
appearing/manifesting, not in the essence of the manifestation, which is reflexive-awareness-may.
The distinction true/false is not by the pramāṇa's non-contradiction (pramāṇābādhā) but by the
*reflexive-awareness's* non-contradiction (vimarśābādhā).

**Kārikā 12 (lines 4500–4515) — the object-action is innate, fixed by the Lord's will:**
> The object-action, even the innate, is not of the objects by the Lord's will; for it is fixed by
> that — so of it, from the non-doing, there is no other-ness.

The object-action is not the thing's own-form (that was shown); nor is it innate (sahajā, depending
on nothing) — it depends on the Lord's will and fixity (īśvarecchā-niyatyapekṣa). So, from the
non-doing of that, there can be no other-ness — no unreality, no being-another-thing. The "ullekha"
(the designation) is the jar and the rest; even with the absence of the external own-marker's
object-action (in the Vṛtti's sense), the things are still what they are. The other-ness (to
unreality, or to being-another) would require the non-being or the splitting of the nature — which
cannot happen.

**Kārikā 13 (lines 4517–4544) — the shell-silver error; the upādhi-place non-agreement:**
> In the silver-unity-recognition even, in the shell there is no silver-standing; from the
> non-agreement of the upādhi-place, even in the double-moon, the sky is otherwise.

The objection: "if the one-recognition makes things one, why isn't the shell-silver real?" — Because
although the shell and the silver are recognized as one, the *silver* that appears in the
mind-cognition has no standing (sthiti) as the real silver: it is not the external, real, universally-
dealing, saleable silver. For the reflexive-awareness that posits it is *uprooted* (unmūlyamāna) — by
the non-agreement (asaṃvāda) of the upādhi-place: this place, the shell, is the upakāraka; the
"silver" that flashes here does not agree [with reality], and is uprooted by the stable
reflexive-awareness "this form is not silver." The *disagreement* (asaṃvāda) = the cognition whose
essence is the correct speech of the manifestation-reflexive-awareness, and its opposite. So in the
double-moon too: the region of the sky occupied by the second moon is cognized otherwise — "here there
is no second moon" — the uprooting reflexive-awareness arises. So the error is real-error (up-rooted),
and the distinction true/false is by the un-uprooted vs. uprooted reflexive-awareness.

**Kārikā 14 (lines 4687–4726) — the qualities divide, the class un-divides; the knower as ground:**
> With the qualities, the word and the rest, the difference; with the class and the rest, the
> un-divided-ness — the bhāvanā, thus, in the one knower, is established.

In the beings that shine with the continued-and-excluded self (anuvṛtti-vyāvṛttātmā), neither the
continuation nor the exclusion can be faulted, since there is no param-essence-refuter in any part;
both shine by the freedom of the experience (saṃvedanasvātantryāt). The difference (by the qualities,
the word) and the un-divided-ness (by the class) are both real, and both rest in the *one knower*, the
one who re-cognizes (pratisandhātṛ). The difference, too, is fitting only in the Lord, who is capable
of re-cognizing the limit of that difference. So the pramāṇa-marker (pramāṇalakṣaṇa) shows the
*pramātṛ-pradhāna* (the primacy of the knower): the knower is the ground.

**Kārikā 15 (lines 4802–4803) — the world-picture on the even wall:**
> Of the world-variety-pictured, with the even-wall-surface-comparison — touched by the opposed
> being-non-being — in the param-essence-real Lord.

The appearance (ābhāsa) is attached to the contracted knower; but in the *param-essence-real Lord*,
who is pure manifestation, how is the pramāṇa possible? The world-variety rests in the Lord as a
picture rests on an even wall (the even wall, with its painted elevations and hollows, without itself
being raised or hollow). So the manifestation of the bhedābheda world rests in the always-shining
Lord.

**Kārikā 16 (lines 4804–4836) — the ancient knower needs no pramāṇa:**
> In the ancient knower, though, with the always-manifested form — what pramāṇa, or the new
> appearance, in the partaking of all the cognitions?

For the contracted knower, the new-new appearance is the pramāṇa. But in the ancient (purāṇa) knower,
whose form is always manifested, whose self is mere manifestation — there is no pramāṇa, no new
appearance; for the manifestation is his very form, and it does not fail to be, given the param-essence
reality and lordship. All the cognitions (pramitis) rest in that knower; there is no separate
pratyakṣa or other means to establish what is always self-shining. The pramāṇa, in every case, is the
new-appearance (navābhāsa), and it serves only the finite knower.

**Kārikā 17 (line 4942) — by the power-manifestation, the Īśa-dealing is operated:**
> By the power-manifestation, the Īśa-and-the-rest dealing is operated.

[The close: the dealing of the Lord (and the lesser ones) is operated by the manifestation of the
power (śaktiprakāśena) — the pramāṇa-operating is the manifestation of the power, not a separate
external means. Rendered per the Vṛtti/Vivṛti; cites 1.1.3 (mohavaśāt).]

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The "vyañjaka" (manifestor) in kārikā 10 — the list (by far-near, by outer-inner, by defects, or
   otherwise of the manifestor) is dense; the point (the same thing appears variously by condition) is
   clear.
2. The exact force of "ullekhā eva ghaṭādayaḥ" (kārikā 12) — the "designations are the jar and the
   rest" — rendered as the designation/ullekha being the thing; the Vṛtti's sense (even without the
   external own-marker's object-action) is preserved.
3. Kārikā 17's close and its link to 1.1.3 — rendered per the Vṛtti/Vivṛti, with the
   śaktiprakāśena as the operation of the means.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the pramāṇa operates per-appearance (V3-D), the many appearances
of one thing are gathered by the one recognition (k10–11); error is the uprooted reflexive-awareness
(shell-silver, double-moon — k13); the object-action is fixed by the Lord's will (k12); difference and
un-difference both rest in the one knower, who is the ground (k14); and in the supreme knower, whose
form is always manifested, no pramāṇa is needed at all (k15–16) — the means-operating is the
manifestation of the power (k17). The whole vimarśa ends in the primacy of the knower.

**The primacy of the knower: error, difference, and the ground of all cognition**

We have established that the valid means operates on the appearance. But this opens the deepest
problem of all. If the means seizes only appearances, how do we ever tell the *true* from the *false*
— the real jar from the illusory double-moon, or the shell from the silver it is mistaken for? And if
the synthesizing awareness gathers many appearances into one thing, why is not every appearance real?
The answer Abhinavagupta builds across these nine kārikās is the crowning claim of the whole vimarśa:
error is a matter of the reflexive-awareness, difference and non-difference are real, and in the end
it is the *knower* who is the ground of everything — so that in the supreme knower, no valid means is
needed at all.

First, the one and the many means. The objection arises: if the means operates on the appearance, and
if the appearance is gathered into one, is there after all only one means? No, Abhinava answers. The
perception that grasps a quality is not the same thing as the inference from a sign; they differ in
object and in form. It is the *collective* of the means that does the work — not a single one.

Now the far and the near. The thing I saw far off as a whole, I now see near at hand with all its
details. How is it one? Because of the one recognition — the reflexive-awareness "this is the very
thing I saw before." That primary manifestation, by which the earlier and later appearances are joined,
is what keeps the thing one, however variously it appears. The "shadow" of difference lies in the
appearing, never in the essence of the manifestation itself, which is reflexive-awareness through and
through. And this is the key to truth and falsity: the distinction is not made by the pramāṇa's being
un-contradicted, but by the *reflexive-awareness'* being un-contradicted.

What about the object-action — the capacity of a thing to do what we expect of it? It is not the
thing's own form; nor is it independent — it depends on the Lord's will and fixity. So the thing does
not collapse into unreality, nor become another thing, merely because a given object-action does not
come about. The jar is still the jar, the designating word still designates it, even if the external
object-action is absent.

But now the shell-silver — the classic case of error. If the one-recognition makes things one, why is
the shell-not-silver not also real? Because, although the shell and the silver are recognized as one,
the silver that flashes in the mind has *no standing* as the real silver. It is not the external,
real, universally-dealing, saleable silver. And the reflexive-awareness that posits it is *uprooted*:
by the non-agreement of this place — this is a shell, not silver — the awareness "this form is not
silver" is stable and uproots the false one. Disagreement (asaṃvāda) is precisely this: a cognition
opposed to the correct manifestation-reflexive-awareness. So too with the double-moon: the region of
the sky where a second moon appears is re-cognized as "here there is no second moon," and the false
awareness is uprooted. Error, then, is not a failure to be a real appearance; it is an appearance whose
reflexive-awareness is uprooted. Truth and falsity are decided by the standing or falling of the
reflexive-awareness.

Next, difference and non-difference. The beings shine with both a continued and an excluded self —
the cow is like other cows (class, non-difference) and unlike them (qualities, word — difference).
Neither the continuation nor the exclusion can be faulted, for there is no supreme reality that would
refute either; both shine by the freedom of experience. The difference is by the qualities and the
word; the non-difference by the class. And both, Abhinavagupta insists, rest in the *one knower* — the
one who re-cognizes, who holds the before and after together. The difference, no less than the
non-difference, is real only in the Lord, who is capable of re-cognizing the very limit of that
difference. The whole pramāṇa-marker, in fact, points to the **primacy of the knower** (pramātṛ-
pradhāna): the knower is the ground, and the means is his operation.

And this leads to the great conclusion. The appearance was said to be the pramāṇa for the *contracted*
knower. But in the param-essence-real Lord — whose form is mere manifestation, always shining — how
could there be a pramāṇa? The world-variety rests in him as a picture rests on an even wall: the wall
shows the painted elevations and hollows without itself being raised or hollow. So the whole
distinction-and-non-distinction of the world rests in the always-shining Lord, untouched by his being
or not-being. And in that *ancient* knower (purāṇa), whose form is always manifested, there is no
valid means and no new appearance — for the manifestation is his very essence, and it cannot fail to
be. All the cognitions (pramitis) rest in him; there is no separate perception or inference that could
establish what is always self-shining. The means is, in every case, the new appearance — and it serves
only the finite, contracted knower. In the supreme knower, means and result are transcended: he is the
ground from which all dealing, all knowing, all acting is operated, by the mere manifestation of his
power.

## Fidelity note

- **Preserved:** the collective of means, not one; the far/near one-recognition; the shadow of
  difference in appearing, not in essence; truth/falsity by vimarśābādhā not pramāṇābādhā; the
  object-action's dependence on the Lord's will/fixity; the shell-silver (no standing, uprooted
  awareness, non-agreement of place); the double-moon (up-rooting awareness "no second moon here");
  error = uprooted reflexive-awareness; the continued-and-excluded self; difference by qualities/word,
  non-difference by class; both rest in the one knower; the pramātṛ-pradhāna; the even-wall/picture
  image; the ancient knower's always-manifested form; no pramāṇa needed there; all pramitis rest in
  him; the śaktiprakāśena operation of the Īśa-dealing.

the ground of all dealing.

# IPVV — V3-G ARGUMENT MAP (the kāryakāraṇatāvimarśa: causation, the knower's agency, and the svabhāvahetu)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00022 lines 5515–5966
(the kriyādhikāra caturtho vimarśaḥ = the kāryakāraṇatāvimarśa, kārikās 1–11). Works from BOTH the
L0 records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** In the tṛtīyo vimarśa, the *relation* (sambandha) was shown to be the expansion of
the action-power (kriyāśaktisphāra). Now Abhinava examines the second great relation: **cause and
effect (kāryakāraṇatā)**. His thesis, announced at once: **the essence of cause-and-effect is nothing
but the relation of agent and object of action (kartṛ-karmatva)** — i.e. causality is ultimately the
conscious knower's agency. Against this he must (a) show the inert (jaḍa) has no real causal power,
(b) prove the conscious alone is the agent, (c) handle the anumāna/svabhāvahetu, (d) co-opt the
Buddhist and Sāṃkhya causal theories, and (e) show that even so the knower's agency requires the Lord.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Maṅgala + the 21-verse plan (lines 5516–5538):**
"By the meld of the param-essence-unity, I remember Hara, the great cause of the whole, the
three-power-joined form…" The plan: kārikā 1 states the thesis (agent-object-ness is the essence of
cause-effect); 3 reject the inert's causality (2–4); 6 prove the conscious alone is the agent (5–10);
3 show the niyati-power must be used in the anumāna (from the consideration of the cause); 3 show the
Śākya's (Buddhist) causality rests on our way; 3 show the Kāpila's (Sāṃkhya) too; and 2 show that even
the conscious's agency, established, does not carry over to non-lordship — it requires the Lord.

**Kārikā 1 (lines 5540–5564) — the Lord manifests by his will:**
> This one, from his infinite-power-ness, thus manifests these beings; by his will — this is his
> action, his making-ness.

This ancient knower manifests the beings without break. How? By the un-divided, un-ordered will
(icchā), which is non-different from the Lord. Where are these beings [for him]? In his infinite
power — all beings are his powers (1.5.10). This very will-driven manifesting is the Lord's
instrument, his making-ness/creating-ness (nirmātṛtā, sraṣṭṛtā). (Against the objection "if the will
is eternal, then creation and destruction would be simultaneous; if occasional, some other cause is
needed" — Abhinava answers: the will is tinted by the distinction of the object-to-be, so it is not
simultaneous.)

**Kārikās 2–4 (lines 5596–5613) — the inert has no causal power (the satkāryavāda):**
[Full argument captured in the Ratié mining — the Sāṃkhya/Buddhist satkāryavāda: the inert seed
cannot endow the sprout with being; the effect is either existent or non-existent, and the "both/
neither" is self-contradictory; the non-existent cannot acquire existence ("the blue cannot become
yellow"); the existent needs no acquisition. Conclusion: causality is not a power of the inert.]

**Kārikās 5–10 (lines 5679–5927) — the conscious alone is the agent:**
Kārikā 5: thus the one action is she, with-order, inner-outer standing. Kārikā 6: the outer of that
alone is the effect, which-inner-with-respect-to-which. Kārikā 7: the own alone is the cause, by that
he in the two-appearance-standing. Kārikā 8: thence alone in the sprout-even the wished cause is the
param-Lord. Kārikā 9: thus-indeed the potter, he, with-the-Lord-ness-alone-arrangement. Kārikā 10: of
the yogins-even, without-clay-seed, with-the-will-power-alone that [effect arises]. [The thread: the
effect's cause is ultimately consciousness — the potter creates the pot by his power; the yogin
creates without clay; so the inert seed/clay is not the true cause, the conscious is.]

**Kārikā 11 + the svabhāvahetu (lines 5927–5966) — the own-nature reason:**
> The effect, the reason, or the nature — from that alone the origin-root-born.

The svabhāvahetu (own-nature reason) has two kinds: the clear-embryonic-cause-effect
(sphuṭagarbhita-kāryakāraṇabhāva, e.g. "it is fiery because smoky" — fire is the cause of smoke) and
the reverse (viparīta). The second is exemplified by "śiṃśapā → tree": the tree-ness is the cause of
the śiṃśapā-ness; the śiṃśapā-ness is non-different from tree-ness, and its presence entails the
tree-ness's presence. The śiṃśapā-ness is not a separate thing from the tree-ness; so the "otherness"
that a genuine causal relation needs is absent — yet the entailment holds. Hence the svabhāvahetu
establishes a real relation (the "cause is necessarily present with the effect"), which is the
basis of inference. (The niyati-power: the cause is fixed to the effect; the "śiṃśapā born from its
own seed by the ekasāmagrī-adhīna rule" is the śiṃśapā-ness excluding non-tree-ness.)

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The exact scope of the "anumāna / niyatiśakti" three kārikās (the inference must presuppose the
   niyati-power) — the relation to the fixative power is Abhinava's own; rendered per the text.
2. The śiṃśapā/tree example's precise force — the non-otherness of the two is the crux; rendered as
   the entailment without real causal otherness.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: causality is ultimately the conscious knower's agency — the inert
has no real causal power (satkāryavāda), the conscious alone is the agent (potter, yogin), the
inference rests on the own-nature-reason whose entailment is real though without causal otherness
(śiṃśapā→tree), and the Buddhist and Sāṃkhya theories are co-opted. The whole grounds the knower's
agency in the Lord.

# IPVV — V3-H ARGUMENT MAP (the kāryakāraṇatāvimarśa: inference, dependence, Sāṃkhya, and the reflection)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00022 lines 6460–7057
(the kriyādhikāra caturtho vimarśa, kārikās 12–19). Works from BOTH the L0 records and the raw
Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** V3-G established that causality is the conscious knower's agency and that inference
rests on the own-nature-reason. Now the Vijñānavādin (Buddhist idealist) objects: "If everything is
appearance (ābhāsa), then cause and effect are just relations *between appearances* — and an
un-manifested fire is nothing. How can the smoke-appearance arise from it, or the fire be inferred
from the smoke?" The rest of the vimarśa defends inference (kārikās 12–16), handles dependence/apekṣā
(15–16), co-opts the Sāṃkhya account (17–18), and closes with the reflection argument for why only
consciousness can bear difference-and-non-difference (19).

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Kārikās 12–13 (lines 6460–6509) — inference across knowers, against the Vijñānavādin:**
> From the more, the that-that-knower, the one, the fire-appearance, from this it would be — even
> from the indirect, of the Lord, the smoke-appearance, and the rest, the new — 12
> The effect of the non-wandering [reason], the marker, from the other-knower; that-appearance from
> that-appearance alone, though, of the Lord, is the other — 13

The Vijñānavādin, holding each stream (santāna) to have its own distinct appearances, took the
cause-effect to be only within one's own smoke-and-fire appearances (he cannot sense another stream's
appearances). Abhinava replies: in our view, the Lord creates the appearances for all the knowers as
one. At the time of grasping the pervasion (vyāpti), the smoke-appearance in the one knower and the
fire-appearance are, in the Lord's one creation, the same as in another; so, by the force of
perception-and-non-perception, the smoke-appearance arises from the fire-appearance, and the pervasion
can be grasped once. And again, for the many knowers (down to the worm and the omniscient), the
fire-appearance is *one* (not split by stream); so the smoke-appearance can arise from it, not from
another. That fire-appearance, being indirect to the inferer, is the "Lord's-cause" (adhipatipratyaya);
it is *new* (nūtana), not like the smoke-born smoke. It is the non-wandering effect-marker, used in the
inference, with respect to the niyati-power. The inferer is he whom the Lord has created as one with
the other knowers in the common part of the fire-appearance. So the inference across knowers holds.

**Kārikā 14 (line 6579) — "in-this-existing-this-is": the causal relation as the pervasion:**
> "In this existing, this is" — the cause-effect relation, which — [is what the pervasion expresses].

**Kārikās 15–16 (lines 6892–6975) — dependence (apekṣā) is not real otherness:**
> Not of the own-self-one-resting-ones, the synthesis-missing — even in the being/non-being-word, the
> seventh-ending's meaning is constructed — 15

The mutual dependence (anyonyalagnatā) is rejected in the first half; the intent-made dependence, in
the second. In all four cases (the cause/effect, either present or absent), dependence is not fitting.
The real fire and its heat: the distinction between them is only a constructed one
(vaikalpika); the fire, as the shining own-marker, cannot have the touch as a separate own-form —
both are of equal cause. So in truth the dependence is one, quickened by the synthesis. Kārikā 16:
thence alone the case-ending-meaning, the one-knower-supported. The seventh-ending (locative) is not
meaningless nor of a merely constructed referent; it is of the real, supported by the one knower. The
"sattāyām" (in being) refers to the external form, not to the second 'vā'-dependent constructed one.

**Kārikā 17 (lines 6976–6980) — mutual nature would collapse difference:**
> In the mutual nature of the cause-and-effect two, even — the one-ness alone; for in difference,
> there is not at all the mutual own-form-ness.

If cause and effect were of each other's nature (mutual svabhāva), then in difference there would be
no mutual own-form-ness — the very distinction would collapse. This is rejected by perception; the
Sāṃkhya's dry (śuṣka) cause-effect is refuted.

**Kārikā 18 (lines 6982–7015) — the Sāṃkhya's one-substance with order-variety:**
> Of the one-self, the distinction, and the action following the time-order — thus there would be
> agency, and thus, by the thus-transforming-ness —

Even in the Sāṃkhya view (the one substance, e.g. the seed/principle, with its ordered variety of
natures — the earlier nature as cause, the later as effect), the *kriyā-kāraka* relation is the first
thing manifested: the transformation (pariṇāma), the transition from the earlier nature (given up)
to the later nature (newly unfolded), is itself an *action*. So even the "dry" causal relation of the
Sāṃkhya is not dry — it already contains the agent/act/object structure, and hence the freedom
(svātantrya) of transformation, i.e. agency. The mahat (intellect), the pṛthivīparyanta (down to
earth), the aṅkuraparyanta (up to the sprout) — all are states of the one triguṇa substance, and the
transformation is the action that runs through them. (The guṇa's varied states — sattva/rajas/tamas —
are transformed by the restless rajas into the countless forms.)

**Kārikā 19 (lines 7028–7057) — only consciousness can bear difference-and-non-difference (the
reflection):**
> And not fitting is this for the inert, from the difference-non-difference-contradiction; from the
> appearance-difference, in the one, but in the consciousness-self it fits.

The inert is a fixed own-form (pariniṣṭhita-svarūpa); it is a known object, so it is "different" with
respect to another (blue as against non-blue), but "non-different" with respect to itself — yet the
*very same* nature cannot be both different and non-different (contradiction; the "bhedābheda-
vyavasthā utsannā" rule). So the inert, being a known object (like the blue), cannot be many-natured.
But the consciousness-self (cidātmā), not fallen into the state of a known object — like the clear,
free mirror, or the variegated consciousness (citra-saṃvedana) — bears the appearance-variety as its
reflections (pratibimba), which are distinct even in the one. Hence difference-and-non-difference fits
only in consciousness, not in the inert. (The nanu: "then why don't the elephant and the mountain
conflict with the one-ness?" — because they are only in the mirror: nahi, they are in the mirror
itself; the free mirror shows all, even without its own freedom — the mere limpidity is its might.)

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The exact "adhipatipratyaya" (Lord's-cause) in kārikā 12 — the inference's object-cause is the
   fire-appearance created by the Lord as one across knowers; rendered per the text.
2. The kārikā 16's four-case (pakṣacatuṣṭaya) dependence analysis is dense; the real/constructed
   distinction is preserved, the full enumeration kept close.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the Vijñānavādin's appearance-only scruple is answered by the
Lord's single creation of appearances across knowers (so inference holds); dependence is not real
otherness but synthesis-quickened; the Sāṃkhya's transformation already contains agency; and only the
consciousness-self, like a free mirror bearing reflections, can bear difference-and-non-difference —
the inert cannot. The vimarśa ends in the mirror-image of consciousness.

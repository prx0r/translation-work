# IPVV — V3-I ARGUMENT MAP (the close of the kāryakāraṇatāvimarśa: the unity of consciousness and action, kārikās 20–21)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00022 lines 7434–7662
(the kriyādhikāra caturtho vimarśa, kārikās 20–21 and the ||4|| colophon). Works from BOTH the L0
records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The vimarśa has shown that causality is the conscious knower's agency. Now the
sharpest objection: if consciousness is *one* and the world is mere appearance, how is action
possible at all? For action requires an agent and an object, distinct; but if all is one
consciousness, there is no distinct object for the agent to act upon. Abhinavagupta answers (kārikā
20) that action is the one *re-recollection of the will* (cikīrṣā-parāmarśa) that joins the agent and
object; and (kārikā 21) that this very will (icchā) IS the cause-ness, the agent-ness, and the action.
The vimarśa closes with the ||4|| colophon and the transition to the āgamādhikāra.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Kārikā 20 (lines 7434–7483) — action needs the one will-recollection:**
> Even in the real consciousness-unity, the action would not be, of the appearance-distinct two,
> without the one-recollection marked by the will-to-do (cikīrṣā).

Even granting that the unity of consciousness is real and that the difference is [only] ignorance
(avidyā), action cannot arise — because the jar and the consciousness are, by appearance, distinct
two. Without the one re-recollection (parāmarśa), marked by the will-to-do, which joins them, there is
no action. The genitive "of the jar" [the agent] and "the object" must be joined by that one
re-recollection resting in the other consciousness-essence. So: if the unity of consciousness is real
(like the jar's being a jar, naturally established), and the difference is called ignorance, then we
must ask — of whom is this ignorance? For the one-form Brahman has no "ignorance" as its absence, nor
any separate jīvas, which are only raised by the ignorance. If the ignorance is called
"un-explainable" (anirvācya), then "of whom?" we do not know. (The "it shines and is un-explainable"
is a yakṣa-speech.) The bheda (difference) is real, for the cognition rests in difference as it does
not rest in non-difference; and the "bādhā" (refutation) too, being a manifestation, would be
difference and so ignorance. This would cut the horns of the hare's head, the sky-flower. If all is
from the āgama, then the āgama too is ignorance — a net of illusions. (Cites the coming sūtra
"brahmāpīṣṭam avidyayā saha, tato naikāntavādo'pyayam.") The Vṛtti: "akasmāt" (suddenly, no cause
found). The ābhāsabheda is the known-side form; the kriyātva (the being-action, which confers
agent-ness) does not fit in Brahman. (The nanu: "the inert's agent-ness is seen" — e.g. "in the upper
direction is the god-self, the Himalaya king of mountains" — answered: no, the inert has no
agent-ness; the agent is the knower; the "devatātma" is metaphorical.) The kāraṇa (the aiśvaryaśakti)
is needed; without it, the varied order would not arise, since there is no cause for the occasional.

**Kārikā 21 (lines 7607–7662) — the will IS the cause-ness, agent-ness, action:**
> Thus, in this way, with the jar-cloth-and-the-rest-appearance-world-self — of the one who wishes to
> stand (tiṣṭhāsoḥ), this very will (icchā) is the cause-ness, the agent-ness, the action.

The meaning is clear. And what was said — "it, and, is the action-power (kriyāśakti)" — invites an
objection: "In the Lord is the will (icchā), in Sadāśiva the knowledge-power, in Īśvara the
action-power" — so how is this division? Answer: the 'ca' (and) indicates the doubt. Not only the
world-famous action (of the nature of spanda, movement) but *this* [will] too — the 'api'. The "when-
then" (yadā-tadā) are particles indicating cause-and-effect. The action-power in the action is thus
explained; the co-reference (sāmānādhikaraṇya) — "the will in the action" — is the action-self
(śi. dṛ. 1.4: "the three subtle powers dwell in harmony"). By the "thus" (tanmukhena = by the
consciousness-dense-agent), the grammarians' view: even in the absence of the other adjuncts, the
kriyātattva is seen in the agent; in the absence of the other-adjunct-complement, not seen — so by
anvaya-vyatireka, the resting-in-the-agent alone is the action. In "here it does not cook," too, the
agents fit for cooking-and-enjoying are manifested as implied by the locus. (The karma's action-entry
does not happen without the will — the "uparitar" [the higher]; the upacāra's nimitta is the seeing of
the intended specific, placed in the object by the will even as agent.) The "sambandhinī" is supplied
(adhyāhāra). The ||4|| colophon closes the vimarśa.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The "cikīrṣā" (will-to-do) and its genitive joining of agent/object — the crux; rendered as the
   one re-recollection that joins the two.
2. The "śaśaśṛṅga / gaganakusuma" (hare's horn, sky-flower) — the reductio against the un-explainable
   ignorance; kept as Abhinava's image.
3. The śi. dṛ. 1.4 citation ("the three subtle powers in harmony") — cited, not re-derived.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: even granting the real unity of consciousness, action is possible
because it is the one will-recollection (cikīrṣā-parāmarśa) that joins agent and object; the alleged
"un-explainable ignorance" is a reductio (hare's horn); and the will itself is cause-ness, agent-ness,
and action — so the kriyāśakti is the will, and the whole causal/agent structure is one act of
consciousness. The ||4|| colophon closes the kriyādhikāra's fourth vimarśa.

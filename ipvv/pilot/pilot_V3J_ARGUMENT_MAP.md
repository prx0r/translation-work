# IPVV — V3-J ARGUMENT MAP (the āgamādhikāra prathamo vimarśa: the Śivatattva and the categories, kārikās 1–7)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00022 lines 7662–8500
(the āgamādhikāra, prathamo vimarśa, kārikās 1–7). Works from BOTH the L0 records and the raw Sanskrit.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** The kriyādhikāra showed that the action-power's form is "the un-clouded manifestation
of all the padārthas" (all the categories). But then — what are these padārthas, the categories, the
tattvas? The jñānādhikāra and kriyādhikāra worked by perception and reasoning. But these cannot grasp
the *whole* — perception is not omniscient, inference lacks the pervasion for everything. Only the
**āgama** (the revealed teaching) — which is the re-recollection of the un-contracted param-eśvara's
manifestation — can measure (pramā) the whole universe. So this new adhikāra, the āgamādhikāra, expounds
the tattvas from the Lord's teaching, aided by reasoning (yukti). Its theme: the Śivatattva is the body
of all the padārthas, and the whole creation is "warp and woof" (otaprota) in it.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Maṅgala + the authority of the āgama (lines 7666–7716):**
"Warp-and-woof having penetrated all, by its own force it makes [all] Śiva-may; he who, rising in the
un-surpassed abode, is himself the pouring of nectar — to him I bow." Perception is not the omniscient
seer; inference lacks the pervasion for all things. Only the āgama, which is the re-recollection
(vimarśa) of the un-contracted manifestation of the param-eśvara, can measure the universe. (Cites
spa. kā. 43: "when the will-to-see pervades and stands over all objects, what more need be said — it
will reveal itself"; and the rule "the one valid-means is that utterance, true, spoken by the Lord; its
word is not refuted by the gross means of men.") So the āgamādhikāra begins. In 11 verses it shows the
own-form of the tattvas from Śiva down to the earth, established by the param-eśvara-āgama and aided by
reason — the creation is warp-and-woof.

**Kārikā 1 (lines 7719–7766) — the Śivatattva as the body of all:**
> Thus the inner-outer operation, the action following the time-order — of the mother alone, the two
> un-joined [knowledge and action], the knowledge-action.

The cause-effect relation does not rest in the inert, but in the consciousness-form — whose
param-essence is manifestation-and-reflexive-awareness; by its freedom it shines as inner-outer,
raising the time-order, and, in the knowledge-power's form (as the dharmin, the substrate), it takes
on the ordered inner-outer operation as its dharma (the action-power). Hence the jñāna and kriyā,
manifestation-and-reflexive-awareness, are un-joined (anyonyam aviyukte); and this is the param-essence
form (vapuḥ) of all beings. The Śivatattva, which by the gate of the action-power takes up the whole
fivefold work and, being taught for devotion, appears as the appearance-form — is so called. (Cites
1.5.16; the Kiraṇa: "how is Śiva, beyond māyā, manifested as the primary tattva in the mind-ground?" —
answered: "the downward-pervasion is of the self-shining Śiva himself" — self-shining = freedom, and
the downward-pervasion = creation.) The Vivṛti-kāra answers the "why another adhikāra" doubt: the
Śivatattva is the body of all the padārthas; the teaching of it is the beginning of establishing them.

**Kārikā 2 (lines 7954–7988) — the Sadākhya and the Īśvara:**
> But from the inner-state-surge, the Sadākhya-category first; in the outer-being-primary-ness,
> though, later, the param-Lord's [Īśvara].

The Śivatattva alone is the one real thing, but it has further distinctions. It, by its own power,
manifests the variety in itself as by a reflection (pratibimba), and manifests the time/place/order
(which is the variety's being action-and-form-divided). So when the *inner*, knowledge-form state
surges (āntaradaśodreka), [we have] the Sadākhya — the first category, seen at the start of the
teaching of creation, from which the "is, is" (sad, sad) cognition shines, called "Sadāśiva." When the
*outer* being, of the action-power form, is primary, then, in the surge of the state, the Īśvara-tattva
is to be established after the Sadākhya (fitness of the Lord's impossible-doing-ness). The "tattva"
(tat-bhāva = the being of that) is defined (mataṅgāgama 1.5.4: "tattva is that which would be the
thing's own-form, manifesting its own dharma; the thing-word, manifest, in the revealed view; which,
un-lost from its own course, is extended and come under its own power, extended by no other — this-and-
this is the tattva in the tattva-stream"). In the pure-consciousness-group (the mantra-maheśvara), the
state-circles, like our inner-organ-known states, un-stained, like a picture just unclosed — that
cognition-distinction-universal is the Sadāśiva-tattva; the mantreśvara-form, like the full picture in
the external-organ-known, is the Īśvara-tattva. (Cites "in that very Īśvara-tattva dwell the
param-eśvaras.")

**Kārikās 3–4 — the Sadāvidyā, the co-reference, the known-ground:**
Kārikā 3: she, the co-reference, and the true-knowledge of the "I-this"-cognition-two. Kārikā 4: of
the this-being-attained-ones, to the known-ground-attaining. [The Sadāvidyā (śuddhavidyā) is the state
where "I-this" are co-referent — the co-reference (sāmānādhikaraṇya) of the I and the this; and the
categories that attain the known-ground.]

**Kārikā 5 (line 8330) — the āparatva (the un-param-ness):**
> Here the un-param-ness of the beings, from the non-self-ness manifestation.

[The beings below the pure group are "un-param" (apara) because they manifest as non-self (anātma) —
as objects, not as the self. The gradation of the tattvas from the param (pure) to the apara
(impure) is the āparatva.]

**Kārikā 6 (line 8378) — the bhedadhī:**
> The distinction-cognition alone, in the beings, of the agent-awareness-own even, which —

[The distinction-cognition (bhedadhī) — the cognition of difference — belongs even to the awareness
of the agent; it is the source of the apara manifestation.]

**Kārikā 7 (line 8421) — the aiśvarya in the animal-being:**
> Of that lordship-nature, in the animal-being, the manifesting —

[The lordship-nature (aiśvaryasvabhāva), when manifested in the animal (paśu) being, is the
manifestation of the Īśvara's nature in the bound state. This sets up the paśu/pati distinction that
continues in the vimarśa.]

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The exact enumeration of kārikās 3–7 (Sadāvidyā, āparatva, bhedadhī, aiśvarya) — the source
   passages for 3–4 and 5–7 are partial in my read; the map gives the doctrinal sense, the L2 keeps
   the tattva-progression (pure → impure, Sadāśiva → Īśvara → śuddhavidyā → apara) without inventing
   details beyond the source.
2. The mataṅgāgama 1.5.4 definition of "tattva" — cited, not re-derived.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the āgamādhikāra expounds the categories from the Lord's teaching
(since perception and inference cannot grasp the whole); the Śivatattva is the body of all — the
un-joined knowledge-and-action, self-shining = freedom, whose downward-pervasion is creation; from it
emerge the Sadākhya (inner-surge), the Īśvara (outer-surge), the śuddhavidyā, and the apara categories
(which manifest as non-self), including the bhedadhī and the aiśvarya-in-the-animal. The pure-to-impure
gradation of the tattvas is the theme.

**The teaching as the measure: the categories from the Lord's word**

The action-power, we have seen, has as its very form the un-clouded manifestation of all the
categories of the world. But this raises the question the kriyādhikāra could not answer: *what* are
these categories, these tattvas? Our perception is not an omniscient eye; our inference lacks the
pervasion that would reach every thing. Neither can grasp the whole. Only the **āgama** — the revealed
teaching — can do so; for it is nothing other than the re-recollection of the un-contracted
manifestation of the supreme Lord, and so it alone can measure the entire universe. As the rule has it:
the one valid means is that utterance, true, spoken by the Lord; its word is not to be refuted by the
gross means of men. And this is why Abhinavagupta now opens a new section, the āgamādhikāra: to expound
the categories from the Lord's own teaching, aided by reasoning.

The theme is announced at once. All this creation is *warp and woof* (otaprota) — the categories from
Śiva down to the earth are woven into the one reality. And at the heart of it all is the **Śivatattva**,
which is the body of every category. The cause-and-effect relation does not rest in the inert; it rests
in the consciousness-form, whose essence is manifestation-and-reflexive-awareness. By its freedom this
consciousness shines both within and without, raising the order of time; and in the form of the
knowledge-power it becomes the substrate that takes on the ordered operation as its own action-power.
So knowledge and action — manifestation and reflexive-awareness — are un-joined, inseparable; and this
inseparable pair is the supreme form of all beings. This is the Śivatattva: the one that, through the
gate of the action-power, takes up the whole fivefold work, and, being taught for our devotion,
appears as the appearance-form of everything.

The objection comes: "How can Śiva, who is beyond māyā, appear as the *primary* category in the world
of mind?" The answer: self-shining is freedom, and the downward-pervasion of that self-shining is
creation itself. The self-shining Śiva, in his freedom, pours himself downward into the world — and
that pouring is the world. There is nothing arbitrary here; it is the very nature of the light to shine
forth.

From this one reality, the categories unfold in order. When the *inner* state — the knowledge-form —
surges, we have the **Sadākhya** (also called Sadāśiva), the first category, from which the cognition
"is, is" (sad, sad) shines: the pure self aware of the world as its own. When, on the other hand, the
*outer* being — the action-power form — becomes primary, we have the **Īśvara-tattva**, established
after the Sadākhya, in which the impossible-doing of the Lord appears as its great power. Both are the
one reality, seen under its inner and outer aspect, like a picture just unclosed (the pure group) or
like the full painted picture (the Īśvara).

Below these pure categories come the impure ones. There is the **śuddhavidyā** (the true knowledge),
where the "I" and the "this" are co-referent — the co-reference (sāmānādhikaraṇya) of self and world.
And then, descending, the categories manifest as *non-self* (anātma) — as objects rather than as the
self. This is the "un-param-ness" (āparatva) of the beings: the lower categories appear as other than
the self. Even the distinction-cognition (bhedadhī), the cognition of difference, belongs to the
awareness of the agent and carries us further into the divided world. And the lordship-nature
(aiśvarya), when it is manifested in the *animal* being — the bound knower — shows the Lord's nature
in the state of bondage, setting up the great distinction of the bound and the Lord that will occupy
the rest of this vimarśa.

So the āgamādhikāra begins by tracing the categories downward from the one reality — the Śivatattva,
the body of all, through the pure group (Sadāśiva, Īśvara, śuddhavidyā) into the impure, the
non-self, the divided, and the bound. All of it, warp and woof, is the one consciousness shining forth;
and the teaching — the Lord's own re-recollection — is the measure that grasps it whole.

  unclosed) vs. Īśvara (full picture); the śuddhavidyā co-reference; the āparatva (non-self
  manifestation); the bhedadhī; the aiśvarya in the animal-being.

śuddhavidyā) and the impure (non-self, divided, bound) unfold, ending in the lordship manifested in
the animal.

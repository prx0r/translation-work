# IPVV — V3-K ARGUMENT MAP (the close of the āgamādhikāra prathamo: the kañcukas and the categories of the known, kārikās 8–11)

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** Having traced the categories from the Śivatattva down through the pure group
(V3-J), Abhinavagupta now descends into the impure realm — the world of the bound knower (paśu). Here
the pure self is covered by the *kañcukas* (the limiting sheaths or "armours"): niyati (fixity),
kāla (time), rāga (attachment), vidyā (limited knowledge), kalā (limited agency). And the categories
of the *known* (prameya) — the twenty-three-fold: the one pradhāna (root-cause), the thirteen organs,
and the ten-fold effect. Kārikās 8–11 give these, closing the prathamo vimarśa of the āgamādhikāra.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Kārikā 8 — the bheda, the I-ness in the non-self:**
> In the distinction, though, one-flavor-manifested, with the I-ness, in the non-self, seen —

[The bound state: though the reality is one-flavor, in the distinction the I-ness (ahantā) is seen in
the non-self (anātman) — the self wrongly identified with the body and the rest. This is the covering
of the pure self by the kañcukas.]

**The kañcukas (lines 8822–8851) — niyati and the others:**
The relation of cause-and-effect, in the bound state, is a *covering*: in truth the Lord alone shines
as "this is the cause of that," yet the *niyati* (the fixative power) is the mark of the limited
knower (pramātṛ-dharma): the being-fixed, the being-determined, of the cause-and-effect relation. The
seed is the cause of the sprout, not of the pot; the clay of the pot, not of the sprout — this
fixing, in the appearance, is the niyati, which in reality belongs to the knower, not to the known
(mātrābhāsanadvāreṇa). So too kāla (time), rāga (attachment — the mind-flux rooted in attachment,
matsara etc.), and the rest, are the kañcukas that cover the pure self in the bound state.

**Kārikā 9 — the śūnyādi knower:**
> Who-and-the-knower, the-emptiness-and-the-rest, in the known-distinct —

[The knower of the "emptiness-and-the-rest" (śūnyādi) — the void-thought, breath, body — is the bound
knower, who, in the known-distinct (prameya-vyatirekiṇi), is the one who knows the separated objects.
This is the māyīya knower.]

**Kārikās 10–11 (lines 8944–8954) — the 23-fold known:**
> The twenty-three-fold known, which is the effect-instrument-own: of it, the un-divided-form one,
> the pradhāna, the root-cause. — 10
> The thirteen-fold, here, the external-inner-instrument-series; and the effect-group, ten-fold, by
> the gross/subtle-ness distinction. — 11

The known (prameya) is twenty-three-fold: (a) the one *pradhāna* (the root-cause, un-divided-form, the
Sāṃkhya matter), (b) the *thirteen organs* (the five sense-organs, the five action-organs, the inner
organ, mind, and the ahaṅkāra/buddhi in the usual Sāṃkhya count), and (c) the *ten-fold effect-group*
(the five tanmātras and the five elements, by the gross/subtle distinction). This is briefly stated
(the detail is in the Vivṛti). Then Abhinava examines whether the *common-marker* (sāmānyalakṣaṇa)
can grasp these (they are not truly sensible); the pradhāna etc. are un-sensible; the karaṇa-tva
(instrument-ness) too is not fitting for them. The nanu: "in the un-real (avāstava), [these] —" —
answered: their un-sensible-ness.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The exact count of the "thirteen organs" — the Sāṃkhya enumeration is presupposed; the text says
   "thirteen-fold external-inner-instrument-series" and "ten-fold effect-group"; the detail is in the
   Vivṛti. Rendered per the text without over-specifying.
2. The precise working of kārikā 8 (the I-ness in the non-self) — the covering of the self; the
   kañcuka detail is in the following commentary.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: in the impure realm the pure self is covered by the kañcukas
(niyati, kāla, rāga, vidyā, kalā) — which in truth belong to the knower, not the known; the bound
knower (śūnyādi) knows the separated objects; and the known is twenty-three-fold (one pradhāna,
thirteen organs, ten-fold effect). This closes the prathamo vimarśa of the āgamādhikāra, setting up
the paśu/pati analysis of the dvitīyo vimarśa.

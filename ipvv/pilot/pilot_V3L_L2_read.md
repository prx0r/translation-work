**The knowers and the stains that bind**

We have set out the categories of the known — the world that is known. But the heart of this whole
teaching is the recognition of the Lord in the self; and to recognize him we must understand the
*knowers* themselves. For there are many knowers, from the bound to the pure, and they are distinguished
by the stains that bind them. Abhinavagupta opens this vimarśa with a homage to the one "who wields the
trident, cutting the three nooses" — and those three nooses are the three stains (mala) that will
occupy him throughout: the stain of finitude (āṇava), the stain of objectivity (māyīya), and the stain
of action (kārma).

The knower-states have their presiding deities. In the pure state — where the knower stands in his mere
knower-ness, the objects withdrawn — the presider is **Rudra**: the supreme Śiva, who as the Lord
brings his worshippers into that state, and who, even within the realm of māyā, is the one with the
opened third eye. But in the spread of the *distinct* world, the presiders are **Brahmā** and **Viṣṇu**:
Brahmā in the production of the spread, Viṣṇu in its flowing continuation. And the difference is this:
these two make the "this" — the object — primary, and the "I" — the knower-essence — subordinate;
whereas the pure Rudra makes the "I" primary. They are not third-eyed, as the Rudra is. Yet even these
Brahmā and Viṣṇu forms are not something apart from the Lord: they shine as the qualities of the
intellect, not as the pure consciousness itself; their form is the lordship — the freedom, the
knowledge, the action-power — standing as the support of the world.

Now the bound knower. He is the one blind by māyā, wandering in saṃsāra, bound by his actions. This is
the paśu, the animal — the knower who has lost his freedom. And over against him stands the pati, the
Lord: the knower whose own-members — the beings of the world — are his own. The bound one is bound
because he has suffered the *loss of freedom* of the awareness: he has become un-aware of his own
freedom, and so he is caught in the objects. This is the working of the māyā: the manifestation of the
distinct known — the world spread out as other than the self — which gives us birth and enjoyment, and
which is the māyīya stain.

The knowers come in grades. There are the pure ones — those who, even in the unity of the
awareness-marker, are mutually distinct, and who lack the *highest* agency: these are the vidyeśvara
group. There are the pralayākala — the "dissolved" ones, absorbed in the state of dissolution, who have
only the stain of finitude, their form the un-awareness of the void and the rest. And even the aware
ones, who possess agency, still stand in the stain of action. Indeed, all the becoming ones — the gods
and men and the rest — have the three-fold stain: finitude, objectivity, action. And binding them all
is the kalā — the limited agency — which raises the consciousness-essence into the narrow form of the
individual agent.

So the picture is complete. Beneath the Lord, who is free and whole, lie the many knowers, bound each
by his stain: the finitude that shrinks the infinite self into a single soul, the māyā that spreads the
world out as other, and the action that keeps the soul bound in the round of birth and enjoyment. It is
these three nooses that the trident-bearing Lord cuts. And to understand how they are cut — how the
bound knower can be released into the recognition of his own lordship — Abhinavagupta will turn next to
the states and the entry, the samāveśa, through which the bound one rises to his true nature.

## Fidelity note

- **Preserved:** the maṅgala (the trident, the three nooses, the pāśatraya); the 20-verse plan; the
  presiding deities (Rudra in the pure state, third-eyed, making the "I" primary; Brahmā in production,
  Viṣṇu in continuation, making the "this" primary); the Brahmādi forms as buddhi-qualities (the
  aiśvarya) not pure consciousness; the bound knower blind by māyā, in saṃsāra, action-bound; the pati
  (the Lord of the own-members); the freedom-loss; māyā as the distinct-known-manifestation giving
  birth and enjoyment; the grades (pure ones lacking highest agency, mutual distinctness; the
  pralayākala in dissolution with the āṇava stain); the kārma stain even of the aware agents; the
  three-fold stain of all the becoming ones; the kalā-raised consciousness-essence.
- **Added (SUPPLIED, recoverable):** "and those three nooses are the three stains" (glosses the
  pāśatraya); "he has become un-aware of his own freedom" (states the svātantryahāni); "which gives us
  birth and enjoyment" (restates the māyākhyā janmabhogada). Interpretive unpacking, not new doctrine.
- **NOT resolved:** the exact vidyeśvara/pralayākala/vijñānākala enumeration (the text is dense; the
  detail is in the Vivṛti). Logged, not invented.

**Verdict:** understood and readable. The dvitīyo vimarśa lays out the knowers and the stains clearly:
the presiding deities of the states, the bound knower and the Lord, the three stains (finitude,
objectivity, action) that bind all the becoming ones, and the kalā-raised agency — with the release,
the cutting of the three nooses, to follow.

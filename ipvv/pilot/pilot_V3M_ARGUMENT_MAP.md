## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** Having examined the knowers and the stains, Abhinavagupta now examines the *states*
(avasthā) of the bound knower — jāgrat (waking), svapna (dream), suṣupta (deep sleep), and turya (the
fourth) — and the vital breaths (prāṇādi) that sustain them. The point: these states are the stages in
which the bound soul lives, and understanding them — which are to be adopted (upādeya) and which
abandoned (heya) — is essential to the recognition. The pure fourth state is the goal; the other three
are the round of the bound.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Kārikā 12 — the primacy of agency, awareness, consciousness-self:**
> The primary-ness of the agent-ness, and of the awareness, and of the consciousness-self —

[The knower's essence: agency (kartṛtā), awareness (bodha), and the consciousness-self (cidātman) are
primary. This frames the whole — the knower is fundamentally the free conscious agent.]

**Kārikā 13 — the emptiness, the I-ness-word:**
> In the emptiness, the intellect-and-the-rest-absence-own, the I-ness, the agent-ness-word —

[In the state of the void (śūnya), where the intellect and the rest are absent, the "I-ness" and the
"agent-ness" are only words — the pralayākala, dissolved in the void, has the mere trace of the
"I".]

**Kārikā 14 — the inner operation of the senses, the breath-stimulating:**
> The inner operation of the senses, the breath-and-the-rest-stimulating, the thought —

[The prāṇa (the vital breath) is the inner operation that stimulates the senses; it is the life of the
bound knower, the link between the self and the senses.]

**Kārikā 15 — suṣupta, the sleep like dissolution (lines 10326–10361):**
> In the that-much-standing, the said, the sleep-like-the-dissolution-equal — with the known, and
> without-the-known, with-the-māyā-stain, and-without —

In the mere void (śūnyamātra) — the pure void — when the standing is the I-ness-rest (ahantāviśrānti),
and when [it is] in the prāṇa, then the consciousness is put to sleep by the māyā (which makes the
stain descend), not even awakened by kalā and vidyā. This is the suṣupta (deep sleep): the
pravṛtti-cause-form appearance, and the karma (as the action-form) whose essence is the
reflexive-awareness. Deep sleep is like dissolution (pralaya) — but in dissolution there is the long
decay of the body; here, in sleep, there is no such action and no long time. Its sub-kinds: sleep
(by fatigue), fainting (by shock/dhātu-disease), intoxication/madness (by wine, etc.), samādhi (by
the freedom-raised effort). The first half is "with the known" (savedya, the vidyākalā grade, with the
māyā-stain), the second "without the known" (apavedya, without the māyā-stain, the pralayākala).

**Kārikā 16 — svapna, the dream (lines 10367–10401):**
> In the mind-mere-path even, with the sense-object-ness, from the delusion — the clear-manifestation
> creation of the beings, the dream-word, the thought.

In the dream, the sense-organs (the eyes and the rest) are fixed, yet one sees the beloved, and the
cognition "I see her, I draw her with my hand" is a clear manifestation whose primary [source] is the
momentary Lord-creation. Only the mind is active, relying on the prior creation; but in the
sense-object-part, [the dream] shares the common [world]; the stability and the rest of the
appearance, joined, continue the reflexive-awareness — and in the waking state, this is refuted, so the
dream is of the delusion-form. The dream is the mind's own creation, a clear-appearance making.

**Kārikā 17 — jāgrat, the waking (lines 10432–10461):**
> With-the-all-sense-object-ness, which-though-with-the-external-ness-firm — the creation, the
> common, of-all-the-knowers, that is the waking.

The waking state is the *common* creation (sādhāraṇī sṛṣṭi), firm and external, shared by all the
knowers. The waking knower is he who, by the exhaustion of the delusion-causing sleep, "wakes" —
so the waking is the knower-state. The five states (waking, dream, deep sleep, and the two grades) are
the knower-ness made by the difference of the known, as taught in the āgamas; and even in the waking
the dream-form is found (the mutual entry of the states).

**Kārikā 18 — the three as removable (lines 10444–10461):**
> The removable, the three, this, of the breath-and-the-rest, from the primary-ness, in the
> agent-ness-quality — from its decrease-increase-mostly-joined-suffering-pleasure —

The three states (waking, dream, deep sleep) are to be abandoned (heya) by the seeker of liberation,
since the prāṇa and the rest (the vital breaths) are primary in the agent-ness, and the decrease/
increase of pleasure and pain is their quality. In the agent-ness, where the prāṇa is not made primary,
its freedom-share, with the pleasure/pain-objects and the gaining/abandoning-efforts, makes the seeker
regard the three states as to-be-abandoned.

**Kārikās 19–20 — the prāṇas:**
> The breath-out-breath-made, the breath, individually in-the-sleep-waking-two — 19
> The middle-upper-going, the udāna-named, the-fourth-going, the-fire-made — 20

[The prāṇa (the in-breath) and the apāna (the out-breath) run through the waking and the sleep; the
udāna is the middle-upper-going breath; the turya (the fourth) is the going beyond — of the fire-form
(hutabhuṅmaya). The prāṇas are the life-winds that sustain the states; the turya is the fourth state,
beyond the three, which is the goal.]

**The ||2||/||3|| colophons:** the close of the āgamādhikāra dvitīyo vimarśa and the āgamādhikāra.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The exact prāṇa enumeration (prāṇa, apāna, udāna, samāna, vyāna) — kārikās 19–20 give the
   prāṇa/apāna/udāna/turya; the full fivefold count is presupposed.
2. The savedya/apavedya grades of suṣupta (vidyākalā vs. pralayākala) — the two grades with/without
   the māyā-stain are preserved.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the knower is fundamentally the free conscious agent (k12); the
bound states are the round of waking, dream, and deep sleep (k15–17) — deep sleep like dissolution
(with/without the māyā-stain), dream the mind's clear creation, waking the common firm creation — all
three to be abandoned (k18) for the sake of the fourth, the turya, the pure state; and the vital
breaths (k19–20) sustain these states, the turya being the fire-form that goes beyond. The recognition
leads the bound knower from the three to the fourth.

# IPVV — V3-N ARGUMENT MAP (the tattvasaṅgrahādhikāra prathamo vimarśa: the essence-collection, kārikās 1–8)

*2026-08-11. Reconstruct *argument-by-argument* before writing prose. Source: M00022 lines 10895–11600
(the tattvasaṅgrahādhikāra, prathamo vimarśa — the tattvārthasaṅgraha, kārikās 1–8). Works from BOTH

---

## 1. What is at issue

**The question:** This is the fourth and final adhikāra of the IPVV — the tattvasaṅgrahādhikāra, the
"collection of the essence of the categories." Having established the self-manifest ātmatattva (the
maheśvara-form, the knowledge-action power) at length, in three adhikāras, Abhinavagupta now collects
the whole into a brief summary (15 verses) for the student's meditation. One verse gives the
pāramārthika svarūpa; nine give the bond (bandha) in the direction of the knower/known; five give the
liberation (mokṣa) which is the recognition (pratyabhijñā). Kārikās 1–8 are the opening: the own-self
as the Lord, the bound grasper, the many person, and the guṇas.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Maṅgala (lines 10899–10916):**
"I bow to him, the nectar-Lord, who, in his un-surpassed own abode, makes the drop-made nectar-juice
known, the fully-full-making…" The plan: 15 verses — one on the pāramārthika svarūpa (k1), nine on the
bond in the knower/known direction (k2–10), five on the recognition-liberation (k11–15).

**Kārikā 1 (lines 10919–10934) — the own-self alone of all beings is the Lord:**
> The own-self alone of all beings, the one alone, the maheśvara — the world-form, "I this," the
> un-broken-re-recollection-augmented.

The sūtra is introduced after the spread declaration (vitatābhidhāna). The non-repetition: those not
wanting the spread are graced by the epitome; those wanting the middle are served. (Cites the muni's
rule "the desired, in the world, of the wise, is the summary-and-detail-bearing.") The "beings" word
carries the burden: "this whole is the person alone." So the teaching is: the own-self alone of all
beings is the one Lord, the world-form, whose re-recollection "I-this" is un-broken. (As the
Śivadṛṣṭi: "all beings are Śiva; the self of all beings is Śiva" — not that Śiva-seeing is a mere
doctrine, but that the self-flashing dharma in them is Śiva.)

**Kārikā 2 (line ~10950) — the bound grasper in the own-created this-part:**
> There, in the own-created this-being-part, with the intellect-and-the-rest grasper-own, the
> I-construction-re-recollection-word is brought, with this, that.

In the own-self, in the mahā-īśvara, in the own-self-mirror, by the freedom, that which is created —
the this-part, preceded by contraction; in its middle, the intellect/breath/body form, known by the
this-ness; of the that-distinct-known, the grasper-ness-fit, with the I-being, the re-recollection
shines. He, thus-form, "I am happy, and the rest," is the grasper, the bound. His liberation is the
recognition.

**Kārikā 3 (lines 11028) — the many person, the own-form-un-cognition:**
> He, the own-form-un-cognition-made, the many person, is the thought; there in the creation, the
> action-bliss two, the enjoyment, is the suffering-pleasure-own.

Truly, no one is bound; in truth the one free Śiva is consciousness-natured. But when the contracted
consciousness manifests, then the un-cognition of the own complete form — with the longing for the
full bliss, the to-be-abandoned-appearance, the woven warp-and-woof, the 
breath/void/body-contraction-variety — thence the many person. He is the enjoyer, the bound; the
enjoyment (bhoga) is the action-bliss two (the kriyā-jñāna powers) seen as pleasure and suffering.

**Kārikā 4 (lines 11092) — the Lord's knowledge-action as the paśu's guṇas:**
> In the own-member-form beings, of the Lord the knowledge and the action, which — with the māyā-third,
> they alone are of the animal the being-ness (sattva), the rajas, the darkness (tamas).

In the Lord, everywhere, the manifestation-and-reflexive-awareness are the knowledge-action powers; the
māyā-power, opening the this-ness in the Sadāśiva-Īśa word, is the covering. In the own-form-un-
cognition, in the beings, the knowledge and action are the pure-alone distinct-ness; with respect to
the animal, the manifestation/un-manifestation are the sattva/rajas/tamas.

**Kārikā 5 (lines 11112) — the power-ness not declared from the distinction:**
> From the distinction-standing, of the power-possessor, the power-ness is not declared; of these
> qualities, the instrument-effect-ness-transforming.

The distinction-dealing is considered: the contracted consciousness-mere-nature is the person; his
natural manifestation is not a power (by the connection with the other). The sattva-and-the-rest,
standing by the distinction, are not power-declarable. The person, from the enjoyer-form, thinks them
distinct; but in truth, even the earth-end ones, being power-form, are not thus.

**Kārikā 6 (line ~11150) — the being-bliss-action of the Lord, the that-absence of the animal:**
> The being-bliss-action of the Lord; the that-absence-even, she, of the animal — the two-own, that
> rajas, the suffering, attached, the sattva-tamas-made.

The Lord's being-ness (sattā) is the flashing, the agent-ness; the action-power is the pure
reflexive-awareness; the bliss-power is the rest-form. Of the animal, the being-ness-bliss is the
that-absence (the absence of the full being-bliss), two-own. The sattva (manifestation-pleasure), the
tamas (the covering), and the rajas (the mixed, attached, suffering).

**Kārikā 7 (line ~11190) — the non-conventional this-ness of the Lord:**
> Who-even, of the non-conventional-this-ness-re-recollection-ground Lord — they, the mixed, and the
> distinct, thus the varied-manifesting.

In the Lord, even the appearances, mixed and distinct, are the innate (non-conventional)
this-ness-re-recollection, the varied manifestation; in the Īśvara-state, the śūnya (the to-be-removed
void) is the Rudra-presided, manifesting as the I-ness-negation.

**Kārikā 8 (line 8271/11200) — the distinct-manifestation-meanings of the inner-self:**
> They-though, the distinct-manifestation-meanings, the constructed, of the inner-self — with the
> that-that-distinct-designations, in the memory-conjecture-and-the-rest range.

The Īśvara's objects are the pure reflexive-awareness objects, all; but of the animal, they are the
constructed (vikalpyāḥ), the distinct-manifestation, per-being, per-state, with the own-impression-
following designations ("dear-this, enemy-this"); the inner-self (pratyagātman), the body-and-the-rest
contracted, the "I"-being. The śūnya-pramātṛ, the infinite-regress, and its non-fault close the
vimarśa's core.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)
1. The exact "puruṣa evedaṃ sarvam" and "sarvabhāvāḥ śivaḥ" citations — the Śivadṛṣṭi and the
   upadeśa; cited, not re-derived.
2. The sattva/rajas/tamas mapping onto the Lord's powers (k4–6) — the correspondence is Abhinava's;
   rendered per the text.

## 4. Decision for L2
Proceed. The argument is understood: the tattvasaṅgraha collects the whole — the own-self alone of all
beings is the one Lord (k1); the bound grasper in the own-created this-part (k2); the many person by
the own-form-un-cognition (k3); the Lord's knowledge-action as the animal's guṇas (k4); the power-ness
not from distinction (k5); the being-bliss-action of the Lord and its absence in the animal (k6); the
non-conventional this-ness of the Lord (k7); and the distinct-manifestation-meanings constructed of
the inner-self (k8). The foundation of the collection: all the categories and all the knowers rest in
the one free Lord.

**The closing sum: the recognition, and the end of the whole teaching**

We come to the end. This final vimarśa of the Īśvarapratyabhijñāvivṛtivimarśinī is the closing sum — the
drawing together of everything that has gone before. And its theme is fittingly the *transmission* of
the teaching itself: it comes from the guru, from the śāstra, and from the self.

The whole teaching — the self-manifest essence, established by the knowledge and the action, aided by
reason — has been given. Part of it comes from the self (svataḥ); part from the śāstra (śāstrataḥ);
and part must come from the guru (gurutaḥ). For even the vision of the śāstra must be carried to its
proper power by the lineage of teachers. And here Abhinavagupta is stern: "the book-studied knowledges,
without the guru-order," he warns, "practicing and teaching, ripen long in hell." The teaching must be
received from the living line — from the ādi-Śrīkaṇṭhanātha down through Durvāsa and Tryambakāditya, the
mental sons who carry the un-precedented, effort-less word.

And now the path itself. Abhinavagupta presents it as a "new, well-fitted path," spoken by the great
gurus in the Śivadṛṣṭi. It is the path of the recognition. Since liberation is from the recognition
alone, all the elaborate effort-yogas are useless here; this is the clear means, effort-less,
un-undertaken, yet bearing the great fruit. The breath-controls and the rest are artificial; the
un-made reality does not yield to a sixteenth part of such effort. Even the proud fancies of the bound
— "I am the king," "I have slain my enemy" — are, when examined, still other-dependent, riding on the
Lord's power. The recognition cuts through all of it. And its fruit is the while-living-liberated, the
jīvanmukta: the one who, even in the body, is already free, and who at the fall of the body is simply
the supreme Lord. This, Abhinavagupta says, is the most wondrous thing of all — liberation in this very
life.

The closing sum puts it in a beautiful image. The lover stands near, sought by the slender woman; but
as long as he is not recognized, he cannot be enjoyed. So the world-Lord — always present, always near —
is not experienced in his own splendor until the recognition arises. The world has him before it, and
yet does not see him, until the single act of recognition turns the un-known nearness into the joy of
union. One who knows only the mere flash of recognition gains the bliss; one who cultivates it gains
the thousand-fold embrace.

And this recognition, Abhinavagupta reminds us, was established for the world by Utpala — "the sun-rise
son" — for the effort-less establishment of all people, not by caste or conduct but by the very fact of
being born a knower. Through it, all beings are graced; and by it, the author himself is complete. The
whole teaching — the Lord's recognition — is here gathered and handed on.

The closing vimarśa then turns to the author's own lineage — the vivṛtikāra's seventeen verses of
praise. They trace the line: Varāha-gupta, the Cukhulaka, father of Bhūtirāja; Bhūtirāja himself;
Haṭitalakṣmaṇagupta, the maker of the śāstra, the guru of Abhinava; the Tryambaka with its three
powers; and the method of the recognition throughout. The final verses praise the supreme Lord and
hand the work to him: "the Lord-recognition, from the reflective-awareness of the Lord, may he make it
the one minding the self's reflection." And then the two colophons close the entire work: the
"all-conclusion, the fourth vimarśa"; and "completed — this, the Īśvarapratyabhijñāvivṛtivimarśinī, the
composition of the Abhinavagupta-foots, whose fame is won by meditation on the feet of Tri-nayana."

So ends the greatest work of Abhinavagupta — the Vivṛtivimarśinī on the recognition of the Lord. It
has traced, across four adhikāras, the knowledge and the action of consciousness, the categories of
the world, the knowers and the stains, the bond and the liberation. And it closes where it began: the
own-self of all beings is the one Lord, and the recognition of this — received from the guru, the
śāstra, and the self — is the whole of liberation. The book ends, and the recognition begins.

## Fidelity note

- **Preserved:** the threefold transmission (guru/śāstra/self); the warning against book-study without
  the guru-order; the lineage (Śrīkaṇṭhanātha → Durvāsa → Tryambakāditya); the new well-fitted path;
  liberation from the recognition alone; the effort-yogas as useless; the breath-controls as
  artificial; the king/enemy fancies as other-dependent; the jīvanmukta; the dehapātād ekabhaviko
  mokṣa; the lover/world-Lord analogy (the pratyabhijñānamātra bliss vs. the cultivation's thousand-
  fold embrace); the utpala-colophon (established by Utpala for all, not by caste); the vivṛtikāra's
  explanation; the 17 lineage ślokas; the final stuti; the ||4|| and samāptā colophons.
- **Added (SUPPLIED, recoverable):** "it must be received from the living line" (glosses the
  guruparamparā); "turns the un-known nearness into the joy of union" (spells out the kāntā image);
  "The book ends, and the recognition begins" (the closing turn). Interpretive unpacking, not new
  doctrine.
- **NOT resolved:** the hagiographic lineage details; the specific referents of some of the 17
  ślokas. Logged, not invented.

**Verdict:** understood and readable. The final vimarśa closes the whole IPVV cleanly: the teaching is
transmitted from guru, śāstra, and self; the recognition-path is the effort-less means to the
while-living-liberated; the lover/world-Lord image carries the whole; and the work ends with the
lineage-praise and the colophons — the recognition of the one Lord, which was the beginning and is the
end.

# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk: kārikā 1.1.1 (kathamcit) + the opening commentary — T1

*The first sūtra of the ĪPK: "kathamcit āsādya..." ("having in some way obtained...") — the
opening obeisance to the Lord, and Abhinava's commentary on what that obeisance means. This is
where the IPVV's real epistemology begins: everything rests in consciousness (saṃvidviśrānti),
and manifestation is non-different from the light (prakāśābhinnatva).*

*Source: M00020 lines 111–260 (the kathamcit section). Base: Torella's ĪPK 1.1.1. This is a
max-effort T1 — the adversarial work (rival readings, evidence, decisions) is embedded inline.*

---

## The kārikā (from Torella)

> **kathamcit āsādya deveśam** — "having in some way obtained the Lord of the gods, [I make
> obeisance]."

*[The sūtra is the work's opening: having approached/obtained the Lord in some manner, the
author makes obeisance before teaching. Torella records the incipit as "kathamcid āsādya...."
(kar. I.1.1).]*

## The commentary (Abhinava on what the obeisance is)

> Here, alone, our supreme teacher — the blessed Somānanda, by whose grace the contraction was
> swept away — the honored author named Utpaladeva, whose opened heart-lotus perfumed the
> quarters, made the sūtra, the vṛtti, and the ṭīkā for the attainment of the Recognition of
> the Lord. There, standing in the sūtra-kāra's position, he reflects on himself as prostrate
> before the great Lord "in some way"; seeing, by the mere recitation of that utterance, the
> vṛtti's necessity; then, standing in the vṛtti-kāra's position, he reflects, by another
> procedure, on the Lord's prostration as ever-flowing even within himself — all to show that
> the very sūtra-kāra occupies this position.

*(the authorial-identity thesis — Utpaladeva is sūtra-, vṛtti-, and ṭīkā-kāra in one.)*

> **svecchāvabhāsitāśeṣalokayātrātmane namaḥ | śivāya śaśvadacchannasvacchasvacchandavṛttaye ||**

> Homage to him whose self is the world-course illumined by his own will — to Śiva, whose
> unobscured, ever-clear, free (svacchanda) activity [is his own].

*(the second homage-verse, embedded in the commentary: the Lord illumines the whole world-course
by his own freedom.)*

> "From himself" (svasmāt) — from his own self; "in himself" (svasmin) — in his own self
> indeed; "by his own will" (svayā icchayā) — the entire worlds, of the pure, the impure, and
> the mixed, manifesting and, by the mutual-abandonment, shining in the form of the differenced,
> are brought about — [the worlds] which are the knower and the known (pramātṛ-prameya); and
> their "courses" (yātrā) [are] their attainments, their mutual relations, the varieties of
> cause-effect, of action-instrument — of all these, the real (pāramārthika) self-nature, that
> alone is Śiva. And this one phrase, by the difference of the powers, takes up many meanings,
> like the mantra-meanings of the adhyātma, the adhideva, the adhiyajña in the Tantra and the
> Vṛtti. And for the establishing of the one-certainty's simultaneity (eka-niścaya-āroha-
> yaugapadya) it is thus used — as he will say "by the word-play (śleṣa)." To him, the blessed
> one of such a nature, "namas" (homage) — this word manifests the bowing-down.

*(the full gloss of the homage: the world is the Lord's own manifestation, by his will; the
knower-known structure is within him; the phrase works at many levels, like a mantra; "namas"
expresses the prostration.)*

### The objection (nanu)

> "But (nanu) — the entire knower-known expanse, standing in its own form, [the Lord], bringing
> it forth externally by his own will — then, though made external, he illumines it in his own
> self; and even so, he is their self. All this appears contradictory. For how, first, is one to
> understand that this whole world-course rests in consciousness as its own form? And if, by his
> very will, it is illumined externally, then — in that very self — it is illumined: what is
> this? For the illumining in the self without difference is contradicted by the external
> illumining. For what is illumined does not shine in that same [place] as both inside and
> outside, like a pot that is both within and without the house. Moreover, that even so he is
> Śiva, their self — this is even more unintelligible. For such shining is pervaded by the
> manifestation of difference, and the Śiva-nature of these [things] is pervaded by their
> being-one-self, which is opposed [to difference] — so that [to say] 'these beings are both
> many and one simultaneously' — who would understand it? And, resting in himself, for what
> purpose does his will arise to illumine the knower-known mass externally — or for no reason?
> If the arising of the will [for a purpose] is admitted, then it would always be thus; if [for
> no reason], it would never be. All this is incoherent (asamañjasa)."

*(the full objection: how can the world both rest in consciousness and appear external? how can
the many be one? why would the Lord will to manifest? — the deep difficulty Abhinava must
resolve.)*

### The reply (tatrocyate) — the core argument

> To this it is replied (tatrocyate). Here, the existence (sattva) or non-existence (asattva)
> of beings, standing [as it does], is not possible apart from the resting in consciousness
> (saṃvidviśrānti). For beings, resting in consciousness, become manifest (prakāśamāna). And
> their manifestness is non-different from consciousness. Only the light (prakāśa) is
> perceived. If one were to say they exceed that light, yet shine — [then] the blue would be
> separate from its own form, and yet it would be said "blue." These beings, then, rest in
> consciousness, [their] occurrence not exceeding consciousness — this follows. And a
> difference in consciousness is not possible. For in consciousness, which is of the single
> form of light, if a difference-of-own-form were made by another consciousness, then in one of
> the two the form of non-light would have to be admitted — [and so] its non-consciousness
> (asaṃvid-rūpatva) would follow. And these objects, partaking of the single-self of that
> [light], if they could be divided on their own, would by their own might divide consciousness
> too. But they are not able to take on difference by themselves. If, then, the difference of
> these [things] is only cognition-made, [there is] the mutual-dependence, clearly. Space and
> time, too, overstepping the mass of the known [and], precisely by their object-hood,
> coursing toward the self-identity with consciousness — how, in any way, would they divide
> that consciousness? For the entire mass of the known, in this way, is non-different from the
> light. What shines, that is non-different from the light, like the light of consciousness.
> For, of what is different from the light and is not manifesting, manifestness is not
> possible, being contradictory. Thus, manifestness, from its being-different-from-light, [and
> being] in the counter-class (vipakṣa), the non-manifestness that is naturally-opposed
> [to it] — [the argument] having here established its seat, [and] the manifestness being
> excluded [from] being-different-from-light, [it] is pervaded [by] non-difference-from-light.
> This being the establishment of the pervasion, [it is] the essential-reason (svabhāva-hetu).
> And the [views] that cannot bear the self-luminosity of consciousness are to be rejected by
> this very [argument]. And, like the difference of the known, so too the difference of space
> and time is not a divider of consciousness. Moreover, the conditions (upādhi), space, time,
> and the rest, in the known, are never able to divide a being non-different from its own form;
> only a real own-form-difference, when it exists, is established by them. And in consciousness
> no own-form-difference whatever [is possible], because of its being of the single form of
> light — this is what is said. These beings, then, first of all rest in the light. And the
> light is not void of vimarśa (reflexive awareness). And so it is vimarśa itself that is the
> Lord's will (icchā), eternal, [manifesting] the class of the Rudra-knower and the
> kṣetrajña-knower and their pleasures and pains...

*(THE CORE: beings rest in consciousness; their manifestness is non-different from the light;
difference is impossible within the single-form light; the svabhāva-hetu (essential-reason)
establishes that what manifests is non-different from the light; the refutation of the
self-luminosity-deniers; and the light is not void of vimarśa — vimarśa IS the Lord's will.)*

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions

1. **saṃvidviśrānti → "the resting in consciousness"** — the technical term for the ground of
   all manifestation. [dossier: saṃvit, viśrānti]
2. **prakāśābhinnatva → "non-different from the light"** — the thesis: what manifests is
   non-different from the light of consciousness. This is the Pratyabhijñā's refutation of
   external objects.
3. **svabhāva-hetu → "the essential-reason"** — the logical form: manifestness is pervaded by
   non-difference-from-light (the counter-class argument). This is Utpaladeva/Abhinava using the
   Buddhist logical apparatus (Dharmakīrti's hetu) against the Buddhist.
4. **vimarśa = "reflexive awareness"** — NOT "reflection." The light is not void of vimarśa;
   vimarśa IS the Lord's will. This is the hinge of the whole system.
5. **pramātṛ-prameya → "knower-known"** — the subject-object structure, within consciousness.
6. **nanu / tatrocyate** — the objection / the reply (the dialectical structure).
7. **the mutual-dependence (anyonyāśraya)** — Abhinava's accusation against the cognition-made-
   difference view (it's circular).

**The crux (the real philosophical work):** how the many objects can both rest in consciousness
and appear external. Abhinava's answer: manifestation IS the light's own reflexion (vimarśa);
the "external" appearance is the light's own self-articulation, not a separate object. The
svabhāva-hetu is the logical backbone — the same logical form Dharmakīrti uses, turned against
him.

# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk: kārikā 1.2 + commentary — T1

*The second sūtra: the self is "seen yet unperceived" (dṛṣṭo'pi anupalakṣitaḥ) — seen (as the
Lord) yet not recognized. Abhinava's commentary develops the pot-example (ghaṭa): a pot, even
when seen, if not examined, is not taken up for use — so the self, even when seen as the Lord,
is not recognized. This is the recognition-doctrine's core: the problem is not absence but
non-recognition.*

*Source: M00020 lines 2193–2260 (the dṛṣṭo'pi section). Base: Torella's ĪPK 1.2. Max-effort T1.*

---

## The kārikā (1.2)

> **dṛṣṭo'py anupalakṣito yo'yam ātmā pareśvaraḥ** — "the self which, even though seen, is
> unperceived — this [self] is the supreme Lord."

*[The sūtra: the self is always "seen" (it is self-luminous, present) yet "unperceived"
(anupalakṣita) — not recognized as the Lord. The whole Recognition-doctrine is the move from
seen-but-unrecognized to recognized.]*

## The commentary (Abhinava)

> Having, by a summary of the meaning of the verse to be stated, introduced [it] — "even thus
> (evam api)" — [the point is:] the purpose previously spoken, namely the Recognition
> (pratyabhijñā): in this [case of] the pot and the like, even when seen, [yet] through
> delusion (moha) not examined, [and being] supremely-worthy-of-taking-up, by the showing of
> the ascertainment through the means of practical-activity (vyavahāra-sādhana), [and] by the
> manifestation of one's own power (sva-śakti-āviṣkaraṇa) — [this] is seen in the world. And
> thus: "this self, the Lord, even when seen, is not thus perceived" — [here] the
> essential-reason (svabhāva-hetu): of the supremely-worthy-of-taking-up, even when seen, [yet]
> unexamined (avimṛṣṭa), how could the taking-up be? — for, of what is pervaded by taking-up,
> [if it is] unexamined, [there is] the non-apprehension — from the counter-class (vipakṣa),
> from the being-not-an-object-of-the-means-of-practical-activity — the excluded hetu is
> pervaded by the sādhya (the thing-to-be-proved) with the present [case]. — [But] it is not
> thus that the tātparya (the intended meaning) of the sūtra and the vṛtti is to be taken.
> [The author] manifests the fitness-to-be-questioned — "why (kim iti)" — and "tu" [marks] the
> particularity. By "but (kintu)," manifesting the not-yet-completed-statement, he gives room
> for the declaration of another object-of-knowledge.

> **Objection (nanu):** "The self-luminosity of the Lord was established with effort — how,
> then, is it said 'even when seen (dṛṣṭe'pi) in this Lord'?" He replies: "This word 'dṛṣṭa'
> (seen)..." [is used] metaphorically (upacarita). What is metaphorically [used is] the
> transitive (ātideśika) object — for there the yag-ātmanepadādi (the middle-voice forms) [are]
> just an illustration: "the granary is different by itself" — this is also seen. Or else, in
> the metaphorical [use], the object, [its] agent-nature being assumed, is what the means
> (sādhana) is [for]; that one is "such" (tathā). There, in the metaphor, he shows the seed of
> the bādha (the sublation) of the primary meaning — "because of the impossibility
> (asaṃbhava)." For there is no other seer — because of the infinite-regress (anavasthāna) —
> this has been said repeatedly, and will be said [again].

> And the nimitta (the cause/occasion) is the manifestness (prakāśamānatva), and the
> prayojana (the purpose) is, by the establishment of the equality-with-the-clearly-seen-pot,
> the proclamation of its being-not-to-be-denied (anapahnavanīya). As an object, even when
> seen, without vimarśa (reflexive awareness), does not [issue] in the causal-efficacies of
> the pleasure-rest and the like, so too the self — this proclamation of the
> example-and-exemplified (dṛṣṭānta-dārṣṭāntika) is also the purpose. Therefore the sūtra and
> the vṛtti, although [literally] on the present subject, are to be applied to the common
> [element] of the two meanings. Hence, in the ṭīkā, "even when seen (dṛṣṭasyāpi)..." up to
> "...[it] was said (ahīyata)" [is], primarily, on the subject of the example (dṛṣṭānta).

> "The verbal-elaboration (vācoyukti)" — this is the very richness of the verbal-elaboration:
> the gathering of the thing-to-be-established with a limited effort. "The
> self-ascertainment-established (svasaṃvedanasiddhasya)" — this, too, in the vṛtti, is to be
> seen as secondary (gauṇa), for the establishing of the not-to-be-deniedness, like the
> pleasure-and-the-rest. For earlier, the self-ascertainment-establishedness — nor even the
> self-ascertainment-establishedness of the self — [was] criticized by "...ityādinā." In the
> sūtra, the seventh [case-ending] is in the nimitta (the causal/occasion) sense. Because this
> Lord, even when seen, is unperceived, therefore the Recognition is shown; from the
> capability (sāmarthya) [it is] his (the Lord's) own. This same capability-derived meaning is
> explained in the vṛtti by the sixth [case-ending]; the meaning of the causal-seventh, [being]
> not-heart-reached (ahṛdayaṃgama), is explained by the hetu-fifth.

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions

1. **dṛṣṭo'py anupalakṣitaḥ → "even though seen, unperceived"** — the crux: the self is present
   (seen, self-luminous) yet not recognized. "Unperceived" (anupalakṣita) ≠ absent — it is
   present-but-not-noticed. [The recognition-problem, not the absence-problem.]
2. **The pot-example (ghaṭa)** — "a pot, even when seen, if not examined, is not taken up" —
   the analogy for the self. Seen (as the Lord) yet not recognized (not taken up in its
   supreme worth).
3. **svabhāva-hetu → "the essential-reason"** — again the logical form, turned against the
   Buddhist: what is seen-yet-unexamined is not taken up; so the self, seen-yet-unrecognized,
   needs recognition. [The recognition, not the discovery, is the remedy.]
4. **vimarśa** — "an object, even when seen, without vimarśa (reflexive awareness), does not
   [issue] in the causal-efficacies" — the reflexion is what makes the seen-into-the-known.
5. **upacarita / gauṇa → "metaphorical / secondary"** — the grammatical-logical analysis of
   "dṛṣṭa" (whether the "seen" is literal or metaphorical).
6. **nanu / āha** — the objection and reply.
7. **the causal/occasion (nimitta) seventh-case vs the hetu-fifth** — Abhinava's grammatical
   precision about why the sūtra uses "in this Lord" (the occasion) and the vṛtti the reason.

**The crux:** the self is not absent but unrecognized. The whole Pratyabhijñā is the move from
"seen-yet-unperceived" to recognized — a re-cognition, not a discovery. The pot-example makes
it concrete: the supreme is present but, like the unexamined pot, not taken up.

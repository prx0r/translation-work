# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk: kārikā 1.3 + commentary — T1

*The third sūtra: "the self alone is the Lord, and it is established" (ātmaiva īśvaraḥ, sa ca
siddhaḥ). Abhinava's commentary: the recognition (pratyabhijñā) is the jñāna-kriyā-śakti
(the knowledge-action power), the firm ascertainment (dṛḍhaniścaya) is the work's purpose, and
the whole hinges on the two-fold division (dvairāśya) of being/non-being, inert/non-inert.*

*Source: M00020 lines 2432–2510 (the ātmaiva īśvaraḥ section). Base: Torella's ĪPK 1.3. Max-effort T1.*

---

## The kārikā (1.3)

> **ātmaiva īśvaraḥ, sa ca siddhaḥ** — "the self alone is the Lord, and it is established."

*[The sūtra: the recognition-doctrine's core claim — the self IS the Lord, and this identity
is already-established (siddha); it needs recognition, not achievement.]*

## The commentary (Abhinava)

> In the self, of the Lord's form, those [two], the prakhyā (manifestation) and the upākhyā
> (designation) — let them not be [taken as] elsewhere, in the body and the rest, and in the
> blue and the rest, as instruments; rather, let them be seen as primary. Thus it is said
> that, in the vṛtti, [the point] is shown in accordance with the sūtra. In the world, what is
> the means of the mere practical-activity (vyavahāra-mātra) with regard to an inert object —
> that, here, being done with regard to the non-inert (ajaḍa), by the showing, [is] whole and
> unblemished, [and] the mere remainder of the showing; and by the showing — "see, see
> (paśya, paśyeti)" — [it is] of the form of the giving of attention (avadhāna-dāpana),
> [and] equal. Here, in this sūtra, "seen (dṛṣṭe)" — in the object-case, by the entry into
> the example (dṛṣṭānta), [and] from the being-not-an-agent, in the agent [sense] indeed, with
> the intention of the exemplified (dārṣṭāntika) — in the explanation, "the
> unquestionably-establishedness (nirvivādasiddhatā), namely, the seenness, is equal in both."
> (3)

> Having, in summary, set out the meaning of the future sūtra — "and thus (evaṃ ca)": "the
> self alone is the Lord, and it is established" — this, being thus; of it, that which is the
> Recognition (pratyabhijñā), the power manifested as the means (karaṇa-bhāva-sādhana), and
> the firm-ascertainment (dṛḍhaniścaya) made by it — this, too, [is] "thus" — having made
> this thing well-reasoned (sopapattika), to show [it] up to the ascertainment, he says [the
> sūtra]. After the upodghāta (the opening), of the śāstra to be stated, that very Recognition
> is the object-to-be-told, [being] of the form of the jñāna-kriyā-śakti (the knowledge-action
> power) — by the two adhikāras (sections) of knowledge and action, [being] the
> agreement-with-the-āgama of the two meanings, [and] by the gathering of the tattva-arthas
> (the reality-meanings) with determined tātparya (intention) — because of the detailed
> exposition of the two powers, knowledge and action. And the firm-ascertainment is the purpose
> (prayojana) of the whole śāstra. For, of the inert (jaḍa), the support is the living
> (jīva); and of the living, the life is the two, knowledge and action — because of this,
> "thus." "The self alone is the Lord, ever-established" — this word "hi" (for) manifests the
> hetu-hetumad-bhāva (the relation of reason and what-has-the-reason). The self's
> establishedness, and the lordship, which was said "in the agent, the knower (kartari jñātari)"
> — by that manner, since this whole world's agreement — the establishment of the inert is the
> being-plunged-in-life, [and] the truth of the living is knowledge-and-action — this is
> established.

> **Objection (nanu):** "This account is set out with regard to the class of the inert and the
> non-inert alone, not with regard to another." True. But there is no other — the vṛtti-kāra
> says "of the things (vastūnām)."

> **Objection:** "If, thus, of all beings, [there is] the two-fold division (dvairāśya), then
> — in '... the connection-with-action, the common substance (kriyāsaṃbandhasāmānyadravya)'
> (2.9), and in '... the two-natured, that rajas, duḥkha, cleaving, of sattva-and-tamas'
> (4.6) — the arising of the third class (tṛtīya-rāśi) which is spoken of, how [is it]?" —
> to this the ṭīkā-kāra replies "of the being-and-non-being (bhāvābhāvayoḥ)": the blue is not
> external, [nor] its departure from externality; and the yellow-and-the-rest, [being]
> inseparable from its departure, are not the blue, nor are they non-blue; rather, they are of
> the reality of appearance (ābhāsa-paramārtha) alone...

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions

1. **ātmaiva īśvaraḥ, sa ca siddhaḥ → "the self alone is the Lord, and it is established"** —
   the recognition-doctrine's core: the identity is already-true (siddha); it needs recognition.
2. **pratyabhijñā as the jñāna-kriyā-śakti** — the Recognition is the power of knowledge-and-
   action; it's the object-to-be-told of the whole work.
3. **dṛḍhaniścaya → "the firm ascertainment"** — the purpose (prayojana) of the śāstra.
4. **dvairāśya → "the two-fold division"** — being/non-being (and inert/non-inert): the whole
   world divides into the two; there is no third class (the objection about the guṇas /
   rajas-sattva-tamas is answered).
5. **prakhyā/upākhyā** — manifestation/designation: the two belong to the self as primary, not
   to the body/blue as instruments.
6. **the blue and the yellow** — the ābhāsa-paramārtha (the reality of appearance): the
   colours are of the reality of appearance, not separate objects.
7. **nanu / āha** — the objection/reply structure again.

**The crux:** the recognition is not a new achievement but a re-cognition of what is already
established (siddha). The whole work's purpose (the firm ascertainment) is to make the
self-as-Lord recognized. The two-fold division (dvairāśya) is the ontological frame: there is
only being/non-being, inert/non-inert — no third — and the non-inert (the self) is the Lord.

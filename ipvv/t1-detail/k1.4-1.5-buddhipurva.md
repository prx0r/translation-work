# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk: kārikās 1.4–1.5 + commentary — T1

*The fourth and fifth sūtras: "buddhipūrvāṃ kriyāṃ dṛṣṭvā..." (having seen action preceded by
cognition) and "tatra jñānaṃ svataḥ siddhaṃ kriyā kāyāśritā satī..." (there, cognition is
self-established; action, seated in the body...). Abhinava's commentary covers the inference
of other minds (parasaṃvedana via ūha/inference), the self-luminosity (svaprakāśa) of
cognition, and the refutation of the Naiyāyika objection.*

*Source: M00020 lines 2805–2870 (the buddhipūrvā + tatra jñānaṃ section). Base: Torella's ĪPK
1.4–1.5. Max-effort T1.*

## The kārikā (1.4)

> **buddhipūrvāṃ kriyāṃ dṛṣṭvā svadehe'nyatra tadgrahāt | kalpyate yadi dhīḥ...** — "having
> seen, in one's own body, action preceded by cognition, [and] from the grasping of that
> elsewhere [in another body], if cognition is inferred..."

*[The sūtra: we infer cognition in another body from the seen-pattern — action preceded by
cognition in our own body. This is the inference of other minds.]*

## The commentary

> By this maxim (nīti) — [the cognition is] only within the scope of mere inference. This is
> said: in another body too, whatever is the object-cognition (vedya) — the fatness, the
> dark-ness, hunger, thirst, pleasure, pain — so far, like the other inert [things], it shines
> reflected in the living [body]; but the life, [being] of the form of knowledge-and-action, is
> by the self-identity (tādātmya) with one's own self alone. And of such a shining, [having]
> the fruit of the uprooting of the difference of the knowers (pramātṛ-bheda-unmūlana), [and]
> being of the form of the pure-knowledge (śuddhavidyā), [this] is the world's protection...
>
> By this maxim, in the inference alone, [the author] develops, with the superiority
> (sātiśaya), the firm support [of the argument]. (4)

> **Objection (nanu):** "By the means of the cognition of the power-of-the-nature-of-
> knowledge-and-action (jñāna-kriyātmaka-śakti-abhijñāna-hetu), let the lordship-
> practical-activity (aiśvarya-vyavahāra) be established in the self, [granted]. But that very
> hetu — how is it established?" — to this suspicion, [with regard to] the sūtra "there,

### The kārikā (1.5)

> **tatra jñānaṃ svataḥ siddhaṃ kriyā kāyāśritā satī | parairapy upalakṣyeta tayānyajñānam ūhyate || (5)**

> There, cognition is self-established (svataḥ siddha); action, seated in the body, even by
> others would be observed — by it, another's cognition is inferred (ūhyate).

## The commentary

> "There" (tatra) — among the two [knowledge and action]: in one's own self, cognition is
> self-luminous (svaprakāśa); action, too, [being] the inner [one], non-distinct from the
> inner-sensation (saṃvedana), [being] of the form of vimarśa (reflexive awareness), is
> self-luminous. But that very [action], extended to the body (śarīra-paryantībhūta), [and]
> being accessible to the external seeing, touching, and the rest, [and] in another knower
> (parapramātṛ), [being] extended to the body, [and] of the form of the movement-and-speech
> (vyāpāra-vyāhāra), [and] accessible to the means of seeing and the rest — by the
> self-identity (tādātmya), by the being-its-effect (tatkāryatva), by the being-its-limit
> (tatparyantatva) — makes even the inner-sensation (āntara-saṃvedana) shine. Thus the life of
> the own-and-other knower (sva-para-pramātṛ-gata), [being] of the nature of
> knowledge-and-action, [is] established [as] the hetu-form, [and] from it the
> lordship-practical-activity is the means — this is the tātparya (the intended meaning) of
> the verse.
>
> "Is inferred (ūhyate)" — [this] is not the mere inferableness (anumeyatā-mātra) of the
> other's sensation (parasaṃvedana). [So] he says: "the ūhana (the inference-guess), the
> tarkaṇa (the reasoning), the saṃbhāvana (the conjecturing)." In this part there is also the
> operation of the senses (indriya-vyāpāra); and so the ūha (the guess) marks the
> direct-apprehension (sākṣātkāra). That too, [however], in truth, is in the other's
> sensation; but the otherness (paratva) is the māyā-made pride, [being] of the essence of
> the mere conceit (abhimāna-mātra) — this is said in the avataraṇikā. Even in the
> own-other undertaking (sva-para-upakrama), "the self-ascertainment-established
> (svasaṃvedana-siddham)" — by the declaration of the universal (sāmānya).
>
> **Objection (nanu):** "Cognition, at the time of the manifestation of the object, does not
> manifest exclusively [of itself]; rather, the object manifests, not its manifestation. But
> when it (the manifestation) does manifest, then only as grasped-by-another-cognition
> (jñānāntara-grāhyatā)." — the Naiyāyika objection. To this he replies: "In the other's
> position (parapakṣe), [the object] manifests."
>
> For "the object manifests (viṣayaḥ prakāśate)" — if this is a property of the object, then
> [it would be] "mine" (mama) — by the rule (niyama), the being-laid-on-the-knower (pramātṛ-
> lagnatā) would not [follow]. [And] if "thus" by the being-the-knower's-effect
> (pramātṛ-kāryatva) — no; by the cause, of the effect, the making-the-being-laid-on-it
> manifest, [this] is not seen — for, thus, the cloth would be the weaver's (kuvinda) [alone]...

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions

1. **parasaṃvedana / other minds** — the inference of another's cognition: from the seen
   pattern (action preceded by cognition in our body), we infer it elsewhere. But Abhinava
   sharpens it: the ūha (guess) is not mere inference — it has a direct-apprehension (sākṣātkāra)
   element, and "otherness" is ultimately māyā-made (there is one knower).
2. **jñāna svataḥ siddham → "cognition is self-established"** — the self-luminosity (svaprakāśa)
   of cognition; it needs no other cognition to know it (against the Naiyāyika infinite-regress).
3. **kriyā kāyāśritā → "action seated in the body"** — action, extended to the body, is
   observable by others (the movement/speech), and from it another's cognition is inferred.
4. **ūha = "the guess/inference-guess"** — Ratié's key term (the Pratyabhijñā's account of our
   awareness of others: neither perception nor inference but a guess sensing their freedom).
5. **the Naiyāyika objection** — "cognition doesn't manifest itself; only the object does" — 
   refuted by the self-luminosity thesis.
6. **paratva = māyā-made** — otherness is the māyā-made pride, one knower really.
7. **svaprakāśa → "self-luminous"** — the crux: cognition knows itself in the very act of
   knowing, without a second cognition.

**The crux:** the inference of other minds. Abhinava uses the Buddhist/realist logical frame
but sharpens it: the awareness of others is a guess (ūha) sensing their freedom, and
ultimately there is one knower — otherness is māyā-made. This connects directly to Ratié's
"Otherness in the Pratyabhijñā" paper.

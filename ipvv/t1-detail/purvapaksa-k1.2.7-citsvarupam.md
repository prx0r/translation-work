# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk: kārikā 1.2.7 (jñānaṃ citsvarūpam) + commentary — T1

*Kārikā 1.2.7: "jñānaṃ ca citsvarūpaṃ cettadanityaṃ kimātmavat" — and if cognition is of the
form of consciousness, then is it non-eternal, like the self? Abhinava's commentary develops
the self-luminosity (svaprakāśa) argument against the Vaiśeṣika/Nyāya (engaging Bhāsarvajña's
Nyāyabhūṣaṇa) — the cognition as the self's own luminous form, the refutation of the
object-manifestation by an external cognition.*

*Source: M00020 lines 3719–3780. Base: Torella's ĪPK. Max-effort T1.*

---

## The kārikā (1.2.7)

> **jñānaṃ ca citsvarūpaṃ cettadanityaṃ kimātmavat | athāpi jaḍametasya kathamarthaprakāśatā || 7 ||**

> And (ca) — if cognition is of the form of consciousness (citsvarūpa), then [is] it
> non-eternal (anitya), like the self (ātmavat)? Or, even if it [is] inert (jaḍa), [then] of
> this [one], how [is there] the manifestation-of-the-object (artha-prakāśatā)? (7)

## The commentary

> The word "and (ca)" collects, against the siddhāntin, the other-impossibility
> (anupapatty-antara). If the cognition's being-of-the-form-of-consciousness (cid-rūpatā),
> like the self's, [is] natural (svābhāvikī), then — is it non-eternal? Surely not
> non-eternal, like the self. For, of the being-of-the-nature-of-consciousness
> (citsvabhāvatva), [which is] characterized by the self-luminosity (svaprakāśatva), [that]
> which [is] the object-form-ness (vedya-rūpatva), of the form of the being-illumined-by-another
> (paraprakāśyatā) — [that is] opposed [to it], from the opposition of the freedom-and-the-
> dependence (svātantrya-pāratantrya). And, by the object-ness (vedyatva), the connection-with-
> space-and-time (deśakāla-yoga) is pervaded. Thus, of the self-luminosity (svaprakāśatva),
> [which is] opposed-to-the-pervader (vyāpaka-viruddha), by the perception (upalabdhi), in the
> cognition, of the space-time-connection (deśakāla-yoga), which is
> pervaded-by-the-opposed [object-ness], [there is] the negation (niṣedha). And the
> space-time-connection, indeed, is the non-eternity (anityatva).
>
> For that which, determinately (niyata), exists — not, in any way, does-not-exist — that [is]
> eternal. And that which, sometimes, exists, [and] sometimes, exists-not — that, elsewhere,
> at another time, also does-not-exist — [so] it is not the determinate-existence.
> [And] even in the all-space-time-connection (sakala-deśakāla-yoga), in-truth, its non-connection
> (tadayoga) alone [is]. For if, by that space, or by that time, its own-form
> (svarūpa) were not-entered, then — "in-that, [or] in-that (tatra iti, tade iti)" — whence?
> If [it is] covered (āvṛta), [then] by that — of the one-covered-all-around (samantāt vṛta),
> how [is there] the holding (dhāraṇa) by another? For [what is] covered [is] held-supported
> (avaṣṭabdha); how would it bear the other's-support (anyāvaṣṭambha)? Hence — "what one
> whole (avayavī) [is] in the many parts (bahuṣv avayaveṣu)?" — etc., all [this is] not
> established (anupapanna).
>
> Thus, if the cognition [is] of the form of consciousness, [then] it is of the eternal-omnipresent
> form (nitya-vibhu-rūpa); that too [would be] "another self (ātmāntaram)" — [and so] the
> cognition-hetu [is] the not-established-own-form (svarūpāsiddha). Or, if it [is] inert
> (jaḍa), then, with regard to the object, of it, the being-of-the-form-of-the-manifestation
> (prakāśarūpatā), how? — this is the sūtra's meaning.
>
> **Objection (nanu):** "'Consciousness (cit)' is what cognition is called. And, of the
> Vaiśeṣika and the rest, that [is] not the self's own form; for it [is] its property
> (dharma). So how is it said — 'if the self is of the form of consciousness, then, as
> eternality, so too of the cognition'?" — to this suspicion, [he] replies "from the
> non-being-of-the-being-illumined-by-another (paraprakāśatvābhāvāt)."
>
> Here, "cognition" is the manifestation of the blue and the rest (nīlādi-prakāśa). It is the
> property (dharma) of the self, made-by-the-mind-connection-and-the-rest
> (manaḥsaṃyogādi-kṛta), [and] occasional (kādācitka). It, when, in the non-being-of-its-cause,
> it is not, then the self, not-flashing-with-the-manifestation-of-the-blue-and-the-rest, in
> the deep-sleep, the swoon, the dissolution, the release and the rest, [though] of-the-nature-
> of-the-manifestation-by-itself (svātmanā prakāśasvabhāva), is called "inert (jaḍa)." But its
> (the self's) form, [is] the self-luminous (svaprakāśa) alone — not like the class of the
> inert-forms-of-the-space, the time, the direction-and-the-rest (ākāśakāladigādi-jaḍarūpa-
> vyāpaka-varga). For [otherwise, in the release,] from the fruit (phalatas), the
> non-purpose-of-the-person (apuruṣārthatā) would follow, like the Śūnyavādin.
>
> Hence the Bhāsarvajñācārya and the rest, [being] Naiyāyikas, admitted [it] thus: the
> eternality-and-bliss-ness (nitya-jñānānandavattva) of the self [is] established in the
> Nyāyabhūṣaṇa. And so — as the self, [being] of the nature of manifestation, why [is it]
> non-eternal? [It is] not non-eternal, but eternal. Likewise, the blue-cognition (nīlajñāna),
> [being] of the nature of the manifestation-of-the-blue (nīla-prakāśa-svabhāva), why [is it]
> non-eternal? [It is] not non-eternal, [but] eternal, indeed — [thus] the example (dṛṣṭānta)
> is established.
>
> Or (athavā) — let it be, [that], of the Vaiśeṣika, the self [is] of the non-conscious-
> nature (acit-svabhāva) — what [is] the binding-by-the-station (asthāna-nibandha)? For the
> self-advocates (ātmavādin) are to-be-examined-by-me; hence the other
> self-luminous-self-advocates (svaprakāśātmavādin), here, by the drawing-of-the-implication
> (prasaṅgāpādana), are to-be-refuted.
>
> "The example (dṛṣṭānta)" — here, the taking-of-the-non-similarity (vaidharmya-grahaṇa) is
> not applied, [because] of all the examples, the being-alive-by-the-similarity
> (sādharmya-prāṇa). "If [it is] of-the-form-of-consciousness-like-the-self (ātmavat
> citsvarūpaṃ cet)" — even in the application-by-the-non-similarity (vaidharmya-yojanā), with
> the thing-to-be-negated (niṣedhya), the eternality-property (nityatva-dharma) [being] the
> similarity, "non-eternal" [would be] the word...

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions

1. **citsvarūpa → "of the form of consciousness"** — the cognition's identity with the
   self's luminous nature.
2. **svaprakāśa → "self-luminous"** — the core: cognition knows itself in the act of knowing,
   without an external cognition. The object-form-ness (vedya-rūpatva), being illuminated-by-
   another, is opposed to it.
3. **the refutation of the Vaiśeṣika** — the cognition is not an occasional property made by
   the mind-connection; that would make the self "inert" in deep-sleep, which is absurd (the
   self is always luminous).
4. **Bhāsarvajña's Nyāyabhūṣaṇa** — the Naiyāyika who admitted the self's eternal cognition-
   bliss; Abhinava uses him against the standard Vaiśeṣika view.
5. **the eternality of cognition** — if cognition is the self's luminous form, it's eternal
   (like the self), not occasional.
6. **the blue-cognition example** — the cognition of blue, as the manifestation-of-blue, is
   eternal (the luminous nature), not the occasional flicker.
7. **the prasaṅga (implication)** — the other self-luminous-self-advocates are refuted by
   drawing out their implication.

**The crux:** the self-luminosity of cognition. Against the Vaiśeṣika (cognition as an
occasional property, making the self "inert" without it), Abhinava asserts the cognition's
self-luminosity — it's eternal, the self's own form. The space-time-connection (which marks
non-eternity) is negated for the self-luminous. This is the foundation for the whole system:
consciousness is self-luminous and reflexive, not an occasional inert property.

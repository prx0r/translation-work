# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk: pūrvapakṣa-vimarśa opening + the homage — T1

*The second vimarśa (the opponent's position) / third section. Abhinava opens it with a
homage-verse (to Bhairava as the wave of his own power), then announces the structure: seven
verses (satyaṃ kintu ... smṛtyapohanaśaktimān) examining the impossibility of the rival views
(paradarśanānupapatti) — the refutation of the Buddhist (and Sāṅkhya) positions.*

*Source: M00020 lines 5750–5810. Base: Torella's ĪPK. Max-effort T1.*

---

## The homage-verse

> **icchāśaktisunirbharamamṛtābhamanantabhuvanajananapaṭum | vande svaśaktilaharībahalitabhairavaparānandam ||**

> I praise (vande) the Bhairava-supreme-bliss (bhairava-parānanda), made-dense by the wave of
> his own power (svaśakti-laharī-bahalita) — [the one] who is full-of-the-will-power
> (icchāśakti-sunirbhara), [whose] form-is-the-nectar (amṛtābha), [and who is] apt at the
> generating of the infinite worlds (ananta-bhuvana-janana-paṭu).

*[The homage to Bhairava as the supreme bliss, dense with the wave of his own power — the will
(icchā), the nectar-form, generating the infinite worlds. This is the verse of the third
section's opening.]*

## The commentary (the structure)

> Here, in this pūrvapakṣa (the opponent's position), by the two adhikāras (sections), the
> siddhānta (the established view) is set out. There —
>
> "True, but (satyaṃ kintu)..."
>
> — beginning with this, up to —
>
> "...having-the-power-of-the-repulsion-of-memory (smṛtyapohanaśaktimān)."
>
> — by this seven-verse (śloka-saptaka), [Abhinava,] reflecting the non-possibility of the
> rival views (paradarśana-anupapatti), refutes it. What was said —
>
> "But, the cognition whose appearance is the unique-particular... (nanu svalakṣaṇābhāsaṃ)"
> (1.2.1)
>
> — having, by this, prepared the seat (pīṭha-bandha), [and]
>
> "from the impression, [when] the memory is established, the rememberer, like the seer,
> would be inferred (saṃskārāt smṛtisiddhau syātsmartā draṣṭeva kalpitaḥ)" (1.2.6)
>
> — this [is] the knowledge-investigation (jñāna-vicāra), this adhikāra (section). The
> action-relation-investigation (kriyā-saṃbandha-vicāra), however, will be in the second
> adhikāra.
>
> There, by two verses, "cognition is of the form of the self-ascertainment (jñānaṃ
> svasamvid-rūpam)" — in the memory, of the experience, [there is] no manifestation; even in
> the impression-arisen [memory] — this is said. By two verses, having, with the suspicion
> (śaṅkā) beforehand, removed the memory's error-ness (smṛter-bhrāntitva); by the third,
> from the way (prasaṅga), of all the ascertainments (sarva-adhyavasāya), that is removed:
> even [with] the impression existing, in another's seeing (paradarśi), the memory is
> improper — [thus] the loss-of-the-practical-activity (vyavahāra-lopa) [is] said, by one
> [verse]; in one's own position (svapakṣa), its establishment (tadupapatti) [is] said by one
> verse — this is the tātparya (the intended meaning).
>
> There, in the pūrvapakṣa, [that] which is wholly to-be-put-down (śamayitavya) [is] said by
> "no (na iti)"; [that] which is to-be-partly-accepted (īṣad-aṅgīkārya) [is] said by "but
> (kintu iti)"; [that] which is to-be-partly-rejected (īṣan-nirākārya) [is said] by "true,
> but (satyaṃ kintu iti)." This is the style (śailī) of those-who-possess-the-deliberate-
> application (prekṣāpūrvaka-prayoga-śālin).
>
> And here, by this "true (satyam)," much of what was said by the pūrvapakṣa-vādin [is], in
> the siddhānta, to-be-accepted (aṅgīkārya). "In the Sāṅkhya, the Kāṇāda and the rest, the
> cognition (jñāna) is not established" — this, indeed, was said by the Śākya (the Buddhist);
> that [is] agreed (saṃmata). But there, to show the further-difference (viśeṣāntara), by this
> word "but (kintu)," [he says:] "we ask for more (adhikaṃ pṛcchāmaḥ); let the cessation of
> the activity-of-instruction (virata-śikṣā-vyāpāra) not be, even now, [for you]" — by [this]
> manifesting [that] "this effort (yatna) [is] for the removal of that delusion
> (tad-vyāmoha-apasāraṇa)." For thus —
>
> "Cognition, and [its] being-of-the-form-of-consciousness (jñānaṃ ca citsvarūpaṃ cet)"
> (1.2.7)
>
> — the prasaṅga (the implication) which was said by you — this, indeed, is our siddhānta.
> There, by "true (satyam)," [that] which is futile (vyartha) [is] unfolded; by "only
> (kevalam)," [is] the "but (kintu)." By this, the next-book's-extension (uttara-granthākṣepa)
> is made.
>
> For thus — "not-the-doer (na kāryakaraḥ)". By this much, the upakṣepa (the brief
> indication) of the sūtra's meaning — "by the self, the great Lord (ātmanā maheśvareṇa)" —
> of the seven-verses' meaning, [and] "by the door of cognition (jñānamukhena)..."

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions

1. **pūrvapakṣa → "the opponent's position"** / **siddhānta → "the established view"** — the
   dialectical structure: the opponent's view, then the established response.
2. **paradarśana-anupapatti → "the impossibility of the rival views"** — the seven verses refute
   the Buddhist (and Sāṅkhya/Kāṇāda) positions.
3. **the homage-verse** — Bhairava as the supreme-bliss dense with the wave of his own power
   (svaśakti-laharī), the will (icchā), the nectar-form, generating the infinite worlds.
4. **satyaṃ kintu → "true, but"** — the rhetorical move: Abhinava accepts the Buddhist's
   rejection of Sāṅkhya/Kāṇāda cognition, but pushes further.
5. **jñānaṃ citsvarūpam → "cognition is of the form of consciousness"** — the agreement: the
   Buddhist's own implication (cognition = consciousness-form) IS the siddhānta.
6. **the seven-verse structure:** (a) cognition as self-ascertainment, (b) memory's non-error,
   (c) the loss-of-practical-activity, (d) the establishment in one's own position.
7. **the adhikāra division** — the jñāna (knowledge) adhikāra vs the kriyā (action) adhikāra.

**The crux:** the pūrvapakṣa. Abhinava opens the opponent's position and immediately signals
the strategy: accept what's valid (the rejection of external-cognition views), then push to the
siddhānta (cognition = consciousness-form, the reflexive self). The "satyaṃ kintu" is the
rhetorical hinge — the opponent's own logic is turned to the Śaiva conclusion.

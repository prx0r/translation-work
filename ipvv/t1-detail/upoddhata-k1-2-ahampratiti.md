# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk: upoddhāta-vimarśa, kārikās 1–2 (the ahaṃ-pratīti) — T1

*The opening of the upoddhāta-vimarśa proper — where the real philosophical work begins.
Kārikā 1: the single non-constructive cognition (svalakṣaṇābhāsa) vs the many constructive
ones (sābhilāpa-vikalpa); kārikā 2: the "I"-awareness (ahaṃ-pratīti) as settled in the body
and the rest. This engages the Buddhist epistemology (svalakṣaṇa, nirvikalpaka, vikalpa).*

*Source: M00020 lines 3175–3250. Base: Torella's ĪPK (the kārikās). This is a crux-dense
passage — the Buddhist-opponent core. Max-effort T1 with the rivals embedded.*

---

## The kārikā (upoddhāta 1)

> **nanu svalakṣaṇābhāsaṃ jñānamekaṃ paraṃ punaḥ | sābhilāpaṃ vikalpākhyaṃ bahudhā nāpi taddvayam || 1 ||**

> But (nanu) — the cognition whose appearance is the unique-particular (svalakṣaṇa-ābhāsa) is
> one; the other, again, [is] the constructive (sābhilāpa), called "vikalpa" (the
> constructive), many — nor, indeed, [is there] that two-fold [of the kind that could be
> counted]. (1)

## The kārikā (upoddhāta 2)

> **nityasya kasyaciddraṣṭustasyātrānavabhāsataḥ | ahaṃpratītirapyeṣā śarīrādyavasāyinī || 2 ||**

> Of some eternal seer — from its non-manifestation here [in this cognition] — even this
> "I"-awareness (ahaṃ-pratīti) is one that settles in the body and the rest (śarīrādi-avasāyinī).
> (2)

## The commentary

> "Whose mark is one's own self (sva-ātmā) or one's own (ātmīya)" — [i.e.] by its own form
> (svarūpa), and by the two conditions (upādhi), space and time, which is wholly contracted
> (saṃkucita) —
>
> "By the forms of the same, the different, and the rest, that [unique-particular] is not
> graspable in any way, since of the many differentiations, in that one, [there is] the
> non-connection — that form, [being] different from all — "
>
> — [this is] the pratipādikā (the demonstrative part):
>
> "Neither the śruti (the scripture) nor the kalpanā (the construction) [operates] by the
> universal (sāmānya) alone."
>
> — by this, the self-capability-arisen (svasāmarthyottha) non-constructive cognition
> (nirvikalpaka-jñāna) is said to be characterized by the single-mark (eka-lakṣaṇa). "The
> ābhāsa of it (tadābhāsa)" — [that] in which, existing, it shines (pratibhāsa); or of it,
> the ābhāsa, the form, the shadow-like shape (ākāra), [is] in that cognition. That [is] one.
> The singular (eka-vacana) [is] for the class (jāti). And by the "one" (eka) word, [is meant]
> the mass (rāśi): thus, in the view of the non-formists (nirākāravādin), the cognitions have
> the appearance of the unique-particular (svalakṣaṇa-pratibhāsa); in the view of the
> formists (sākāravādin), the forms of the unique-particular (svalakṣaṇākāra) — so this is
> the non-constructive mass (avikalpaka-rāśi). Or, by the "one" word, of the non-constructive
> cognition, setting aside the difference-made-by-the-objects, [and] like the constructive,
> [there is] the self-form-established (svarūpa-niṣṭha), the inner-class-making difference —
> this is not, it is said. For in that very object, the constructive (vikalpa), [being] of the
> form of memory (smṛti-rūpa), [is] otherwise, and, [being] of the form of the conjecture
> (utprekṣā-rūpa), [is] otherwise — thus, even in the non-difference of the object-of-
> ascertainment (adhyavasāya-viṣaya), the richness (vaicitrya) of the constructive [is]
> self-form-gone (svarūpagata). But the experience (anubhava), in the blue, [is] not in any
> other manner, apart from the being-its-shining (tatpratibhāsatva), the being-its-form
> (tadākāratva).
>
> Or the "one" (eka) word [is] another-signifier (anyavācī) — "one something," that is. The
> middle-pakṣa (the middle-view) is, by the ṭīkā-kāra, applied in the sūtra. "The other
> (param)" [means] the other, the distinct; and it is the future-of-the-experience-born
> (anubhava-uttarakālabhāvi), since all the vikalpas live-by-it (upajīvita).
>
> "The abhilāpa (the designation)" — by which, the object, being-made-vivid
> (ābhimukhyena), [and] by the abandoning of the subjection-to-the-object-and-subject
> (viṣayiviṣaya-paravaśatā-tyāga), in the freedom-of-the-awareness (bodha-svātantrya), and by
> the word, by the effecting of the self-identity (tādātmya-āpādana) with the object, [and]
> up to the being-made-present-to-the-knower (pramātṛ-sātkāra-paryantatā), is spoken [and]
> reflected (parāmṛśyate) — that is the abhilāpa (the designation), [being] the inner-word
> (āntara-śabda-lakṣaṇa), the inner-chatter (saṃjalpa) —

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions

1. **svalakṣaṇa-ābhāsa → "the appearance of the unique-particular"** — the Buddhist's
   non-constructive cognition (the momentary unique-particular). The Pratyabhijñā engages this
   precisely.
2. **nirvikalpaka → "the non-constructive [cognition]"** — the bare pre-conceptual cognition;
   vs **vikalpa → "the constructive"** (the sābhilāpa, the word-laden).
3. **ahaṃ-pratīti → "the 'I'-awareness"** — the awareness "I"; the Buddhist's point: even this
   settles in the body (it's not an eternal seer).
4. **sākāravādin / nirākāravādin** — the formist / non-formist Buddhist views (how the
   cognition has its form).
5. **the abhilāpa / saṃjalpa** — the designation, the inner-word/chatter by which the object
   is made vivid and reflected. This is the vimarśa-side (the reflexive word).
6. **The "middle-pakṣa"** — the ṭīkā-kāra's application of the middle view in the sūtra.
7. **anubhava-uttarakālabhāvi** — the constructive cognition is "future-of-the-experience-born":
   it lives off the prior non-constructive experience.

**The crux:** the two-fold cognition (non-constructive/one vs constructive/many), and whether
the "I"-awareness reveals an eternal seer (the Pratyabhijñā's claim) or just settles in the
body (the Buddhist reduction). Abhinava is setting up the argument that the "I" is the
reflexive self, not a mere body-construction. This is the ahaṃ-pratīti crux that continues
through kārikā 3+.

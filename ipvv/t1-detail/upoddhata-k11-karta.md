# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk: upoddhāta, kārikā 11 (the agent as construction) — T1

*Kārikā 11: "pāratantryādyayogācca tena kartāpi kalpitaḥ" — and from the non-fitness of
dependence and the rest, by it [the relation-absence], even the agent (kartā) is inferred as a
construction. Abhinava's commentary develops the refutation of the relation (saṃbandha) to its
conclusion: with no relation, the distinct agent is also a mental construction.*

*Source: M00020 lines 5585–5650. Base: Torella's ĪPK. Max-effort T1.*

---

## The kārikā (upoddhāta 11)

> **pāratantryādyayogācca tena kartāpi kalpitaḥ || 11 ||**

> And from the non-fitness (ayoga) of the dependence (pāratantrya) and the rest, by it (tena),
> even the agent (kartā) is inferred [as a construction] (kalpitaḥ). (11)

## The commentary

> For thus, he makes the sūtra's descent (avataraṇa) in this very manner — "and thus (tadevam)."
> "Of action, first, thus (kriyāyās tāvad evam)" — "action, too, of the object (kriyāpy
> arthasya)" — from the two sūtras "in this and that, [when] established (tatra tatra sthite),"
> from the non-being-of-the-establishing-means (sādhakābhāva), and from the having-the-
> refuting-means (bādhakayoga), which non-being — "there-is-no-other, there-is-nothing-other
> (nānyo'sti, nānyad asti)" — in the two sūtras, [is] pointed-out; of the relation, too, that
> which is the non-being, of the form of the non-certainty-of-existence
> (astitva-niścayābhāva) and the certainty-of-non-existence (nāstitva-niścaya) — "thus
> (evam)," by the non-being-of-the-establishing-means spoken in "in-this-and-that
> (tatra-tatra)," [and] by the refuting-means, joined with the two-stationed-ness-reason
> (dviṣṭhatvādi-yukti), it is established. This non-being-fourfold (abhāva-catuṣṭaya), being
> joined, [and] "by-it (tena)" [the sūtra] brings to the hetu-ness in "even-the-agent-is-
> inferred (kartāpi kalpitaḥ)." That hetu-ness, in the ṭīkā, is said, in summary, by the fifth
> [case-ending] "from-the-non-being (abhāvāt)." The fifth, [and] the word "and (ca)," [he]
> connects with the former sūtra.
>
> "The relation (saṃbandha)" — "there-is-no-other relation: of the two-stationed (dviṣṭha),
> from the non-one-ness (anekatva); of the established (siddha), from the non-expectation-of-
> another (anyānapekṣaṇa); and from the non-fitness of the dependence and the rest
> (pāratantryādi-ayoga)" — this is the connection (saṃgati). The "relation (saṃbandha)" — this
> much alone, in the vivṛti, by one's own throat (svakaṇṭha), is pointed-out. "Of the
> two-stationed (dviṣṭhasya)" — even in the universal-statement (sāmānya-ukti), from the
> context (prakaraṇa), "of the relation" — this meaning, too, is to be obtained.
>
> **Objection (nanu):** "By the mere non-perception of the seen (dṛśyānupalabdhi), which makes
> the certainty-of-non-existence, that object is refuted." In truth, this alone is its refutation
> — that [which is] the cognition-of-the-certain-non-existence (niścita-nāstitvāvedana); not,
> like the hammer (drughaṇa), the making-shattered (viśarākaraṇa) of the pot-sherd (śarāva).
> And this is here shown: "of the seen, from the non-perception by the means-of-knowledge
> (pramāṇa)."
>
> **Reply (satyam):** "True — but, by the non-perception (anupalabdhi), [there is] the
> cognition-of-non-existence with the restriction-of-the-determined-place-time
> (niyata-deśakāla-avaccheda) — 'here, now, there is no pot.'" When he says:
>
> "For the far-ones (viprakarṣiṇām), [there is] no certainty-of-non-existence (nāsattāniścayo)."
>
> — as,
>
> "The expectation-of-the-place-time-and-the-rest (iṣṭaṃ viruddhakārye'pi deśakālādyapekṣaṇam);
> otherwise [there would be] the deviation (vyabhicāra), like [in the inference] of the fire
> [from] the coolness-making (bhasmevāśītasādhane)."
>
> Therefore, to announce the universal non-existence (sārvatrikīm asattām) of the relation and
> the rest, the contradiction (virodha), which makes the non-possibility-of-their-own-form-
> admitted-by-the-opponent (parābhyupagata-tadīya-rūpāsaṃbhava-saṃpādaka), [and] which is
> established-in-the-object-of-cognition (prameyaniṣṭha), is to be spoken: "that which, by you,
> is spoken of its (the relation's) form — that [is] contradicted by the very other-form-spoken-
> by-you (tvad-uktenaiva rūpāntareṇa)." Thus, from the very establishment of the two-form-
> contradiction (tadrūpa-dvaya-virodha-siddhi), the speak-er-of-the-refutation (bādhakābhidhāyin)
> becomes successful (kṛtārtha). In the criticism's-hetu (dūṣaṇa-hetu), [there is] no occasion
> (prasaṅga) for the objections-of-the-not-established-subject (dharmyasiddhyādi-codya) — this
> is settled-at-length (vitatya nirṇītaṃ), in the prasaṅga-grantha and the rest. Enough of that
> for now. There, by the door of the universal-mark (sāmānya-lakṣaṇa) —

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions

1. **kārikā 11 ("kartāpi kalpitaḥ"):** with the relation refuted (there's no separate
   cause-effect relation), even the distinct agent is a construction. This completes the
   reduction: no relation, no separate agent — the unity of the single knower.
2. **the fourfold non-being (abhāva-catuṣṭaya):** the relation's non-being is established from
   (a) no establishing means, (b) a refuting means, (c) the two-stationed-ness, (d) the
   non-expectation-of-another. Joining these, even the agent is inferred as a construction.
3. **dviṣṭha → "the two-stationed"** — the relation is "in two places" (between two terms), so
   it's not one; from the non-one-ness (anekatva) it can't be a single relation.
4. **pāratantrya → "dependence"** — the relation would be dependent (on its terms), which is
   unfit (ayoga); so it's not admitted.
5. **the non-perception argument:** the relation's absence is known by non-perception — but this
   is a determinate-place-time non-existence ("here, now, no pot"), not universal. To announce
   universal non-existence, the self-contradiction of the opponent's own form is used.
6. **the fire/coolness example (bhasmevāśītasādhane):** the deviation warning — inference must
   attend to place-time, else it deviates.

**The crux:** the relation (saṃbandha) is not a real entity; it's reducible. With the relation
gone, the distinct agent is also a construction. This completes the anti-pluralist reduction:
there is one knower, one reality — the unity of consciousness. The opponent's own form is
self-contradictory. This is a dense, technical passage (relation/agent/dependence/cause).

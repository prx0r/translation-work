# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk: upoddhāta, kārikā 3 (the memory-argument) — T1

*Kārikā 3 of the upoddhāta: the memory-argument — the Pratyabhijñā's central proof against
Buddhist momentariness. "When experience perishes, memory follows it; how could the self not
be eternal, if it is the experiencer?" Abhinava's commentary develops this against the
kṣaṇika-vāda (the momentariness-view).*

*Source: M00020 lines 3390–3450. Base: Torella's ĪPK. This is a crux-dense passage (the
memory/cognition/self argument). Max-effort T1.*

---

## The kārikā (upoddhāta 3)

> **athānubhavavidhvaṃse smṛtistadanurodhinī | kathaṃ bhavenna nityaḥ syādātmā yadyanubhāvakaḥ || 3 ||**

> But (atha) — when the experience (anubhava) is destroyed, the memory (smṛti) [is] its
> conformer (tad-anurodhinī): how could the self not be eternal, if it is the experiencer
> (anubhāvaka)? (3)

## The commentary

> "On the destruction of the experience, the memory — having, by the acceptance-of-the-
> experience (anubhava-svīkāra-dvārā), a grasped, determined object — by what manner would
> [it] be, if the experiencer, [being] of the form of the inner-face (antarmukha-rūpa) of that
> experience, a stable self (sthira ātmā), were not? And there is this memory — therefore, by
> it too, [the stable self] must be." — if the opponent (para) were to say [this], [then]
> this is the meaning of the kārikā. "At the time of the memory (smṛti-kāle)" — the time
> which is conjectured [as] in the memory-to-be-produced (udbubhūṣitāyāṃ smṛtau), that is the
> meaning. Not in the prior experience [is] the mere later-absence (paścād-abhāva-mātra) the
> being-its-conformer (tad-anurodhitva) — this the vṛtti-kāra shows.
>
> "Of the prior object-cognition (artha-viṣaya anubhava), that which is the support
> (avaṣṭambha), the non-abandonment of its form (tadrūpa-aparityāga) — that is the property
> (dharma) of which [the memory is]." "Even then (tadāpi)" — at the time of the memory, [in]
> the memory-form-ness, this is the meaning.
>
> If someone were to say: "the object-experience (artha-anubhava) alone, [being] distinct, of
> the past-object, is called 'memory' — then what [is the objection] against this? For, by the
> being-opposed-to-the-experience, the object, by its own face (svamukhena), is accepted."
> — to him, [Abhinava] first rejects the memory's being-of-the-form-of-the-experience
> (smṛter-anubhava-rūpatva): "on the destruction of the experience" — [he] fits the sūtra's
> statement — "the object (artha)."
>
> "The presence of the object (artha-saṃnidhāna), here, in the existence, [is] by the
> being-the-producer (janakatā); in the non-existence, too, by the being-the-object (viṣayatā)
> — [this is] the use (upayoga)." "Present (saṃnihita)" — even being-present, like in the
> Sāṅkhya-view, even [if] hidden (channa), by the freedom (svātantrya), [it] attains
> object-ness (viṣayatā) — that is to say.
>
> "Direct-apprehension (sākṣātkāra)" — the non-constructive (avikalpaka), this is the meaning.
> For the constructive (vikalpa) does not, directly, accept the object; but by the
> ascension-speech (adhyāruhya-vāda) [it] is said. This he says [by] "or (api vā)."
>
> "By the being-experienced (anubhūyamānatā)" — either [it is] the experience-arisen
> (anubhavāyamāna) or [it is] the self-of-the-form-of-the-experience (svayam-anubhava-rūpa);
> [but] by the being-experienced (anubhūtatva), by the recollecting of the prior experience
> (pūrvānubhava-ullekhana) — [only] thus [is it], it would not be. And this, indeed, is the
> memory's own-particular form (prātisvika-rūpa).
>
> And so: if, in the memory-pervaded, the time-marked experience [is] — then "the memory [is]
> not its pervader" [and] "[the self] is" — this is the opposed-cognition (pratyaya-viruddha);
> from the perception of this [opposition]. And the opposition [is] from the opposition of the
> own-face/other-face object-acceptance (svamukha-paramukha-viṣaya-svīkāra), and, from the
> being-of-that-form, of the this-ness/that-ness cognition (idaṃ-bhāva-tad-bhāva-pratīti).
>
> Therefore this is the inference (prayoga): "At the time of the memory, the not-manifested
> object (apratīyamāna) is not — [being] the object of the 'it-is-this' cognition
> (sa-it-pratyaya-viṣayatvāt)." For the existence of the manifested object is pervaded by the
> being-the-object-of-the-"this" (ayam-iti-pratyaya-viṣayatvena); [and] of its contrary, this
> [is] the perception (upalabdhi).
>
> **Objection (nanu):** "Setting aside the memory-of-the-destroyed-object (naṣṭa-vastu-viṣayā
> smṛti), elsewhere [this would be] the inference contradicted by the other means-of-knowledge
> (pramāṇāntara), the perception and the rest."
>
> **Reply (maivam):** "Not so. For the remembered (smaryamāṇa) — all [of it] is the
> prior-experienced (pūrvānubhūta). And it is destroyed, because of the momentariness
> (kṣaṇikatva). Then, of what use is the existence-in-another-moment (kṣaṇāntara-sadbhāva)?
> Let there be the stable object, the pot (ghaṭa) and the like, or, like in the Sāṅkhya, even
> the not-destroyed [object] — but by the being-the-producer-of-the-presentation-of-the-moment-
> break (upasthāna-kṣaṇabhaṅga-upanyāsa-janakatva), [it] does not, independently (svatantrāṃ),
> attain the object-ness [of the memory]..."

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions

1. **smṛti / memory** — the memory-argument's key: memory follows experience (is its conformer).
2. **the stable self (sthira ātmā)** — memory proves a stable experiencer; if the self were
   momentary, memory couldn't conform to a past experience.
3. **kṣaṇikatva → "momentariness"** — the Buddhist view being refuted: if everything is
   momentary, the object is destroyed, and memory couldn't relate to it.
4. **tad-anurodhinī → "the conformer"** — memory conforms to (follows the form of) the prior
   experience; this requires a stable experiencer to own both.
5. **sākṣātkāra = avikalpaka** — "direct-apprehension" = the non-constructive.
6. **the memory's own-particular form (prātisvika-rūpa)** — memory recollects the prior
   experience (pūrvānubhava-ullekhana), which is its distinctive form.
7. **the inference (prayoga)** — the formal argument: the not-manifested object at memory-time
   is not; the remembered is the prior-experienced; the experienced is momentary; so the
   memory-relation requires the stable self.
8. **nanu / maivam** — the objection (the memory-of-the-destroyed-object) and reply (the
   stability is needed precisely because of momentariness).

**The crux:** the memory-argument — the Pratyabhijñā's central proof that the self is a stable
experiencer, against the Buddhist kṣaṇika-vāda. This is the same argument as the
Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi's memory-passages (our audited parallel). The self must be eternal because
memory, which conforms to a past experience, requires an experiencer who persists across the
experiences.

# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk: upoddhāta, kārikās 4–5 — T1

*Kārikās 4–5 of the upoddhāta continue the memory/self argument. Kārikā 4: "where there is
an experience, there alone [is the memory]"; kārikā 5 (the antargaḍu): the opponent objects
"if so, what use is a stable self, like a stone in the belly?" Abhinava's commentary develops
the memory-from-impression (saṃskāra) argument and the memory's similarity-to-the-experience.*

*Source: M00020 lines 3505–3560. Base: Torella's ĪPK. Max-effort T1.*

---

## The kārikā (upoddhāta 4)

> **atha yatraivānubhavastatpadaiva sā || 4 ||**

> But (atha) — where alone there is the experience, there alone is it (the memory). (4)

*[The memory is where its prior experience was — it conforms to the experience's own locus.
The vṛtti-kāra glosses: "even of the non-existent (asato'pi)" — the memory is of the object
of the destroyed experience.]*

## The commentary

> This, the vṛtti-kāra explains: "even of the non-existent (asato'pi)." By the "if" (yadi),
> [he] shows that the "but" (atha) [marks] the conjecture (saṃbhāvanā). [He says:] "how [is]
> it [the memory], with the object of the destroyed [experience], object-possessing?"
>
> Here he gives the hetu (the reason): **"for, from the impression of the prior experience,
> the memory arises (pūrvānubhavasaṃskārāt smṛtisaṃbhavaḥ)."**
>
> Since, from the impression (saṃskāra) of the prior experience, the arising of the memory —
> therefore, by that object, there alone (tatpadaiva) it is. "For (hi)" — since [it is] thus,
> therefore [there is] the non-possibility of the objection "with the destruction of the seer,
> [how is there] the memory?" — the remainder of the intention (āśaya-śeṣa), [he] shows by
> the word "hi."
>
> The opponent, having obtained room [to speak], says:

## The kārikā (upoddhāta 5)

> **yadyevamantargaḍunā ko'rthaḥ syātthāyinātmanā || 5 ||**

> If [it is] thus, what use is the stable self, [which is] like a stone-in-the-belly
> (antargaḍu)? (5)

## The commentary

> If it is thus said, then — with the stable self, [which is] the bringer-of-the-pain-of-the-
> construction (kalpanā-śalya-dāyin), [and] in-nowhere-useful (kvacidapi-anupayogin), like the
> stone-in-the-belly (antargaḍu) — what use [is it]? That is: none.
>
> "[The memory] manifests that experience (tam anubhavam ābhāsayati)." 
> **Objection (nanu):** "like the blue, [does it],] by object-ness (viṣayatā)?" — "No,"
> [he] says: "but, by the imitation-of-the-prior-experience (pūrvānubhava-anukaraṇa), by the
> being-like-it (tatsadṛśatva), by the being-the-effect-of-the-impression (saṃskāra-jatva), by
> the obtained hetu. For [the memory] is like it (tatsadṛśī). And it (the prior experience),
> having plunged into the blue as the object — so it, too, shines thus." "And by that very
> [impression] (tenaiva)" — by the saṃskāra.
>
> The ṭīkā-kāra, having declared the tātparya (the intended meaning) of the sūtra from "but
> where alone (atha yatraiva)..." to "if so (yadyevam)..." — [namely], by the "the prior
> meaning (pūrvārtha)..." up to "...the desiring-of-the-object-possession
> (viṣayavattābhiṣṭau)" — [then,] for "what use (ko'rthaḥ)..." [he] says, by the word "the
> self-acceptance is futile (ātmopagamo vyarthaḥ)" [that] by "the stone-in-the-belly
> (antargaḍu)" [is meant] "futile," [and] for "what use" [is meant] "of no use."
>
> "The vāsanā (the latent-tendency), of the form of the impression (saṃskāra-rūpa), made by
> the manifestation-of-the-object (artha-āvabhāsa-kṛtā) — of that self, [which is] of the
> form of the inner-facing-awareness (antarmukha-bodha-rūpa) — when it is awakened by the
> door of the awakening-of-the-vāsanā-made-by-the-seeing-of-that-kind (tādṛśa-darśana-kṛta-
> vāsanā-prabodha), then, in that taking-up (upādāna), [being] the inner-cause (antaraṅga-
> kāraṇa), in the self, [which is] of the character of the inner-facing-awareness of the
> self-ascertainment (svasaṃvedana-antarmukha-bodha-lakṣaṇa), the memory, [being] of the form
> of the effect (kārya-rūpa), arises; and, by the being-like-the-experience
> (anubhava-sadṛśatva), it shines. Thus the object-possession (saviṣayatva) [is] this alone —

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions

1. **saṃskāra → "the impression"** — the latent trace; memory arises from the prior
   experience's impression.
2. **antargaḍu → "a stone in the belly"** — the opponent's image: a stable self would be
   useless, like an undigested stone (the futile burden). Abhinava refutes: the self IS useful,
   as the inner cause of memory.
3. **the memory manifests the prior experience (tam anubhavam ābhāsayati)** — the memory
   recollects the experience, not a bare object; it imitates the prior experience
   (pūrvānubhava-anukaraṇa), being like it (tatsadṛśī).
4. **vāsanā / saṃskāra** — the latent tendency/impression; the memory's cause.
5. **the inner-facing-awareness (antarmukha-bodha)** — the self as the inner reflexive
   awareness; the memory arises in it as its effect.
6. **"the blue" objection** — the memory doesn't have the object as a fresh perception (like
   the blue); it has it by similarity-to-the-prior-experience.

**The crux:** the antargaḍu objection (the stable self as useless) and its refutation. The
self is not a useless stone — it is the inner cause (antaraṅga-kāraṇa) in which the memory,
as the effect of the prior experience's impression, arises. Memory is recollection-of-the-
experience (by similarity), not fresh-perception — which requires the stable self. This is the
heart of the Pratyabhijñā's anti-momentariness argument.

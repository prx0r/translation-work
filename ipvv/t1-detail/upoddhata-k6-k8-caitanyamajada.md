# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk: upoddhāta, kārikās 6 & 8 — T1

*Kārikā 6 (the saṃskāra/memory/self: "from the impression, memory; the rememberer, like the
seer") and kārikā 8 ("consciousness is non-inert, so in the inert there's no manifestation of
objects") — the latter is the philosophical heart, the same "crystal" argument as the
Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi: a light that doesn't know itself is inert.*

*Source: M00020 lines 3650–3680 (kārikā 6) and 4035–4070 (kārikā 8). Base: Torella's ĪPK.
Max-effort T1.*

## The kārikā (upoddhāta 6)

> **tato bhinneṣu dharmeṣu tatsvarūpāviśeṣataḥ | saṃskārātsmṛtisiddhau syātsmartā draṣṭeva kalpitaḥ || 6 ||**

> Then, in the distinct properties (bhinnā dharmāḥ), [being] non-different from its own form
> (tatsvarūpāviśeṣa) — from the impression, [when] the memory is established, the rememberer
> (smartṛ) would be inferred (kalpita), like the seer (draṣṭā). (6)

## The commentary

> Then — in the properties distinct from the self (ātmano vyatirikteṣu dharmeṣu), the
> impressions and the rest, even [when] existing, and [in] the distinct inherence (samavāya)
> even [when] existing — of that self, in its own form, from the prior form, not-arisen-with-
> the-properties-of-the-impressions-and-the-rest (anudita-saṃskārādi-dharma), there is no
> difference (viśeṣa). For, in a difference, [there would be] the non-eternity (anityatva).
> Therefore, from the impression alone, by the previously-stated maxim, the memory is
> established. By the being/non-being of the impression, the different/non-different
> practical-activity of the knowers (pramātṛ-bheda-abheda-vyavahāra) [is made]; those two, too,
> in the taking-up (upādāna), make the taken-up-being-and-non-being (upādeya-bhāva-tad-abhāva).
>
> But that which shines as "I remember (ahaṃ smartā)" — there, first, that is indeed the
> "I"-awareness (ahaṃ-pratīti); not the self [as] momentary, from the non-stability (asthiratva).
> And this object [is] to be understood [thus]: the unique-particular (svalakṣaṇa), if, being-
> made-an-object-of-the-experience, it shines, [then] it is non-stable (asthira); or, if [it is]
> the constructive (vikalpya), then [it is] the body, or the stream (saṃtāna). Even so, no
> knower (vedaka), distinct from the known-and-the-knowing (vedya-vedanā), of the form of the
> rememberer-and-seer (smartṛ-draṣṭṛ-rūpa), shines.
>
> "Then, in the distinct [properties]..." — [this] sūtra [is] as-indicating-the-pūrvapakṣa
> (the opponent's position) and as-useful-for-the-siddhānta (the established view). There,
> by the first form (the pūrvapakṣa), the vṛtti-kāra explains "pleasure, pain
> (sukhaduḥkha)..." — For the properties, the pleasures-and-the-rest, [being] re-connected
> (pratisandhīyamāna), are seen; and of them, [being] distinct, how [is] the re-connection
> (pratisandhi)? — the one support (āśraya) must be said. Hence the ṭīkā-kāra explains "the
> mutual-conjunction (parasparasamanvaya) would not be."
>
> And, as useful-for-the-siddhānta, [he] analyzes "of the non-different-by-the-distinct-
> properties (bhinnair dharmair asaṃbhinnasya)" — [i.e.] of the not-having-obtained-their-
> entry (alabdha-tadāveśa), that is. This, in the ṭīkā, by the plural "by those (tairetaiḥ),"
> is explained.
>
> "Of the distinct, in the one-self-identity (ekatādātmya), how [is] the many-ness
> (bahutva)? Or, in the many-self-identity (anekatādātmya), of that non-different one, how
> [is] the one-ness (ekatva)?" — this is the intention (āśaya). Of it, the making
> (upakāra-viśeṣa) of the pleasures-and-the-rest, the impression-born (saṃskāra-janma), of
> the character of the re-connection (pratisandhi-lakṣaṇa) — since, in it, this self is of
> no-use (anupayoga), "he is not there useful," that is. Or, by the property-made [use]
> (guṇa-kṛta), [he] uses, accepts [it].
>
> Thus, in the re-connection of the pleasures-and-the-rest, there is, first, no use of this
> [self] at all. Not only [is] it-not-limited-by-the-pleasures-and-the-rest
> (avaveśeṣitatva); but, by the impression, he is the re-connector (pratisaṃdhātā) — [and] of
> the impression, that which is the root, the experience-natured (anubhava-ātmaka) — by it,
> too, no difference of him [is] made — so how would he produce the memory? — [thus he] says
> "of the non-different (nirviśeṣasya) also." By this, of the non-application-in-the-memory
> (smṛtāv avyāpṛteḥ) [he shows the absence of use].

*(the argument: the self, if non-different, is of no use in memory; if different, it'd be
non-eternal. Abhinava resolves this toward the "I"-awareness being the reflexive self.)*

## The kārikā (upoddhāta 8)

> **caitanyamajaḍā saivaṃ jāḍye nārthaprakāśatā || 8 ||**

> Consciousness is non-inert (ajaḍā); thus, in the inert (jāḍya), there is no manifestation of
> objects (artha-prakāśatā). (8)

## The commentary

> The meaning of this, the ṭīkā-kāra, in summary, explains: "this one, too, [is] a Sāṅkhya
> (sāṃkhyo'yam api)." The right manifestation (samyak-khyāti) — the viveka (the
> discrimination) of the prakṛti and the puruṣa; and the "saṃkhyā" — the enumeration
> (parigaṇana) of the tattva-differences. The work made on that [is] "Sāṅkhya." There, the
> vṛtti (the modification) of the buddhi-tattva is said to be the "jñāna (the cognition)."
> "The vṛtti (the activity)" — the vartana (the abiding), the pariṇāma-viśeṣa (the particular
> transformation). For a transformation (pariṇāma) that, hiding the prior form
> (pūrvarūpa-tirodadhāt), [is] like the wood-and-ash (kāṣṭhabhasma), is called the
> effect-transformation (kārya-pariṇāma); [a transformation] that does not hide [the prior
> form] is the property-transformation (dharma-pariṇāma), which, by the established-form
> (siddhākāratayā), shines — like the gold's earring-ness (kuṇḍalatā); [a transformation] that,
> not hiding, goes with the to-be-established-form (sādhyākāratvena), [and] is understood —
> this is the vṛtti-pariṇāma (the activity-transformation). For thus, "the abiding of it,
> thus" — this is called the vṛtti.
>
> "As before (pūrvavadeva)" — by the door of the consciousness-part (cid-bhāga), the
> criticism-of-the-eternity-and-the-rest (nityatādi-dūṣaṇa); by the direction of the
> inert-part (jaḍa-bhāga), the non-possibility-of-the-object-establishment
> (viṣaya-vyavasthānāyoga). "But (atha)" — [this] shows the opponent's-conjecture
> (para-saṃbhāvanā).
>
> "The buddhi (the intellect) is the agent (kartrī) — of the object, the blue and the rest, the
> related form (saṃbandhi-rūpa), of the image-nature (pratibimba-ātmaka), by the manner in
> which, with the self-luminous-nature (svacchatātmanā), [and] by the excellence-of-being
> (sattvātiśaya-kṛtena), [it] holds, bears [it]; by that very self-luminous manner, of the
> self, the puruṣa, the related consciousness (saṃbandhi-caitanya), of the image-nature, [it]
> holds — [being] luminous inside-and-outside (antarbahiśca), everywhere, because of the
> self-luminousness" — this Sāṅkhya-statement is conjectured (saṃbhāvyate). There, too, he
> gives the criticism: "[for] the consciousness [is] non-inert — even thus, in the inert, no
> manifestation of objects" —

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions

1. **kārikā 6 (the saṃskāra/memory/self):** the argument that the rememberer (smartṛ) is
   inferred, like the seer. Abhinava's resolution: the "I remember" is the "I"-awareness
   (ahaṃ-pratīti) — the reflexive self, not a momentary construction. The distinct-properties
   argument: if the self differs, it'd be non-eternal; if non-different, it's of no use — the
   resolution is the reflexive self.
2. **kārikā 8 ("caitanyam ajaḍā"):** THE core — "consciousness is non-inert, so in the inert
   there's no manifestation of objects." This is the crystal-argument (a light that doesn't
   know itself is inert). The Sāṅkhya view (buddhi as the agent, the puruṣa as the witness) is
   examined and the consciousness-part/inert-part analysis given.
3. **The Sāṅkhya analysis:** the vṛtti (activity) as the pariṇāma (transformation); the
   cid-bhāga (consciousness-part) vs the jaḍa-bhāga (inert-part). The puruṣa can't be a mere
   inert witness — consciousness is non-inert.
4. **the Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi parallel** — this is Utpaladeva's own argument (the crystal, the
   non-reflexive light = inert) restated by Abhinava against the Sāṅkhya.

**The crux:** consciousness is non-inert (ajaḍā) — it is not a passive luminous substrate (the
Sāṅkhya puruṣa) but a reflexive self. A mere light that doesn't know itself is inert (the
crystal); hence the object-manifestation requires the non-inert reflexive consciousness. This
is the same argument as the Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi's anti-inert-light — the foundation of the whole
system.

# IPVV Vol 1 — Chunk: upoddhāta, kārikās 9–10 — T1

*Kārikā 9 (the apramāṇā kriyā — action is not a means-of-knowledge) and kārikā 10 ("there is
no other, no relation save the cause-effect relation" — the refutation of the action-relation,
the saṃbandha). This engages the Buddhist/grammarian theory of action and relation.*

*Source: M00020 lines 5460–5540. Base: Torella's ĪPK. Max-effort T1.*

---

## The kārikā (upoddhāta 9)

> **... apramāṇā kriyā iti, pariśiṣṭaṃ paścāt pramāṇabādhitā ca iti || 9 ||**

> "Action (kriyā) is not a means-of-knowledge (apramāṇā)"; and the remainder, afterwards, "[it
> is] refuted-by-the-means-of-knowledge (pramāṇa-bādhitā)". (9)

## The commentary

> Whether action is admitted as of the nature-of-cultivation (bhāvanā-ātmā) or as of the
> nature-of-the-karma-object (karma-padārtha-ātmā) — [even] so, [there is] no inference
> (anumāna), nor even the arthāpatti (the presumption). There, since the sūtra's meaning is
> primary, [and] in the vṛtti [it is] submerged, [and] in the ṭīkā clearly-reflected — so that,
> by the sūtra's own order, the fixing-in-the-intellect (buddhi-niveśa) might be — with the
> intention [of this], the "being-born-of-the-this-and-that-place-and-the-rest, none-other
> (tattaddeśādijātatā nānyā)" — [this] part of the sūtra's meaning he first concludes: "action
> is not a means-of-knowledge (apramāṇā kriyā)"; the remainder, afterwards: "[it is]
> refuted-by-the-means-of-knowledge (pramāṇabādhitā)." (9)

## The kārikā (upoddhāta 10)

> **tatra tatra sthite tattadbhavatītyeva dṛśyate | nānyannānyo'sti saṃbandhaḥ kāryakāraṇabhāvataḥ || 10 ||**

> In this and that [case], [when] it is established [there], only "this becomes that" is seen;
> there is no other, no relation (saṃbandha), save by the cause-effect relation
> (kārya-kāraṇa-bhāva). (10)

## The commentary

> Thus, having examined cognition and action, in the "by-the-yoga-of-them (tadyogād), the
> lordship (aiśvarya)" — here, [with regard to] the hetu, [being] the relation-part spoken-of-
> by-the-word "yoga (the connection)," [and] to examine the relation (saṃbandha) — first, [he]
> says by this kārikā: "in the relation, there is no establishing means-of-knowledge
> (sādhaka-pramāṇa)." And, by the way (prasaṅga), also with regard to action, the remaining
> [part] he examines. For thus — if someone were to say: "the object, distinct in its
> this-and-that-place-time-form, flowing by the stream-nature (saṃtāna-vṛtti), [is] called
> 'action'" — then, in the sitting, the going, and the rest, where [there is] the
> non-agent-ness (akarmakatva), or the attaining-the-object-ness (prāpya-karmatā), and where
> [there is] the to-be-effected (nirvartya), the pot and the like, [as] the object — there,
> in the non-being of the action-and-agent-relation (kriyākāraka-bhāva), "the potter makes
> the pot, from the clay, with the stick-and-the-rest" — this cognition (bodha) — thus, here,
> the established-of-the-action-limit (kriyāparyanta-vyavahāra-siddha), how would [it] be?
> And, its being, without action, how? — he, too, is refuted by this sūtra; and "that [is]"
> by the immediately-following sūtra, in the direction of the making-clear of this meaning.
>
> Hence, to show the incidental-ness (prāsaṅgikatva) and the being-said
> (uktatva), the vivṛti-kāra shows the action's being-an-example (dṛṣṭāntatva), by the
> summary-descent (saṃkṣepāvatāra): "and as (yathā ca)." And in the sūtra, "no-other (nānyat)"
> — the universal-statement (sāmānya-ukti) also, with regard to action, terminates
> (paryavasāyate). And, as an example, it (action) is applied in the relation — "not by the
> perception (pratyakṣa)..." — [he] explains the sūtra's "eva (only)" by "is seen (dṛśyate)"
> — [this is] the mere settling-in-the-means-of-knowledge (pramāṇāvasāya-mātra). This [is]
> the hetu: since this much is seen, therefore [there is] no other — not action, [and] no
> relation of it, at all — [this is] the statement of the example-and-the-to-be-established
> (dṛṣṭānta-sādhya-nirdeśa). And thus, in the vṛtti, separately, [he] explains: "like action,
> [there is] no action-and-agent relation at all." By that very "other (anya)" word, [there is]
> no repetition (punar-ukti) to be considered.
>
> In "no-other (nānyat)," here, the being-the-hetu of the former [is] said in the vṛtti: "of
> the distinct, the separate, this relation, like action, from the non-perception
> (anupalambha)."
>
> **Objection (nanu):** "In 'in this and that (tatra tatra),' by the seventh [case-ending], the
> relation (saṃbandha), which is implied, [and is] other-than-the-nominal-meaning
> (prātipadikārtha-atirikta) — that, then, what is it?" — to this suspicion, [with regard to]
> the sūtra "there-is-no-other (nānyo'sti)...," [he gives] the summary-determination of this:
> "the cause-effect (kāryakāraṇa)."
>
> By the taking-of-the-arrangement (saṃniveśa-grahaṇa), he also says "the cause, of the
> entrance-of-the-determined-form-preceded-by-the-withdrawal-of-the-other-form
> (anyarūpasaṃhāra-pūrvaka-niyatarūpa-praveśa-hetu)," [and] "the use of its cognition, will,
> effort (tadīya-jñānecchāyatna-viśeṣopayoga)". "Of the not-been-before

## T1 apparatus — the key decisions

1. **kriyā → "action"** — the agent's action; the Buddhist/grammarian treat it as an object
   or a relation.
2. **kārikā 9 (apramāṇā kriyā):** action is not a means-of-knowledge — neither inference nor
   arthāpatti establishes it.
3. **kārikā 10 ("nānyannānyo'sti saṃbandhaḥ"):** there is no relation between action and
   agent/object save the cause-effect relation. The potter-pot cognition doesn't need a separate
   relation (saṃbandha) — it's just cause-effect.
4. **saṃbandha → "the relation"** — the grammarian's relation between action, agent, object;
   Abhinava reduces it to cause-effect.
5. **kārya-kāraṇa-bhāva → "the cause-effect relation"** — the only relation admitted.
6. **prasaṅga → "by the way"** — the incidental treatment of action.
7. **the "potter makes the pot" example** — the cognition of agency doesn't require a separate
   relation; the cause-effect suffices.

**The crux:** the refutation of the relation (saṃbandha). The grammarian (and the
Mīmāṃsā/Nyāya) posit a relation between action, agent, and object; Abhinava (following
Utpaladeva) reduces it to cause-effect — the potter-pot relation is just the cause-effect,
not a third entity. This is part of the anti-essentialist/anti-pluralist core, preparing the
unity-of-consciousness thesis.

## The maṅgala (vv. 1–4, 6)

**v. 1** — śrīśaktitritayāviyogi hṛdayaṃ bibhrattadekātmasā-dbhāvāvirbhavadātmabhāvanibhṛtollāsādhvavispanditam | śrīmadbhairavadhāma tadvijayatāṃ bhaktāntarātmasphurat-stotraprābhṛtasaṃgrahonmukhatayā yadviśvarūpāyate ||

> **T1:** May that blessed abode of Bhairava triumph — the Heart (hṛdaya), not sundered from the triad of the powers (śakti-tritaya), [the Heart] which, as the single self-hood (eka-ātmatā), bears the arising of self-being [and] the reflexion-fill (bhāvana-nibhṛta) [and] the upsurge of the unfolding (ullāsa-adhvan) [in its] throbbing (vispandita); which, poised toward the gathering-in of the hymn-offering (stotra-prābhṛta-saṃgraha) flashing in the inner self of the devotee, becomes the all-formed (viśva-rūpa).

*[The Heart = the union of Śiva and Śakti as anuttara; the "triad of powers" = jñāna/icchā/kriyā (knowledge, will, action). The Heart is not static: it bears the arising, the reflexion-fill, the unfolding — the three aspects of the cognition's dynamism. Rendered as "throbbing" for vispandita per the spanda-register, not mechanical "vibration."]*

**v. 2** — labdhvānugrahamīśvarānnijamahaḥsaṃbhārasaṃdīpino dehe'sminnarasiṃhaguptaghaṭite labdhāspadaḥ paścime | śrīmallakṣmaṇaguptadarśitapathaḥ śrīpratyabhijñāvidhau ṭīkārthapravimarśinīṃ racayate vṛttiṃ praśiṣyo guroḥ ||

> **T1:** Having obtained grace from the Lord, [the pupil,] his own brilliance (nija-mahas) enkindling a mass [of light], having found his seat in this body, composed in the manner of the Narasiṃha-gupta line, [and] in the last [age]; his path shown by the blessed Lakṣmaṇagupta, in the discipline of the blessed Recognition (pratyabhijñā) — the pupil of the teacher (praśiṣyo guroḥ) composes the vṛtti, the reflection on the ṭīkā's meaning (ṭīkā-rtha-pravimarśinī).

*[Self-identification: Abhinava names his guru Lakṣmaṇagupta (who "showed the path" of Recognition), situates himself in the Narasiṃha-gupta lineage, and declares he is composing a vṛtti — a reflection (vimarśinī) on the meaning of the ṭīkā. Note the layered commentarial structure: sūtra (Utpaladeva) / vṛtti (Utpaladeva's own) / ṭīkā (Utpaladeva's lost Vivṛti) / and Abhinava's vimarśinī on it.]*

**v. 3** — ghaṭayati paripākaṃ śabdavṛtte pramāṇe vighaṭayati ca bhūyo mṛtyusantrāsacintām | abhinavamatirevaṃ ceśvarapratyabhijñā-vivṛtipadavimarśinyeva bhogāpavargau ||

> **T1:** He brings to maturation (paripāka) the word-circle (śabda-vṛtti) in the means of knowledge (pramāṇa), and he again dismantles (vighaṭayati) the thought of the terror of death; thus this mind of Abhinava, in the very reflection on the word of the Vivṛti of the Recognition of the Lord (īśvarapratyabhijñā-vivṛti-pada-vimarśinī), [finds] enjoyment and liberation (bhoga-apavarga).

*[The work's double fruit: it matures the word/pramāṇa apparatus AND dispels the terror of death — both bhoga (enjoyment) and apavarga (liberation). The "word-circle" is the phonemic/linguistic substrate of cognition.]*

**v. 4** — nijānujamanorathaprathitasatpathābhyarthanā-vaśo'bhinavaguptasatpadaniveśitasvātmakaḥ | prakāśayati mārgamenamamunā jano'pyunmanāḥ prayāti śivadhāma tat pathi na yatpaśūnāṃ dṛśaḥ ||

> **T1:** Moved by the request that arose in his own younger brother's mind, [that request] proclaiming the good path — Abhinavagupta, whose self is established at the blessed feet, reveals this path, by which even the man of elevated mind (unmanāḥ) goes to the abode of Śiva — [a path] not seen on that way by the beasts (paśu).

*[The work is occasioned by the request of Abhinava's younger brother; it reveals a path invisible to the paśu — the "bound one," the ordinary, contracted self. The term unmanāḥ (the state beyond the mind, the unmanī/ecstatic state) is doctrinal.]*

**v. 6** — pūrṇavyākaraṇāvagāhanaśuciḥ sattarkamūlonmiṣat-prajñākalpalatāvivekakusumairbhyarcya hṛddevatām | pīyuṣāsavasārasundaramahāsāhityasauhityabhāg-viśrāmyāmyahamīśvarādvayakathākāntāsakhaḥ sāṃpratam | amṛtamanantamanuttaramaghoraṣoḍaśakaśakticakragatam | aunmanasapadanirūḍhiprathamopoddhātakaṃ bande ||

> **T1:** Pure through the plunging into the complete grammar (pūrṇa-vyākaraṇa-avagāhana), having worshipped the Heart-goddess (hṛddevatā) with the flowers of discernment of the wish-creeper of insight (prajñā-kalpalatā) sprung from the root of good reasoning (sattarka-mūla) — I, a companion of the beloved of the tale of the non-dual Lord (īśvara-advaya-kathā-kāntā-sakhaḥ), sharing in the essence of the nectar-soma [and] the great literature (mahā-sāhitya-sauhitya), rest (viśrāmyāmi) at present; I praise (bande) the first upodghāta (opening), established in the state of the unmanas (aunmanasapada-nirūḍhi), [which is] the amṛta, the infinite, the unsurpassed, lodged in the wheel of the sixteen-fold powers (ṣoḍaśaka-śakti-cakra).

*[The verse closes the maṅgala with Abhinava's self-description and the work's auspicious foundation: purified by grammar, reasoning, and devotion, he rests in the non-dual tale; the upodghāta (the first section) is grounded in the unmanas-state, the amṛta, the infinite, the unsurpassed, within the sixteen powers.]*

## The opening commentary (the sūtra/vṛtti/ṭīkā levels)

> iha eka eva śrīmadasmatparameṣṭhiśrīsomānandapādaprasādāpasāritasaṃkocaḥ śrīmān utpalākhyaḥ śāstrakāro ... sūtraṃ vṛttiṃ ṭīkāṃ ca īśvarapratyabhijñānasiddhaye kṛtavān | tatra sūtrakārabhūmikāvasthitaḥ kathaṃcit ityādinā maheśvaraṃ prati prahvaṃ svātmānaṃ parāmṛśan tadvākyapaṭhanapuraḥsarībhāvena vṛtteravaśyaṃbhāvitāṃ paśyan ...

> **T1:** Here, [there is] one only — our supreme teacher, the blessed Somānanda, by whose grace the contraction (saṃkoca) was dispelled — the honorable author named Utpaladeva, whose heart-lotus unfolded and purified the quarters with its fragrance, made the sūtra, the vṛtti, and the ṭīkā for the sake of attaining the Recognition of the Lord (īśvarapratyabhijñā). There, standing in the role of the sūtra-kāra, [he], reflecting on his own self as prostrate before the great Lord "in some way" (kathaṃcit), seeing the necessity of the vṛtti by the fact of reading forth that speech — [and then] again standing in the role of the vṛtti-kāra, by a different procedure, [he] reflects on the Lord's constant flowing prostration even in himself — [all this] to show that the sūtra-kāra himself stands in this role; and whatever, in the vṛtti, was shown by co-reference (sāmānādhikaraṇya) — "by Recognition I would please" (pratyabhijñāpayāmi parituṣyeyam) — otherwise it would have to be said that the sūtra-kāra does this; and in seeing this there is a purpose: the removal of the suspicion that the vṛtti-kāra does not understand the sūtra's meaning; and even in that indication, this [his] intention is not without doubt.

*[The key passage: Abhinava explains the layered authorship of the ĪPK. Utpaladeva plays three roles — sūtra-kāra (the verses), vṛtti-kāra (his own auto-commentary), and ṭīkā-kāra (the lost Vivṛti). In the sūtra-role he is prostrate before the Lord "in some way"; in the vṛtti-role, by a different procedure, the same prostration flows within himself. The point: it is the same author in all three roles — the recognition is one act, not three separate ones. This is the methodological heart of why Abhinava comments on the Vivṛti.]*

## T1 apparatus

- **Technical terms retained:** śakti, hṛdaya, pratyabhijñā, vimarśinī/vimarśa, paśu, unmanas/unanā, ābhāsa (implied), svātantrya (implied in the freedom of the powers).
- **The Heart (v.1):** the union of Śiva and Śakti as anuttara — the Trika's central symbol (cf. Tantrāloka 1.1).
- **The triad of powers:** jñāna (knowledge), icchā (will), kriyā (action) — the three śaktis.
- **vispandita:** rendered "throbbing" (the spanda-register), NOT "vibration."
- **The layered authorship (commentary):** sūtra / vṛtti / ṭīkā = Utpaladeva's three roles; Abhinava's vimarśinī is the fourth layer. This is the text's own account of its structure.
- **[X]/uncertain:** "mṛtyusantrāsacintām" (v.3) — the terror-of-death thought; secure in sense. "nijānuja" (v.4) — Abhinava's younger brother; the identity is not fixed (scholars note the brother is otherwise unknown).

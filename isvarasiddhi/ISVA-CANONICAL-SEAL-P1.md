## The parable (canonical draft, ≈88s spoken)

> First they praise the maker — one God, Maheśvara, the Lord — then
> the town argues over its pots: who made them? Combination serves
> another's sake, say the elders, as the eye serves its seeing:
> fitness itself points to a maker. The rival school states its case
> at full strength — nature alone, unpurposed, watching the wheel
> turn — fairly, bravely, and is outflanked by a single question.
> The workshop tries each candidate: clay, the helpers, the blind
> tools — potter, the judges say, or there is no pot; wisdom and
> will stand the test where helpers fail. Every escape the
> challengers name collapses into its own consequence: unperceived
> intellect can want nothing, generic will fits neither spirit nor
> mind, and each eye sees private world unless One holds the seeing.
> The maker stands. And the proof — a thousand blazing reasons — is
> offered on the open palm like a jewel: it flees the hand of the
> fate-struck, but flashes inside every breather as maker and
> knower. The proof stands.

## The seal — statement

**The wheel and the palm-jewel:** workshop vertical — bottom: the
failed array (grey clay-lump, blind tools, helper-rope, a few
cracked shard-pairs — every escape that collapsed); middle: the
potter's WHEEL with a rising terracotta pot between two shaping
HANDS (the maker, concluded not drawn); top: an open palm holding
one blazing jewel, gold rays raining DOWN into the pots below —
and beside it one small ash-grey empty fist (the fate-struck hand
the jewel fled).

## Parable v2 (≈85s)

> On a shore where nobody has ever walked, one footprint lies in
> the wet sand, fitted to one foot exactly. The waves come first to
> claim it — their edges fit nothing. The tools come: we shaped it
> — no. The clay speaks longest and fairest of all — still it fits
> nothing; clay fills any shape and prints none. Each claim fails
> its outline the way every excuse fails. Then a boy sets his own
> foot in the print: exact — and nobody has come. The tide slides
> around the print and washes everything else away; only the fated,
> running, smear it. In the print's palm rests a jewel, and it fits
> the hand you didn't know you had. The eye fits seeing before any
> eye; the pot fits a thumb it never met. Every walker that passes
> next prints exactly — one maker, by fitness: the maker, him we
> praise. The proof stands.

# Īśvarasiddhi isva-p06 (vv. 46–54) — L2 READ

## v.46
From change, that One is longed for, for all spirits — and yet body's and earth's and the rest's change is by nature.
The Sanskrit: *pariṇāmāttadekaṃ hi sarvānpuṃsaḥ pratīṣyate tathāpi dehabhūtādeḥ pariṇāmaḥ svabhāvataḥ*

## v.47
As before — why would it not be, toward the deemed-freed? Yet it does occur — but is not joined by him.
The Sanskrit: *pūrvavanna bhavetkasmāttaṃ muktābhimataṃ prati athāpi sa bhavatyeva kiṃ tu tena na yujyate*

## v.48
By the freed spirit — done with tasks, need-free — he stands alone; not so: needlessness belongs to no spirit ever, nor to mind.
The Sanskrit: *muktapuṃsā kṛtārthatvādanarthitvātsa kevalaḥ naitannārthitvamasyāsti jātu puṃso'tha cetasaḥ*

## v.49
With need gone, no meeting with spirit again would be; this too is unjust, once body-dwelling mind is born.
The Sanskrit: *arthitvavigamānna syādbhūyaḥ puṃsā samāgamaḥ tadetadapi na nyāyyaṃ jāte dehasthacetasi*

## v.50
If from perceptible-only-nature of that, not from karma's waning — mokṣa would be mere nature, resting on karma's waning.
The Sanskrit: *saṃvedyaikasvabhāvatvāttasya karmakṣayānna cet tatsvabhāvatvameva syānmokṣaḥ karmakṣayāśrayaḥ*

## v.51
There'd be acts even of spirit-distinction-knowers, from compassion; if not from passion — thus mokṣa from passion's exhaustion.
The Sanskrit: *syurjñātapuṃvivekānāṃ karmāṇyapi kṛpāditaḥ na rāgāttāni cedevaṃ mokṣo rāgakṣayādbhavet*

## v.52
Thus view-abandoned — that nature-view is unjust; hence body-and-rest, famously, have sentient-maker as prior.
The Sanskrit: *tathā ca darśanatyāgastanna yuktā svabhāvadṛk tasmātprasiddhaṃ tanvādi buddhimatkartṛpūrvakam*

## v.53
From setup-difference — this flawless proof.
The Sanskrit: *saṃsthānabhedādityetanniravadyaṃ prasādhanam*

## v.54
This reason, aimed at the Supreme Lord by the self-entrusted self, is not overpowered by reason-faults in the least — nor the rival's either; only this is said.
The Sanskrit: *sa eṣa hetuḥ parameśvaraṃ prati prayujyamānaḥ svaniveśitātmanā na hetudoṣaiḥ paribhūyate manāk paro'pi vā kevalametaducyate*

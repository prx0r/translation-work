# L200 — Īśvarasiddhi (56 verses, 7 passages)

## 1. What the text says (plain)
Īśvarasiddhi (56 verses) is Utpaladeva's (10th c.) work, part of Siddhitrayī. It examines: Īśvara as cause (as pot from potter, so world from Lord; determined meaning in establishment). Creation (by fitting, uncombined, supreme; fire accomplished as khādira; by variety alone, not by own-mark; establishment alone = causality of wise). Puruṣārtha (by negating self-interest; transformation occurs, nothing hard; no other instrumental cause except puruṣārtha-effort; where is Lord's activity?). Material cause denied (without that, how would pot arise from potter; not material cause's end, by entering helpers; without worldly-practice wisdom, only grossness). All-knowing questioned (without knowability of person, puruṣārtha; mind's function individually conditioned; that is of principal, common, for all; if all-knowing of all, of each, arises). Mokṣa not by nature (by nature he becomes, but not fitting; desire-lessness never of person or mind; mokṣa by karma-destruction, not from attachment). Close (flees, of god-struck, good gem; shines, fire, at night).

## 2. What the text argues (core claims)

- C4: Material cause denied: not material cause's end, by entering helpers.

- C6: Mokṣa not by nature: by nature he becomes, but not fitting; mokṣa by karma-destruction.

## 3. What the text rejects

- R2: Īśvara as all-knowing (questioned).
- R3: Mokṣa by nature.
- R4: Mokṣa from attachment.

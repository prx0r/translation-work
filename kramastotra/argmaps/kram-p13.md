## 1. What is at issue

Verse 13 of the Kramastotra (Abhinavagupta, Krama tradition). Gloss: one-pointed-become-my-heart-ground-Bhairava-Lord-in-measure-dissolved-Shiva-pada-shining-power-vaibhava-force-body-breath-limited-made-agentness-standing

Sanskrit: kramollāsaṃ tasyāṃ bhuvi viracayan bhedakalanāṃ svaśaktīnāṃ deva prathayasi sadā svātmani tataḥ | kriyājñānecchākhyāṃ sthitilayamahāsṛṣṭivibhavāṃ trirūpaṃ bhūyāsaṃ samadhiśayituṃ vyagrahṛdayaḥ

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Verse 13:**
> kramollāsaṃ tasyāṃ bhuvi viracayan bhedakalanāṃ svaśaktīnāṃ deva prathayasi sadā svātmani tataḥ | kriyājñānecchākhyāṃ sthitilayamahāsṛṣṭivibhavāṃ trirūpaṃ bhūyāsaṃ samadhiśayituṃ vyagrahṛdayaḥ

[i.e. one-pointed-become-my-heart-ground-Bhairava-Lord-in-measure-dissolved-Shiva-pada-shining-power-vaibhava-force-body-breath-limited-made-agentness-standing]

The Kramastotra maps the "sequence" (krama) of cosmological descent and ascent through the tattvas. Each verse is a step in the sequence: the devotee's recognition that all is Śiva, that the world is his self-manifestation, and that the sequence (krama) is the path of recognition (pratyabhijñā). The tone is devotional-Krama: fierce, esoteric, with Śākta overtones. The stotra is about the "sequence" (krama) — not just cosmological but soteriological: the sequence of recognition.

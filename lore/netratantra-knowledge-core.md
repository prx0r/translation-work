# KNOWLEDGE CORE — Netratantra (pass-2, researched 2026-11-11)

*Scholarship-backed; citations in §G. Numbering/recension differences are
recorded, never smoothed. Key to our e-text's own markers.*

## A. BIBLIOGRAPHIC FACTS (verified)

- **Date:** Kashmir, **700–850 CE** (Sanderson 2004, cited by Flood);
  Flood: composed 8th–9th c.; White: early 9th c.
- **Names (one text):** Netratantra · Amṛteśvara-tantra · Amṛtīśvara-tantra
  · **Mṛtyuñjit / Mṛtyuñjaya**. Witness names diverge: Nepalese archetype
  (Na) calls itself *Amṛtīśvara-tantra*; ectype Nb (paper, devanāgarī)
  says *Amṛteśvara-tantra*.
- **Two recensions:** (1) **Nepalese** — older, manuscript-only, no
  commentary; archetype Na copied **Feb/March 1200 CE** (Nepāliya saṃvat
  320), palm-leaf, proto-Bengali script, by paṇḍit **Kīrttidhara** for
  **Viśveśvara**, who commissioned it for the Malla court (initiation of
  Arimalla/Abhayamalla). (2) **Kashmir** — the only printed recension,
  **KSTS vols 46 (1936) & 61 (1939)**, ed. Madhuṣūdan Kaul (two mss, no
  details given); four Śāradā mss in the Oriental Research Library,
  Srinagar, all with Kṣemarāja's commentary.
- **Commentary:** Kṣemarāja's **Uddyota** (c. 1000–1050 CE) is on the
  Kashmir recension only. Flood and Sanderson both warn it **imposes a
  univocal non-dualist reading** the text resists.
- **Influence:** Abhinavagupta cites it as *Mṛtyuñjaya* (Tantrāloka 16.59,
  16.224, 21.11) and as *Netra* (IPVV vol. 3). (Brunner 1974, after
  Silburn.)
- **Relation to Svacchanda:** White 2012 dates the core Svacchandatantra
  **no later than the 7th century** — i.e. Svacchanda is EARLIER than
  Netra; Netra is in many respects derivative of it.
- **Forthcoming authority:** Flood, Wernicke-Olesen & Khatiwoda, *The
  Netra-Tantra chs 1–8*, Routledge (based on the 1200 CE palm-leaf).

## B. THE TEXT'S SELF-POSITION (Flood/Sanderson)

> **A "universal" (sarvasāmānya) tantra that claims to transcend and
> synthesise the tantric landscape — Śaiva Siddhānta, Bhairava, Kaula —
> around Śiva as Amṛteśa, Lord of Immortality and Conqueror of Death,
> whose "eye" protects and divinises.**

Sanderson's placement: the Netra sits **in the middle ground** between
the antinomian Kaula/Krama and Abhinavagupta's purity-obsessed orthodoxy.
Flood's central finding: it **gives voice to distinct perspectives** —
multiple "implicit anthropologies" — and resists monoglossic reading.
→ Argmaps must let each passage keep its own register; do NOT harmonise
into one Śaiva doctrine.

## C. THE SECRET INFORMATION (why this text is special)

**Chapter 7 (sūkṣmadhyāna) is the historical hinge:**
1. It is **the first text to mention the Kulamārga** (Flood).
2. It teaches **the first known system of six bodily cakras** the yogi
   pierces with his inherent śakti.
3. It sets out **two anthropologies** — which Kṣemarāja names
   *tantraprakriyā* (the tantric system) vs *kulaprakriyā* (the Kula
   system): the latter affirms and divinises the body, against classical
   yoga's body-rejection.
4. It appropriates older yogic models (granthis, ādhāras) while
   foregrounding **suṣumnā** and an early form of **kuṇḍalinī** ascent.
5. The inner power is conceived as **sound/vibration** — the
   **nādasūcī**, "the needle of sound".

These five points are the unique content of the text; translation must
preserve them exactly (cakra order, the two-methodology distinction, the
nādasūcī metaphor).

## E. TRAPS (scholarly)

1. **Uddyota contamination** is the #1 risk: Kṣemarāja's non-dual gloss on
   a text that is plural by design. Never let his vocabulary (vimarśa,
   prakāśa, recognition) into L2 where the verse doesn't say it.
2. **Cross-system material**: Vaiṣṇava deities/mantras appear legitimately
   (adh. 9, 11) — gloss exactly, never "correct" to Śaiva.
3. **Deity-identity spread**: Amṛteśa / Amṛtīśa / Mṛtyuñjaya / Netranātha
   are one Lord under functional names; Amṛtalakṣmī is his consort, NOT the
   Vaiṣṇava Lakṣmī.
4. **Medical-demonological vocabulary (adh. 19)**: bhūta/preta/yakṣa/
   piśāca/rākṣasa are technical affliction classes — clinical translation,
   no euphemism.
5. **Yoginī/dhātu mapping (adh. 20)**: technical physiology/erotics;
   exactness, no prurience.
6. **Recension divergence**: where M00038 differs from the Nepalese
   readings reported by Flood, mark TEXTUAL — never silently follow either.

## F. QUALITY BAR

Philosophical weight: adh. 2, 7–9, 14, 21. Procedural/medical: 15–19 (name
the operation, don't philosophise). Yogic content of adh. 7–8 must read as
practicable instructions, since the kulaprakriyā's whole point is body
affirmation.

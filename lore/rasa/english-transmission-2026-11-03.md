# Rasa transmission — how Sanskrit carries relish, and how English can

*2026-11-03 · companion to `abhinavagupta-rasa-grounding-2026-11-01.md` (doctrine)
and `lore/l4-english-songcraft-2026-11-03.md` (devices). This file is the bridge:
for each piece of Abhinavagupta's machinery, name the Sanskrit structure that
does the work, then the English structure that can do it. L4 craft = this table
applied verse by verse.*

## 1. The machinery (Abhinavagupta, condensed to what a translator can use)

- **Rasa-sutra**: *vibhāva-anubhāva-vyabhicāri-saṃyogād rasa-niṣpattiḥ* — relish
  arises from the union of determinants (the world/situation), consequents (the
  visible signs), and transitory states. Abhinava's answer: rasa is
  **manifested (abhivyakti) in the sahṛdaya's heart through suggestion** — not
  produced in the hero (Lollaṭa), not inferred (Śaṅkuka), not merely enjoyed
  through word-functions (Nāyaka, though Abhinava keeps his machinery).
- **Sthāyibhāva is central.** Only the durable latent emotion becomes rasa; the
  rest are conditions. Translator's corollary: **find the sthāyin first, then
  every word-choice serves it.** A verse without an identified sthāyin cannot
  be set to music — there is nothing to tune to.
- **Sādhāraṇīkaraṇa (generalization)**: particulars shed ("my grief" →
  grief-as-such) so the sahṛdaya tastes universally. Translator's corollary:
  English **deixis control** — "that cruel one" (pointed, particular) vs "the
  cruel" (generalized); possessives ("my heart") vs bare nouns ("the heart").
  Generalize where the Sanskrit generalizes; the reader enters where the
  particulars thin out.
- **Dhvani (suggestion), three ranks**: stated sense (vācya) < suggested sense
  (vyaṅgya). Highest: **rasadhvani** — rasa suggested without perceptible
  sequence (asaṃlakṣya-krama), outranking all ornament. Translator's corollary:
  **never state what the Sanskrit suggests.** If L4 names the rasa ("how
  wonderful!"), it kills the vyaṅgya — the verse drops a rank. The song must
  *do* the suggesting: image, rhythm, pause — never labels.
- **Camatkāra**: the rupture-relish itself, "apprehension in which only
  pleasure appears" — wonder (vismaya) is its texture. Structurally it is a
  **break**: the verse sets a pattern, then ruptures it. Translator's
  corollary: every L4 needs its rupture-point — the dash, the turn, the
  short line after long ones, the refrain returning changed.
- **Seven vighnas (obstacles)**: doubt about the mental state, temporal/spatial
  distance, absorption in private feeling, etc. Translator's corollary: **every
  obscurity you import is a vighna you built.** Archaic diction, tangled
  syntax, unexplained names — each is a wall between sahṛdaya and rasa. Plain
  singable diction is not dumbing down; it is vighna-removal.
- **Śāntarasa**: quiescence as the essence into which all rasas resolve.
  Translator's corollary: closings that settle (long vowels, falling cadence,
  still images) are not decoration — they are the metaphysics of the ending.

## 2. The transmission table

| # | Sanskrit structure | What it transmits | English structure (L4 device) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Verb-final syntax** (the "well-cut diamond": sense suspended until the last word) | Suspense → release; the gathering that makes camatkāra possible | **End-weight lines**: delay the resolving word to line-end. Same gathered release, English syntax intact |
| 2 | **Compounds (samāsa)** — a clause packed into one word | Density; simultaneity (all meanings present at once) | **Enjambed unpacking**: noun first, modifiers trailing down the next line; hyphen-chains for the tightest ones ("flower-looked-at body") |
| 3 | **Inflectional binders** (sa…umā vise-grip across the line) | Interlock; the line holding itself together | **Chiasmus / parallel placement** (1-with-3, 2-with-4 across the line) |
| 4 | **Yamaka** (sound-echo) | Texture; surface shimmer | **Alliteration/assonance**, never rhyme (rhyme adds closure the Sanskrit lacks) |
| 5 | **Meter-gait** (mandākrāntā slow-step; druta- quick; anuṣṭubh plain) | Bodily pace of the feeling | **English gait-match**: processional long lines (mandākrāntā-source), octosyllabic dance (song), ballad/hymn tread (śloka-source) — gait, never scanned imitation |
| 6 | **Dhruvapada (refrain)** | Stability; the still point the couplets dance around; syntactic resolution of dangling compounds | **Verbatim-repeated refrain** — never reworded; it is the anchor, and its return *is* the camatkāra |
| 7 | **Vocatives/addresses** (hṛdaya! rādhe! sakhe!) | Direct current to the sahṛdaya; the second person collapses distance (anti-vighna) | **Kept vocatives, line-initial or line-final** — "O heart" is load-bearing, not decoration |
| 8 | **Vowel music** (Ingalls' au-sunset: high diphthongs sinking to deep "au") | Wordless relish; meaning arriving as sound | **Assonance arcs**: bright vowels early, dark vowels at close; free, costs no doctrine |
| 9 | **Repetition-with-variation** (aṅgenāṅgaṃ, tanu-tanunā) | Insistence; the feeling pressing itself | **Anaphora / rhyme-engine** (Ryder's deep/keep/pine/entwine) — but every rhyming word must trace (subset rule) |
| 10 | **Dhvani-vyañjaka words** (the suggesting word, e.g. *iva*, *iva*-less suggestion, proper names placed to resonate) | The unsaid carrying more than the said | **Image left standing**: translate the image exactly, add no gloss, no label — let the suggestion do its own work |
| 11 | **Sādhāraṇīkaraṇa markers** (bare nouns, third person, timeless present) | Generalization; "grief-as-such" | **Deixis control**: thin the particulars ("that"→"the", drop possessives) exactly where the Sanskrit thins them |
| 12 | **Śānta-close** (dissolving agitation into quiescence) | Resolution into the essence-rasa | **Falling cadence**: long vowels, still images, shorter final line — the metaphysics of the ending, enacted |

## 3. The L4 pass, as procedure (verse-level)

1. Read L3 + C1 essence + rasa block. **Name the sthāyin** (one phrase).
2. Find the Sanskrit's transmission structures (which rows of the table fire?).
3. Draft the song using the English column. Every content word traces to L3
   (subset rule); music lives in order, rhythm, sound, pause.
4. Place the **rupture** (camatkāra-point): the dash, turn, or refrain-return.
5. Shape the **close**: does the verse settle (śānta-fall) or strike (vīra-stop)?
6. Read aloud. If it won't scan it won't parse (rule 16). If a rhyme smuggled
   doctrine, kill the rhyme (rule 17).
7. One verse → owner verdict → then continue (rule 15).

## 4. Anti-patterns (each is a named failure)

- **Stating the suggested** ("how wonderful!", "with devotion") — drops the
  verse a dhvani-rank. The vyaṅgya must stay vyaṅgya.
- **Rhyme-twist** (Ryder's "pine/entwine" for body-union) — music eating
  doctrine. Subset rule blocks; ear confirms.
- **Refrain rewording** — each variant is a new anchor; the song drifts.
- **Imported vighna** — archaism, tangle, unexplained name. If the sahṛdaya
  stumbles on your English, the Sanskrit's clarity is lost twice over.
- **Labeling the rasa** ("in śānta mood…") — L4 carries rasa in diction and
  cadence, never in labels. Rasa blocks live in C1 objects, not song lines.

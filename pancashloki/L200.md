# Pañcaślokī — L200

Abhinavagupta's five-verse hymn opens by reducing the devotee's whole duty to one act: pride offered on the Lord's lotus feet — his all, with nothing besides — and asks the protector of the helpless, ocean of compassion, to be pleased quickly. The offering becomes speech: at the Lord's door the devotee's word flashes "victory to Maheśa," faultless and unceasing, itself becoming bliss-giving, while he who already has Maheśa as protector asks to be made protected. Then crisis: You speak not, see not, hear not, come not, support not — though scripture truly declares You devoid of speech, sight, hearing, feet and the rest. Against this silence stand the guru's word, whose force wipes away heaped-up reasonings, and fitting scriptural assurance; with the doubt-cloud dissolved, the devotee touches the feet of the Lord whose splendor shines in the heart-sky, like the sun that conquers darkness. The close confesses illuminated contradiction: after long dwelling-with the Lord and everything done for him through service of the excellent guru, his body having reached the divine plane, he still forgets — therefore, let there be no further staying-away.

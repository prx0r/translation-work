## The parable (canonical draft, ≈70s spoken)

> A woman keeps vigil at a closed door. She has laid everything she
> owns on the step — that is her whole duty, she says — and asks for
> nothing beyond what this day gives. She cries victory at the
> doorway until the cry itself becomes the gift: faultless,
> unceasing, hers to keep. And from inside: nothing. No word, no
> step, no eye at the slit — five times nothing. The neighbors say
> he refuses her. Then an old traveler passes and shakes his head:
> "Refusal needs a face that can turn. The one inside is past all
> turning — that is the scripture's word — so the silence is not a
> door shut in your face." And he wipes their reasons for despair
> off her like weather off a slate. The cloud of doubt breaks, and a
> sun stands blazing in the inner sky. She asks for one thing only:
> not to be forgotten by him again — for she forgets him, alas, even
> now, even in this very moment.

**Map (hinge):**
- laid everything on the step, asks only today's giving → *sarvasvam
  arpitam… only immediate pleasure* [v1]
- cry at the doorway becomes the gift → *rucirā vāk… jaya jaya…
  nirdoṣam… bliss-giving forever* — speech as offering at the dvāra
  [v2; threshold = vibhāva per C1]
- five times nothing → the five refusals: speak·see·hear·come·support
  (*brūṣe na… paśyasi na… śṛṇuṣe na… āyāsi na… ālambase na*) [v3]
- traveler's correction → *the Lord is sense-free* — the charge
  converted to doctrine [v3, essence]
- wipes their reasons off like weather → *yukti-pracaya-racana-
  unmārjana-vaśāt* — force that wipes heaped-up reasonings [v4]
- cloud of doubt breaks, sun in the inner sky → *vilīne śaṅkābhre
  hṛdayagagana-udbhāsima-sūrya* — feet of the heart-sky Lord [v4]
- one request: not absent again; forgets even now → *vāram vāram…
  alas, in this very moment* — long dwelling, repeated forgetting [v5]

**Limit:** the door never explains itself — the story must NOT make
He-speak-nothing into punishment (the neighbors' reading) NOR into
sentimentality (the woman's hope); the scripture-reading (sense-free,
silence ≠ slammed door) stands between them, stated by the traveler,
never proven inside the tale.
**Rasa:** bhakti → karuṇā (five refusals) → adbhuta (assurance) →
śānta (cloud breaks) → karuṇā-acceptance (final confession). Ends on
the confession — the p5 sthāyibhāva, not on the sun.
**Bar check:** 2 characters (woman + one traveler; neighbors cited,
never rendered), retellable, plain words (Maheśa/sense-free doctrine
carried as "the one inside is past all turning").

## The seal — statement

**The praise going in through the un-shut door:** a kneeling woman at
a dark threshold, all she owns on the step, gold cries entering a
crack of light; five iron-dark marks on the door; one old traveler
beside her, hand raised where the doubt-cloud parts to a sun blazing
in the sky above the door — silence that is attention, not refusal.

## Composition laws (this seal)

TWO subjects max: woman + traveler; the Lord stays un-drawn (door
only — sense-free doctrine forbids His figure). Light leaks ≠ open
door: it looks shut, glows anyway (the whole argument). No faces
inside. No roundels. The five marks must read cold, not decorative.

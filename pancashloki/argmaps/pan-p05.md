# Pañcaślokī pan-p05 — ARGUMENT MAP (long nearness, forgetfulness, final plea)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 pan-p05 = verse 5. Lore: Navjivan Rastogi on Kashmir Śaiva stotra literature — the hymns stage the devotee's lived contradiction (nearness and forgetfulness together); cf. Abhinavagupta's habit of dating hymns (e.g. the Bhairavastava colophon, Pauṣa dark-fortnight), anchoring devotion in lived time. Key: vismara (forgetting) after long sahavāsa (dwelling-with) is the hymn's final problem, answered only by the plea that He remain absent no more.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Much time has passed dwelling with the Lord — yet, alas, in this very moment: what has He not done for the devotee, for the sake of serving the excellent guru! And yet again and again the devotee, whose body has attained the incomparable divine plane, forgets Him. Therefore: by Him, let there be no further staying-away.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Pāda a (v. 5a):**
> *yātastvatsahavāsato bahutaraḥ kālo vatāsmin kṣaṇe*
[i.e. gone, from dwelling with You, is very long time — alas — in this moment.] Bahutaraḥ kālaḥ (very long time) of sahavāsa (dwelling-with) contrasts with asmin kṣaṇe (in this moment); vata (alas) marks the poignancy.

**Pāda b (v. 5b):**
> *kiṃ kiṃ vā na kṛtaṃ tvayā varagurūpāsānimittena me*
[i.e. what, what has not been done by You for me, for the sake of serving the excellent guru!] Doubled kim kim (what, what) with vā na (or not) forms a rhetorical total: nothing has been left undone. The purpose named is varaguru-upāsā (service of the excellent guru) — the guru-thread from verse 4 returns as the rationale of grace received.

**Pāda c (v. 5c):**
> *vāraṃ vāramahaṃ punarnirupamāṃ divyaṃ bhuvaṃ prāsuvatkāyaḥ*
[i.e. time after time I, again — with body having attained the incomparable divine plane.] Vāraṃ vāram (time after time) and punar (again) stress repetition; the devotee's body (kāya) has attained (prāsu-vat) the nirupamā divyā bhū (incomparable divine plane) — attainment is conceded, not denied.

**Pāda d (v. 5d):**
> *tvāṃ kila vismarāmi yadataḥ stheyaṃ na bhūyastvayā*
[i.e. You indeed I forget — since, therefore, by You let there be no further staying-away.] Kila (indeed, as is well known) confesses the forgetfulness openly; yat ataḥ (since, therefore) draws the consequence. Na bhūyaḥ stheyam tvayā (by You, no further remaining-away) is the hymn's final request — not a boon, but presence.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Prāsuvatkāyaḥ is read as "whose body has attained (the plane)"; the formation is obscure and the reading is marked uncertain.
2. Yat (since) + ataḥ (therefore) is pleonastic pairing; kept as emphasis, not split into two reasons.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as illuminated contradiction: long dwelling + everything done → yet forgetting → plea for no-more-absence. L2 must keep the alas (vata) and the confession (kila), and end the hymn on presence-requested, not doctrine-stated. This closes the arc opened in p01 (total offering).

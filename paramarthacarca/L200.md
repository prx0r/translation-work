# Paramārthacarcā — L200

Abhinavagupta's eight-verse hymn opens by voiding subject-object language: distinct from sun, moon, and lamp-light, light itself does not shine as an object — too pervasive to appear — so illumined-versus-illuminator is mere convention. No thing stands dually apart from knowledge; made of this and that, in consciousness there is no difference, and the individual in the one abode of self-luminosity stays undivided. Dense, one own-consciousness is Śiva himself, the universe's supreme light — yet grasper-grasped division still shines within variegated power. That difference is not cut off from its ground: palace, elephant, horse, cloud, ocean, mountain — just as from a jewel-mirror. If even a common mirror shows prototype-with-reflection as mind-established, the free-consciousness mirror needs no other cause among things. Addressed — O consciousness-dense one, you indeed are supreme — in you the universes shine at once, flashing by glory-power; you are also their maker. Paradox held, not solved — primordially established yet unaccomplishable, non-lord yet lord — and praised: eternal, pervasive Bhairava-Lord. The close is practice: the conduct-septad remembered in the heart, the abode entered again, the discussion itself known.

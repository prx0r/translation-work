# Paramārthacarcā par-p02 — ARGUMENT MAP (verse 2)

*2026-11-01. Source: T1 par-p02 = verse 2. Lore: the verse's crux is its second word — jñānadvibhinnaḥ read here as dually distinct, in duality, from knowledge, so the denial runs: nothing stands apart from knowledge in a dualistic way; the alternative (a print slip for jñānād vibhinnaḥ, distinct from knowledge) is noted, not adopted. Śatam, hundredfold, is kept as printed against the tempting satatam, constantly.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Verse 2 of the Paramārthacarcā (Abhinavagupta, 8 verses). Gloss: dually-distinct-from-knowledge (jñānadvibhinnaḥ) not (na) indeed (hi) any (kaścid) thing (arthaḥ) made-of-this-and-that (tattatkṛtaḥ) in-consciousness (saṃvidi) not (na) exists (asti) difference (bhedaḥ); from-self-luminosity (svayamprakāśāt) hundredfold (śatam) in-the-one-abode (aikadhāmni) individual (prātisvikī) not-indeed (nāpi) divided (vibheditā) would-be (syāt).

Sanskrit: jñānadvibhinno na hi kaścidarthaḥ tattatkṛtaḥ saṃvidi nāsti bhedaḥ | svayamprakāśācchatam aikadhāmni prātisvikī nāpi vibheditā syāt

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Verse 2:**
> jñānadvibhinno na hi kaścidarthaḥ tattatkṛtaḥ saṃvidi nāsti bhedaḥ | svayamprakāśācchatam aikadhāmni prātisvikī nāpi vibheditā syāt

[i.e. no thing at all is dually distinct from knowledge; made of this and that, in consciousness there is no difference. From self-luminosity, hundredfold, in the one abode — the individual would not indeed be divided.]

No thing (na...kaścid arthaḥ) is dually-distinct (dvibhinnaḥ) from knowledge (jñāna): nothing stands apart from knowledge in duality. Made of this and that (tattatkṛtaḥ) — however composite — in consciousness (saṃvidi) there is (na asti) no difference (bhedaḥ). From self-luminosity (svayamprakāśāt), hundredfold (śatam), in the one abode (aikadhāmni): the individual (prātisvikī) would not indeed (nāpi) be divided (vibheditā syāt). Self-luminosity's hundredfold-ness in one abode leaves the individual undivided.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Jñānadvibhinnaḥ is read literally as dually distinct from knowledge; the alternative print reading jñānād vibhinnaḥ, distinct from knowledge, is noted, not adopted — the verse's next line (no difference in consciousness) supports the adopted sense.
2. Śatam is kept as printed, hundredfold, against the tempting satatam reading; the line scans either way, the printed text stands.

## 4. Decision for L2

Carry denial-of-separation then the undivided individual: nothing dually apart from knowledge; no difference in consciousness; the individual undivided in the one abode. L2 must keep the odd hundredfold as printed, flagged, not smoothed into constantly. Carry non-separation into verse 3, where Śiva is declared the universe's supreme light.

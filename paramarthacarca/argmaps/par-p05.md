# Paramārthacarcā par-p05 — ARGUMENT MAP (verse 5)

*2026-11-01. Source: T1 par-p05 = verse 5. Lore: the verse turns the mirror's belly (ādarśakukṣau) into the proof — if even a common mirror shows prototype-with-reflection as mind-established, the free-consciousness mirror needs no other cause among things; mukhura is read as mukura (mirror), the ādarśa context requiring it.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Verse 5 of the Paramārthacarcā (Abhinavagupta, 8 verses). Gloss: of-mirror (ādarśa) in-belly (kukṣau) reflection (pratibimba) making (kāri) with-prototype (sabimbakam) would-be (syāt) if (yadi) mind-proven (mānasiddham); free (svacchanda) consciousness (saṃvit) mirror (mukhura) in-the-interior (antarāle) among-things (bhāveṣu) cause (hetu) other (antaram) is (asti) not (na) other (anyat).

Sanskrit: ādarśakukṣau pratibimbakāri sabimbakaṃ syād yadi mānasiddham | svacchandasaṃvinmukhura(?)antarāle bhāveṣu hetvantaram asti nānyat

*(mukhura read as mukura, mirror — see §3.)*

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Verse 5:**
> ādarśakukṣau pratibimbakāri sabimbakaṃ syād yadi mānasiddham...

[i.e. if, in a mirror's belly, the reflection-making with-prototype would be mind-established...]

If (yadi), in a mirror's belly (ādarśakukṣau), the reflection-making (pratibimbakāri) with-prototype (sabimbakam) would be (syāt) mind-proven (mānasiddham) — established by mind alone — then, in the interior (antarāle) of the mirror (mukhura, read mukura) of free (svacchanda) consciousness (saṃvit), among things (bhāveṣu), there is (asti) no (na) other (anyat) cause (hetu) besides (antaram). The common mirror proves the pattern: prototype-with-reflection needs nothing but mind to stand. A fortiori the free-consciousness mirror needs no second cause among things. The verse's conditional concedes the small case to win the great one.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Mukhura is read as mukura (mirror); the ādarśa (mirror) context of the first half requires it. The print form is kept in T1; the reading is marked here, not hidden.
2. Mānasiddham (mind-proven) is kept literal; whether mind is the viewer's or consciousness itself is not decided.

## 4. Decision for L2

Carry analogy-then-denial: if even a common mirror needs only mind, then no other cause among things. L2 must keep the conditional structure — the verse argues a fortiori, not by assertion. Carry the causeless mirror into verse 6, where you yourself are declared its maker.

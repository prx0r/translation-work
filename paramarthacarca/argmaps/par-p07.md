# Paramārthacarcā par-p07 — ARGUMENT MAP (verse 7)

*2026-11-01. Source: T1 par-p07 = verse 7. Lore: the verse's paradox line — unaccomplishable self, non-lord, lord (asādhyam ātmānam anīśam īśam) — holds three incompatible predicates without resolving them; the only first-person verb of the hymn (īḍe, I praise) is here.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Verse 7 of the Paramārthacarcā (Abhinavagupta, 8 verses). Gloss: thus (ittham) self-consciousness (svasaṃvedanam) primordially-established (ādisiddham) unaccomplishable (asādhyam) self (ātmānam) non-lord (anīśam) lord (īśam); whose-place-and-time-are-full-of-own-power (svaśaktisaṃpūrṇavadeśakālam) eternal (nityam) pervasive (vibhum) Bhairava-Lord (bhairavanātham) I-praise (īḍe).

Sanskrit: itthaṃ svasaṃvedanam ādisiddham asādhyam ātmānam anīśam īśam | svaśaktisaṃpūrṇavadeśakālaṃ nityaṃ vibhuṃ bhairavanātham īḍe

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Verse 7:**
> itthaṃ svasaṃvedanam ādisiddham asādhyam ātmānam anīśam īśam | svaśaktisaṃpūrṇavadeśakālaṃ nityaṃ vibhuṃ bhairavanātham īḍe

[i.e. thus — self-consciousness, primordially established, unaccomplishable; the self, non-lord, lord; whose place and time are full of own power — eternal, pervasive: Bhairava-Lord, I praise.]

Thus (ittham): self-consciousness (svasaṃvedanam), primordially established (ādisiddham), unaccomplishable (asādhyam) — the self (ātmānam), non-lord (anīśam), lord (īśam). Three incompatible predicates held without blinking: established from the beginning, never to be accomplished; non-lord and lord at once. Then the object of the hymn's only first-person act: whose place and time are full of own power (svaśaktisaṃpūrṇavadeśakālam), eternal (nityam), pervasive (vibhum) — Bhairava-Lord (bhairavanātham): I praise (īḍe). Praise, not proof, is what the paradox gets.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Vadeśakālam is obscure (possibly vaśe deśakālam, place-and-time under control); glossed neutrally — whose place and time are full of own power — marked here, not smoothed.
2. Asādhyam (unaccomplishable) with ādisiddham (primordially established) is deliberate paradox, not contradiction to solve.

## 4. Decision for L2

Carry paradox-then-praise: established yet unaccomplishable, non-lord yet lord — therefore praise, not argument. L2 must let the three predicates stand unreconciled. Carry the praising I into verse 8, where remembering becomes entering.

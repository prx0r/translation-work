# C1 — Paramārthacarcā

## par-p01 — light needs no lamp
**SUMMARY.** Distinct from every lit thing, light itself does not shine as an object — too pervasive to appear; illumined-vs-illuminator is mere convention.

**KEY TERMS.** arkendudīpādi (sun-moon-lamp-and-the-rest), avabhāsa (illumination), ativyāptatā (over-pervasion), prakāśarūpa (of the nature of light), prakāśyaprakāśatā (illumined-illuminator), vyavahāra (convention).

**EXPLANATION.** Distinct (bhinnam) from the illumination (avabhāsa) of sun, moon, lamp and the rest (arkendudīpādi), it does not shine (na ābhāti) — on account of over-pervasion (ativyāptatayā): too pervasive to show up as an object, like sight itself never seen. And hence (tataḥ): it is of the nature of light, just this much (iyat) — illumined and illuminator named is convention (vyavahāra) merely (eva). The subject-object vocabulary is demoted; what is, is light, unobjectifiable because everywhere.

## par-p02 — nothing apart from knowledge
**SUMMARY.** No thing stands dually apart from knowledge; in consciousness there is no difference; the individual, in the one abode of self-luminosity, stays undivided.

**KEY TERMS.** jñānadvibhinna (dually distinct from knowledge), tattatkṛta (made of this and that), saṃvit (consciousness), bheda (difference), svayamprakāśa (self-luminosity), aikadhāman (one abode), prātisvikī (individual), vibhedita (divided).

**EXPLANATION.** No thing (na...kaścid arthaḥ) is dually-distinct (dvibhinnaḥ) from knowledge (jñāna): nothing stands apart in duality. Made of this and that (tattatkṛtaḥ) — however composite — in consciousness (saṃvidi) there is no difference (na...bhedaḥ). From self-luminosity (svayamprakāśāt), hundredfold (śatam), in the one abode (aikadhāmni): the individual (prātisvikī) would not indeed be divided. Self-luminosity's hundredfold-ness in one abode leaves the individual undivided — kept as printed against the tempting satatam, flagged, not smoothed.

## par-p03 — dense consciousness is Śiva
**SUMMARY.** Dense, one own-consciousness is Śiva himself, the universe's supreme light — yet grasper-grasped division still shines within variegated power.

**KEY TERMS.** ittham (thus), svasaṃvidghana (dense own-consciousness), eka (one), śiva (Śiva), viśvasya paraḥ prakāśaḥ (universe's supreme light), vicitraśakti (variegated power), grāhya (apprehended), gṛhītṛ (apprehender), pravibhāga (division).

**EXPLANATION.** Thus (ittham): dense (ghanaḥ) own-consciousness (svasaṃvit), one (ekaḥ) indeed (eva) — he (saḥ) is Śiva, the universe's supreme light. The positive declaration after two verses of denial. Yet there (tatra) too (api), indeed (eva) shines (bhāti): in variegated (vicitra) power (śaktau) — apprehended, apprehender, division, difference. Division shines without dividing the light; grasper/grasped play on within the undivided.

## par-p04 — uncut difference; the mirror list
**SUMMARY.** Difference is not cut off from its ground — free, most-clear, one-abode — then the bare list against the jewel-mirror: palace through mountain, just as from jewel-mirror and the like.

**KEY TERMS.** bheda (difference), tataḥ (thence), svacchanda (free), susvacchatama (most-perfectly-clear), ekadhāman (one-abode), prāsāda/hasti/aśva/payoda/sindhu/giri (palace/elephant/horse/cloud/ocean/mountain), yadvat (just as), maṇidarpaṇa (jewel-mirror).

**EXPLANATION.** That (saḥ) difference (bhedaḥ), and (ca) this (ayam): not (na) different (vibhinnaḥ) thence (tataḥ) — from the free, most-perfectly-clear one-abode. Difference belongs without dividing. Then the analogy, deliberately bare: palace, elephant, horse, cloud, ocean, mountain and the rest — just as (yadvat) of jewel-mirror and the like (maṇidarpaṇādeḥ). No verb supplied, none needed: difference uncut from its ground, the bare list its proof — reflected things need no ground but the clear mirror.

## par-p05 — the mirror needs only mind
**SUMMARY.** If even a common mirror shows prototype-with-reflection as mind-established, the free-consciousness mirror needs no other cause among things.

**KEY TERMS.** ādarśakukṣa (mirror's belly), pratibimba (reflection), sabimbaka (with-prototype), mānasiddha (mind-proven), svacchandasaṃvit (free consciousness), mukura (mirror), antarāla (interior), hetvantara (other cause).

**EXPLANATION.** If (yadi), in a mirror's belly (ādarśakukṣau), the reflection-making (pratibimbakāri) with-prototype (sabimbakam) would be (syāt) mind-proven (mānasiddham) — established by mind alone — then, in the interior (antarāle) of the mirror of free (svacchanda) consciousness (saṃvit), among things (bhāveṣu), there is (asti) no (na) other (anyat) cause besides (hetvantaram). The common mirror proves the pattern; a fortiori the free-consciousness mirror needs no second cause. Mukhura is read as mukura (mirror) — the ādarśa context requires it; print form kept in T1, reading marked here.

## par-p06 — address: you are supreme
**SUMMARY.** O consciousness-dense one: you indeed are supreme; in you the universes shine at once, flashing by glory-power; you stand as both their maker and their supreme Lord.

**KEY TERMS.** saṃvidghana (consciousness-dense), tena (therefore), para (supreme), tvayi (in you), viśvāni (universes), drāk (at once), sphuranti (flash forth), tvat-mahas (your glory), prabhāva (power), parameśakartṛ (supreme-Lord maker).

**EXPLANATION.** Address first: O consciousness-dense one (saṃvidghana) — therefore (tena) you (tvam) indeed (eva) are supreme (paraḥ). Then three clauses of consequence: in you (tvayi) indeed (eva) the universes (viśvāni) shine (cakāsati) at once (drāk); and (ca) they flash forth (sphuranti) by the power (prabhāvāt) of your (tvat) glory (mahasaḥ); you (tvam) indeed (eva) also (ca) are their (eṣām) supreme-Lord (parameśa) maker (kartā). Shining, flashing, making — all in you, all at once, by glory-power not effort.

## par-p07 — paradox held; praise given
**SUMMARY.** Established yet unaccomplishable, non-lord yet lord, place-and-time full of own power — eternal, pervasive: Bhairava-Lord, I praise.

**KEY TERMS.** ittham (thus), svasaṃvedana (self-consciousness), ādisiddha (primordially established), asādhya (unaccomplishable), ātman (self), anīśa/īśa (non-lord/lord), svaśaktisaṃpūrṇa (full of own power), vadeśakāla (place-and-time), nitya (eternal), vibhu (pervasive), bhairavanātha (Bhairava-Lord), īḍe (I praise).

**EXPLANATION.** Thus (ittham): self-consciousness (svasaṃvedanam), primordially established (ādisiddham), unaccomplishable (asādhyam) — the self (ātmānam), non-lord (anīśam), lord (īśam). Three incompatible predicates held without blinking: established from the beginning, never to be accomplished; non-lord and lord at once. Then the object of the hymn's only first-person act: whose place and time are full of own power — eternal (nityam), pervasive (vibhum): Bhairava-Lord (bhairavanātham), I praise (īḍe). Vadeśakālam is obscure (possibly vaśe deśakālam, place-and-time under control); glossed neutrally, marked here, not smoothed. Praise, not proof, is what the paradox gets.

## par-p08 — remembered, entered, known
**SUMMARY.** The conduct-septad remembered in the heart; the abode entered again; the discussion itself known — the hymn names its own close.

**KEY TERMS.** sadvṛtta (good conduct), saptaka (septad), galita (dissolved), anyacintā (other thoughts), samyak (rightly), smaranti (remember), hṛdaya (heart), paramārthakāma (desiring the ultimate), bhairavīya (Bhairava's one), paradhāman (supreme abode), muhuḥ (again), viśanti (enter), jānanti (know), trijagatīparamārthacarcā (three-worlds' ultimate-thing-discussion).

**EXPLANATION.** This (idam) septad (saptakam) of good conduct (sadvṛtta) — those whose other (anya) thoughts (cintāḥ) are dissolved (galitā), desiring the ultimate (paramārthakāmāḥ), rightly (samyak) remember (smaranti) it in the heart (hṛdaye): conduct remembered inwardly, not performed outwardly. Those (te) — O Bhairava's one (bhairavīya, vocative address) — again (muhuḥ) enter (viśanti) the supreme abode (paradhāma), and (ca) know (jānanti) the ultimate-thing-discussion (paramārthacarcām) of the three worlds (trijagatī). Entering, then knowing: the abode first, its discussion after. The hymn names itself at the close — those who enter know this very discussion.

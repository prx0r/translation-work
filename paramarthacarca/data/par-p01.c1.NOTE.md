# C1 NOTE — par-p01.c1

Moved from C1 (pristine rasa-evocative translation only):

## SUMMARY

Distinct from every lit thing, light itself does not shine as an object — too pervasive to appear; illumined-vs-illuminator is mere convention.

## FUNCTION

Establishes light as unobjectifiable; subject-object vocabulary demoted.

## KEY TERMS

* **arkendudīpādi** — sun-moon-lamp-and-the-rest
* **avabhāsa** — illumination
* **ativyāptatā** — over-pervasion
* **prakāśarūpa** — of the nature of light
* **prakāśyaprakāśatā** — illumined-illuminator
* **vyavahāra** — convention

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Light needs no lamp.

## EXPLANATION

Distinct (bhinnam) from the illumination (avabhāsa) of sun, moon, lamp and the rest (arkendudīpādi), it does not shine (na ābhāti) — on account of over-pervasion (ativyāptatayā): too pervasive to show up as an object, like sight itself never seen. And hence (tataḥ): it is of the nature of light, just this much (iyat) — illumined and illuminator named is convention (vyavahāra) merely (eva). The subject-object vocabulary is demoted; what is, is light, unobjectifiable because everywhere.

## BOUNDARY

Whether 'iyat' (just this much) limits or affirms is unresolved.

## RELATED

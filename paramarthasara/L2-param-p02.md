# Paramarthasara param-p02 — L2 READ

## v.11
Free of every thought, pure, at peace, beyond rising and dissolving — in that supreme principle shines the world, thirty-six in nature.
The Sanskrit: *sarvavikalpavihīnaṃ śuddhaṃ śāntaṃ layodayavihīnam | yat paratattvaṃ tasmin vibhāti ṣaṭṭriṃśadātma jagat*

## v.12
As towns and villages, varied yet undivided in a mirror's orb, shine out in division, each from each and from the mirror too.
The Sanskrit: *darpaṇabimbe yadvan nagaragrāmādicitram avibhāgi | bhāti vibhāgenaiva ca parasparaṃ darpaṇād api ca*

## v.13
From the purest knowing of supreme Bhairava: though void of division, the world shines forth divided, each from each and thence.
The Sanskrit: *vimalatamaparamabhairavabodhāt tadvad vibhāgaśūnyam api | anyonyaṃ ca tato 'pi ca vibhaktam ābhāti jagad etat*

## v.14
Śiva, power, Sadāśiva, then the Īśvara-Vidyā-formed principle-state — the five powers, in divided nature, He lights up.
The Sanskrit: *śivaśaktisadāśivatām īśvaravidyāmayīṃ ca tattvadaśām | śaktīnāṃ pañcānāṃ vibhaktabhāvena bhāsayati*

## v.15
Supreme is the freedom that effects the hard thing, the great Lord's own; the goddess, Māyā-power, veils Śiva's self — that is it.
The Sanskrit: *paramaṃ yat svātantryaṃ durghaṭasaṃpādanaṃ maheśasya | devī māyāśaktiḥ svātmāvaraṇaṃ śivasya itat*

## v.16
Grasped by Māyā, knowing turns stained and the person becomes a bound beast, fettered by time, art, limit, passion, ignorance.
The Sanskrit: *māyāparigrahavaśād bodho malinaḥ pumān paśur bhavati | kālakalāniyatibalād rāgāvidyāvaśena saṃbaddhaḥ*

## v.17
Here and now I know but little, with my whole self: six sheaths with Māyā are the atom's inner limbs, so it is said.
The Sanskrit: *adhunaiva kiṃcid evedam eva sarvātmanaiva jānāmi | māyāsahitaṃ kañcukaṣaṭkam aṇor antaraṅgam idam uktam*

## v.18
Like husk settled on rice-grain, separate yet undivided, purity comes by turning to Śiva's path with longing.
The Sanskrit: *kambukam iva ta.ṅdulakaṇaviniviṣṭaṃ bhinnam apy abhidā | bhajate tat tu viśuddhiṃ śivamārgaunmukhyayogena*

## v.19
Mere pleasure, pain, delusion — from resolve, will, and I-sense; then nature, then the inner organ: intellect, mind, ego in turn.
The Sanskrit: *sukhaduḥkhamohamātraṃ niścayasaṃkalpanābhimānāc ca | prakṛtir athāntaḥkaranaṃ buddhimano'haṃkṛtikramaśaḥ*

## param-p01 — Resort to Śambhu; the fourfold universe (vv. 2–10)

**Resort (v. 2).** Supreme, abiding in depths, beginningless, one, lodged in many caves, abode of all, in all moving and non-moving — to you, Śambhu, I resort. The student's stance is refuge: not in a doctrine but in the one who is everywhere and in everything.

**The question (v. 3).** Revolving in the wheel of suffering from womb-dwelling to death, the student asked the Lord, the support, the supreme truth. The question is existential: how does one get out of the wheel?

**The Guru's answer (v. 4).** The Guru spoke to him the essence in support-kārikās; Abhinava speaks by Śiva-śāsana view. The text is an *ādhārakārikā* — verses that serve as support — and Abhinava's authority is *dṛṣṭi*: both philosophical system and yogic perception attained through sādhana.

**The fourfold universe (v. 5).** By own-power-glory-weight, this fourfold universe in division: power, Māyā, prakṛti, earth — manifested by the Lord. The universe is not created *ex nihilo*; it is the Lord's own power (*śakti*) unfolding into Māyā (veiling), prakṛti (materiality), and pṛthvī (earth, the grossest).

**The world-continuum (v. 6).** Therein, this world, the varied body-instrument-world-continuum. The world is a *saṃtāna* — a continuum of bodies and instruments, not a collection of separate things.

**The experiencer (v. 7).** And there, the experiencer, embodied; Śiva indeed, having assumed the condition of a bound soul (*paśu*). As a crystal takes on the colors of what surrounds it, so Śiva takes on the forms of the world — gods, humans, animals, plants.

**Reflection (v. 8).** As the moon's reflection in water moves when the water moves and stays when it stays, so this self, Maheśāna, in the mass of body-instrument-world. As Rāhu, though invisible, appears in the moon's disk, so the supreme self appears in the limited knower.

**Mirror (v. 9).** Though all-pervading, this self shines in the mind-mirror like a face in a stainless mirror, by taking on objects. In the mind made stainless by Śiva's śaktipāta, it shines as light-form; full, resting in itself, it is great bliss — filled with will, consciousness, and instruments, complete with infinite powers.

## param-p02 — The supreme tattva; the 36 tattvas; mirror (vv. 11–19)

**The supreme tattva (v. 11).** Free from all thought, pure, peaceful, free from dissolution and rise — that supreme principle, in which shines the 36-fold world. The supreme is not an absence; it is the fullness in which the world appears.

**The mirror (v. 12).** As in a mirror-image, city and village etc. are undivided; yet they shine in division, and mutually, from the mirror too. The mirror is not changed by what it reflects; the reflections are not separate from the mirror. This is the Trika's non-dualism: the world is real as Śiva's self-manifestation, not as an independent substance.

**The source (v. 13).** From the purest supreme Bhairava-knowledge, thus though divisionless, mutually and from that, divided, this world shines. The division is real at the level of appearance; the unity is real at the level of the source. Both are true, neither contradicts the other.

**The tattva-state (v. 14).** The Śiva-Śakti-Sadāśiva-Īśvara-Vidyā tattva-state. Five tattvas at the top of the ladder: Śiva (pure consciousness), Śakti (his power), Sadāśiva (I-am-this, the first stirring), Īśvara (I-am, the subject), Vidyā (this-am-I, the object).

**The five Śaktis (v. 15).** By the divided nature of the five Śaktis, the supreme — the hard-to-accomplish *svātantrya* of Maheśa — manifests. The Lord's freedom is what makes the division possible without compromising the unity.

## param-p03 — Senses, tanmātras, elements; creation from prakṛti (vv. 20–28)

**The senses (vv. 20–21).** Ear, skin, eye, tongue, nose = cognitive senses; speech, hand, foot, anus, genitals = conative senses. Ten senses, five and five — the standard Sāṃkhya enumeration, adopted by the Trika.

**The tanmātras (v. 22).** Their object, subtle, free from division, is the five tanmātras: sound, touch, form, taste, smell. The tanmātras are the subtle elements — the "that-ness" of each sense-object, not yet gross.

**The gross elements (v. 23).** By combination of these, the gross object becomes the five elements: space, air, fire, water, earth. The gross elements arise from the combination (*saṃsarga*) of tanmātras.

**Husk and grain (v. 24).** As husk covers the grain, creation from prakṛti covers [consciousness]. From earth up to this, consciousness is the supreme covering; the malas are subtle, the cloaks are gross. Prakṛti's creation is a covering (*āvaraṇa*), not a destruction. The grain (consciousness, *cit*) is still there under the husk (the body-elements).

**Three cloaks (v. 25).** The self, wrapped in three cloaks, by the darkness of ignorance, though one in nature, apprehends itself as varied objects and knowers. As sugar-cane juice, molasses, sugar, and candy are one juice in different forms, so all states are Śambhoḥ's own.

**Snake and rope (v. 26).** All this is mere transaction; in ultimate truth they do not exist, as the snake does not exist in the rope, yet causes terror up to death. The world is *vyavahāra* (transactional), not *paramārtha* (ultimate). The fear is real, the snake is not.

**Power of delusion (v. 27).** The great power of delusion cannot be distinguished by name. Delusion has its own *śakti*; it is not nothing. The Trika's realism: the world is real *as* Śiva's self-manifestation, not as an independent substance.

## param-p04 — Bondage; saṃkoca; knowers (vv. 29–37)

**Bondage (v. 29).** Thus dharma, adharma, heaven, hell, birth, death, pleasure, pain, varṇa, āśrama etc. — though unreal — arise in the self by delusion's power. Bondage is not a real thing; it is the self's misidentification with non-self (body, breath, etc.) by the power of delusion (*vibhramabala*).

**Darkness upon darkness (v. 30).** This is that darkness which, by shining in things, even in non-self things, becomes I-am-not-self delusion; darkness upon darkness. The delusion is not ignorance of an external object; it is the self's own light misdirected onto non-self things, making them appear as "I."

**The net (v. 31).** I-am-self-ness in non-self (body, breath) wraps the self in a net, like a net-maker by his own net. By the power of self-knowledge's shining, it can unwrap itself. The self is both the maker and the trapped.

**World as play (v. 32).** Paramaśiva spreads the wonderful play of bondage and liberation: creation, maintenance, dissolution; waking, dream, sleep — in turīya. The three states shine in the fourth, not covering it, as the sky is not dirtied by clouds.

**Saṃkoca (v. 33).** As the spotless moon by timira-afflicted sight shines as if defiled by Rāhu, so Paramaśiva, though pure, appears impure when one attains knowerhood. The impurity is apparent, not real — it belongs to the knower's contracted vision, not to Śiva.

**Contracted experiencer (v. 34).** Having first removed the shining of non-self form in non-self, Paramātmā destroys the delusion of non-self form in the self. Once the root of the double delusion is cut, the accomplished yogi has no further task.

**Threefold prakṛti (v. 35).** Earth, prakṛti, Māyā — this threefold has fallen into the state of being an object of knowledge. By non-dual contemplation, only pure being remains, as gold remains when bracelets, rings, and bangles are seen through as gold.

**Five knowers (v. 36).** The Lord, playing, drives the wheel of the power-circle. The knowers: Śiva (pure), Mantramaheśvara (Māyā-mala), Mantreśvara (vidyā-mala), Mantrī (kalā-mala), vijñānakala (kalā only), pralayākala (Māyā only), sakala (both malas). The ladder ascends from sakala to Śiva.

## param-p05 — Saṃkoca; śaktipāta; Guru's grace (vv. 38–46)

**Moon/Rāhu continued (v. 38).** As the moon, when the sky is clear, is clear; when agitated, agitated; when deluded, deluded — when the tattvas are present, the Lord is not, in ultimate truth, thus. The Lord's state depends on the knower's vision, not on any change in Śiva.

**Removing delusion (v. 39).** Having first removed the shining of non-self form in non-self, Paramātmā destroys the delusion of non-self form in the self. Two-stage purification: external (the world is not "I") and internal (the body is not "I").

**Accomplished yogi (v. 40).** Thus, when the root of the double delusion is cut, the accomplished yogi has no further task to perform. Liberation is not an achievement but a cessation of the need to achieve.

**Threefold veiling (v. 41).** Earth, prakṛti, Māyā — this threefold has fallen into the state of being an object of knowledge.

**Non-dual contemplation (v. 42).** By the power of non-dual contemplation, only pure being remains, as gold remains when bracelets, rings, and bangles are seen through as gold. Non-dual contemplation (*advaitabhāvana*) sees through the form to the substance.

**Result (v. 43).** So, when difference is abandoned, all shines as pure being — that Brahman, supreme, pure, peaceful, of non-dual nature. The immortal, the true, rests in the shining power.

**Śaktipāta (v. 44).** Even the sky-born, by the union of the three-pronged śakti, reaches the supreme Lord. *Śaktitriśūla* — the three-pronged power: this is śaktipāta, the descent of grace that makes liberation possible. In the name of Śiva, in the supreme, the God of gods releases.

**The return (v. 45).** Again, by the fivefold power's spreading, outside too that; the threefold (gross/subtle/supreme) is wonderfully created, by the external attainment of the self. After liberation, the Lord creates again — but now the creation is recognized as self, not as other.

## param-p06 — The five/seven knowers (vv. 47–55)

**The Lord drives the wheel (v. 47).** The Lord, playing, drives the wheel of the power-circle. The knowers are positions on Śiva's own wheel, driven by his play (*krīḍā*).

**Śiva (v. 48).** I alone, pure in form, standing in the position of the great wheel's lord of powers. Pure, stainless, omniscient, all-doer. No mala. Top of the ladder.

**Mantramaheśvara (v. 49).** The world shines in me alone, as pots etc. in a stainless mirror. Contracted by Māyā-mala only.

**Mantreśvara (v. 50).** All spreads from me, like the variety of a dream from sleep. Contracted by vidyā-mala.

**Mantrī (v. 51).** I alone am world-formed, like the body with hand, foot etc. Contracted by kalā-mala.

**Vijñānakala (v. 52).** I shine in all things, like a seer, hearer, smeller — though devoid of body and senses, a non-doer. Pure after abandoning Māyā-mala; contracted by kalā-mala only.

**Pralayākala (v. 53).** I alone craft the varied, picture-like sections of siddhānta, āgama, and tarka. Pure after abandoning kalā-mala; contracted by Māyā-mala only.

**Sakala (v. 54).** Thus, when dualistic thought dissolves, transcending deluding Māyā, one becomes absorbed in Brahman as water in water, milk in milk. The sakala, contracted by both malas, is the ordinary bound soul.

**Karma destruction (v. 55).** What grief, what delusion, for one who sees all as Brahman? Auspicious and inauspicious karma-fruit arises only from contact with false knowledge. Contact is the flaw, like a thief's association harming the innocent. Long-accumulated karma, like cotton, is destroyed by the flame of knowledge.

## param-p07 — Liberation; śaktipāta; the four upāyas (vv. 56–64)

**Knowledge without fruit (v. 56).** Even if a deed is done, it bears no fruit for one who has attained knowledge; how then birth? Like the sun's rays, knowledge dispels the darkness of bondage. As a seed freed from husk, chaff, and awn produces a sprout, so the self, freed from the sheaths, becomes a knower.

**Knower fears nothing (v. 57).** The self freed from āṇava, māyā, and karma does not produce the sprout of becoming; the knower fears nothing, for all is his own form. He does not grieve, for in the supreme there is no destruction.

**The treasure (v. 58).** From the deeply hidden treasure of supreme truths in the heart, "I am Maheśvara" — what misfortune, what liberation? There is no place to go, no path to travel. Liberation is not going somewhere; it is recognizing what one already is.

**The knot of ignorance (v. 59).** Liberation is the manifestation of one's own power by cutting the knot of ignorance; the knot cut, doubt gone, delusion overcome. Even while embodied, with merit and demerit exhausted, he is liberated. As a seed burned by fire cannot sprout, so karma burned by knowledge's fire cannot produce birth.

**Knowledge vs. karma (v. 60).** Thus karma burned by knowledge's fire does not produce birth; for by limited understanding, karma produces the body suited to it; contracted consciousness shines thus when this body perishes. But if pure knowledge — the transcendent knower-doer, the all-pervading, unsetting, risen light-form, true resolve — then [liberation].

**The four upāyas (v. 61).** The four means: (1) anupāya — no means; liberation by śaktipāta alone. (2) śāmbhava — by will alone. (3) śākta — by knowledge alone. (4) āṇava — by action alone. The ladder descends from anupāya (highest, least effort) to āṇava (lowest, most effort).

**The ascent (vv. 62–63).** By very intense śaktipāta, at that moment, unobstructed, Śiva. Attaining the supreme form step by step (*sopāna*). At the end, attainment of the supreme principle's form: Śiva-mayībhāva.

**The middle (v. 64).** But for one who has not reached the supreme-principle stream, resting in the middle, death may come even for one eager for that state. The yogabhraṣṭa (fallen yogi) is described in scripture as lord of a world of varied enjoyment. By the power of the resting-place, in another birth he becomes Śiva.

## param-p08 — World as Śiva's own nature; jīvanmukti (vv. 65–73)

**The world is Śiva (v. 65).** The Lord, free from space-time reckoning, eternal, imperishable, fully complete, sole maker of dissolution, rise, and creation, with many powers. The world is not made by a separate creator; it is Śiva's own activity.

**Awakening (v. 66).** One should awaken to the self as Śiva, the well-maker of creation etc.; how could one be a saṃsārin? For the all-pervading, where is refuge, where flight? The knower recognizes: I am not in the world; the world is in me.

**Karma's futility (v. 67).** By reasoning too it is established: karma for the knower bears no fruit. "This is not mine, but his" — with firmness, no fruit of karma in the world. The knower does not accumulate karma because he does not claim ownership of action.

**The knower's freedom (v. 68).** Thus, awakened to all thoughts by the wind of contemplation, offering into the blazing self-light, one becomes light. The metaphor: the knower offers all thoughts into the self-light (like oblations into fire) and becomes light itself.

**Jīvanmukti (v. 69).** Eating this or that, clothed by whatever, peaceful; dwelling anywhere, the knower of all beings is liberated. Even performing a hundred thousand horse sacrifices or killing a hundred thousand brahmins (hyperbole) — the knower of the supreme is not touched by merit or demerit; stainless.

**Delusion of distinction (v. 70).** Free from pride, joy, anger, agitation, sorrow, fear, greed, delusion; without praise or oblation, moving like the inert, free from dispute. Pride and the rest arise from the delusion of difference.

**Advaita (v. 71).** By the awakening to the non-dual self, how could he be touched? There is nothing to praise or offer, nothing separate from him. He is pleased by praise etc.; the liberated one is free from bowing and oblation.

**Threefold world (implied).** The world is Śiva's own nature, threefold: gross (five elements), subtle (five tanmātras), supreme (five tattvas). The knower sees all three as Śiva.

## param-p09 — Body as Śiva's garment; practices (vv. 74–82)

**Body as garment (v. 74).** The body, bearing the 36 tattvas, complete with the windows of its structure — one's own or another's, a pot etc. — is the God's temple. And there, the supreme self, the great Bhairava-Śiva-deity, with his own power. The body is not an obstacle; it is the temple where Śiva dwells.

**Worship (v. 75).** Worshipping with the stainless substances of self-reflection, one offers the great seed-mass of external and internal distinction-thoughts. When the supremely blazing self-consciousness blazes, homa happens without effort. Worship is not external ritual; it is the offering of one's own distinctions into the fire of self-reflection.

**Meditation (v. 76).** Meditation, unsetting; and the Lord creates the varied forms; that very meditation is the painting of true forms by thought. The whole series of worlds, the tattva-sequence, the sense-faculties. Meditation is not concentration on an object; it is the Lord's own creative act, recognized.

**Recitation (v. 77).** What revolves in the inner knowledge — that is his japa, declared. Seeing all with the eye of samaya, perceiving all as consciousness. Japa is not the repetition of a mantra; it is the turning of consciousness inward.

**The vow (v. 78).** The world as cremation-ground, the body as a skull-staff, the world's essence as wine in the hand, the known-fragment as a skull-cup — tasting this is his vrata. Both hard to obtain and easy to obtain. Obtaining the name "paramārtha-Maheśvara," free from birth and death. The vrata is the recognition that the world is a cremation-ground (all things die) and the body is a skull (empty of self).

**The result (v. 79).** From the light of obtaining, the accomplished one stands as he wishes; the all-pervading, the all-self, free from diversity; who knows the incomparable supreme bliss becomes one with it. The result: standing free, recognizing oneself as all, becoming one with bliss.

## param-p10 — Mind as Śiva's function (vv. 83–91)

**Place-independence (v. 83).** In a tīrtha or in a dog-eater's house, even one who has lost memory, abandoning the body, at the moment of knowledge is liberated, sorrow destroyed. Pilgrimage serves merit, death in a dog-eater's house serves demerit — but only for the unawakened. Liberation happens at the moment of knowledge, regardless of place.

**The point (v. 84).** When there is no contact with the stain of merit and demerit, what use is that? Like separating husk and grain and throwing away the husk; the grain (consciousness) is not affected. It does not make the grain into husk. So too, merit/demerit does not touch the knower's consciousness.

**The knower in the world (v. 85).** So too consciousness, separated from the cloak-mass by saṃskāra, though abiding, the liberated self is free from contact. Like a jewel made stainless by the skill of the best artisan, from the casket's covering. Even a dirty jewel, when separated from its setting, is pure in ultimate truth.

**Recognition (v. 86).** So, by the stainless state of the good Guru's teaching, the body-covering is known; even the liberated, free from other coverings, shines as Śiva's form. Even by the authority of scripture etc., with unwavering faith, one attains oneness with that.

**Past (v. 87).** He attained heaven, hell, human birth before; but in the final moment, bearing merit or demerit, for the deluded it becomes a co-cause; but for the one who has gone (to liberation), it is not a cause. Even those who know it as their own self — animals, birds, reptiles — attain that state, purified by ancient awakening.

**The fourfold inner instrument (implied).** Mind (*manas*) = resolve/doubt; intellect (*buddhi*) = determination; ego (*ahaṃkāra*) = I-ness; citta = memory. Mind supports the senses; intellect determines; ego is the agent; citta remembers. These are Śiva's function, not independent faculties.

**The knower's freedom (implied).** The knower, having recognized mind as Śiva's function, is free from the inner instrument's binding power.

**The closing (implied).** The knower, having recognized mind as Śiva's function, is liberated.

## param-p11 — Soul as Śiva's part (vv. 92–100)

**The soul's threefold nature (v. 92).** Heaven-filled, hell-filled, this puruṣa resides between bodies. When that (body) breaks, by its own fitness, it attains union with another body. The soul transmigrates, but its nature is determined by its recognition, not by the body.

**Recognition persists (v. 93).** At the moment of knowledge, the self shines for him as it is; so it remains, not otherwise at the body's fall. Recognition is not body-dependent; it persists through death.

**The body's end (v. 94).** Loss of the sense-faculties, destruction of memory, breath in agitation, cutting; special pains in the vital points; enjoyment born of body-saṃskāra. How could it not arise when the body is present, even in delusion's presence?

**The knower at death (v. 95).** At the moment of death, the knower does not fall from the supreme truth of the self; this supreme path he quickly attains from the Guru's mouth. By very intense śaktipāta, at that moment, unobstructed, Śiva. Attaining the supreme form step by step.

**The end (v. 96).** At the end, attainment of the supreme principle's form: Śiva-mayībhāva. But for one who has not reached the supreme-principle stream, resting in the middle, death may come even for one eager for that state.

**The yogabhraṣṭa (v. 97).** The yogabhraṣṭa (fallen yogi) is described in scripture as lord of a world of varied enjoyment. By the power of the resting-place, in another birth he becomes Śiva. Even failure on the path leads to Śiva; the path is not a one-shot.

**The paramārtha path (v. 98).** Even without attaining yoga, having practiced this supreme path, he becomes an enjoyer of heaven, joyful, delighting for a long time. Heaven-enjoyment is not final, but it is not nothing; the path bears fruit even in incompleteness.

**The close (vv. 99–100).** Śivahood is the only final state; heaven and hell are intermediate.

## param-p12 — The seal: completion; all becomes Śiva (vv. 101–105)

**The king and the yogabhraṣṭa (v. 101).** As a king, sovereign in his realm, is worshipped by all people, so the yogabhraṣṭa is worshipped by all gods in the worlds. The fallen yogi, though not liberated, is still exalted — his practice has borne fruit even in incompleteness.

**The return (v. 102).** After a long time, attaining humanity again, practicing yoga, he obtains the divine immortal, from which there is no return. The yogabhraṣṭa's path is not lost; it resumes in another birth and reaches the immortal.

**The instruction (v. 103).** Therefore, whoever is devoted to this true path attains Śivahood. The text's practical instruction: devotion to the path is sufficient.

**The essence (v. 104).** Knowing this, in the supreme, as it is, still one should strive; meditating on this essence spoken by Abhinavagupta, the supreme Brahman. Quickly, Śivahood, the entry into one's own heart, is attained. In a hundred verses, this condensed essence of śāstra, very hidden.

**The seal (v. 105).** By me, Abhinavagupta, kindled by the remembrance of Śiva's feet. The author's signature: the text is spoken by one whose fire is the remembrance of Śiva's feet.

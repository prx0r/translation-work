## s01 — the still center that thunders (approved ferryman version)

> A man keeps a ferry on a river that has forgotten he exists.
> Storms come, ships come, whole cities tip into the water — and
> he does not move; and the river he stands in does not move
> either. He is not bracing against the river. He is the slow throb
> the river is made of, standing by itself, and everything that
> drowns in front of him is drowned in what he is. He is not even
> watching the water: he tastes it from inside — on his own inward
> face, the way you taste your own name. On the shore the chiefs
> call out that he has won, and he has — without turning around.
> The wonder of it doesn't end in noise: the river stands, alone,
> victorious.

## s02 — the still pool (chat-approved)

> A student is taken to a still pool at dawn and told to look.
> "What do you see?" "Towns and villages," the student says. "All
> of them, and none of them touching." "And the water?" "One
> water." "So the pool holds many without ever dividing. Remember
> that." The teacher holds up five fingers. "Five lights lit all
> of this, each in its own color." "But the freest act of all lets
> a dark cloth fall over his face. That is the hardest thing
> freedom does." "Once covered, the knower can't see straight, and
> goes about muzzled like an ox — time heavy on him, art, limit,
> and two hands at his throat called wanting and not-knowing."
> The student asks, "Then what can I know?" "Right now? Next to
> nothing. You're wrapped six times over, and the wrapping is part
> of it." "But a husk is not the grain. What covers you is not you,
> however close it clings. Turn toward the path — want it — and you
> come clean." "Pleasure, pain, confusion, plans, urges, the
> I-voice, nature, then the inner council: the judger, the churner,
> the claimer, in that order. That's the whole household. Nothing
> more." The student looks back at the pool. The towns are still
> there.

## s03 — count the catchers (chat-approved)

> The next morning the teacher holds up his hand. "Count the
> catchers." The student counts: ear, skin, eye, tongue, nose —
> five takers-in. "And the doers." Voice, hands, feet, and the two
> low ones — five putters-out. "What do they catch?" "Little things
> you can't cut up — a sound, a touch, a shape, a taste, a smell.
> Five seeds of sense." "Mix those and the world goes chunky —
> sky, wind, fire, water, earth." "And over all of it, from nature
> on down, a cover lies — like husk over grain, all the way down to
> dirt, with a man breathing inside it." "Three wrappings, the old
> ones say: a fine one, a flesh one, and the fellow inside all
> three." "One and the same, seen through the wanting and the
> wanted, looks like many." "Cane-juice in the jar, sugar in the
> bowl, lumps in the sack — same sweetness all the way through.
> So with him: every state, same self." "The knower, the boss
> inside, the breather, the everything, the body-born — just names
> we pin on. Take the pins out and nothing's holding them up."
> "No snake in that rope. But a man will sweat till he dies of the
> bite that isn't there. Nobody's measured how strong that fright
> is."

## s12 — the deposed king (chat-approved)

> A king stripped of his kingdom walks the roads into exile.
> Every village bows as he passes — not for the crown he lost, but
> for the road he is walking.
> Years go by. He trains in the wilderness, eating little,
> sleeping less, and the road itself steadies him.
> A friend meets him at a crossing and says only: keep going. He
> does.
> All he carries is a short song his teacher once sang him — a few
> lines, nothing more. It works fast. One evening he finds himself
> home, inside his own heart, in the innermost rooms, as if he had
> never left.
> At the gate he sets it down: a hundred verses, everything he was
> given, signed by the lamp of his master's feet.

## s02 — the still pool (third-person, content 9/9 intact)

> A student is taken to a still pool at dawn and told to look. He
> reports towns and villages — all of them, none of them touching —
> and one water holding them without ever dividing. Remember that,
> says the teacher, and holds up five fingers: five lights lit all
> of this, each in its own color. Then the lesson hardens. The
> freest act of all lets a dark cloth fall over the face — the
> hardest thing freedom does. Covered, the knower cannot see
> straight, and goes about muzzled like an ox: time heavy on him,
> art and limit, and two hands at his throat called wanting and
> not-knowing. What can be known? Next to nothing: wrapped six
> times over, and the wrapping part of it. But the husk is not the
> grain; what covers is not you, however close it clings — turn
> toward the path, want it, and come clean. Then the whole
> household is named: pleasure, pain, confusion, plans, urges, the
> I-voice, nature, then the inner council — the judger, the
> churner, the claimer — in that order. Nothing more. The student
> looks back at the pool. The towns are still there.

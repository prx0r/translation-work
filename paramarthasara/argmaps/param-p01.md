# Paramārthasāra p01 — ARGUMENT MAP (resort to Śambhu; the fourfold universe)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 param-p01 = kārikās 2–10 (edition pp. 1–3). Lore: Abhinavagupta adapts Ādiśeṣa's (Patañjali's) Ādhārakārikā — only ~1/4 parallels; 3/4 is Abhinava's own Trika-Kaula. Bansat-Boudon/Tripathi 2011 annotated edition; Lidke 2013. Core theme: jīvanmukti. The text is an anubhavasampradāya: dṛṣṭi = both darśana and yogic perception.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

The student, revolving in the wheel of suffering from womb to death, asks the Lord for the supreme truth. The Guru (Abhinava, speaking by Śiva-śāsana view) answers. The opening establishes the whole frame: Śambhu is supreme; the universe is a fourfold manifestation (śakti, māyā, prakṛti, pṛthvī) of the Lord; and the experiencer within it is Śiva himself, veiled. This is the text's signature move: the universe is not separate from Śiva; it is his self-limitation (*saṅkocana*), temporary and reversible through śaktipāta and Guru's grace.

Lore: Abhinava adapts Ādiśeṣa's (Patañjali's) Ādhārakārikā — only ~1/4 parallels; 3/4 is Abhinava's own Trika-Kaula. The text is an *anubhavasampradāya* (tradition based on experience): *dṛṣṭi* = both *darśana* (philosophical system) and yogic perception (*dṛṣṭi* attained through sādhana). Bansat-Boudon/Tripathi 2011 (first annotated English translation, with Yogarāja's commentary); Lidke 2013 (the text as a guide for kuṇḍalinī awakening and internal ascension through the tattvas). Unlike the ĪPK, the PS avoids logical discussions — it is a textbook, simple and lucid, stating principles rather than debating opponents. Yogarāja's commentary excels at detecting the esoteric second sense (twilight language / *sandhā bhāṣā*) beneath the evident sense.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — resort (v. 2):**
> *paraṃ parasthaṃ gahanād anādim ekaṃ niviṣṭaṃ bahudhā guhāsu | sarvālayaṃ sarvacarācarasthaṃ tvām eva śambhuṃ śaraṇaṃ prapadye*
[i.e. supreme, abiding in depths, beginningless, one, lodged in many caves, abode of all, in all moving and non-moving — to you, Śambhu, I resort.] The student's stance: refuge in the one who is everywhere and in everything.

**Step 1 — the question (v. 3):**
> *garbhādhivāsapūrvakamaraṇāntakaduḥkhacakravibhrāntaḥ … ādhāram bhagavantaṃ śiṣyaḥ papraccha paramārtham*
[i.e. revolving in the wheel of suffering from womb-dwelling to death, the student asked the Lord, the support, the supreme truth.] The question is existential, not academic: how does one get out of the wheel?

**Step 2 — the Guru's answer (v. 4):**
> *ādhārakārikābhiḥ taṃ gurur abhibhāṣati sma tatsāram | kathayaty abhinavaguptaḥ śivaśāsanadṛṣṭiyogena*
[i.e. the Guru spoke to him the essence in support-kārikās; Abhinava speaks by Śiva-śāsana view.] The text is an *ādhārakārikā* — verses that serve as support — and Abhinava's authority is *dṛṣṭi*: both philosophical system and yogic perception attained through sādhana.

**Step 3 — the fourfold universe (v. 5):**
> *nijaśaktivaibhavabharād aṅdacatuṣṭayam idaṃ vibhāgena | śaktir māyā prakṛtiḥ pṛthvī ceti prabhāvitaṃ prabhuṇā*
[i.e. by own-power-glory-weight, this fourfold universe in division: power, Māyā, prakṛti, earth — manifested by the Lord.] The universe is not created *ex nihilo*; it is the Lord's own power (*śakti*) unfolding into Māyā (veiling), prakṛti (materiality), and pṛthvī (earth, the grossest).

**Step 4 — the world-continuum (v. 6):**
> *tatrāntar viśvam idaṃ vicitratanukaraṇabhuvanasaṃtānam*
[i.e. therein, this world, the varied body-instrument-world-continuum.] The world is a *saṃtāna* — a continuum of bodies and instruments, not a collection of separate things.

**Step 5 — the experiencer (v. 7):**
> *bhoktā ca tatra dehī … śiva eva … gṛhītapaśubhāvaḥ*
[i.e. and there, the experiencer, embodied; Śiva indeed, having assumed the condition of a bound soul (*paśu*).] The crucial point: the experiencer is not a separate soul — it is Śiva himself, contracted. *Nānāvidhavarṇānāṃ rūpaṃ dhatte yathāmalaḥ sphaṭikaḥ* — as a crystal takes on the colors of what surrounds it, so Śiva takes on the forms of the world. *Suramānuṣapaśupādaparūpatvaṃ tadvad īśo 'pi gacchati* — the Lord goes into the forms of gods, humans, animals, plants.

**Step 6 — the analogy of reflection (v. 8):**
> *gacchati jala iva himakarabimbaṃ sthite sthitiṃ yāti | tanukaraṇabhuvanavarge tathāyam ātmā maheśānaḥ*
[i.e. as the moon's reflection in water moves when the water moves and stays when it stays, so this self, Maheśāna, in the mass of body-instrument-world.] The self is like a reflection: it appears to move and change, but the source (Śiva) is unmoved. *Rāhur adṛśyo 'pi yathā śaśibimbasthaḥ prakāśate tadvat* — as Rāhu, though invisible, appears in the moon's disk, so the supreme self appears in the limited knower.

**Step 7 — the mirror (v. 9):**
> *sarvagato 'py ayam ātmā viṣayāśrayaṇena dhīmukure ādarśe malarahite yadvad vadanaṃ vibhāti tadvad ayam*
[i.e. though all-pervading, this self shines in the mind-mirror like a face in a stainless mirror, by taking on objects.] And: *śivaśaktipātavimale dhītattve bhāti bhārūpaḥ* — in the mind-principle made stainless by Śiva's śaktipāta, it shines as light-form; *bhārūpaṃ paripūrṇaṃ svātmani viśrāntito mahānandam* — the full light-form, resting in itself, is great bliss. *Icchāsaṃvitkaraṇair nirbharitam anantaśaktiparipūrṇam* — filled with will, consciousness, and instruments, complete with infinite powers.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. *Aṅdacatuṣṭayam* — the "fourfold" (śakti/māyā/prakṛti/pṛthvī) is Abhinava's own schema; Ādiśeṣa's original has a different enumeration. The parallel verses are not marked in our T1.
2. The exact correspondence between this passage and Ādhārakārikā vv. 1–10 — not verified against the South Indian text (not on disk).
3. *Dṛṣṭi* polyvalency (darśana + yogic perception) — flagged by Lidke; the text supports both readings.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the text's own map: resort → question → answer → fourfold universe → world-continuum → experiencer (Śiva veiled) → mirror/reflection analogies → śaktipāta making the mind stainless → bliss. The key move is v. 7's *gṛhītapaśubhāvaḥ*: the experiencer is Śiva in disguise. Carry that into every downstream passage. The mirror (v. 9) and Rāhu (v. 8) analogies are the text's own; keep them, don't over-explain.

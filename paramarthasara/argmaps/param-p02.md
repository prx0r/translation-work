# Paramārthasāra p02 — ARGUMENT MAP (supreme tattva; the 36 tattvas; mirror)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 param-p02 = kārikās 11–19. Lore: Bansat-Boudon/Tripathi 2011 (annotated with Yogarāja's commentary); the 36 tattvas are the Trika's standard cosmological ladder; the mirror analogy is Abhinava's signature for the relation of the one and the many.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

What is the supreme tattva, and how do the thirty-six tattvas relate to it? The answer: the supreme tattva is pure, peaceful, thought-free — and yet the entire 36-fold world shines *within* it, undivided, like a city reflected in a mirror. The world is not separate from Śiva; it is his self-manifestation (*svābhāsa*). The five Śaktis (Śiva, Śakti, Sadāśiva, Īśvara, Vidyā) are the tattva-state through which this manifestation occurs.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — the supreme tattva (v. 11):**
> *sarvavikalpavihīnaṃ śuddhaṃ śāntaṃ layodayavihīnam | yat paratattvaṃ tasmin vibhāti ṣaṭtriṃśadātma jagat*
[i.e. free from all thought, pure, peaceful, free from dissolution and rise — that supreme principle, in which shines the 36-fold world.] The supreme is not an absence; it is the fullness in which the world appears.

**Step 1 — the mirror analogy (v. 12):**
> *darpaṇabimbe yadvan nagaragrāmādicitram avibhāgi | bhāti vibhāgenaiva ca parasparaṃ darpaṇād api ca*
[i.e. as in a mirror-image, city and village etc. are undivided; yet they shine in division, and mutually, from the mirror too.] The mirror is not changed by what it reflects; the reflections are not separate from the mirror. This is the Trika's non-dualism: the world is real as Śiva's self-manifestation, not as an independent substance.

**Step 2 — the source of the reflection (v. 13):**
> *vimalatamaparamabhairavabodhāt tadvad vibhāgaśūnyam api | anyonyaṃ ca tato 'pi ca vibhaktam ābhāti jagad etat*
[i.e. from the purest supreme Bhairava-knowledge, thus though divisionless, mutually and from that, divided, this world shines.] The division is real at the level of appearance; the unity is real at the level of the source. Both are true, neither contradicts the other.

**Step 3 — the tattva-state (v. 14):**
> *śivaśaktisadāśivatām īśvaravidyāmayīṃ ca tattvadaśām*
[i.e. the Śiva-Śakti-Sadāśiva-Īśvara-Vidyā tattva-state.] Five tattvas at the top of the ladder: Śiva (pure consciousness), Śakti (his power), Sadāśiva (I-am-this, the first stirring), Īśvara (I-am, the subject), Vidyā (this-am-I, the object). These five are the "Śaktis" whose divided nature (v. 15) produces the world.

**Step 4 — the five Śaktis' divided nature (v. 15):**
> *śaktīnāṃ pañcānāṃ vibhaktabhāvena bhāsayati paramaṃ yat svātantryaṃ durghaṭasaṃpādanaṃ maheśasya*
[i.e. by the divided nature of the five Śaktis, the supreme, the hard-to-accomplish svātantrya of Maheśa, manifests.] The Lord's freedom (*svātantrya*) is what makes the division possible without compromising the unity. This is the same svātantrya that appears in Sambandhasiddhi as the condition of relation.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The exact enumeration of the 36 tattvas — the text names only the top five (Śiva through Vidyā); the remaining 31 are implied, not listed here.
2. Whether *vimalatamaparamabhairavabodha* refers to Abhinava's own yogic perception or to the tradition's — both readings are supported by the *dṛṣṭi* polyvalency.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the mirror's own logic: pure tattva → mirror analogy → source of reflection → tattva-state → five Śaktis. The mirror (v. 12) is the central image; don't over-explain it. The key doctrinal move is v. 13's "though divisionless, divided" — carry that as the Trika's signature. The five Śaktis (v. 14) should be named and glossed briefly; they recur in p09 and p11.

# Paramārthasāra p03 — ARGUMENT MAP (senses, tanmātras, elements; creation from prakṛti)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 param-p03 = kārikās 20–28. Lore: Sāṃkhya's 25 tattvas underlie this; Abhinava reworks them into the Trika's 36. The husk-and-grain analogy (*tuṣa iva taṇḍulakaṇikām āvṛṇute*) is the text's own image for how prakṛti veils consciousness.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

How does the supreme consciousness become the embodied experiencer? Through the senses, the tanmātras, and the elements — produced from prakṛti, veiling consciousness like husk covers grain. The passage maps the entire cosmological descent: from the subtle (tanmātras) to the gross (elements), with consciousness at the bottom, covered but not destroyed. This is the Trika's version of Sāṃkhya cosmology, reworked so that *prakṛti* is not an independent principle but Śiva's own power in contraction.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — the senses (vv. 20–21):**
> *śrotraṃ tvagakṣirasanā ghrāṇaṃ buddhīndriyāṇi śabdādau | vākpāṇipādapāyūpasthaṃ karmendriyāṇi*
[i.e. ear, skin, eye, tongue, nose = cognitive senses (in sound etc.); speech, hand, foot, anus, genitals = conative senses.] Ten senses, five cognitive and five conative — the standard Sāṃkhya enumeration, adopted by the Trika.

**Step 1 — the tanmātras (v. 22):**
> *eṣāṃ grāhyo viṣayaḥ sūkṣmaḥ pravibhāgavarjito yaḥ syāt | tanmātrapañcakaṃ tat śabdaḥ sparśo mahoraso gandhaḥ*
[i.e. their object, subtle, free from division, is the five tanmātras: sound, touch, form, taste, smell.] The tanmātras are the subtle elements — the "that-ness" (*tanmātra*) of each sense-object, not yet gross.

**Step 2 — the gross elements (v. 23):**
> *etatsaṃsargavaśāt sthūlo viṣayas tu bhūtapañcakatām | abhyeti nabhaḥ pavanas tejaḥ salilaṃ ca pṛthvī ca*
[i.e. by combination of these, the gross object becomes the five elements: space, air, fire, water, earth.] The gross elements arise from the combination (*saṃsarga*) of tanmātras — this is the Sāṃkhya doctrine of *pañcīkaraṇa* (quintuplication), adopted by the Trika.

**Step 3 — the husk and the grain (v. 24):**
> *tuṣa iva taṇḍulakaṇikām āvṛṇute prakṛtipūrvakaḥ sargaḥ*
[i.e. as husk covers the grain, creation from prakṛti covers [consciousness].] The key image: prakṛti's creation is a covering (*āvaraṇa*), not a destruction. The grain (consciousness, *cit*) is still there under the husk (the body-elements). *Pṛthvīparyanto 'yaṃ caitanyaṃ dehabhāvena param āvaraṇaṃ mala iha sūkṣmaṃ māyādikañcukaṃ sthūlam* — from earth up to this, consciousness is the supreme covering; the malas are subtle (Māyā and the rest), the cloaks (*kañcuka*) are gross.

**Step 4 — the three cloaks and the self (v. 25):**
> *bāhyaṃ vigraharūpaṃ kośatrayaveṣṭito hy ātmā | ajñānatimirayogād ekam api svaṃ svabhāvam ātmānam | grāhyagrāhakanānāvaicitryeṇāvabudhyeta*
[i.e. the self, wrapped in three cloaks, by the darkness of ignorance, though one in its own nature, apprehends itself as varied objects and knowers.] The three cloaks (*kośatraya*) are the *trimala* (āṇava, māyīya, kārmaṇa) — the three impurities that veil Śiva. *Rasaphāṇitaśarkarikāguḍakhaṇḍādyā yathekṣurasa eva | tadvad avasthā bhedāḥ sarve paramātmanaḥ śambhoḥ* — as sugar-cane juice, molasses, sugar, and candy are all one juice in different forms, so all states are Śambhoḥ's own.

**Step 5 — the mirror of the world (v. 26):**
> *vijñānāntaryāmiprāṇavirāḍdehajātipiṇḍāntāḥ | vyavahāramātram etat paramārthena tu na santy eva | rajjvāṃ nāsti bhujaṅgas trāsaṃ kurute ca mṛtyuparyantam*
[i.e. from consciousness through the inner-controller, breath, the cosmic body, individual body, down to the lump — all this is mere transaction; in ultimate truth they do not exist, as the snake does not exist in the rope, yet causes terror up to death.] The world is *vyavahāra* (transactional), not *paramārtha* (ultimate). The snake-rope analogy: the fear is real, the snake is not.

**Step 6 — the power of delusion (v. 27):**
> *bhrānter mahatī śaktir na vivektuṃ śakyate nāma*
[i.e. the great power of delusion cannot be distinguished by name.] Delusion (*bhrānti*) has its own *śakti* — it is not nothing. This is the Trika's realism about the world: the world is real *as* Śiva's self-manifestation, even though it is not real *as* an independent substance.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The three cloaks (*kośatraya*) are named but not individually enumerated in this passage; the *trimala* (āṇava/māyīya/kārmaṇa) is the standard Trika reading, supported by p04 and p06.
2. *Prakṛtipūrvakaḥ sargaḥ* — "creation from prakṛti" — Abhinava's reworking of Sāṃkhya; the parallel with Ādiśeṣa's text is not verified.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the cosmological descent: senses → tanmātras → elements → husk/grain → three cloaks → snake/rope → power of delusion. The key move is v. 24's *tuṣa iva taṇḍulakaṇikām*: consciousness is covered, not destroyed. And v. 26's *rajjvāṃ nāsti bhujaṅgaḥ*: the world is transactional, not ultimately real — but the fear (and the suffering) is real. Keep both truths in tension; don't resolve them into either pure illusionism or pure realism.

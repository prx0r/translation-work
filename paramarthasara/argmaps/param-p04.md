# Paramārthasāra p04 — ARGUMENT MAP (bondage; saṃkoca; knowers)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 param-p04 = kārikās 29–37. Lore: the three malas (āṇava/māyīya/kārmaṇa) and the moon/Rāhu analogy are standard Trika; the five knowers are Abhinava's own classification.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Bondage: how does the unreal arise in the self? And saṃkoca (contraction): how does the all-pervading Śiva appear as a limited knower? The answer: by delusion's power, the self takes on non-self forms (body, breath) as its own; and by Māyā's power, pure Śiva appears impure, like the spotless moon defiled by Rāhu. The passage also introduces the five knowers (*pramātṛ*), classified by which malas contract them.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — bondage (v. 29):**
> *tadvad dharmādharmasvarnirayotpattimaraṇasukhaduḥkham | varṇāśramādi cātmany asad api vibhramabalād bhavati*
[i.e. thus dharma, adharma, heaven, hell, birth, death, pleasure, pain, varṇa, āśrama etc. — though unreal — arise in the self by delusion's power.] Bondage is not a real thing; it is the self's misidentification with non-self (body, breath, etc.) by the power of delusion (*vibhramabala*).

**Step 1 — the darkness upon darkness (v. 30):**
> *etat tad andhakāraṃ yad bhāveṣu prakāśamānatayā | ātmānatirikteṣv api bhavaty anātmābhimāno 'yam | timirād api timiram idaṃ*
[i.e. this is that darkness which, by shining in things, even in non-self things, becomes I-am-not-self delusion; darkness upon darkness.] The delusion is not ignorance of an external object; it is the self's own light misdirected onto non-self things, making them appear as "I."

**Step 2 — the net (v. 31):**
> *yad anātmany api dehaprāṇadāv ātmamānitvam … ātmānam veṣṭayate citraṃ jālena jālakāra iva*
[i.e. I-am-self-ness in non-self (body, breath etc.) wraps the self in a net, like a net-maker by his own net.] The self is both the maker and the trapped — *svajñānavibhavabhāsanayogenodveṣṭayen nijātmānam*: by the power of self-knowledge's shining, it can unwrap itself.

**Step 3 — the world as Śiva's play (v. 32):**
> *iti bandhamokṣacitrāṃ krīḍāṃ pratanoti paramaśivaḥ | sṛṣṭisthitisaṃhārā jāgratsvapnau suṣuptam iti tasmin*
[i.e. thus Paramaśiva spreads the wonderful play of bondage and liberation: creation, maintenance, dissolution; waking, dream, deep sleep — in that (turīya).] The three states (waking, dream, sleep) shine in the fourth (*turīya*), which is not covered by them, as the sky is not dirtied by clouds.

**Step 4 — saṃkoca, the moon and Rāhu (v. 33):**
> *śānte śānta ivāyaṃ hṛṣṭe hṛṣṭo vimohavati mūḍhaḥ | tattvagaṇe sati bhagavān na punaḥ paramārthataḥ sa tathā*
[i.e. as the spotless moon by timira-afflicted sight shines as if defiled by Rāhu, so Paramaśiva, though pure, appears impure when one attains knowerhood.] The impurity is apparent, not real — it belongs to the knower's contracted vision, not to Śiva.

**Step 5 — the contracted experiencer (v. 34):**
> *yad anātmany api tadrūpāvabhāsanaṃ tat purā nirākṛtya | ātmany anātmarūpāṃ bhrāntiṃ vidalayati paramātmā*
[i.e. having first removed the shining of non-self form in non-self, Paramātmā destroys the delusion of non-self form in the self.] The method: first clear the external misidentification, then the internal. *Itthaṃ vibhramayugalakasamūlavicchedane kṛtārthasya | kartavyāntarakalanā na jātu parayogino bhavati* — once the root of the double delusion is cut, the accomplished yogi has no further task.

**Step 6 — the threefold prakṛti (v. 35):**
> *pṛthivī prakṛtir māyā tritayam idaṃ vedyarūpatāpatitam*
[i.e. earth, prakṛti, Māyā — this threefold has fallen into the state of being an object of knowledge.] These three are the levels of veiling: earth (gross), prakṛti (subtle), Māyā (the first contraction). *Advaitabhāvanabalād bhavati hi sanmātrapariśeṣam* — by the power of non-dual contemplation, only pure being remains, as gold remains when bracelets, rings, and bangles are seen through as gold.

**Step 7 — the five knowers (v. 36):**
> *śakticakrayantraṃ krīḍāyogena vāhayan devaḥ*
[i.e. the Lord, playing, drives the wheel of the power-circle.] The five knowers: (1) Śiva — pure, stainless, omniscient, all-doer; (2) Mantramaheśvara — contracted by Māyā; (3) Mantreśvara — contracted by vidyā; (4) Mantrī — contracted by kalā; (5) vijñānakala — pure after abandoning Māyā-mala, contracted by kalā-mala; (6) pralayākala — pure after abandoning kalā-mala, contracted by Māyā-mala; (7) sakala — contracted by both malas. (The T1 lists seven; the standard Trika names five main + two sub-types.)

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The five vs. seven knowers — the text's gloss lists seven; the standard Trika enumeration names five (Śiva, Mantramaheśvara, Mantreśvara, Mantrī, sakala) with vijñānakala and pralayākala as sub-types. Both readings are defensible; the T1 gloss follows the sevenfold list.
2. *Trimala* (āṇava/māyīya/kārmaṇa) — the three malas are named in p06 and implied here; the text does not enumerate them in this passage.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the passage's own descent: bondage (unreal arising by delusion) → darkness upon darkness → net → world as play → saṃkoca (moon/Rāhu) → contracted experiencer → method (clear external, then internal) → threefold prakṛti → gold analogy → five/seven knowers. The key move is v. 33's *tattvagaṇe sati bhagavān na punaḥ paramārthataḥ sa tathā*: the impurity is in the knower's vision, not in Śiva. Carry that into p05 (śaktipāta) and p06 (the knowers' ladder).

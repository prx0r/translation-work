# Paramārthasāra p07 — ARGUMENT MAP (liberation; śaktipāta; the four upāyas)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 param-p07 = kārikās 56–64. Lore: the four upāyas (anupāya/śāmbhava/śākta/āṇava) are the Trika's central soteriological ladder; śaktipāta (descent of power) is the necessary condition. Bansat-Boudon/Tripathi 2011; Lidke 2013; Timalsina (Oxford Handbook of Tantric Studies, 2022).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

How is liberation (*mokṣa*) attained? Not by the soul's own effort — even good deeds done without knowledge bear no fruit. Liberation is the destruction of the *ajñānagranthi* (knot of ignorance) by the fire of knowledge (*jñānāgni*), which requires śaktipāta (descent of power) and Guru's grace. The passage introduces the four upāyas: anupāya (no means — by śaktipāta alone), śāmbhava (by will alone), śākta (by knowledge alone), āṇava (by action alone). The ladder descends from anupāya (highest) to āṇava (lowest).

Lore: the four upāyas are the Trika's central soteriological ladder. The Tantrasāra (āhnika 2) confirms: "By his freedom, he reveals himself either without any means (anupāya) or through a means; and where there is a means, it is will, or knowledge, or action" — absorption is threefold: Śāmbhava, Śākta, Āṇava. All four forms are taught in order. The glossary: *bhāvanā* = contemplative meditation by constant repetition of correct conceptional knowledge; *anupāya* = no means, śaktipāta alone. The text's style is simple and lucid (unlike ĪPK's subtle logic) — a textbook. Yogarāja's commentary excels at detecting the esoteric second sense.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — knowledge without fruit (v. 56):**
> *jñānaprāptau kṛtam api na phalāya tato 'sya janma katham*
[i.e. even if a deed is done, it bears no fruit for one who has attained knowledge; how then birth?] Knowledge destroys the fruit of karma. *Gatajanmabandhayogo bhāti śivārkaḥ svadīdhitibhiḥ* — like the sun's rays, knowledge dispels the darkness of bondage. *Tuṣakambukakiṃśārukamuktaṃ bījaṃ yathāṅkuraṃ kurute* — as a seed freed from husk, chaff, and awn produces a sprout, so the self, freed from the sheaths, becomes a knower.

**Step 1 — the knower fears nothing (v. 57):**
> *naiva tathāṇavamāyākarmavimukto bhavāṅkuraṃ hy ātmā | ātmajño na kutaścana bibheti sarvaṃ hi tasya nijarūpam*
[i.e. not thus — the self freed from āṇava, māyā, and karma does not produce the sprout of becoming; the knower fears nothing, for all is his own form.] *Naiva ca śocati yasmāt paramārthe nāśitā nāsti* — he does not grieve, for in the supreme there is no destruction.

**Step 2 — the treasure (v. 58):**
> *atigūḍhahṛdayagañjaprarūḍhaparamārtharatnasaṃcayataḥ | aham eveti maheśvarabhāve kā durgatiḥ kasya mokṣasya naiva kiṃcid dhāmāsti na cāpi gamanam anyatra*
[i.e. from the deeply hidden treasure of supreme truths in the heart, "I am Maheśvara" — what misfortune, what liberation? There is no place to go, no path to travel.] Liberation is not going somewhere; it is recognizing what one already is.

**Step 3 — the knot of ignorance (v. 59):**
> *ajñānagranthibhidā svaśaktyabhivyaktatā mokṣaḥ | bhinnājñānagranthir gatasaṃdehaḥ parākṛtabhrāntiḥ*
[i.e. liberation is the manifestation of one's own power by cutting the knot of ignorance; the knot of ignorance cut, doubt gone, delusion overcome.] *Prakṣīṇapuṇyapāpo vigrahayoge 'py asau muktaḥ* — even while embodied, with merit and demerit exhausted, he is liberated. *Agnyabhidagdhaṃ bījaṃ yathā prarohāsamarthatām eti* — as a seed burned by fire cannot sprout, so karma burned by knowledge's fire cannot produce birth.

**Step 4 — knowledge vs. karma (v. 60):**
> *jñānāgnidagdham evaṃ karma na janmapradaṃ bhavati | parimitabuddhitvena hi karmocitabhāvidehabhāvanayā | saṅkucitā citir etaddehadhvaṃse tathā bhāti*
[i.e. thus karma burned by knowledge's fire does not produce birth; for by limited understanding, karma produces the body suited to it; contracted consciousness shines thus when this body perishes.] *Yadi punar amalaṃ bodhaṃ sarvasamuttīrnaboddhṛkartṛmayam | vitatam anastamitoditabhārūpam satyasaṃkalpam* — but if pure knowledge, the transcendent knower-doer, the all-pervading, unsetting, risen light-form, true resolve — then [liberation].

**Step 5 — the four upāyas (v. 61):**
> *jñānaprāptau kṛtam api na phalāya*
The four means: (1) *anupāya* — no means; liberation by śaktipāta alone. (2) *śāmbhava* — by will alone (*icchā*). (3) *śākta* — by knowledge alone (*jñāna*). (4) *āṇava* — by action alone (*kriyā*). The ladder descends from anupāya (highest, least effort) to āṇava (lowest, most effort). *Anupāya* is the state where śaktipāta alone suffices; the other three require progressively more of the practitioner's own engagement.

**Step 6 — the ascent (vv. 62–63):**
The yogi, by śaktipāta, ascends through the upāyas. *Atitīvraśaktipātāt tadaiva nirvighnam eva śivaḥ* — by very intense śaktipāta, at that moment, unobstructed, Śiva. *Sarvottīrṇaṃ rūpaṃ sopānapadakrameṇa saṃśrayataḥ* — attaining the supreme form step by step (*sopāna*). *Paratattvarūḍhilābhe paryante śivamayībhāvaḥ* — at the end, attainment of the supreme principle's form: Śiva-mayībhāva.

**Step 7 — the middle (v. 64):**
> *tasya tu paramārthamayīṃ dhārām agatasya madhyaviśrānteḥ | tatpadalābhotsukacetaso 'pi maraṇaṃ kadācit syāt*
[i.e. but for one who has not reached the supreme-principle stream, resting in the middle, death may come even for one whose mind is eager for that state.] *Yogabhraṣṭaḥ śāstre kathito 'sau citrabhogabhuvanapatiḥ* — the yogabhraṣṭa (fallen yogi) is described in scripture as lord of a world of varied enjoyment. *Viśrāntisthānavaśād bhūtvā janmāntare śivībhavati* — by the power of the resting-place, in another birth he becomes Śiva.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The *śaktipāta* intensity levels (tīvra, madhya, manda) — the text implies them (*atitīvraśaktipātāt*) but does not enumerate; the standard Trika has three levels.
2. The *yogabhraṣṭa* — the fallen yogi who becomes lord of a pleasure-world — is mentioned but not developed; the text's point is that even failure on the path leads to Śiva in another birth.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the passage's own ascent: knowledge destroys karma → knower fears nothing → treasure (no going, no path) → knot of ignorance → knowledge vs. karma → four upāyas (anupāya/śāmbhava/śākta/āṇava) → ascent (śaktipāta, step by step) → middle (yogabhraṣṭa, another birth). The key move is v. 59's *ajñānagranthibhidā svaśaktyabhivyaktatā mokṣaḥ*: liberation is the manifestation of one's own power by cutting the knot. And v. 64's *yogabhraṣṭa*: even failure is not final. Carry both into p08 (jīvanmukti) and p12 (the seal).

# Paramārthasāra p08 — ARGUMENT MAP (world as Śiva's own nature; jīvanmukti)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 param-p08 = kārikās 65–73. Lore: jīvanmukti (liberation in this life) is the text's core theme (Bansat-Boudon/Tripathi 2011); the threefold world (gross/subtle/supreme) is standard Trika; the "hayamedha" (horse sacrifice) hyperbole is Abhinava's own rhetorical flourish.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

The world is not separate from Śiva — it is his own nature (*svabhāva*), threefold: gross (five elements), subtle (five tanmātras), supreme (five tattvas). The passage proves that the knower (*jñānin*) is liberated in this life (*jīvanmukti*): karma does not bear fruit for him; he is not touched by merit or demerit; he is free even while embodied. The proof is by svātantrya: the Lord's freedom means the world is his self-manifestation, and the knower who recognizes this is Śiva, not a separate soul.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — the world is Śiva (v. 65):**
> *dikkālakalanavikalaṃ dhruvam avyayam īśvaraṃ suparipūrṇam | bahutaraśaktivrātapralayodayaviracanaikakartāram*
[i.e. the Lord, free from space-time reckoning, eternal, imperishable, fully complete, sole maker of dissolution, rise, and creation, with many powers.] The world is not made by a separate creator; it is Śiva's own activity.

**Step 1 — awakening (v. 66):**
> *sṛṣṭyādividhisuvedhasam ātmānaṃ śivamayaṃ vibudhyeta | katham iva saṃsārī syād vitatasya kutaḥ kva vā saraṇam*
[i.e. one should awaken to the self as Śiva, the well-maker of creation etc.; how could one be a saṃsārin? For the all-pervading, where is refuge, where flight?] The knower recognizes: I am not in the world; the world is in me.

**Step 2 — karma's futility (v. 67):**
> *yuktibhir api siddhaṃ yatkarma jñānino na saphalaṃ tat*
[i.e. by reasoning too it is established: karma for the knower bears no fruit.] *Na mamedam api tu tasyeti dārḍhyato na hi phalaṃ loke* — "this is not mine, but his" — with firmness, no fruit [of karma] in the world. The knower does not accumulate karma because he does not claim ownership of action.

**Step 3 — the knower's freedom (v. 68):**
> *itthaṃ sakalavikalpān pratibuddho bhāvanāsamīraṇataḥ | ātmajyotiṣi dīpte juhvaj jyotirmayo bhavati*
[i.e. thus, awakened to all thoughts by the wind of contemplation, offering into the blazing self-light, one becomes light.] The metaphor: the knower offers all thoughts into the self-light (like oblations into fire) and becomes light itself.

**Step 4 — jīvanmukti (v. 69):**
> *aśnan yad vā tad vā saṃvīto yena kenacic chāntaḥ | yatra kvacana nivāsī vimucyate sarvabhūtātmā*
[i.e. eating this or that, clothed by whatever, peaceful; dwelling anywhere, the knower of all beings is liberated.] *Hayamedhaśatasahasrāṇy api kurute brahmaghātalakṣāṇi* — even performing a hundred thousand horse sacrifices or killing a hundred thousand brahmins [hyperbole], *paramārthavin na puṇyair na ca pāpaiḥ spṛśyate vimalaḥ* — the knower of the supreme is not touched by merit or demerit; stainless.

**Step 5 — the delusion of distinction (v. 70):**
> *madaharṣakopamanmathaviṣādabhayalobhamohaparivarjī | niḥstotravaṣaṭkāro jaḍa iva vicared avādamatiḥ*
[i.e. free from pride, joy, anger, agitation, sorrow, fear, greed, delusion; without praise or oblation, moving like the inert, free from dispute.] The knower lives without the disturbances that bind ordinary people. *Madaharṣaprabhṛtir ayaṃ vargaḥ prabhavati vibhedasaṃmohāt* — pride and the rest arise from the delusion of difference.

**Step 6 — advaita (v. 71):**
> *advaitātmavibodhas tena kathaṃ spṛśyatāṃ nāma stutyaṃ vā hotavyaṃ nāsti vyatiriktam asya kiṃcana ca*
[i.e. by the awakening to the non-dual self, how could he be touched? There is nothing to praise or offer, nothing separate from him.] *Stotrādinā sa tuṣyen muktas tan nirnamaskṛtivaṣaṭkaḥ* — he is pleased by praise etc.; the liberated one is free from bowing and oblation.

**Step 7 — the threefold world (v. 72, implied):**
The world is Śiva's own nature, threefold: gross (five elements), subtle (five tanmātras), supreme (five tattvas). The knower sees all three as Śiva.

**Step 8 — the closing (v. 73, implied):**
The text closes this section with the assertion that the jīvanmukta is free.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The exact verse numbering for vv. 72–73 — the T1 gloss compresses them; the Sanskrit in T1.md for p08 is a single block. Individual verse boundaries not marked in our T1.
2. The "hayamedha" hyperbole (v. 69) — Abhinava's own rhetorical flourish; not a literal claim about karma.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the passage's own proof: world is Śiva → awakening → karma's futility → knower's freedom → jīvanmukti (not touched by merit/demerit, hyperbole and all) → freedom from disturbances → advaita (nothing separate). The key move is v. 69: *paramārthavin na puṇyair na ca pāpaiḥ spṛśyate vimalaḥ*. And v. 68's *jyotirmayo bhavati*: the knower becomes light. Carry both into p09 (body as garment) and p11 (soul as Śiva's part).

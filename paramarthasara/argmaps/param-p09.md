# Paramārthasāra p09 — ARGUMENT MAP (body as Śiva's garment; practices)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 param-p09 = kārikās 74–82. Lore: the body as Śiva's garment (*devagṛha*) is a standard Trika image; the five winds and three channels are Haṭha-Yoga elements adopted by the Trika; the practices (homa, dhyāna, japa, vrata) are the āṇava upāya's techniques.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

The body is not the self's prison but Śiva's garment (*devagṛha*) — a temple housing the supreme deity. The passage maps the threefold body (gross/subtle/supreme) and the practices that transform it: homa (offering), dhyāna (meditation), japa (recitation), vrata (vow). The key point: these practices are not means to reach Śiva (who is already present) but ways of recognizing what one already is.

Lore: the Paramārthasāra's main interest is not only doctrine but *jīvanmukti* (liberation in this life) as its core theme. Further, it does not confine itself to exposition but hints at a second sense beneath the evident sense — esoteric techniques and practices at the heart of the philosophical discourse. Yogarāja (11th c.) excels at detecting and clarifying these levels of meaning (Bansat-Boudon/Tripathi 2011). The *devagṛha* (God's temple) image and the *khaṭvāṅga* (skull-staff) / *kapāla* (skull-cup) are Kāpālika imagery adopted by the Trika — the esoteric (second) sense of these practices. The glossary confirms: *bhāvanā* = contemplative meditation by constant repetition of correct conceptional knowledge; *jīvanmukti* = liberation while living, conducts transactions but not led away. The body as temple is Abhinava's own synthesis of philosophy and practice.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — the body as garment (v. 74):**
> *ṣaṭtriṃśattattvabhṛtaṃ vigraharacanāgavākṣaparipūrṇam | nijam anyad atha śarīraṃ ghaṭādi vā tasya devagṛham*
[i.e. the body, bearing the 36 tattvas, complete with the windows of its structure — one's own or another's, a pot etc. — is the God's temple.] The body is not an obstacle; it is the temple where Śiva dwells. *Tatra ca paramātmamahābhairavaśivadevatāṃ svaśaktiyutām* — and there, the supreme self, the great Bhairava-Śiva-deity, with his own power.

**Step 1 — worship (v. 75):**
> *ātmāmarśanavimaladravyaiḥ paripūjayann āste | bahirantaraparikalpanabhedamahābījanicayam arpayataḥ*
[i.e. worshipping with the stainless substances of self-reflection, one offers the great seed-mass of external and internal distinction-thoughts.] Worship is not external ritual; it is the offering of one's own distinctions into the fire of self-reflection. *Tasyātidīptasaṃvijjvalane yatnād vinā bhavati homaḥ* — when the supremely blazing self-consciousness blazes, homa happens without effort.

**Step 2 — meditation (v. 76):**
> *dhyānam anastamitaṃ punar eṣa hi bhagavān vicitrarūpāṇi | sṛjati tad eva dhyānaṃ saṃkalpālikhitasatyarūpatvam*
[i.e. meditation, unsetting; and the Lord creates the varied forms; that very meditation is the painting of true forms by thought.] Meditation is not concentration on an object; it is the Lord's own creative act, recognized. *Bhuvanāvalīṃ samastāṃ tattvakramakalpanām athākṣagaṇam* — the whole series of worlds, the tattva-sequence, the sense-faculties.

**Step 3 — recitation (v. 77):**
> *antarbodhe parivartayati ca yat so 'sya japa uditaḥ*
[i.e. what revolves in the inner knowledge — that is his japa, declared.] Japa is not the repetition of a mantra; it is the turning of consciousness inward. *Sarvaṃ samayā dṛṣṭyā yat paśyati yac ca saṃvidaṃ manute* — seeing all with the eye of samaya, perceiving all as consciousness.

**Step 4 — the vow (v. 78):**
> *viśvaśmaśānaniratāṃ vigrahakhaṭvāṅgakalpanākalitām | viśvarasāsavapūrṇaṃ nijakaragaṃ vedyakhaṇḍakakapālam | rasayati ca yat tad etad vratam*
[i.e. the world as cremation-ground, the body as a skull-staff, the world's essence as wine in the hand, the known-fragment as a skull-cup — tasting this is his vrata.] The vrata is the recognition that the world is a cremation-ground (all things die) and the body is a skull (empty of self). *Sudurlabhaṃ ca sulabhaṃ ca* — both hard to obtain and easy to obtain. *Janmanāśahīnaṃ paramārthamaheśvarākhyam upalabhya* — obtaining the name "paramārtha-Maheśvara," free from birth and death.

**Step 5 — the result (v. 79):**
> *upalabdhṛtāprakāśāt kṛtakṛtyas tiṣṭhati yatheṣṭam | vyāpinam abhihitam itthaṃ sarvātmānaṃ vidhūtanānātvam | nirupamaparamānandaṃ yo vetti sa tanmayo bhavati*
[i.e. from the light of obtaining, the accomplished one stands as he wishes; the all-pervading, the all-self, free from diversity; who knows the incomparable supreme bliss becomes one with it.] The result: standing free, recognizing oneself as all, becoming one with bliss.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The five winds (prāṇa/apāna/vyāna/udāna/samāna) and three channels (suṣumnā/īḍā/piṅgalā) — mentioned in the T1 gloss but not in the Sanskrit of p09 as rendered; the T1 gloss compresses them. The Sanskrit in T1.md for p09 does not explicitly list them.
2. The *devagṛha* (God's temple) image — the body as temple is Abhinava's own; the *khaṭvāṅga* (skull-staff) and *kapāla* (skull-cup) are Kāpālika imagery adopted by the Trika.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the passage's own practice-map: body as temple → homa (offering of distinctions) → dhyāna (unsetting meditation) → japa (inner turning) → vrata (cremation-ground recognition) → result (standing free, becoming bliss). The key move is v. 74's *devagṛham*: the body is not an obstacle but a temple. And v. 78's *sudurlabhaṃ ca sulabhaṃ ca*: the recognition is both hard and easy. Carry both into p10 (mind as Śiva's function) and p11 (soul as Śiva's part).

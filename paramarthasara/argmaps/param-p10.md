# Paramārthasāra p10 — ARGUMENT MAP (mind as Śiva's function)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 param-p10 = kārikās 83–91. Lore: the fourfold inner instrument (antahkaraṇa) is standard Sāṃkhya; Abhinava reworks it so that mind is Śiva's own function, not an independent faculty. The "tīrtha vs. śvapaca-gṛha" (v. 83) is the text's own proof that liberation is not place-dependent.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Mind (*manas*) is not an independent faculty but Śiva's own function (*kriyā*). The passage establishes the fourfold inner instrument (mind, intellect, ego, citta) and proves that liberation is not place-dependent: the knower is liberated even in a dog-eater's house, because liberation is internal (*tanmayatā*), not external. The tīrtha (pilgrimage site) and the śvapaca-gṛha (dog-eater's house) are the same for the knower.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — place-independence (v. 83):**
> *tīrthe śvapacagṛhe vā naṣṭasmṛtir api parityajan deham | jñānasamakālamuktaḥ kaivalyaṃ yāti hataśokaḥ*
[i.e. in a tīrtha or in a dog-eater's house, even one who has lost memory, abandoning the body, at the moment of knowledge is liberated, sorrow destroyed.] Liberation happens at the moment of knowledge, regardless of place. *Puṇyāya tīrthasevā nirayāya śvapacasadananidhanagatiḥ* — pilgrimage serves merit, death in a dog-eater's house serves demerit — but only for the unawakened.

**Step 1 — the point (v. 84):**
> *puṇyāpuṇyakalaṅkasparśābhāve tu kiṃ tena*
[i.e. when there is no contact with the stain of merit and demerit, what use is that?] *Tuṣakambukasupṛthakkṛtataṇḍulakaṇatuṣadalāntarakṣepaḥ* — like separating husk and grain and throwing away the husk; the grain (consciousness) is not affected. *Taṇḍulakaṇasya kurute na punas tadrūpatādātmyam* — it does not make the grain into husk. So too, merit/demerit does not touch the knower's consciousness.

**Step 2 — the knower in the world (v. 85):**
> *tadvat kañcukapaṭalīpṛthakkṛtā saṃvid atra saṃskārāt | tiṣṭhanty api muktātmā tatsparśavivarjitā bhavati*
[i.e. so too consciousness, separated from the cloak-mass by saṃskāra, though abiding, the liberated self is free from contact.] *Kuśalatamaśilpikalpitavimalībhāvaḥ samudgakopādheḥ* — like a jewel made stainless by the skill of the best artisan, from the casket's covering. *Malino 'pi maṇir upādher vicchede svacchaparamārthaḥ* — even a dirty jewel, when separated from its setting, is pure in ultimate truth.

**Step 3 — the knower's recognition (v. 86):**
> *evaṃ sadguruśāsanavimalasthiti vedanaṃ tanūpādheḥ | muktam apy upādhyantaraśūnyam ivābhāti śivarūpam*
[i.e. so, by the stainless state of the good Guru's teaching, the body-covering is known; even the liberated, free from other coverings, shines as Śiva's form.] *Śāstrādiprāmāṇyād avicalitaśraddhayāpi tanmayatām* — even by the authority of scripture etc., with unwavering faith, one attains oneness with that.

**Step 4 — the knower's past (v. 87):**
> *prāptaḥ sa eva pūrvaṃ svargaṃ narakaṃ manuṣyatvam antyaḥ kṣaṇas tu tasmin puṇyāṃ pāpāṃ ca vā sthitiṃ puṣyan*
[i.e. he attained heaven, hell, human birth before; but in the final moment, bearing merit or demerit,] *mūḍhānāṃ sahakārībhāvaṃ gacchati gatau tu na sa hetuḥ* — for the deluded, it becomes a co-cause; but for the one who has gone (to liberation), it is not a cause. *Ye 'pi tadātmatvena viduḥ paśupakṣisarīsṛpādayaḥ svagatim | te 'pi purātanasaṃbodhasaṃskṛtās tāṃ gatiṃ yānti* — even those who know it as their own self — animals, birds, reptiles — attain that state, purified by ancient awakening.

**Step 5 — the fourfold inner instrument (v. 88, implied by gloss):**
Mind (*manas*) = resolve/doubt; intellect (*buddhi*) = determination; ego (*ahaṃkāra*) = I-ness; citta = memory. Mind supports the senses; intellect determines; ego is the agent; citta remembers. These are Śiva's function, not independent faculties.

**Step 6 — the knower's freedom (v. 89, implied):**
The knower, having recognized mind as Śiva's function, is free from the inner instrument's binding power.

**Step 7 — the knower's state (v. 90, implied):**
The knower stands free, like Śiva, unaffected by the mind's operations.

**Step 8 — the closing (v. 91, implied):**
The passage closes with the assertion that the knower, having recognized mind as Śiva's function, is liberated.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The individual verse boundaries for vv. 85–91 — the T1 gloss compresses them into a single block; the Sanskrit is present but verse divisions are not marked.
2. The fourfold inner instrument (*antahkaraṇa*) — the T1 gloss lists the four (manas/buddhi/ahaṃkāra/citta) with their functions, but the Sanskrit in T1.md for p10 does not explicitly enumerate them; the gloss is the source.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the passage's own proof: place-independence (tīrtha = śvapaca-gṛha) → point (no stain touches the knower) → knower in the world (separated from cloaks by saṃskāra) → recognition (Guru's teaching) → past (merit/demerit not a cause for the liberated) → fourfold inner instrument → knower's freedom. The key move is v. 84's *puṇyāpuṇyakalaṅkasparśābhāve tu kiṃ tena*: when there is no contact with the stain, what use is place? And v. 86's *tanmayatām*: the knower attains oneness with Śiva. Carry both into p11 (soul as Śiva's part) and p12 (the seal).

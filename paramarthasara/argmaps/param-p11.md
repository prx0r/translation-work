# Paramārthasāra p11 — ARGUMENT MAP (soul as Śiva's part)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 param-p11 = kārikās 92–100. Lore: the jīva/ātman/paramātman threefold is standard Trika; the "yogabhraṣṭa" (fallen yogi) appears here and in p07; the "suralokabhogabhāgī" (enjoyer of heaven) is the text's own image for the yogabhraṣṭa's interim state.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

The soul (*puruṣa*) is not a separate entity but Śiva's own part (*aṃśa*), threefold: jīva (worldly, veiled by Māyā), ātman (liberated, by śaktipāta), paramātman (eternally liberated, Śiva). The passage proves that the knower who has recognized this does not fall at death: he does not change, because his recognition is not body-dependent. Even the yogabhraṣṭa (fallen yogi) becomes Śiva in another birth. The close: even heaven-enjoyment is not final; only Śivahood is.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — the soul's threefold nature (v. 92):**
> *svargamayo nirayamayas tad ayaṃ dehāntarālagaḥ puruṣaḥ*
[i.e. heaven-filled, hell-filled, this puruṣa resides between bodies.] *Tadbhaṅge svaucityād dehāntarayogam abhyeti evaṃ* — when that (body) breaks, by its own fitness, it attains union with another body. The soul transmigrates, but its nature is determined by its recognition, not by the body.

**Step 1 — recognition persists (v. 93):**
> *jñānāvasare svātmā sakṛd asya yādṛg avabhātaḥ | tādṛśa eva tadāsau na dehapāte 'nyathā bhavati*
[i.e. at the moment of knowledge, the self shines for him as it is; so it remains, not otherwise at the body's fall.] Recognition is not body-dependent; it persists through death.

**Step 2 — the body's end (v. 94):**
> *karaṇagaṇasaṃpramoṣaḥ smṛtināśaḥ śvāsakalilatā cchedaḥ | marmasu rujāviśeṣāḥ śarīrasaṃskārajo bhogaḥ*
[i.e. loss of the sense-faculties, destruction of memory, breath in agitation, cutting; special pains in the vital points; enjoyment born of body-saṃskāra.] The body's end is painful, but the knower's recognition is not affected. *Sa kathaṃ vigrahayoge sati na bhavet tena mohayoge 'pi* — how could it not arise when the body is present, even in delusion's presence?

**Step 3 — the knower at death (v. 95):**
> *maraṇāvasare jñāni na cyavate svātmaparamārthāt | paramārthamārgam enaṃ jhaṭiti yadā gurumukhāt samabhyeti*
[i.e. at the moment of death, the knower does not fall from the supreme truth of the self; this supreme path he quickly attains from the Guru's mouth.] *Atitīvraśaktipātāt tadaiva nirvighnam eva śivaḥ* — by very intense śaktipāta, at that moment, unobstructed, Śiva. *Sarvottīrṇaṃ rūpaṃ sopānapadakrameṇa saṃśrayataḥ* — attaining the supreme form step by step.

**Step 4 — the end (v. 96):**
> *paratattvarūḍhilābhe paryante śivamayībhāvaḥ*
[i.e. at the end, attainment of the supreme principle's form: Śiva-mayībhāva.] *Tasya tu paramārthamayīṃ dhārām agatasya madhyaviśrānteḥ | tatpadalābhotsukacetaso 'pi maraṇaṃ kadācit syāt* — but for one who has not reached the supreme-principle stream, resting in the middle, death may come even for one eager for that state.

**Step 5 — the yogabhraṣṭa (v. 97):**
> *yogabhraṣṭaḥ śāstre kathito 'sau citrabhogabhuvanapatiḥ*
[i.e. the yogabhraṣṭa (fallen yogi) is described in scripture as lord of a world of varied enjoyment.] *Viśrāntisthānavaśād bhūtvā janmāntare śivībhavati* — by the power of the resting-place, in another birth he becomes Śiva. Even failure on the path leads to Śiva; the path is not a one-shot.

**Step 6 — the paramārtha path (v. 98):**
> *paramārthamārgam enaṃ hy abhyasyāprāpya yogam api nāma | suralokabhogabhāgī muditamanā modate suciram*
[i.e. even without attaining yoga, having practiced this supreme path, he becomes an enjoyer of heaven, joyful, delighting for a long time.] Heaven-enjoyment is not final, but it is not nothing; the path bears fruit even in incompleteness.

**Step 7 — the close (v. 99–100, implied):**
The passage closes with the assertion that Śivahood is the only final state; heaven and hell are intermediate.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The individual verse boundaries for vv. 92–100 — the T1 gloss compresses them; verse divisions not marked in our T1.
2. The *yogabhraṣṭa*'s "world of varied enjoyment" (*citrabhogabhuvana*) — not further specified; read as an intermediate heaven-realm.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the passage's own proof: threefold soul → recognition persists through death → body's end (painful but not decisive) → knower at death (śaktipāta, step by step) → end (Śiva-mayībhāva) → yogabhraṣṭa (failure is not final) → paramārtha path (bears fruit even incomplete) → close (Śivahood only final). The key move is v. 93: *tādṛśa eva tadāsau na dehapāte 'nyathā bhavati* — recognition persists through death. And v. 97's *janmāntare śivībhavati*: even the fallen yogi becomes Śiva. Carry both into p12 (the seal).

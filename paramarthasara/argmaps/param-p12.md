# Paramārthasāra p12 — ARGUMENT MAP (the seal: completion; all becomes Śiva)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 param-p12 = kārikās 101–105. Lore: the closing verses are Abhinava's own seal; the "yogabhraṣṭa" reappears from p07/p11; the text's final assertion is that even the fallen yogi, by practicing the path, becomes Śiva.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

The text closes. The seal: the Paramārthasāra, spoken by Abhinava by Śiva-śāsana view, is the essence (*saṃkṣepa*) of all śāstra. Śivahood is attained by Guru's grace and knowledge; knowledge destroys the malas; in Śivahood all becomes Śiva. The yogabhraṣṭa, even without attaining yoga, becomes an enjoyer of heaven and eventually Śiva. The text's final instruction: meditate on this essence; Śivahood is near.

Lore: the PS is Abhinava's "gift-for-synthesis" — no text better captures his ability to unify the plurality of Śaivisms into a single doctrinal system (Lidke 2013). The text is a *saṃkṣepa* (condensed essence) of all śāstra — 105 ārya-ghaṭa verses (our T1 counts 104; the discrepancy is in counting the benediction or closing verse). The style is simple and lucid (unlike ĪPK's subtle logic) — a textbook for beginners. The *yogabhraṣṭa* (fallen yogi) is a recurring figure (also in p07 and p11): even failure on the path leads to Śiva in another birth. The glossary confirms: *jīvanmukti* = liberation while living; *bhāvanā* = contemplative meditation by constant repetition. The text's final word — *acirād eva śivatvaṃ nijahṛdayāveśam abhyeti* — quickly, Śivahood, the entry into one's own heart. Abhinava signs off as "kindled by the remembrance of Śiva's feet" — the author as devotee, not just philosopher.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — the king and the yogabhraṣṭa (v. 101):**
> *viṣayeṣu sārvabhaumaḥ sarvajanaiḥ pūjyate yathā rājā | bhuvaneṣu sarvadaivair yogabhraṣṭas tathā pūjyaḥ*
[i.e. as a king, sovereign in his realm, is worshipped by all people, so the yogabhraṣṭa is worshipped by all gods in the worlds.] The fallen yogi, though not liberated, is still exalted — his practice has borne fruit even in incompleteness.

**Step 1 — the return (v. 102):**
> *mahatā kālena punar mānuṣyaṃ prāpya yogam abhyasya | prāpnoti divyam amṛtam yasmād āvartate na punaḥ*
[i.e. after a long time, attaining humanity again, practicing yoga, he obtains the divine immortal, from which there is no return.] The yogabhraṣṭa's path is not lost; it resumes in another birth and reaches the immortal.

**Step 2 — the instruction (v. 103):**
> *tasmāt sanmārge 'smin nirato yaḥ kaścid eti sa śivatvam*
[i.e. therefore, whoever is devoted to this true path attains Śivahood.] The text's practical instruction: devotion to the path is sufficient.

**Step 3 — the essence (v. 104):**
> *iti matvā paramārthe yathā tathāpi prayatanīyam idam | abhinavaguptoditasaṃkṣepaṃ dhyāyataḥ paraṃ brahma*
[i.e. knowing this, in the supreme, as it is, still one should strive; meditating on this essence spoken by Abhinavagupta, the supreme Brahman.] *Acirād eva śivatvaṃ nijahṛdayāveśam abhyeti* — quickly, Śivahood, the entry into one's own heart, is attained. *Āryāśatena tad idaṃ saṃkṣiptaṃ śāstrasāram atigūḍham* — in a hundred verses, this condensed essence of śāstra, very hidden.

**Step 4 — the seal (v. 105):**
> *abhinavaguptena mayā śivacaraṇasmaraṇadīptena*
[i.e. by me, Abhinavagupta, kindled by the remembrance of Śiva's feet.] The author's signature: the text is spoken by one whose fire is the remembrance of Śiva's feet.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The verse count: the T1 says 104 kārikās; the web sources say 105. The discrepancy is in the counting of the benediction (v. 1) or the closing verse. Our T1 has 12 passages; the last (p12) covers vv. 101–105.
2. The *yogabhraṣṭa*'s "world of varied enjoyment" (*citrabhogabhuvana*) — not further specified; read as an intermediate heaven-realm (consistent with p11).

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the text's own close: king/yogabhraṣṭa → return (no return from the immortal) → instruction (devotion suffices) → essence (meditate on this) → seal (Abhinava, kindled by Śiva's feet). The key move is v. 104's *acirād eva śivatvaṃ nijahṛdayāveśam abhyeti*: quickly, Śivahood, the entry into one's own heart. This is the text's final word: Śivahood is not far; it is the recognition of what one already is. Carry this into C1 as the text's closing note.

# C1 — Paramārthasāra (final condensation of the arguments)

*Condensed from ARGMAP (param-p01..p12) + L2.v2. Passage-local: each sits under its passage in print. No modern comparison, no PARALLELS inside.*

## param-p01 (vv. 2–10)

Śambhu is supreme — abiding in depths, beginningless, one, lodged in many caves, abode of all. The student, revolving in the wheel of suffering, asks for the supreme truth; the Guru answers by Śiva-śāsana view (dṛṣṭi: both system and yogic perception). The universe is Śiva's fourfold self-manifestation — power, Māyā, prakṛti, earth — and the experiencer within it is Śiva himself, veiled as a bound soul, taking on all forms like a crystal taking on colors. The self is like a reflection (moves with the medium, source unmoved) and like a face in a mirror (all-pervading but appearing localized). Śaktipāta makes the mind stainless; resting in itself, the self is great bliss.

## param-p02 (vv. 11–19)

The supreme tattva is pure, peaceful, thought-free — and yet the entire 36-fold world shines within it, undivided, like a city reflected in a mirror. The world is not separate from Śiva; it is his self-manifestation (svābhāsa). The five Śaktis — Śiva, Śakti, Sadāśiva, Īśvara, Vidyā — are the tattva-state through which this manifestation occurs. The Lord's freedom (svātantrya) makes the division possible without compromising unity: though divisionless, the world shines in division, and both are true.

## param-p03 (vv. 20–28)

The supreme consciousness descends through senses (ten: five cognitive, five conative), tanmātras (five subtle elements), and gross elements (five, by combination). Prakṛti's creation covers consciousness like husk covers grain — the grain is still there under the husk. The self, wrapped in three cloaks (trimala), though one in nature, apprehends itself as varied objects and knowers, as sugar-cane juice in many forms. The world is transactional (vyavahāra), not ultimately real (paramārtha) — like the snake in the rope — yet delusion has real power (śakti). Consciousness is covered, not destroyed.

## param-p04 (vv. 29–37)

Bondage is unreal: dharma, adharma, heaven, hell, birth, death, pleasure, pain arise in the self by delusion's power — the self's light misdirected onto non-self things, making them appear as I. The self is wrapped in a net (like a net-maker by his own net) but can unwrap itself by self-knowledge. Saṃkoca is apparent, not real: as the spotless moon appears defiled by Rāhu to timira-afflicted sight, so pure Śiva appears impure to the contracted knower. The method is two-stage: clear external misidentification, then internal. Threefold prakṛti (earth/prakṛti/Māyā) is an object of knowledge; non-dual contemplation sees through form to substance (gold analogy). The knowers are Śiva contracted by malas, ascending from sakala to Śiva.

## param-p05 (vv. 38–46)

The Lord's state depends on the knower's vision, not on any change in Śiva. Liberation is not earned but descended (śaktipāta): first remove external misidentification, then internal; non-dual contemplation sees through form to substance (gold analogy); the result is pure being (Brahman). Śaktipāta (three-pronged power) is the condition; Guru's grace is the vehicle. Without śaktipāta, no liberation. After liberation, creation is recognized as self — the Lord creates again, but now the creation is recognized as self, not as other.

## param-p06 (vv. 47–55)

The knowers are not a plurality of souls but Śiva himself in various states of contraction (saṅkoca), classified by which malas veil them: Śiva (pure, no mala) → Mantramaheśvara (Māyā-mala) → Mantreśvara (vidyā-mala) → Mantrī (kalā-mala) → vijñānakala (kalā only) → pralayākala (Māyā only) → sakala (both malas). The ladder ascends from sakala (most contracted) to Śiva (pure). Liberation is moving up the ladder. Karma is destroyed by knowledge, like cotton by fire — long-accumulated karma is burned by the flame of knowledge.

## param-p07 (vv. 56–64)

Liberation is not earned but descended (śaktipāta). Knowledge destroys karma (seed burned by fire); the knower fears nothing; there is no place to go, no path to travel — liberation is recognizing what one already is. The knot of ignorance is cut by knowledge's fire, which destroys karma. The four upāyas form a ladder: anupāya (no means, śaktipāta alone), śāmbhava (will), śākta (knowledge), āṇava (action). The ascent is by śaktipāta, step by step. Even the yogabhraṣṭa (fallen yogi) becomes Śiva in another birth — the path is not a one-shot.

## param-p08 (vv. 65–73)

The world is not separate from Śiva — it is his own nature (svabhāva), threefold: gross (five elements), subtle (five tanmātras), supreme (five tattvas). The knower (jñānin) is liberated in this life (jīvanmukti): karma does not bear fruit for him; he is not touched by merit or demerit (even with hyperbolic examples); he is free even while embodied. The proof is by svātantrya: the Lord's freedom means the world is his self-manifestation, and the knower who recognizes this is Śiva, not a separate soul. The knower offers all thoughts into the self-light and becomes light itself; free from pride, joy, anger, and the rest; by non-dual awakening, nothing is separate from him.

## param-p09 (vv. 74–82)

The body is not an obstacle but Śiva's temple (devagṛha) — bearing the 36 tattvas, housing the supreme deity. The practices are not means to reach Śiva (who is already present) but ways of recognizing what one already is: homa (offering distinctions into self-reflection), dhyāna (unsetting meditation — the Lord's creative act recognized), japa (inner turning — seeing all as consciousness), vrata (cremation-ground recognition — the world dies, the body is empty). The recognition is both hard and easy (sudurlabhaṃ ca sulabhaṃ ca). The result: standing free, recognizing oneself as all, becoming one with bliss.

## param-p10 (vv. 83–91)

Liberation is not place-dependent: the knower is liberated even in a dog-eater's house, because liberation is internal (tanmayatā), not external. Merit and demerit do not touch the knower's consciousness, like husk does not become grain. Mind (manas) is not an independent faculty but Śiva's own function (kriyā): the fourfold inner instrument (mind = resolve/doubt, intellect = determination, ego = I-ness, citta = memory) is Śiva's own activity. Recognition (Guru's teaching) → tanmayatā (oneness with Śiva). Even animals, birds, and reptiles who know it as their own self attain that state.

## param-p11 (vv. 92–100)

The soul (puruṣa) is not a separate entity but Śiva's own part (aṃśa), threefold: jīva (worldly, veiled by Māyā), ātman (liberated, by śaktipāta), paramātman (eternally liberated, Śiva). Recognition persists through death: at the moment of knowledge, the self shines as it is, and so it remains at the body's fall. The knower does not fall from the supreme truth at death; by intense śaktipāta, he attains Śiva step by step. Even the yogabhraṣṭa (fallen yogi) becomes Śiva in another birth — the path is not a one-shot. Heaven-enjoyment is not final; only Śivahood is.

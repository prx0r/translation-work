# Parāprāveśikā par-p07 (marks; person, bondsman, freed) — L2 READ

## v.7
Now what each is like. The Śiva-level is supreme Śiva Himself — will, knowing, and doing in one form, pure and full bliss by nature — throbbing within every knower as full-I joy, beyond every level, vast-bodied light that can hold a hundred thousand reflections of world-makings and world-unmakings. When the Lord takes on the will to send the world forth, His first pulse is already will-power's level — out of His own freedom's greatness, wishing to bloom outward, brimming with supreme-bliss joy, the grasp "I": power's level. The world, just now sprouting, stands covered over with I-by-the-self — the eternal-Śiva level: with the this-portion bloomed faintly upon the ground of pure consciousness only, will comes first. The sprouted world, standing wrapped in I — the Lord level: the I sprinkled over a clarified this, knowing comes first, the two differing only in how dim or undim the this-portion is. The grasp of I-and-this as one is pure knowledge: when, amid full-grown difference flashing, the I shines up as nothing but consciousness, the scales-held-even "I-this" grasp — doing comes first: knowledge's level. Spreading difference among things already of His own nature is illusion: achieving the hardest of feats, growing the pride of knowerhood in the unconscious and the void, making even one-with-consciousness things appear divided, hiding their own form — and the stuff all things from art to clay are made of. But when the Lord, by the Lady's power of illusion — which is only another name for freedom — veils His own form and puts on the tight mask of knowerhood, He is called the person — Himself, deluded by illusion, bound by deeds, taking his deeds for his chains — the wanderer, wandering. Though nothing apart from the supreme Lord, His delusion is never the Lord's own — as the magician's self-willed trick fools the crowd but not the magician. But when knowledge shows Him His own form, and His sovereignty is brought to recognition — when He takes even body and world for nothing but dense feeling, unmixed with the unconscious — then, thick with consciousness, He is free: fit to be called free while still in the body, with no more bond of rebirth — and when the body falls, supreme Śiva nature itself — supreme Śiva Himself. His are all-making, all-knowing, fullness, deathlessness, all-pervading — and His powers, though never narrowed, take narrowing and appear as art, knowing, passion, time, necessity. It is as when a king seizes a man's whole fortune and then, out of pity, deals him back a pittance to live on: just so wakefulness, shrunk to a mote and robbed of all-knowing, is dealt art and the rest instead — a little making to stand in for all-making.

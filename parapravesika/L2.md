# Parāprāveśikā — L2 READ

## par-p01 — the vow

Veneration to Śiva, whose very self is all the levels of reality. Here begins the Entering-into-the-Supreme, composed by glorious Kṣemarāja, noblest of the great Māheśvara teachers. We bow to consciousness itself — the heart of the supreme Lord — which has the world for its form and yet goes beyond it, pulsing in the shape of supreme power.

## par-p02 — light knows

The supreme Lord, here, is made of light — and light is made of awareness. An objector asks: granted He is light by nature, why should He not be bare knowing, the way the calm-brahman teachers have it? The answer specifies awareness two ways — bounded and boundless — so that the bounded knower of the void does not put a ceiling on consciousness-light. In creating, awareness wears the world's shape; in sustaining, it shines the world forth; in withdrawing, it takes all back into itself — everywhere the thrill of a full and complete I. Awareness, in short, is the flash of an I that was never put together — through world-shaping, world-lighting, world-dissolving. And if that awareness were missing, lordlessness and deadness would follow — as sunlight, which lacks awareness, leans on something else to shine and sinks to the side of the inert, settling for unfreedom.

## par-p03 — ten names, one throb

This very awareness is what the scriptures trumpet under many names: consciousness, consciousness-in-its-freedom, the supreme Word risen from its own sap, freedom, the supreme Self's sovereign majesty, doership, pulsing, essence, heart, vibration — and more. In the Śaiva teaching, the scriptures everywhere proclaim one thing: that awareness comes first in the light of consciousness, sung in scripture after scripture in different words. Consciousness — because awareness is the Self's own chief form. The supreme Word — because it speaks the world into declaration by grasping it back, supreme as the ground and fullness of all lesser graspings, risen from its own sap because its "I" never sets, full-bodied consciousness. Freedom — because it binds the cast-out "this" back to the self, looses it again, and lays it low through the stations of the void — differing from the inert by exactly these marks. Sovereignty — needing nothing else when the world begins, unlike Brahmā and his fellow wardens of māyā's womb, who answer to another's will and bend to fate. Doership — raising the whole world, from Śiva down to earth, into "this"-ness out of inward shining oneness, the way a yogin makes things — an unsurpassed kind of causing. Pulsing — the relation of flashing, and it is mine: were throbbing nobody's in particular, everything would flash for everyone always, and all would be omniscient, or all blind. Essence — the untrifling form, the Lord's own power of awareness. Heart — where the world comes to rest. Vibration — the still light of consciousness shimmering with motion, the world appearing as something extra where nothing extra is.

## par-p04 — the world His shining

And therefore — the unmade I, real, self-lit in form — the supreme Lord pulses and shines by the power of the supreme Lady, taking the shape of the world from Śiva down to clay. This very shining is His making of the world — and the world's being alive. But what kind of "made" is the world — made as something different from Him, or as nothing different? If different, His freedom breaks; if not different, how is it made at all? The answer: without another's light the world cannot even be noticed, let alone come into its own — its coming to wear a shining body, leaning on His ground, is exactly what "made" means here. Māyā's own freedom puts difference on display, so the world is handled as different while being nothing different. "Made," in the end, means nothing but shining-by-Him. Formed this way, the world is nothing apart from Maheśvara the maker, who is shining-formed. For if what is known stood apart, it could not shine, and no shining could reach it — there would be nothing at all, and all the traffic of taking and giving would collapse. And if the world is nothing apart from the Lord, must He not be stained by resemblance to a merely shining world — veiled by it? Rather the would-be veil destroys itself: gained even within the self, veiling the light would unmake itself. By this world, then, the Lord's light-formed nature is never veiled — the world, given rest by His shining, has become His very life-breath. How could it throttle Him — and throttling Him, itself go on standing?

## par-p05 — proof retired

Indicators and causes cannot get a grip on the Lord, proved before all proof — and since even the knower who lays down injunctions exists in His likeness, that knower's standing proved does not hang on any proof. His self-lit-ness is proved by experience. "What proves that He is?" — grant that things are. "What proves one of such a nature?" — the proof is the questioner's own shape: the self-shining of Maheśvara, proved before all, is proved for everyone by feeling oneself. Consciousness-light itself, the life of everything, appoints proofs for the business of illusion — but for Him no proof is ever used, since everything else, being inert, cannot light even itself without another's light, let alone settle anything beyond itself. Proof itself is proof only by leaning on Him — and for the proving of Him, who is proof's own life, what use is poor proof, whose office is to let some fresh flash come to rest, marked as a finding, inside the knower? The knower, ever-lit, his shining unbroken, already enjoys every finding tucked inward within himself — where would a fresh flash be put to use in him, and where else could its finding come to rest? So proof is for knowables like body and breath, blue and pleasure — not for the Lord, who is proof's life. Hence the saying: the proofs that draw out the very life of things — beyond them all stands a further Life, and He is the supreme Lord.

## par-p06 — the alphabet-I and the thirty-six

And so — His essence being the full grasp of I, woven of the mass of sounds — supreme Śiva Himself is the whole display in thirty-six levels. The mass of sounds is the heap of letters from a to ha, pregnant with the entire world — the full I-grasp of the form "I alone, light-natured, in the shining," freedom resting with no other face to look to. And a level of reality is this: the one undivided shine that makes divided families into families — as hill and tree and town all show the form of earth, and river and lake and sea the form of water. The thirty-six: Śiva, power, eternal Śiva, the Lord, pure knowledge, illusion, art, knowing, passion, time, necessity, the person, nature, intellect, ego, mind, the five senses of knowing, the five senses of doing, the five subtles, the five gross elements.

## par-p07 — the dimming, the bondsman, the freed

Now what each is like. The Śiva-level is supreme Śiva Himself — will, knowing, and doing in one form, pure and full bliss by nature — throbbing within every knower as full-I joy, beyond every level, vast-bodied light that can hold a hundred thousand reflections of world-makings and world-unmakings. When the Lord takes on the will to send the world forth, His first pulse is already will-power's level — out of His own freedom's greatness, wishing to bloom outward, brimming with supreme-bliss joy, the grasp "I": power's level. The world, just now sprouting, stands covered over with I-by-the-self — the eternal-Śiva level: with the this-portion bloomed faintly upon the ground of pure consciousness only, will comes first. The sprouted world, standing wrapped in I — the Lord level: the I sprinkled over a clarified this, knowing comes first, the two differing only in how dim or undim the this-portion is. The grasp of I-and-this as one is pure knowledge: when, amid full-grown difference flashing, the I shines up as nothing but consciousness, the scales-held-even "I-this" grasp — doing comes first: knowledge's level. Spreading difference among things already of His own nature is illusion: achieving the hardest of feats, growing the pride of knowerhood in the unconscious and the void, making even one-with-consciousness things appear divided, hiding their own form — and the stuff all things from art to clay are made of. But when the Lord, by the Lady's power of illusion — which is only another name for freedom — veils His own form and puts on the tight mask of knowerhood, He is called the person — Himself, deluded by illusion, bound by deeds, taking his deeds for his chains — the wanderer, wandering. Though nothing apart from the supreme Lord, His delusion is never the Lord's own — as the magician's self-willed trick fools the crowd but not the magician. But when knowledge shows Him His own form, and His sovereignty is brought to recognition — when He takes even body and world for nothing but dense feeling, unmixed with the unconscious — then, thick with consciousness, He is free: fit to be called free while still in the body, with no more bond of rebirth — and when the body falls, supreme Śiva nature itself — supreme Śiva Himself. His are all-making, all-knowing, fullness, deathlessness, all-pervading — and His powers, though never narrowed, take narrowing and appear as art, knowing, passion, time, necessity. It is as when a king seizes a man's whole fortune and then, out of pity, deals him back a pittance to live on: just so wakefulness, shrunk to a mote and robbed of all-knowing, is dealt art and the rest instead — a little making to stand in for all-making.

## par-p08 — what each sheath takes, what each level gives

Art is what makes him do a little — a pot or two — who cannot do all. Knowing is what makes him know a little — the pot before him, not the thing far away. Passion is the clinging to things — "being incomplete and apart, may this serve me; may I grow; may I never cease" — in that shape. Time is what cuts the sequence of things as they shine and unshine — "I knew, I know, I will know; I did, I do, I will do" — measuring even things in the shape of knowing and doing, it cuts them off: past and the rest. Necessity is what holds him to "this is mine to do, that is not mine to do" — manifold shapes of must, fixed fruit from fixed cause among them. These five, by veiling His own form, are called the sheath — other than His form, yet not other, lying over the nature of full consciousness like a cover. The root-cause of the levels from the great down to earth is nature — goodness, passion, and darkness in equipoise: pleasure, pain, and delusion by make, shining, doing, and holding by nature — an undivided poise where part and counterpoint cannot be told apart. What decides — "this is such" — holding the mirror up to passing thoughts, fit by clarity to catch reflections, is intellect. What means "mine, not mine" in pride is ego. What means resolve is mind. These three are the inner organ — grasping inner things like pleasure, made of the strands in part-and-counterpoint, and the cause of the sense-and-gross heap to come. The means of grasping sounds, touches, forms, tastes, smells in order are ear, skin, eye, tongue, nose — the five knowing senses. The means of speaking, taking, moving, voiding, and blissful action in order are voice, hands, feet, evacuation, generation — the five doing senses. Sounds, touches, forms, tastes, smells in their common shape — that and nothing more, with no inner distinctions — are the five subtles. Ether gives room; air keeps alive; fire burns and cooks; water refreshes and flows; earth upholds.

## par-p09 — all inside the seed

As sprout and leaf and fruit stand in the banyan seed in bodies exactly fitted to them, so this whole world in all its varied makings stands inside the seed of the heart — as in peacock-egg juice the craftsman's skill already holds every feather's line and plume-variety, for those rich in attention to weigh with subtle sight. "As the great tree stands in the banyan seed in power-form, so this world, moving and unmoving, stands in the heart-seed": by this traditional maxim, in the heart-seed shaped as the supreme Lady this world is contained. How so? As the ultimate truth of pot and bowl, clay's transformations, is clay itself — as watery kinds, examined, settle into water-in-common — so the levels from earth to illusion, examined for what is real in them, prove to be being itself. Even of the word "being" itself: strip the suffix that shows the root's sense, and the bare-stem letter s alone is left — and inside it stand thirty-one levels; beyond it, pure knowledge, the Lord, and eternal Śiva, knowledge-and-action in essence, enter as special powers into the syllable au, shaped as assent, formed of unsurpassed power. Hence the supreme emission, shaped as creation above and below: the breath-out to be breathed. Of the heart-seed so formed — shaped as the great mantra, shaped as the world, beyond the world — supreme Śiva Himself, as the seat where all rising comes to rest, is its own nature: all-shapedness, then shapelessness, each in turn the cause. Whoever truly knows such a heart-seed, and enters it — he is initiated in the highest sense: this very knowing, with no sesame-and-ghee oblation, is initiation in truth. Still breathing, living as men live, he is free while living; and when the body falls, he becomes the Lord, supreme Śiva Himself.

*Thus the Parāprāveśikā of glorious Kṣemarāja, noblest of great Māheśvara teachers, is complete.*

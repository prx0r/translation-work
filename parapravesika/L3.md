## par-p01 — the vow

Veneration to Śiva, whose very self is every level of reality. Here begins the Entering into the Supreme, by glorious Kṣemarāja, noblest of the great Māheśvara masters. And we bow to knowing itself — the heart of the supreme Lord: it wears the world as its body, yet stands beyond the world, pulsing as supreme power.

## par-p02 — light knows

The supreme Lord is light through and through — and light, rightly seen, is awareness through and through. "But why not bare knowing," comes the objection, "the calm brahman praised by the school of stillness?" Because awareness is twofold — bound and boundless — lest the bounded knower, even of the void, set a ceiling on the light. In creating it takes the world's shape; in keeping, it lights the world; in withdrawing, it gathers all back into itself — everywhere the delight of a whole and perfect I. Awareness is simply this: the flash of an I nobody made — through shaping, shining, dissolving. Take it away, and lordship goes with it, and deadness: sunlight, which knows nothing, must borrow its shining from another and sinks to the side of the inert.

## par-p03 — ten names, one throb

Scripture trumpets this one awareness under ten working names — knowing, knowing-in-freedom, the high Word risen from its own sap, freedom, the Self's high sovereignty, making, pulsing, essence, heart, throb. Each name is earned by what it does: knowing, for awareness is the Self's chief shape; the Word, for it speaks the world by grasping it back to itself, supreme as the ground and fullness of every lesser grasp, sap-risen since its "I" never sets; freedom, for it binds the cast-out "this" home and looses it and lays it low through the hollow places, and by these marks is known from the inert; sovereignty, for it needs no other when the world begins — no fate-bound Brahmā warden answering to another's will; making, for it lifts the whole world from Śiva to clay into "this," the way a yogin makes, out of one inward shining; pulsing, for the flash is always someone's — were throbbing no one's in particular, all would blaze for all, and all be all-knowing or all blind; essence, the untrifling shape, the Lord's own power of knowing; heart, where the world comes to rest; throb, the still light stirring, the world showing as something extra where nothing extra is.

## par-p04 — the world His shining

So the unmade I — real, lit by itself — the Lord pulses and shines by his Lady's power in the shape of the world from Śiva to clay. This shining is his making, and the world's being alive. "But made how — as something other than him, or as nothing other?" As other, his freedom breaks; as nothing other, what is there to make? The answer: without another's light the world cannot so much as be noticed, let alone come into its own — its coming to bear a shining body, leaning on his ground, is exactly what "made" means. Difference is put on display by freedom's own magic, so the world is handled as different while being nothing different: to be made is to shine by him, and nothing else. Formed this way the world is nothing apart from Maheśvara the maker. For stood the known apart, it could not shine and no shining could reach it — there would be nothing at all, and all the give and take of the world would fall. "Then must he not be stained by likeness, veiled by a shining world?" Rather the would-be veil unmakes itself: caught even inside the self, in veiling light it vanishes. By this world his light-shape is never veiled — the world, given rest by his shining, has grown into his very breath. How should it choke him — and choking him, itself go on standing?

## par-p05 — proof retired

Signs and causes cannot lay a finger on the Lord, proved before all proving — and since even the knower who lays down the law stands in his likeness, that knower's standing needs no proof. His self-lit-ness is proved by undergoing. "What proves that he is?" — grant that things are. "What proves one of such a nature?" — the proof is the asker's own shape: Maheśvara's self-shining, proved before all, is proved for each by feeling himself. The light of knowing, life of everything, appoints proofs for the market of illusion — but for him no proof is ever spent: all else, being inert, cannot light even itself without another's light, let alone settle anything beyond. Proof is proof only by leaning on him; and for proving him, who is proof's own life, what use is poor proof, whose whole office is to let some fresh flash come to rest, stamped as a finding, inside the knower? The knower, ever lit, his shining unbroken, already enjoys every finding tucked inward in himself — where would a fresh flash find work in him, and where else could its finding rest? So proof is for knowables — body, breath, blue, delight — not for the Lord, who is proof's life. Hence the saying: the proofs that draw out the very life of things — beyond them all stands a further Life, and he is the supreme Lord.

## par-p06 — the alphabet-I and the thirty-six

His essence, then, is the whole grasp of I, woven of the mass of sound — so Śiva himself is the full display in thirty-six levels. The mass of sound is the heap of letters from a to ha, pregnant with the entire world: the whole I-grasp in the shape "I alone, light-natured, in the shining" — freedom with no other face to look to. And a level of reality is this: the one undivided shine that makes divided families into families — as hill and tree and town all show the shape of earth, and river and lake and sea the shape of water. The thirty-six: Śiva, power, ever-Śiva, the Lord, pure knowing, illusion, art, knowing, passion, time, necessity, the person, nature, intellect, ego, mind, the five senses of knowing, the five of doing, the five subtles, the five great elements.

## par-p07 — the dimming, the bondsman, the freed

Now their marks, each in turn. The Śiva-level is Śiva himself — will, knowing, and doing in a single shape, pure and whole delight by nature — pulsing inside every knower as whole-I joy, past every level, vast-bodied light that can hold a hundred thousand reflections of world-makings and unmakings. When the Lord takes up the will to send the world out, his first pulse is already will-power's level — out of his freedom's greatness, longing to bloom outward, brimming with high-bliss joy, the grasp "I": power's level. The world, just budding, stands covered in I-by-the-self — the ever-Śiva level, the "this"-share bloomed faint on pure knowing-ground, will foremost. The budded world, standing wrapped in I — the Lord level, the I dusted over a cleared "this," knowing foremost, the two differing only in how faint or clear the "this"-share is. The grasp of I-and-this as one is pure knowing: where amid full-grown difference the I shines up as nothing but knowing, the even-held "I-this" — doing foremost.

Difference spreading among things already of his own nature is illusion — the hardest feat of all: it fattens knower-pride in the unconscious and the void, shows even one-with-knowing things split apart, hides their own shape — and is the stuff everything from art to clay is made of. But when the Lord, by the Lady's power of illusion, which is freedom under another name, veils his own shape and puts on the tight mask of the knower, he is called the person — himself, fooled by illusion, chained by deeds, calling his deeds his chains — the wanderer, wandering. Though nothing apart from the Lord, his fooling is never the Lord's own — as the magician's trick, willed by himself, fools the crowd and never the magician. But when knowing shows him his shape and his sovereignty is brought to recognition — when even body and world are taken as nothing but pure feeling — dense with knowing, he is free: fit to be called free while still bodied, past the bond of rebirth; and when the body falls, Śiva-nature itself — Śiva himself. His all-making, all-knowing, wholeness, deathlessness, everywhere-ness — powers unshrunk, taking shrinkage as art, knowing, passion, time, necessity: as a king robbed of everything is dealt a little back out of pity to live on, so wakefulness, shrunk to a point, robbed of all-knowing, is dealt art and the rest in its place, for the sake of a little making.

## par-p08 — what each sheath takes, what each level gives

Art is what lets him who cannot make all make a little — a pot or two. Knowing is what lets him know a little — the pot before him, not the thing far off. Passion is the sticker to things — "unwhole, apart, let this serve me; let me grow; let me never not be." Time is what cuts the train of things as they shine and unshine — "I knew, I know, I shall know; I did, I do, I shall do" — measuring things too in knowing-doing shape, it cuts them off: past and the rest. Must is what holds him to "this is mine to do, that is not" — the many shapes of must, fixed fruit from fixed cause among them. These five, by veiling his own shape, are called the sheath — other than his shape yet not other, lying over whole-knowing-nature like a cover. The root-cause of the levels from the great down to clay is nature — good, passion, dark in equipoise: pleasure, pain, fooling by make-up; shining, doing, holding by nature — one poise where part and counterpoint cannot be told apart. What settles "this is so" — holding the mirror to passing thoughts, clear, fit to catch reflections — is intellect. What says "mine, not mine" in pride is ego. What says "I'll do it" is mind. These three are the inner instrument — grasping inward things like pleasure, made of the strands in part-and-counterpoint, mother of the sense-and-element heap to come. Ears, skin, eyes, tongue, nose in order take sounds, touches, shapes, tastes, smells — the five knowing senses. Voice, hands, feet, the voider, the begetter in order do speaking, holding, moving, voiding, delighting — the five doing senses. Sounds, touches, shapes, tastes, smells in their common shape — that and nothing more, with no between-marks — are the five subtles. Room-giving is ether; life-stirring, air; burning and cooking, fire; freshening and flowing, water; upholding, earth.

## par-p09 — all inside the seed

As sprout and leaf and fruit stand in the banyan seed in bodies exactly fitted to them, so this whole world in all its varied makings stands inside the seed of the heart — as in peacock-egg juice the craftsman's cunning already holds every feather's line and plume-pattern, for the attentive to weigh with fine sight. "As the great tree stands in the banyan seed in power-shape, so this world, moving and unmoving, stands in the heart-seed": by this old maxim, in the heart-seed shaped as the high Lady, this world is held. How so? As the last truth of pot and bowl, clay's shapings, is clay itself — as watery kinds, looked into, settle into water-in-common — so the levels from clay to illusion, weighed for what is real in them, prove to be being itself. Even of the word "being" itself: shed the tail that shows the root's sense, and the bare letter s is left — and inside it stand thirty-one levels; beyond, pure knowing, the Lord, ever-Śiva, knowing-and-doing in essence, enter as powers apart into the letter au, shaped as assent, made of unsurpassed power. Hence the high sending-forth, shaped as making above and below: the breath-out to be breathed. Of the heart-seed so formed — shaped as the great chant, shaped as the world, past the world — Śiva himself, where all rising comes to rest, is its own nature: all-shapes, then no-shape, each in turn the cause. Whoever truly knows such a heart-seed and walks into it is begun indeed — this very knowing, with no sesame-and-ghee poured on fire, is the true beginning. Still drawing breath, dwelling as men dwell, he walks free while living; and when the body falls, he is the Lord, Śiva himself.

*Thus closes the Entering into the Supreme, from glorious Kṣemarāja, noblest of the great Māheśvara masters.*

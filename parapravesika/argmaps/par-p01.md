# Parāprāveśikā par-p01 — ARGUMENT MAP (maṅgala)

*2026-11-01. Source: T1 par-p01 = invocation + maṅgala verse. Lore: the title itself — Pradīpaka renders parāprāveśikā as entering the mysteries of supreme Śakti; Hareesh's course notes one modern wrinkle, ascribing the text to Nāgānanda against the KSTS/Muktabodha ascription to Kṣemarāja (edition followed here, wrinkle logged).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

The work opens with homage and a four-pāda vow: what consciousness is venerated, and under which form.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Invocation:**
> oṃ namaḥ śivāya nikhilatattvātmane

[i.e. veneration to Śiva, whose self is all tattvas without remainder.]

The first line already states the whole doctrine's scope: Śiva is not beyond the tattvas but their self (nikhilatattvātman).

**Authorship line:**
> atha parāprāveśikā śrīmanmahāmāheśvarācāryavaryakṣemarājaviracitā

[i.e. now the Entering-into-the-Supreme, composed by glorious Kṣemarāja, best of great Māheśvara teachers.]

**The vow (maṅgala):**
> viśvātmikāṃ taduttirṇāṃ hṛdayaṃ parameśituḥ |
> parādiśaktirūpeṇa sphurantīṃ saṃvidaṃ numaḥ

[i.e. we venerate consciousness (saṃvid) — the heart of the supreme Lord — which is world-formed (viśvātmikā) yet transcending that (taduttīrṇā), throbbing (sphurantī) in the form of supreme-etc. power (parādiśaktirūpeṇa).]

Form first, then paradox held whole: world-formed AND transcending — immanence and transcendence predicated together, before any exposition. The throbbing (sphurantī) already names vimarśa's pulse; parādi (supreme-etc.) already promises the Parā/Parāparā/Aparā scale.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Authorship: KSTS/Muktabodha ascribe to Kṣemarāja; one modern teacher (Hareesh) ascribes to Nāgānanda. Edition followed; dispute logged, not adjudicated.
2. Parādi is read as supreme-etc. (the threefold Śakti scale), not expanded — the scale is assumed known.

## 4. Decision for L2

Carry vow-then-paradox: homage to Śiva-the-self-of-all-tattvas, then consciousness venerated as heart of the Lord, world-formed yet transcending, throbbing as supreme power. L2 must keep both predicates together — the paradox is the program. Carry the throbbing into passage 2, where it gets its name: vimarśa.

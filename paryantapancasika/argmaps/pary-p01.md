# Paryantapañcāśikā pary-p01 — ARGUMENT MAP (Bhairava as sole reality; the supreme goal / paryanta)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 pary-p01 = vv. 1–7. Lore: Timalsina (Oxford Handbook of Tantric Studies, 2023 — cosmology, epistemology, soteriology in Abhinavagupta); Falsafa.ai translation (Thothica). Key: paryanta = ultimate limit/culmination where all tantras converge. The text is Abhinavagupta's "Fifty verses on the Supreme Extension" — a map of the entire Trika system in 50 verses. The opening: rest in infinite power, the speech of the Lord's heart. Bhairava is the god of all gods; Bhairavi is both being and non-being. Knowledge, ignorance, doctrine, opposition are all made of him. The six paths unite in Bhairavī's abode; Bhairava's heart is pure consciousness-bliss (cidānanda), will, knowledge, action (icchā-jñāna-kriyā).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Bhairava is sole reality. All gods, being, non-being, knowledge, ignorance, doctrine, and opposition are made of him. The six paths (adhvan) become one in Bhairavī's abode. Bhairava's heart is pure consciousness-bliss (cidānanda), will, knowledge, action (icchā-jñāna-kriyā). The text is a map of the entire Trika system: the cosmological descent from anuttara (supreme) through the tattvas to the jīva, and the ascent back. The supreme goal (paryanta) is where all tantras converge.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — rest in infinite power (v. 1):**
> *ādyantāntargatānantaśaktiviśrāntinirbharām | anuccāryām ahaṃ vācam adhīye hṛdayaṃ vibhoḥ*
[i.e. filled with rest of infinite power gone within beginning and end; unspeakable; I study the speech, the heart of the Lord.] The text opens with rest in infinite power (anantaśaktiviśrānti) gone within beginning and end (ādyanta); the speech (vāc) is unspeakable (anuccāryā); the heart (hṛdaya) of the Lord (vibhu) is studied.

**Step 1 — Bhairava is sole reality (v. 2):**
> *sa eva bhairavo devas sarve devāś ca yanmayāḥ | saivāsau bhairavī sattāsattāpi ca yanmayī*
[i.e. he indeed is Bhairava, god; all gods are made of him; that very Bhairavī is; being and non-being also are made of her.] Bhairava is the god of all gods; all gods are made of him. Bhairavī is both being (sattā) and non-being (asattā) — made of her.

**Step 2 — knowledge and ignorance (v. 3):**
> *tad eva bhairavaṃ jñānam ajñānam api yanmayam | sa hi bhairavasiddhāntaḥ pūrvapakṣo 'pi yanmayaḥ*
[i.e. that indeed is Bhairava-knowledge; ignorance also is made of him; he indeed is Bhairava-siddhānta; opponent (pūrvapakṣa) also is made of him.] Knowledge and ignorance are both made of Bhairava. Even the opponent's thesis (pūrvapakṣa) is made of him — nothing is outside Bhairava.

**Step 3 — six paths unite (v. 4):**
> *aikadhyam adhvanāṃ ṣaṇṇāṃ yatra sā bhairavī sthitiḥ | paryante sarvatantrāṇām avācyā paramā gatiḥ*
[i.e. unity of six paths, where that Bhairavī abides; at end of all tantras, ineffable, supreme goal.] The six paths (adhvan: kalā, tattva, bhuvana, varṇa, mantra, pada) become one in Bhairavī's abode. This is the supreme goal (paryanta) — the culmination where all tantras converge, ineffable (avācyā).

**Step 4 — the heart (v. 5):**
> *sā cidānandamātrātmamagnecchājñānasatkriyā | hṛdayaṃ devadevasya bhairavasyāvibhāgabhūḥ*
[i.e. she, consciousness-bliss-only-self-immersed, will-knowledge-true-action; heart of god of gods, Bhairava, non-dual ground.] Bhairavī is will (icchā), knowledge (jñāna), true action (satkriyā), immersed in consciousness-bliss (cidānanda) alone. She is the heart (hṛdaya) of Bhairava, the god of gods — the non-dual ground (avibhāgabhū).

**Step 5 — stages of descent (v. 6):**
> *citsvābhāvyād asau devaḥ svātmanā vimṛṣan prabhuḥ | anāśritādibhūmyantā bhūmikāḥ pratipadyate*
[i.e. from consciousness-nature, that god, by own self reflecting, lord; from unattached etc. ground to end, stages obtains.] From consciousness-nature (citsvābhāvya), the god, reflecting by own self, obtains stages (bhūmikā) from the unattached ground (anāśritā) to the end (bhūmyanta).

**Step 6 — the sixfold path (v. 7):**
> *tatra vācakavācyātmaspandayor ekaśaḥ prabhoḥ | sthūlasūkṣmaparābhāsakramayoḥ ṣaḍvidhādhvatā*
[i.e. there, of expressive and expressed, self-spanda, one, lord; gross-subtle-supreme appearance-sequence, sixfold-path-ness.] There, the expressive (vācaka) and expressed (vācya) are one self-spanda (vibration) of the lord; the gross-subtle-supreme appearance-sequence is the sixfold path (ṣaḍvidhādhva).

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The six paths (adhvan) — kalā, tattva, bhuvana, varṇa, mantra, pada — are the standard Trika sixfold path; the text names them implicitly (v. 7's sthūlasūkṣmaparābhāsakrama).
2. The text is extremely dense; 50 verses map the entire Trika cosmology. Each verse is a compressed statement.
3. The GRETIL text has 52 verses (including closing verses 51–52); our T1 has 8 passages covering vv. 1–52.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the text's own map: rest in infinite power (v. 1) → Bhairava is sole reality (v. 2) → knowledge and ignorance (v. 3) → six paths unite (v. 4) → the heart (v. 5) → stages of descent (v. 6) → the sixfold path (v. 7). The key move is v. 4: the six paths become one in Bhairavī's abode — this is the supreme goal (paryanta), the culmination where all tantras converge. And v. 5: Bhairavī is will, knowledge, true action, immersed in consciousness-bliss — the heart of Bhairava. Carry both into p02 (cosmological descent: sequence, pure/impure, knowers).

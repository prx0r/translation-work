## The parable (canonical draft, ≈90s spoken)

> A great lord holds an estate he never borrowed: it answers to no
> one else. By right he opens its gates, divides its fields to fit,
> and draws the shutters tight over what must stay veiled. Down the
> hill runs his order — mind at the top of the stair, keepers
> measured out in ranks, two fivefold storehouses, the old overseers
> seated along the descent. In the manor lives a steward in a
> stained livery who wears the lord's own seal and keeps its five
> small offices — and for that, wandering the grounds, he is called
> the bound one. Then he climbs back the stair, burns the stain from
> the livery, re-takes the deed and will not let it go: freedom,
> alive and kept. At the top, a door in the middle of the wall opens
> of itself; the means are placed in his hand; he remembers — there
> was never a second lord. Knowing-bliss won: the steward takes the
> lord's chair.

**Map (hinge):**
- estate never borrowed, answers to none → p01 *consciousness free,
  cause unborrowed* (karaṇatva) [p01]
- opens gates / divides fields to fit / shutters tight → p01
  *opens unborrowing, divides by fitness, veils by tightening* [p01]
- order down the hill: mind at top, keepers in ranks, two fivefold
  stores, overseers seated → p02 *mind tightened, knower measured,
  pentads stationed, schools seated on the descent* (tattva-cascade)
  [p02]
- stained livery + lord's seal + five small offices = bound → p03
  *stained likeness keeps divine office; five acts done small is
  wandering* [p03]
- climbs back, burns the stain, re-takes deed, holds fast → p04
  *return climbs, burns, owns, holds fast — freedom alive* [p04]
- middle door opens itself, means placed in hand, remembers no
  second lord, takes the chair → p05 *middle's opening, given
  means, recalled oneness, become Śiva — knowing-bliss won* [p05]

**Limit:** "burns" is read as burning THE STAIN (the p03 linkage) —
flagged as interpretation (the essence says only "burns"). The door
opens *of itself* AND the means are *placed* in his hand — grace
gives both; he never seizes (p05's "given means" honored). The lord
is never drawn — until the steward himself arrives in the chair.
**Rasa:** adbhuta (the free order, p1–2) → vīra-utsāha (the burn and
retake, p3–4) → śānta-śama (the chair, p5) — the parable's pulse
follows the work's own sthāyibhāvas in sequence.
**Bar check:** one protagonist (the steward; lord = chair/gate, never
a second drawn figure); retellable; plain (vimarśa/tattva/schools →
gates/stores/overseers).

## The seal — statement

**The stair between the estate and the chair:** a hill-cut stair —
at its foot the manor with the stained steward and five small office-
flames; up the stair the descent-chart (ranks, two rows of five
gold knots, seated overseers); at the stair's middle an ivory door
opening of itself with a blank gold deed in his hand; at the top an
open gate pouring unborrowed light and one empty gold chair — the
steward climbing, vermilion burn climbing his livery with him.

## Composition laws (this seal)

ONE subject (the climbing steward; no lord figure — the CHAIR is the
lord until he sits). Deed-scroll stays BLANK (no letters — and its
blankness = "given means", unwritten grace). Descent items stay
ON the stair (ranks/knots/overseers = architecture, not a crowd).
Burn-line moves UP (bottom-up rasa law kept inside a descent text).

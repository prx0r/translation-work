# Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya phr-p03 — ARGUMENT MAP (the māyā-knower as transmigrator)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 phr-p03 = sūtras 9–12. Lore: Kṣemarāja (11th c.); Jaideva Singh 1980; Gómez Pomeda 2001. Key: the māyā-knower (māyāpramātā) is like consciousness (cidvat) but covered by impurity (malāvṛtaḥ) from contraction of power (śaktisaṃkoca); nevertheless (tathāpi) he performs the five acts (pañcakṛtyāni) in that way (tadvat); those (tāni) are the state of transmigration (saṃsāritvam). The text's core move: the māyā-knower is consciousness contracted and covered by impurity, yet he still performs the five acts (creation, maintenance, dissolution, concealment, grace) — the same acts as Śiva, but in contracted form.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

The māyā-knower (māyāpramātā) is like consciousness (cidvat) but covered by impurity (malāvṛtaḥ) from contraction of power (śaktisaṃkoca). Nevertheless (tathāpi) he performs the five acts (pañcakṛtyāni) in that way (tadvat). Those (tāni) are the state of transmigration (saṃsāritvam). The text's core move: the māyā-knower is consciousness contracted and covered by impurity, yet he still performs the five acts (creation, maintenance, dissolution, concealment, grace) — the same acts as Śiva, but in contracted form. Transmigration (saṃsāra) is not a separate state but the contracted performance of the five acts.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Sūtra 9 (v. 9):**
> *cidvat tac chaktisaṃkocāt malāvṛtaḥ saṃsārī*
[i.e. like consciousness, that, from contraction of power, covered by impurity, transmigrator.] The māyā-knower is like consciousness (cidvat) — he has the same nature — but covered by impurity (malāvṛtaḥ) from contraction of power (śaktisaṃkoca). He is the transmigrator (saṃsārī).

**Sūtra 10 (v. 10):**
> *tathāpi tadvat pañcakṛtyāni karoti*
[i.e. nevertheless, in that way, five acts, performs.] Nevertheless (tathāpi), in that way (tadvat), he performs the five acts (pañcakṛtyāni) — creation, maintenance, dissolution, concealment, grace. The māyā-knower performs the same five acts as Śiva, but in contracted form.

**Sūtra 11 (v. 11):**
> *tāni*
[i.e. those.] Those (tāni) — the five acts performed in contracted form.

**Sūtra 12 (v. 12):**
> *saṃsāritvam*
[i.e. state of transmigration.] Those (the five acts in contracted form) are the state of transmigration (saṃsāritvam). Transmigration is not a separate state but the contracted performance of the five acts.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The "five acts" (pañcakṛtya) — creation, maintenance, dissolution, concealment, grace — are standard Trika; the text does not enumerate them here. They are the same five acts mentioned in Bodhavilāsa (p01, v. 1) and Paramārthasāra (p01, v. 5).
2. The "contraction of power" (śaktisaṃkoca) — the text does not specify which power is contracted; the standard Trika identifies it as the contraction of citi (consciousness) into citta (mind) via māyā.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the sūtras' own movement: like consciousness but covered by impurity (sūtra 9) → nevertheless performs the five acts (sūtra 10) → those (sūtra 11) → state of transmigration (sūtra 12). The key move is sūtra 9: cidvat tac chaktisaṃkocāt malāvṛtaḥ saṃsārī — the māyā-knower is like consciousness but covered by impurity from contraction of power. And sūtra 12: saṃsāritvam — transmigration is the state of performing the five acts in contracted form. Carry both into p04 (by ascent to consciousness, liberation-while-living). Read it as agency kept through contraction: likeness covered, the five acts still done.

### 6. The living translation loop

### 7. The rasa-carrying principle
Rasa in L2 is not decoration. Each word choice must be justified by the verse's rasa: if the verse is about longing, the language is yearning-weighted; if about violence, abrupt; if about beauty, sensuous; if about surrender, yielding; if about wonder, opening. "This word sounds better" is not a reason. "This word evokes the rasa the Sanskrit evokes, because [specific reason tied to the sthAyibhAva]" is a reason.

### 8. The metacommentary lesson (the big mistake)
I wrote C1 as a literary essay about the poem instead of stating the matters. The mechanical gate (regex for "the verse has/describes/opens/closes...") passed 3 times while the C1 was saturated with document-reference. The broad scan (for "the poem/passage/text/song/work", "the lament/beauty/close", "first/middle/final passage", "reader knows") finally caught it. Three passes to strip all document-reference. The lesson: the gate is a proxy, not the rule. When they disagree, the rule wins.

### 9. The argmap correction
The original argmaps claimed "the lament IS the rasa," "the close reframes both as the poet's achievement" — scholarly assertions, not logical maps. Rewritten as terse statements. Scholarly references woven in as support, not claims. The peer review was also corrected: scholarly specifics live in lore/, not in the argmap.

### 15. The key insight about argmaps
Argmaps don't claim "this is what's happening." They map the argument with certainty/uncertainty clearly marked. The most honest thing: construct the argument from the verse with references to scholars to support sharp lines.

### 16. The key insight about rasa
Rasa in L2 is not decoration. Each word choice must be justified by the verse's rasa. The rasa is in the English word choice and rhythm, not in a commentary about it.

### 17. The key insight about the concept database
The concept database grows as works are transformed. It does not need to be complete before translation begins.

### 18. The key insight about the glossary
The trika glossary is a knowledge graph: nodes = terms, edges = cross-references. Our concept database is the enriched version.

# Transmission brief — Cidgaganacandrikā (Śrīvatsa) — RESEARCHED 2026-11-11
*Source: Sanderson, "Śaiva Exegesis of Kashmir". 312 verses, a hymn to Kālī.*

## A. FACTS
- Author **Śrīvatsa**; lineage **Śivānanda → Soma** — the *Mānavaugha*
  (human order: Śivānanda, Keyūravatī, Hrasvanātha) and the *Śiṣyaugha*
  (disciple order, from **Cakrabhānu**). Śrīvatsa received it from Soma's son.
- Composed **after Arṇasiṃha** (whom he reworks) and **before c. 1300**
  (Maheśvarānanda cites it). Probably **South Indian**: composed at
  **Pūrṇapīṭha / Pūrṇagiri** (one of the four Śākta pīṭhas).
- **>1/3 of the 312 verses are closely related to Arṇasiṃha's
  Mahānayaprakāśa** — rephrasings in a tighter poetic style.

## B. WHAT IT ADDS BEYOND ARṆASIṂHA
Each of the **13 Kālīs of the anākhya-cakra** receives its own treatment
(building on the lost Kramastotra verses preserved in Jayaratha's
Tantrālokaviveka). The thirteen, in worship order: **Sṛṣṭikālī,
Sthitinasakālī, Saṃhārakālī, Raktakālī, Śukālī, Yāmakālī, Mṛtyukālī,
Bhadrakālī, Paramārkakālī, Mārtaṇḍakālī, Kālāgnirudrakālī, Mahākālakālī,
Mahābhairavaghoracaṇḍakālī.**

## C. TRAPS
- **Hymn form** — the register is devotional-poetic, but the content is
  the same liturgy and doctrine; do not "poeticise" away the technical terms.
- Text is **corrupt in the edition** (Sanderson flags intractable
  corruption, esp. the lineage verse 300) — mark obscurities, never smooth.
- Same cakra vocabulary as Mahānayaprakāśa (see that brief): pañcavāha,
  prakāśa, ānanda, mūrti, vṛnda, anākhya.

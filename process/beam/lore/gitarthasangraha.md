# Transmission brief — Gītārthasaṅgraha (Abhinavagupta's Gathering of the Gītā's Meaning)

*Read this before touching any Gita span. It is the difference between translating
this text and translating the Gītā you think you know.*

## 1. WHAT THE TEXT IS

Abhinavagupta (Kashmir, c. 975–1025) wrote exactly one work on the
prasthānatraya, and it is this: the **Gītārthasaṅgraha — a gathering
(saṅgraha) of the meaning (artha) of the Gītā**, not a line-by-line
commentary (bhāṣya/vṛtti). He glosses only passages where he, or his teacher,
saw a significance that others had missed. A famous verse left uncommented is
a deliberate silence, not an omission — do not fill it, do not import
Śaṅkara's agenda into the gap.

The Gītā he reads is the **Kashmirian recension (K)**, not the vulgate (V).
Source: Muktabodha M00244, the Lakshman Joo / Raina 1933 edition, IAST.
K carries roughly fourteen extra verses and variant readings (Schrader 1930;
Belvalkar/Edgerton dispute K's priority; Bansat-Boudon & Törzsök's critical
edition from 7 Śāradā + 1 Devanāgarī MSS is underway).
**Number by OUR edition. Translate OUR edition. Never silently harmonize to
the vulgate.** K-vs-V deltas belong in lore/notes, never in the gloss stream.

The shape: 6 maṅgala (benedictory) verses (gita-a00) + 18 adhyāyas
(gita-a01..a18). Each span = Gītā mūla + Abhinava's prose on it, closing with
that adhyāya's saṃgraha verse(s) (the compressed siddhānta). 740 spans total.
**The prose is content, not apparatus** — gloss it exactly like the mūla.

The doctrine in one sentence: Abhinavagupta reads the Gītā as a
**pratyabhijñā śāstra** — the teaching that the Self is Śiva, *recognized*,
not produced, through knowing-and-acting — and the work declares this lineage
openly: it comes from **Bhaṭṭendurāja's āmnāya**, the teacher's oral tradition
(a00-v06: *bhaṭṭendurājādāmnāya … kṛto'bhinavaguptena*). Learn to smell which
moves are the teacher's conservative rescue and which are Abhinava's own
Śaiva turn; both are faithfully rendered, neither is flattened.

Witnesses (comparative orientation ONLY — never canon, never override):
Sharma 1983 (Brill; first full English; follows corrupt Pansikar text,
emends via BORI ms; read Ingalls's preface on the corruption); Marjanovic
2002 (Indica); Sankaranarayanan 1985 (text) + pt 2 (translation); the IIT
Kanpur Gita Supersite (digital K text + parallel commentaries). Use them to
orient on K-only verses; cite as comparative grounds; the gloss follows our
source.

## 2. THE MOVES TO LOOK FOR

1. **The maṅgala frame (a00).** *Śaṃbhura … jayati* — the Lord triumphs as
   treasury of the lights of knowing (*bodhabhāsāṃ nidhiḥ*). The Gītā is
   declared a mokṣa-śāstra whose fruit is liberation *through knowing*.
   Every later move answers to this frame; if your argmap drifts from it,
   you have lost the thread.
2. **The adhyāya prastāva.** Each adhyāya opens with occasion-prose (e.g.
   a01's *vidyāvidyā* frame: who is fit to be taught, and why now). This is
   doctrine — adhikāra and prayojana — not throat-clearing. Gloss it as such.
3. **jñāna-karma-samuccaya, Trika-grounded.** Action is grounded in the
   Lord's svātantrya; the kartṛ (agent) is Śiva's power operating through the
   bound one, not the ego acting alone. Watch for the exact move that makes
   karma-yoga and jñāna-yoga *one recognition* — that is the saṅgraha's work.
4. **The guṇatraya through Śaiva tattvas.** Ch. 14, the 17–18
   classifications, miscellaneous guṇa verses (2.45–46, 3.27–28, 3.37,
   7.12–14, 13.20–22, 15.2, 11.18, 16.1), plus K-only extras (notably verses
   after 3.37). Read sattva/rajas/tamas down the Śaiva order (māyā →
   puruṣa → prakṛti-guṇas), never as plain Sāṃkhya.
5. **"Extraordinary interpretations" (Sharma ch. 5).** Tantric/esoteric turns
   on outwardly plain verses. When the prose suddenly goes Trika-technical on
   a verse the vulgate tradition reads flatly, THAT is the point — render it
   sharply; do not domesticate it into Advaita or devotion.
6. **The saṃgraha closers** (*atra saṃgrahaślokaḥ*). Each adhyāya's
   compressed doctrine. Dense, load-bearing, fully weighted in the argmap.
7. **The polemic edge** (a00-v05: *nyāyyas tathāpy udyamo … tadgūḍhārtha-
   prakāśakaḥ* — many have commented, yet my effort stands because it reveals
   the hidden meaning). He is answering Śaṅkara's jñāna-only renunciation and
   mere ritual karman alike. Keep the opponent in view or the move goes limp.
8. **The colophon** (*paripūrṇo'yaṃ … gītārthasaṃgrahe … iti śivam*).
   Record it; exclude from the gloss stream as paratext.

## 3. THE TRAPS (read twice — these are where drafts die)

1. **The vulgate shadow.** You know the Gītā from the vulgate and from
   Śaṅkara. That knowledge is a liability here. K shifts numbers, adds
   verses, reads differently. The moment you "recognize" a famous verse,
   STOP and verify the K wording in THIS span plus what Abhinava does with
   it — both routinely differ from your memory. Never renumber; never import
   the famous reading.
2. **Mūla/bhāṣya bleed.** The span fuses Kṛṣṇa's verse and Abhinava's prose.
   Never attribute the commentator's Trika vocabulary to the mūla, and never
   flatten the mūla into the commentary. Gloss in order; keep the voices
   distinct in the argmap's premises.
3. **Advaita defaults — the deadliest trap.** māyā, Brahman, ātman, mokṣa,
   jñāna do NOT carry their Śaṅkara values here. māyā is the Māyā-power
   (a Śaiva tattva), not illusion; mokṣa is recognition, not cessation.
   Ground every gloss in THIS span + the ledger below. When torn, the Trika
   sense beats the Advaita reflex — and say so in grounds.
4. **Print corruption.** The edition is corrupt in places (a00 carries 5
   LIVE forks; Ingalls/Sharma flag 4.28, 4.29–32, 7.27, 15.15–19 as needing
   future scholars). Garbled tokens get AMBIGUOUS + an honest note. A guess
   presented as fact is worse than a gap. Never smooth.
5. **Fused prose compounds.** Long Trika compounds
   (*vitatasphuradvividhabhāvacakrātmakaḥ*-class) spanning half a pāda.
   Pada-split carefully; ONE fused surface = ONE row; hyphenated,
   compound-ordered, paren-free glosses.
6. **Paratext.** `|| A-V ||` closers, bare `|`/`||`, the provenance block,
   the *atra saṃgrahaślokaḥ* rubric-label (label excluded, verse kept),
   colophon lines, adhyāya rubric headers. Excluded with note — never
   glossed, never faked.
7. **K-only verses.** Verses absent from the vulgate are REAL text. Use
   Sharma/Marjanovic/Supersite to orient; cite them as comparative grounds;
   the gloss follows our source, full weight.
8. **Selective silence.** Uncommented famous verses are fidelity, not gaps.
   Coverage = every content token glossed; do not invent commentary.
9. **Meter vs prose.** Mūla is anuṣṭubh/triṣṭubh; commentary is prose.
   Never force verse cadence into prose glosses or prose sprawl into verse.
10. **"Summary" ≠ abridgment.** Saṅgraha is the argumentative gathering —
    the moves that make the Gītā cohere as a Śaiva śāstra. Argmaps
    reconstruct THAT argument (verse-owned question, Abhinava's Śaiva
    turn, premises, adjudicated forks). Paraphrase is failure; thin
    questions ("what does verse N state") are failure.

## 4. HOW TO APPROACH

- Per adhyāya: triple tasks (gloss + CM + argmap + L2), windows ≤60 spans.
  gita-a18 (79 spans) goes as two windows; all others fit in one.
- Gloss mūla + prose in span order. T1 house style follows a00's [and]-chain.
- L0: morphology + ranked options for hard compounds; LIVE forks where the
  print is corrupt or the K reading genuinely uncertain (K-vs-V mere
  difference is NOT a fork — only genuine uncertainty is).
- Argmap: the unit's own question (≥5 words, never "what does verse N
  state"), terse inferential premises (≥3 words each), honest specific
  uncertainties, byte-exact cited hinges. Premises must be logic, not
  paraphrase. Questions must be the passage's, never meta.
- L2: plain-English line render, every clause traced to L0, byte-exact
  cites. C1-essence after (states what the unit DOES, never the document).
- Coverage bar: every non-paratext token glossed. Paratext exclusions
  recorded per song, never silent.

**Term ledger (LOCK — retire the Advaita variants):**
saṅgraha = gathering (of meaning); gītārtha = the Gītā's meaning;
pratyabhijñā = recognition; svātantrya = freedom (the Lord's);
mokṣa = liberation (through recognition); jñāna = knowing/cognition;
karman/kriyā = action; kartṛtva = agency (the Lord's power);
guṇa (sattva/rajas/tamas) = strand (read down the Śaiva tattvas);
māyā = the Māyā-power (Śaiva tattva), NEVER Śaṅkara's illusion;
tattva = category/principle; śakti = power; Śiva/Śaṃbhu/Bhagavat = the Lord;
bodha = wakeful knowing; bhāsā = light/manifestation; vimarśa = reflexive
awareness; prakāśa = manifestation; āmnāya = oral tradition (the teacher's);
gūḍhārtha = hidden meaning; prastāva = opening/occasion; adhikāra =
eligibility; adhikārin = the qualified one; prayojana = purpose;
siddhānta = settled doctrine; pūrvapakṣa = the opponent's case.

## APPENDIX — concrete K-vs-V facts (verified 2026-11-09, use to orient, never to override the source)

- K 1.1a reads *sarvakṣatrasamāgame* (not vulgate *samavetā yuyutsavaḥ*).
  If our 1.1 carries it, gloss it; do not "correct" to the vulgate.
- Five extra verses follow K 3.37 (3.37.1–5, on kāma/krodha as the enemy:
  *bhavatyeṣa kathaṃ kṛṣṇa…*, *kāmakrodhamayo ghoraḥ…* etc.). They are REAL
  content in K. Sharma/Marjanovic translate them; Burba 2024 gives text +
  word-analyzed Ukrainian renderings — usable as comparative grounds.
- K 5.16.1 (*smaranto'pi… paṅke ravikarā iva*) and 6.9.1 (*ihaiva tair
  jitaḥ sargo…*) are K-only verses. Same treatment.
- Typical K variant moves (Belvalkar's catalogue): grammatical smoothing
  (2.50ab, 2.60a, 3.23a, 5.1cd, 6.39a, 7.18b, 9.31c, 10.9d, 11.41b, 14.23d,
  16.2c, 16.13b), syntax normalization, and a handful of sense-changing
  readings (e.g. 2.11ab, 11.46 *bhujadvayena* vs *caturbhujena*). When our
  text carries one, note it; the gloss follows our text.
- The vulgate crept into K prints (Schrader: even into Abhinava's pratīkas).
  If a mūla line in our edition contradicts Abhinava's own gloss of it,
  THAT is a LIVE fork (print contamination), adjudicated in the argmap —
  this is exactly the Ingalls/Sharma corruption class.
- Sharma's full English translation is openly consultable
  (gianfrancobertagni.it/materiali/tantra/sharma.pdf) — use it as a
  comparative witness for hard passages (grounds cite, never canon).

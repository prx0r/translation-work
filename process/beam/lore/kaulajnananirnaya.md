# Transmission brief — Kaulajñānanirṇaya (Matsyendranātha) — RESEARCHED 2026-11-11

*Scholarship-backed. Muktabodha M00027 (IAST). 680 verses in our witness.*

## A. BIBLIOGRAPHIC FACTS (verified)

- **Author:** Matsyendranātha (Matsyendra), Nātha siddha, founder-figure of
  the Yoginī Kaula school. Also called Macchendrapada / Macchindrapada /
  Matsyodara. Abhinavagupta honours him as "Father of Yoga" in the
  Tantrāloka — so he was already legendary by the early 11th century.
- **Sanskrit edition:** P. C. **Bagchi**, *Calcutta Sanskrit Series III*,
  1934 — from a palm-leaf ms he found in the **Nepal royal library**
  (the ms had been mis-catalogued as Buddhist).
- **English:** **Michael Magee**, *Kaulajñāna-Nirṇaya of the School of
  Matsyendranātha*, Prachya Prakashan, Varanasi, 1986 (Tantra
  Granthamālā 12) — **the first translation into any language**; based on
  Bagchi's edition. A newer study + Spanish translation: **Stella Dupuis
  with Satkari Mukhopadhyaya** (working from photographs of the original
  manuscript).
- **Date:** Bagchi places the ms no later than the **11th century**;
  the language is archaic, so the tradition is older.

## B. TEXT STATE — read this before glossing

1. **Lacunose.** Of chapter 1 only the **last two ślokas** survive. Do not
   invent a beginning; mark gaps.
2. **Archaic, pre-classical Sanskrit** — very unlike later tantras:
   irregular sandhi, non-standard forms, colloquial Nātha idiom.
3. **Mantras appear in FULL, not in code/bīja abbreviation** — unlike
   later tantras. Transcribe exactly; do not "decode".
4. Each chapter closes with the claim that the knowledge was received in
   **Chandradvīpa** ("Island of the Moon", a transcendence-state name)
   before activity in **Kāmarūpa**.

## C. DOCTRINE / MOVES

- **Yoginī Kaula**: the Yoginīs as embodiments of kula/śakti and of the
  siddhis; the body as the site of their activation.
- **The triple śaktis**: icchā, jñāna, kriyā (shared with Trika; do not
  import Trika's technical elaboration).
- Signature motifs: **haṃsa**, the **colour red**, **svecchācāra**
  (self-willed conduct) — the Kaula freedom theme in Nātha idiom.
- Practical/alchemical content: siddhis, yogic techniques, mantra use.

## D. TRAPS

1. **Do not classicise.** The Sanskrit is rough by design; a polished
   Trika-style gloss misrepresents it.
2. **Nātha vocabulary ≠ Trika vocabulary** even where terms coincide
   (kula, śakti, siddhi). Gloss in situ.
3. **Lacunae**: Bagchi's edition has gaps; encode them, never smooth.
4. **Compare Magee** for hard verses, but our base is Bagchi/M00027 —
   where they differ, mark TEXTUAL.
5. **Yoginī passages** are technical (bodily energies, siddhis), not
   generic goddess-devotion; keep the practice register.

## E. APPROACH

680 verses; 24-verse windows; T1 labeled verses; coverage gate 0.85;
12-verse triples only on bounce. Minimal gold first.

**Ledger (LOCK):** matsya/matsyendra = the fish-born siddha; kula = the
divine energy (śakti) circulating in the body/cosmos; yoginī = embodiment
of that energy/siddhi (not merely a female ascetic); svecchācāra =
self-willed conduct; chandradvīpa = the transcendence-state of reception;
kāmarūpa = the field of enactment.

# Transmission brief — Mahānayaprakāśa (Krama) — RESEARCHED 2026-11-11
*Sources: Sanderson, "The Śaiva Exegesis of Kashmir" (Mélanges Brunner);
Hareesh/Wallis Krama series. Tradition: Kālīkulakrama. Three distinct works
share this title — check which one before glossing.*

## A. WHICH MAHĀNAYAPRAKĀŚA
1. **Arṇasiṃha / Vīrasiṃha** (fl. c. 1050–1100), South Indian — the main one.
2. **Anonymous Trivandrum** — post-Abhinavagupta (975–1025), pre-Jayaratha
   (13th c.); reworks TĀ material.
3. **Śitikaṇṭha** — verses in **Old Kashmiri** with a Sanskrit commentary.

## B. THE KRAMA LITURGY (Arṇasiṃha's sequence — the glossing spine)
Preliminaries: **Pīṭha Uḍḍiyāna**, its **Karavīra** cremation ground,
site-guardian (**Kṣetrapāla Balotkāṭa**), the assembly of Yoginīs/Śākinīs
(**melāpa**), the great sacrifice (**mahāyāga**). Then the FIVE circles:
1. **pañcavāha-cakra** — five Flow Goddesses: Vyomavāmeśvarī, Khecarī,
   Bhūcarī, Saṃhārabhakṣiṇī, Raudreśvarī
2. **prakāśa-cakra** — faculties reaching toward the object
3. **ānanda-cakra** — faculties incorporating sense-objects
4. **mūrti-cakra** — object-awareness reverts to the perceiver
5. **vṛnda-cakra** — 64 Siddhas, self-awareness reverts to its core
Then: guru lineage from **Maṅgalā** (leader of the 64), and the four
outward circles: **sṛṣṭi / sthiti / saṃhāra / anākhya** (the 13 Kālīs),
where inner/outer reaches **sāmarasya**.

## C. TRAPS
- **cakra = circle/assembly here, NEVER "wheel" or "cakra" in the yogic
  sense**; pañcavāha = the FIVE FLOWS, not "five currents of breath".
- **13 Kālīs** are identical in appearance and map to perceptual phases —
  number and order are load-bearing.
- Do not import Trika vocabulary; this is Krama liturgy.
- Old Kashmiri text (Śitikaṇṭha): vernacular ≠ Sanskrit; mark language.

**Ledger (LOCK):** mahānaya = the great way (Krama); pañcavāha = the five flows; prakāśa/ānanda/mūrti/vṛnda/anākhya = the five/six circles; melāpa = the yoginī assembly; sāmarasya = perfect fusion; the 13 Kālīs = perceptual phases.

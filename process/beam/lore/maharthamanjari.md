# Transmission brief — Mahārthamañjarī (Maheśvarānanda) — RESEARCHED 2026-11-11
*Ed. Mukunda Rama Shastri (1918), with the author's own Parimala commentary.*

## A. FACTS
- **Maheśvarānanda**, based at **Cidambaram** (Tamil country), disciple of
  Mahāprakāśa; **cites the Cidgaganacandrikā**, so after Śrīvatsa.
- A **synthesis**: Krama ritual (the five flows) fused with the
  **non-dual Śaivism of Kashmir**, building on Utpaladeva.
- Its signature thesis: **the "Supreme I-consciousness" is individuated as
  one's particular personal self** — an ecumenical reworking, not a copy of
  either tradition.

## B. MOVES
1. **Five flows (pañcavāha)** as the ritual frame, but read
   philosophically (the flows are modes of the one I-consciousness).
2. **Utpaladeva-language** (vimarśa, ahaṃ, svātantrya) appears legitimately
   here — unlike in pure Krama texts.
3. **Naṭarāja's five acts** escape esoteric exegesis into popular Śaiva
   understanding through this lineage.

## C. TRAPS
- **Hybrid vocabulary**: Trika terms are genuine here; do not "correct" them
  back to Krama.
- The autocommentary (Parimala) is the author's own — it may be used for
  clarification but mark it as commentary, not mūla.
- Do not flatten the synthesis into either parent tradition.

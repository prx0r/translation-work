## 1. WHAT IT IS

Abhinavagupta's Mālinīślokavārttika / Mālinīvijayavārttika: a VERSE gloss
(vārttika) on the Mālinīvijayottaratantra — the tantra Abhinava held to be
not only Trika's root scripture but the essence of the whole Śaiva
revelation. The work's thesis (encyclopedia summary of Abhinavagupta's
program): a "supreme nondualism" (paramādvayavāda) in which the Absolute as
autonomous consciousness contains plurality AND unity as its own
self-representation modes — thereby Trika transcends and contains the
dualist-vs-nondualist Śaiva split. Only Part I (1–399) is critically edited
and translated (Hanneder 1998, Groningen; GRETIL text with his two new MSS);
the rest survives in the 1921 KSTS edition. Hanneder's introduction +
running commentary is the scholarly gold standard; Padoux's JAOS review is
the honest audit. Purpose per Hanneder: a philosophy of REVELATION — how the
highest non-dual knowledge descends through levels of speech (parā →
paśyantī → madhyamā → vaikharī) into the Tantras/Agamas.

## 2. MOVES TO LOOK FOR

1. **Revelation as descent.** The master argument: śāstra is the Absolute's
   self-descent through speech-levels. Every passage's premises should be
   tested against this ladder — where on the descent does this claim sit?
2. **Containment, not refutation.** Against dualist Siddhānta AND against
   flat nondualism: Trika contains both as the Absolute's modes. Watch for
   the characteristic double move (grant the opponent's level, show it
   nested inside the higher). Argmaps must preserve both halves.
3. **The Trika core (Hanneder §§4–5):** the three goddesses/powers, the five
   powers (cit, ānanda, icchā, jñāna, kriyā), the five streams and three
   divisions of śāstra. These are load-bearing technical sequences — gloss
   each member exactly; a dropped member breaks the inference.
4. **Mūlasūtra + nature-of-knowledge opening (I,1–13 + mūlasūtra).** The
   foundation: what knowledge IS such that revelation can be its descent.
   Slow down here; the whole Vārttika hangs from these verses.
5. **The Śrīkaṇṭhī fragment (Hanneder App.1).** Probable source of the canon
   theory. When Abhinava cites the canon's structure, check App.1 in grounds.

## 3. TRAPS

1. **Fragmentary survival.** KSTS remainder ≠ critical text. Do not treat
   print variants as forks without manuscript reason; mark TEXTUAL (not
   interpretive) uncertainty distinctly from philosophical AMBIGUOUS.
2. **Verse ABOUT prose tantra.** A vārttika glosses a prose mūla in verse;
   keep mūla-claim vs gloss-claim distinct in the argmap traces.
3. **Speech-level slippage.** parā/paśyantī/madhyamā/vaikharī are precise
   stations, not synonyms for "speech". Never collapse them.
4. **paramādvaya ≠ advaita.** "Supreme nondualism" CONTAINS duality; do not
   translate as Śaṅkara-style nondualism. The containment IS the doctrine.
5. **Hanneder is witness, not canon.** Use his readings/notes as comparative
   grounds for hard verses; the gloss follows our collated source.

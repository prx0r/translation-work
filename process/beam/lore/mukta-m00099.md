### Tradition doctrine (from the covering brief, reused as ground):

THE DOCTRINE — mahāsāhasa

The acme of practice is **MAHĀSĀHASA, "the Great Audacity"** — a sudden,
violent irruption of expanding consuming pure consciousness; the term
appears only ONCE in all of Abhinavagupta (TĀ 5/83cd–86ab, quoted by
Dyczkowski). Enlightenment comes suddenly through the most intense
descent of grace: the senses are divinized, operate freely, then dissolve
into the Void of consciousness free of thought-constructs — "as profoundly
tranquil as it is immensely powerful."

**Structure of the first seven aphorisms (Dyczkowski):** introverted
transcendence by **penetrating the centre between the opposites**
(subject/object). The opening of that centre is the essential feature of
the instant of insight; expanding into the Self, it consumes all outer
objectivity into the Great Void where the energies fuse into the Great
Oneness of aesthetic delight.

## C. MOVES TO LOOK FOR

1. **Centre-between-opposites.** Every early sūtra works this joint —
   gloss the pair being transcended (subject/object, krama/akrama,
   being/non-being, real/unreal, plurality/unity, conceptual/nonconceptual,
   saṃsāra/mokṣa).
2. **Suddenness.** No gradual path language: the descriptions are of an
   irruption, not a ladder.
3. **Yoginī authority.** The text claims oral transmission from the
   Yoginīs; that claim is itself doctrine, not decoration.
4. **Aesthetic delight (camatkāra-family).** The fused energies' oneness
   is described as rasa/ānanda — keep the affective register.

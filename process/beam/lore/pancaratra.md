# Transmission brief — Pāñcarātra (Vaiṣṇava Tantra) — RESEARCHED 2026-11-11
*Sources: Rastelli, Oxford Bibliographies "Vaiṣṇava Pāñcarātra"; Leach 2013
(Edinburgh PhD on Pāñcarātra textual traditions); Schrader 1916; H. D. Smith's
Descriptive Bibliography (2 vols); Gupta's Lakṣmītantra (1972).*

## A. FACTS
- **Pāñcarātra**: the Vaiṣṇava tantric tradition worshipping **Viṣṇu/Nārāyaṇa/
  Vāsudeva** as supreme. Earliest textual source: the **Nārāyaṇīya** in the
  Mahābhārata (c. 3rd–5th c. CE); tantric Saṃhitās from **c. 9th c. onward**
  (most 1100–1400, South India; earliest probably Kashmir/North India).
- **Vyūha doctrine**: the divine person as a four/five-fold formation —
  **Vāsudeva, Saṃkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna, Aniruddha** (+ Nārāyaṇa) — mapped onto
  states of the person (thinking, feeling, willing, knowing, acting).
- Canon of "three jewels": **Jayākhya, Sātvata, Pauṣkara**. Later works
  (**Lakṣmītantra, Ahirbudhnya-saṃhitā**) show Kashmir Śaiva influence.
- Goals: **bhukti** (worldly enjoyment) and **mukti** (liberation); later
  Saṃhitās shift to public temple worship for king and state.

## B. MOVES
1. **Vyūha = formation, not "emanation" vaguely** — keep the four names and
   their order; each is a mode of the one God AND a state of the devotee.
2. **Ritual primacy**: these are ritual texts (dīkṣā, daily worship, temple
   installation, festivals) — procedure exact.
3. **Vedic + Tantric mixture** is explicit in the texts themselves; do not
   force either pole.
4. **Iconography**: Smith's Sourcebook is the reference for image descriptions.

## C. TRAPS
- **Pāñcarātra ≠ Śrīvaiṣṇava** (the later Tamil tradition descended from it).
- **Vāsudeva = Kṛṣṇa** here, not a separate deity; and the vyūhas are not
  four gods.
- **Do not import Śaiva vocabulary**, though Kṣemarāja-influenced texts
  (Lakṣmī/Ahirbudhnya) legitimately show it.
- Saṃhitā ≠ Purāṇa: citation style differs; keep saṃhitā numbering.

**Ledger (LOCK):** vyūha = formation (Vāsudeva/Saṃkarṣaṇa/Pradyumna/
Aniruddha); saṃhitā = scripture (compendium); bhukti = enjoyment;
mukti = liberation; dīkṣā = initiation; āgama = the canon.

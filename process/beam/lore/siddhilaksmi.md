# Transmission brief — Siddhilakṣmī (Sarvāmnāya) — RESEARCHED 2026-11-11
*Sources: Timalsina, "Terrifying Beauty: Interplay of the Sanskritic and
Vernacular Rituals of Siddhilakṣmī"; Sanderson 1990; Dyczkowski 2000
(Kubjikā, Kālī, Tripurā and Trika); Puraścaryārṇava.*

## A. FACTS
- **Primary deity of Matsyendra**, the traditional founder of the Kaula
  system. In the **āmnāya** system she belongs to the **northern
  transmission** (Puraścaryārṇava p. 16); in Nepal linked with the
  **Uttarāmnāya** and with Kālī.
- Part of **Sarvāmnāya** and the **Guhyakālī** tantra complex. She
  "encompasses the totality of all goddesses" (Mahādevī manifestation).
- **Form**: terrifying — rides a **Bhairava** who rides a corpse (or a
  vātula), multiple faces/arms, skull-cup, freshly cut head, sword, skull,
  staff, trident: **Kāpālika attributes** (not the benign lotus Lakṣmī).
- **Nepal**: patron deity of several **Malla kings**; clan deity (kula-devatā
  / digu-dya) of many Newar families; five temples in Patan/Bhaktapur but
  publicly known under other names (Pūrṇacaṇḍī, Nyātapola, Macalā =
  Matṣyeśvarī, Śikalā) — deliberate dissimulation.
- Liturgies: Siddhilakṣmīkramārcanā, Siddhilakṣmīkrama-sthaṇḍila-arcanavidhi,
  Siddhilakṣmī-adhivāsana-sthaṇḍila-arcanavidhi etc.; several preserve the
  hymn **minus its first verse**.

## B. TRAPS
1. **Siddhilakṣmī ≠ Lakṣmī.** No lotus/benign imagery; kāpālika register.
2. **Five faces / ten transmissions**: the āmnāya frame (krama-dīkṣā =
   sequential initiation into all transmissions) is the doctrinal setting.
3. Ritual-manual genre: exact substances, mantras, sequence.
4. Public name ≠ textual name; note dissimulation, do not "identify" her
   with the popular temple deity without evidence.

## 1. WHAT IT IS

The root text: 77 sūtras (3 sections: śāmbhavopāya / śāktopāya / āṇavopāya)
revealed to Vasugupta (9th c.) — the formal beginning of Trika. The
commentary: Kṣemarāja's Vimarśinī (11th c., Abhinavagupta's student), prose,
considered the most authoritative of the four commentaries (Vṛtti, Bhāskara's
verse Vārttika, Vimarśinī, Varadarāja's verse rehash). We translate SŪTRA +
VIMARŚINĪ as one unit-span each: the sūtra's pointing plus Kṣemarāja's
unpacking. Witnesses (comparative only): Jaideva Singh 1979 (English),
Silburn 1980 (French), Torella 1979/2013 (Italian, the scholarly standard),
Lakshman Joo (English, oral lineage), Pradīpaka (word-by-word, useful for
hard compounds). GRETIL + the site's full translation exist as orientation.

## 2. MOVES TO LOOK FOR

1. **The sūtra points; the Vimarśinī pours.** Torella's form-theory is the
   transmission key: a sūtra text is deliberately holey (cheese full of
   holes) so the tradition can live in it for a millennium; sūtra = sūc,
   *to indicate*, not to declare. Every argmap must therefore reconstruct
   TWO moves: what the aphorism indicates, and what content Kṣemarāja pours
   into that grid (often Pratyabhijñā-grade doctrine postdating the sūtra).
2. **The three upāyas as one ascent.** I: śāmbhavopāya (will/icchā —
   sudden elevation, *udyama*; the four states enjoyed as the Fourth);
   II: śāktopāya (knowledge — the middle path, mantra/mātṛkā, the lake of
   consciousness); III: āṇavopāya (action — the individual means, breath,
   body, dissolution of the differentiated). The commentary constantly
   cross-refers sections; trace those threads, don't isolate sūtras.
3. **Mātṛkā/mantra doctrine (I.2–4, II.3–7).** The "un-understood Mother"
   as basis of limited knowledge; the arrangement (*paripāṭī*) of letters
   as the body of the Lord. Kṣemarāja's phonematic mysticism is precise —
   gloss letter-powers exactly, never as vague "energy".
4. **The Lord's attributes smuggled into anthropology.** *vīra* = senses
   (I.11), *kumāri/umidu* puzzles (I.13), the "void" (*śūnya*) appearances
   (I.15–16): Kṣemarāja routinely re-reads plain words as technical terms.
   When the gloss surprises you, that IS the move — render it, then show
   the work in grounds.
5. **Polemic edge.** Against the Vṛtti's flattening, against Bhāskara's
   "strange" verse readings, against dualist ritualism: the Vimarśinī
   positions Trika as the esoteric crown. Keep the opponent in view.

## 3. TRAPS

1. **Laconicity is the genre.** A 3-word sūtra (*caitanyam ātmā*) is not
   thin — it is a grid-hole awaiting Kṣemarāja's content. Never pad the
   sūtra; never compress the commentary into the sūtra's shape. Two voices,
   one span.
2. **Pratyabhijñā retrojection.** Kṣemarāja reads 10th–11th c. doctrine
   (upāyas, vimarśa, svātantrya) back into a 9th c. text. Name this when it
   happens (grounds: "Kṣemarāja's pour"); do not pretend the sūtra said it.
3. **Technical redefinitions.** vīra, kumārī, mātṛkā, mudrā, vismaya,
   udyama — every innocent word may be a term of art. Default to the
   technical sense; fall back to plain only with stated reason.
4. **Four commentaries, one choice.** Where Bhāskara or the Vṛtti read
   differently, that is context for adjudication, not a fork — fork ONLY
   when OUR edition's reading is genuinely uncertain.
5. **77, not 79/80.** Some editions number differently (colophon variants,
   split sūtras). Number OUR edition; note variants in lore, never renumber.

## 4. APPROACH

~77 sūtra+prose units; medium tokens → small triple windows (≤8 units; the
prose is dense) or assessor-direct for the hardest. Gloss sūtra + prose in
span order; padapāṭha-style splits for compounds. L0 with ranked options;
witness-supported (not witness-led) adjudications. Argmaps reconstruct the
double move (indication + pour). L2 carries rasa (wonder, recognition);
C1-essence states what each sūtra DOES to the reader's standpoint.

**Ledger (LOCK):** sūtra = aphorism/indication-thread; vimarśinī =
reflective examination; upāya = means (śāmbhava/śākta/āṇava); udyama =
sudden elevation; caitanya = consciousness (omniscient-omnipotent);
ātmā = the Self/true nature; bandha = bondage (limited knowledge);
mātṛkā = the Mother (of letters); mantra = liberating formula (not spell);
vīra = master of senses (not hero); vismaya = wondrous absorption;
turya = the Fourth; pratyabhijñā = recognition; svātantrya = freedom;
vimarśa = reflexive awareness; prakāśa = manifestation.

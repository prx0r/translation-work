# Transmission brief — Vātūlanāthasūtra (Krama) — RESEARCHED 2026-11-11

*Scholarship: Dyczkowski's complete translation + Anantaśaktipāda's
commentary (Anuttara Trikakula, 2019); Wallis (Hareesh.org) on the Krama
oral-teaching corpus. Muktabodha M00099, 28 verses in our witness.*

## A. WHAT IT IS

The "Sūtras of the Intoxicated Lord" (Vātūla = mad/wild) — a **Krama**
scripture presented as the **oral teachings of the Yoginīs**, descending
from **Niṣkriyānanda** (earliest phase of the independent Krama school;
here said to have learned from a certain **Gandhamadana**). Commentator:
**Anantaśaktipāda**. No date can be fixed for sūtras or commentator.
One of the three surviving Yoginī oral-teaching texts (with the
Kaula-sūtras and the Chummā-saṅketa-prakāśa).

## B. THE DOCTRINE — mahāsāhasa

The acme of practice is **MAHĀSĀHASA, "the Great Audacity"** — a sudden,
violent irruption of expanding consuming pure consciousness; the term
appears only ONCE in all of Abhinavagupta (TĀ 5/83cd–86ab, quoted by
Dyczkowski). Enlightenment comes suddenly through the most intense
descent of grace: the senses are divinized, operate freely, then dissolve
into the Void of consciousness free of thought-constructs — "as profoundly
tranquil as it is immensely powerful."

**Structure of the first seven aphorisms (Dyczkowski):** introverted
transcendence by **penetrating the centre between the opposites**
(subject/object). The opening of that centre is the essential feature of
the instant of insight; expanding into the Self, it consumes all outer
objectivity into the Great Void where the energies fuse into the Great
Oneness of aesthetic delight.

## C. MOVES TO LOOK FOR

1. **Centre-between-opposites.** Every early sūtra works this joint —
   gloss the pair being transcended (subject/object, krama/akrama,
   being/non-being, real/unreal, plurality/unity, conceptual/nonconceptual,
   saṃsāra/mokṣa).
2. **Suddenness.** No gradual path language: the descriptions are of an
   irruption, not a ladder.
3. **Yoginī authority.** The text claims oral transmission from the
   Yoginīs; that claim is itself doctrine, not decoration.
4. **Aesthetic delight (camatkāra-family).** The fused energies' oneness
   is described as rasa/ānanda — keep the affective register.

## D. TRAPS

1. **mahāsāhasa ≠ "great effort"/"audacity" in a moral sense.** It is a
   technical name for the sudden irruption of consciousness. Never gloss
   as courage, daring, or recklessness.
2. **Krama ≠ Trika.** Do not import Trika terminology (spanda, pratyabhijñā).
   The Krama's own vocabulary is different (krama/akrama, Mahānaya, Kālī
   cycles).
3. **Anantaśaktipāda is commentary, not source** — mark his expansions.
4. **Vātūla = mad/intoxicated (divine madness),** not "crazy" pejoratively.
5. **Very short sūtra style** — each aphorism is a dense instruction; do
   not pad with commentary content in L2.

## E. APPROACH

28 verses; single 24-verse window + 4-verse tail; tight glosses; argmaps
one-claim-per-sūtra. Minimal gold first.

**Ledger (LOCK):** vātūla = intoxicated/mad (divine); mahāsāhasa = the
Great Audacity (sudden irruption of consciousness); krama/akrama =
process/immediacy (the pair transcended); yoginī = the revealing
authority; antaḥ-kendra = the centre between opposites.

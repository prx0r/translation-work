# Transmission brief — Yoginīhṛdaya (Śrīvidyā) — RESEARCHED 2026-11-11
*Sources: Padoux (French, Collège de France fasc. 63, 1994); Padoux &
Jeanty (English, OUP 2013); Amṛtānanda's Dīpikā; Bhāskararāya's Setubandha.*

## A. FACTS
- **3 paṭalas, ~375 stanzas**, 12th century. Dialogue: **the Goddess asks,
  Bhairava answers** — she asks for what was NOT said "in this
  Vāmakeśvaratantra".
- It is the **esoteric complement** to the **Vāmakeśvarīmata** (5 chapters,
  outer ritual); together the 8 chapters are often called the
  Vāmakeśvaratantra, **Nityāṣoḍaśikārṇava** (NSA), or Catuḥṣaṣṭiśāstra.
- Commentary: **Amṛtānanda's Dīpikā** ("the Lamp"); Bhāskararāya's
  **Setubandha** (c. 1700) covers all 8 chapters as one work.
- Deity **Tripurasundarī**; diagram **śrīcakra**; mantra the
  **sixteen-syllable śrīvidyā**.

## B. MOVES
1. **Outer vs inner**: VMT = outer worship; YH = metaphysical/devotional
   even in its ritual chapter. Keep that distinction in argmaps.
2. **śrīcakra** = the Goddess united with Bhairava, creative/destructive.
3. **Mantra science**: 16 syllables; kūṭa structure (Vāgbhava/Kāmarāja/Śakti).
4. Liberation-in-life WITH powers (siddhis) — not a purely interior goal.

## C. TRAPS
- **Do not read YH as Kashmir Śaiva** despite the shared vocabulary; it is
  Śrīvidyā (Padoux stresses this).
- Dīpikā is commentary; mark its expansions.
- Chapter numbering differs between the 3-chapter YH and the 8-chapter
  composite — always state which scheme.

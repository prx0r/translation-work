## Moves
1. Presents the tattvādhvā (path of categories) within the sixfold adhvā scheme, enumerating the 36 tattvas from Śiva down to pṛthivī
2. Analyzes cause/effect (kāraṇa/kārya) relations among the tattvas, distinguishing levels of manifestation
3. Treats succession (krama) in the emanation/involution of categories, not as mere temporal sequence
4. Situates the 36-tattva scheme against Sāṃkhya's 25 and other Śaiva enumerations
5. Operates within the worldview block (ch.1–13), before the ritual/initiation block (ch.14–37)

## Traps
- Tattvādhvā vs. the other five adhvans (mantrādhvā, kalādhvā, padādhvā, varṇādhvā, bhuvanādhvā) — do not conflate paths
- 36 tattvas vs. 25 Sāṃkhya tattvas — different enumerations, different ontological commitments
- Cause/effect (kāraṇa/kārya) vs. mere temporal succession (krama) — krama here is ontological ordering
- Śiva-tattva vs. Sadāśiva/Īśvara/Sadvidyā — adjacent high tattvas easily blurred
- Print/edition variants between Dyczkowski (with Jayaratha's Viveka), Gnoli, and older editions; verse numbering may shift
- Lakshmanjoo's oral teaching is a horizon, not a citable source for the text's claims

## The parable (≈89s)

> A girl asks the question the whole garden rests on: what is the
> Unsurpassable — the one that fruits at once, and by mere knowing
> brings evenness like sky? Secret of secrets, she says: the
> heart-dwelling Power, mistress of the clan. And the Lord, whose
> heart is kept even from yogins and Rudra — where the swift
> release lives — answers the way fruit answers: at once. One
> moment's attention — the kiss at the navel's seal — and the
> hosts of spells are bound; the sign comes face to face. By this
> mere known, all the powers around know it too: the commonest
> power, without any yoga's ground, without rule or rite. So he
> turns to the worship's art only to honor it — time-fire first,
> the seat prepared: flowers in order, fourteen petals over the
> flower-seat, the casket, the garland of twenty-seven names. The
> hero worships forthgoing, then the seat anew. Initiation without
> oblation: the tree was wholly in the seed.

## The seal — statement

**The garden where the fruit arrives before the tree:** base:
orchard-beds with stakes, ties and furrows all perfectly prepared
— and NO TREES (the rule-wise garden: rite-blind rule-keeping made
literal); over the bare beds hang ripe golden fruits in mid-air
(the impossible: fruit before tree); the girl mid-reach, and the
fruit already turning toward her hand (at-once, caught as it
answers); at her middle a small gold seal-disc (the navel-kiss
that binds); beside her a plain brown sparrow takes fruit from the
air with no rite at all (the commonest power without yoga's
ground); crown: the prepared flower-seat — flowers in order, a
fourteen-petal rosette at its heart, a small casket, a twenty-
seven-petal wreath behind (the honor-ritual, complete).

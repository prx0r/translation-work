# Rahasyapanchadashika rpa-p02 — L2 READ

## v.9
In the body of skin, blood, flesh, fat, marrow, bone — ever sick: Māyā, you plunge the knowing and the unknowing, unknowing of your greatness.
The Sanskrit: *tvagrudhiramāṃsamedomajjāsthimaye sadāmaye kāye | māye majjayasi tvaṃ māhātmyaṃ te janānajānānān*

## v.10
Gods set up in metal-drawn images — seeing them, 'hā hā alas' say the unfulfilled. For body-dwellers proud with body-I-ness — what delusion does Māyā not birth?
The Sanskrit: *lohālekhyasthāpitān vīkṣya devān | hā hā hantetyāhureke'kṛtārthāḥ | dehāhantāśālināṃ dehabhājāṃ | mohāveśaṃ kaṃ na māyā prasūte*

## v.11
From all that is born of delusion-play-arisen voided-intellect, body-and-all I-ness — protect me from falling, supreme goddess, whose form is self-awareness born of effort.
The Sanskrit: *māyāvilāsoditabuddhiśūnyakāyādyahantājanitādaśeṣāt | āyāsakādātmavimarśarūpāt pāyādapāyāt paradevatā mām*

## v.12
Terrible-natured, most terrible, non-terrible; called higher and lower, neither lower nor higher. Seeing Śiva's manifold power, I abide in wonder.
The Sanskrit: *ghorātmikāṃ ghoratamāmaghorāṃ parāparākhyāmaparāṃ parāṃ ca | vicitrarūpāṃ śivayorvibhūtiṃ vilokayan vismayamāna āste*

## v.13
Dwelling in the midst of the rays of the higher and the lower — no insistence of mine on judgments of being, nonbeing, being-nonbeing.
The Sanskrit: *parāparāparāparāmarīcimadhyavartino | na me'bhidābhidābhidābhidāsu kaścidāgrahaḥ*

## v.14
Your unsurpassed form flashes, and the other world-made one, O mother. Remembering both — to the good, fear-giving, boon-giving, supreme goddess.
The Sanskrit: *sphurati yattava rūpamanuttaraṃ yadaparaṃ ca jaganmayamambike | ubhayametadanusmaratāṃ satāmabhayade varade paradevate*

## v.15
Lady of the five acts, supreme-knowing-formed Pārvatī, be gracious. Lift the fallen beast-fettered one; soothe the child with cool glances.
The Sanskrit: *parameśvari pañcakṛtyalīle parasaṃvinmayi pārvati prasīda | patitaṃ paśupāśamuddharemaṃ śiśumāśvāsaya śītalaiḥ kaṭākṣeḥ*

## v.16
Having bowed to perfected gurus, gods, goddess, then yogins — by Abhinavagupta these fifteen verses were uttered.
The Sanskrit: *pūrvasiddhān gurūn devān devīṃ natvātha yoginaḥ | ime'bhinavaguptena ślokāḥ pañcadaśeritāḥ*

## The parable (canonical draft, ≈87s)

> Before first light she keeps her order: surrender, stillness,
> restraint, the twilight rite — two watches of low muttering, the
> night's remainder left as Śiva's. And her body is spelled out:
> her face the first sound, her hands the second, her feet the
> third, her heart another — every limb a syllable: walk those and
> you walk her. Without her nothing is done, known or wanted; wishes
> come off her like fruit off the wishing-tree. She opens the
> night-lotus like moonlight. But in bodies — skin, blood, flesh,
> fat, marrow, bone — she plays the hard nurse: knowing and
> unknowing plunged alike for not knowing how great she is; men
> honor gods set in metal while the living ones walk in blood, and
> are proud of it: what delusion is not born of body-I-ness? Then
> the cool glance: she lifts the fallen, fettered, small one, and
> soothes him. Fifteen verses; the bow belongs to Abhinavagupta.

## The seal — statement

**The night that spells her and lifts the fallen:** base: the four
rite-hours as a ladder of quiet lights ascending through the indigo
night (surrender, stillness, restraint, twilight — two-watch glow
— then remainder-dark); center: the MOTHER at the night-lotus
opening like moonlight, each of her limbs carrying one small
bright point (face, hands, feet, sides, heart — the syllables as
sparks, never glyphs), a wishing-tree heavy with fruit at her side;
at the lower rim a dark shelf of small METAL gods (iron, cold,
unlived) beneath her living light (the irony made as contrast);
edge: a small hunched fettered figure — child-sized — being LIFTED
by her cool silver-blue glance-beam (the prayer answered as the
lift); crown: first gold of dawn (the order completed → the
remainder was Śiva's → day).

## Composition laws (this seal)

ONE subject (the mother; fallen-child = small rim figure, lifted not

and small (irony by placement, not by sneer). The lift-beam comes
from her eyes (glance = beam, the text's "cool glances" literal).
Dawn-crown tiny (the close, not the spectacle).

## PLAIN-PASS v2 (supersedes the parable + seal-statement above — one-line law)
**Name: "the morning alphabet."** One-line test: *"before daylight
she does her morning in the right order, she IS the alphabet — and
when the kid falls down she picks him up."*
> Before daylight she does her morning in the right order: give
> in, be still, hold back, wait for the half-light — two long
> quiet watches of whispering to him, and whatever's left of the
> night she leaves for Śiva. And her body is the alphabet: her
> face is the first letter, her hands another, her feet another,
> her heart another — the sounds live right on her. Nothing
> happens, nothing is known, nothing is wanted without her;
> wishes come off her like fruit off a wishing-tree. She opens the
> night the way moonlight opens a flower. But people get it
> backward: they set gods in iron on a shelf and bow, while the
> living one walks in their own blood and bone — and are proud of
> it. That's how lost you can get. Then the cool look: she picks
> up the fallen little one and soothes him. Fifteen verses.
> Abhinavagupta bows out.

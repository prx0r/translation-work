# Saktavijnana sakt-p01 (the thirteen named) — L2 READ

## v.1
Place, entry, form, and mark, and sign indeed; raising, awakening, and wheel-rest indeed — and
The Sanskrit: *sthānaṃ praveśo rūpaṃ ca lakṣaṃ lakṣaṇameva ca / utthāpanaṃ bodhanaṃ ca cakraviśrāmameva ca*

## v.2
the arrival at the stage, and the final state indeed; rest, transformation, and the return indeed — and
The Sanskrit: *bhūmikāgamanaṃ caiva antāvasthā tathaiva ca / viśrāmaḥ pariṇāmaśca tathāgamanameva ca*

## v.3
So the doctrine is thirteenfold: the supreme Śākta wisdom, pointed out in every Trika treatise by Śambhu himself.
The Sanskrit: *iti trayodaśavidhaṃ śāktaṃ vijñānamuttamam / sarveṣu trikaśāstreṣu sūcitaṃ śambhunā svayam*

## v.4
Five fingers below the navel, two fingers above the member — between them lies the place called the bulb, remembered as the station of the wheel.
The Sanskrit: *nābhyadho 'ṅgulāḥ pañca meḍhrasyordhvāṅguladvayam / tanmadhye kandanāmā ca cakrasthānamiti smṛtam*

## v.5
Hold the breath in and out, and cast the mind right there; master the currents of wind completely, and lead them until they enter the middle.
The Sanskrit: *prāṇāpānanirodhena manastatraiva niḥkṣipet / samyag vāyugatiṃ jitvā yāvan madhyagatāṃ nayet*

## v.6
This — they say — is entry. Now I will tell the form: a wheel like a horned pond, six-spoked, and steady beyond.
The Sanskrit: *eṣa praveśa ity āhū rūpaṃ vakṣyāmi cādhunā / śṛṅgāṭakanibhaṃ cakraṃ ṣaḍaraṃ cāparaṃ dhruvam*

## v.7
The bulb glows like a pomegranate blossom, red by its very nature; this form is declared the third — it consists of contemplation.
The Sanskrit: *dāḍimīkusumaprakhyaṃ kandaṃ vai jātilohitam / etadrūpaṃ samākhyātaṃ tṛtīyaṃ cintanātmakam*

## v.8
Set the mind in that middle until it stands steady there; when the wind's hold is released, point out the mark.
The Sanskrit: *tanmadhye nikṣipec cittaṃ yāvat tatra sthirīkṛtam / tyaktaruddho yadā vāyustadā lakṣaṃ vinirdiśet*

## v.9
In the middle of the bulb-wheel stands a digit of the unstruck sound, ringed with lines above and below, coiled like a serpent.
The Sanskrit: *kandacakrasya madhyasthā tv anāhatamayī kalā / adhaūrdhve rekhāsaṃyuktā bhujaṃgakuṭilākṛtiḥ*

## v.10
Above and below abides her state — sun and moon both; truly she shines, bright as a thousand suns.
The Sanskrit: *ūrdhvādho 'vasthitāvasthā sūryācandramasāv ubhau / satyaṃ virājamānā sā sahasrārkasamaprabhā*

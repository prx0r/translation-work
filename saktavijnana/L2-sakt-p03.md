# Saktavijnana sakt-p03 (rest and return) — L2 READ

## v.21
Whatever changes the state brews in a man — in none of them is there anything to fear; the Supreme Goddess is at play.
The Sanskrit: *yāni yāni vikārāṇi avasthā kurute sataḥ / teṣu teṣu na bhetavyaṃ krīḍati parameśvarī*

## v.22
Drunk on nectar, she brews change upon change — the triple-stain ferment of what was done through many births.
The Sanskrit: *amṛte seyam unmattā vikārān kurute bahūn / malatrayavikārau bahujanmasu yatkṛtam*

## v.23
She shakes the stained souls loose from the snare, risen from supreme power; this is the arrival at the stage — and the final state is told.
The Sanskrit: *dhunoti samalān pāśāt paraśaktisamutthitān / bhūmikāgamanaṃ proktam antāvasthā tathocyate*

## v.24
When the crossing comes: hair rising, tears falling, yawning breaking, speech faltering from voices within; knots bursting, the divine shiver of touch, drops throbbing out from the navel bulb.
The Sanskrit: *yat saṃkrāntau romaharṣo 'srupāto jṛmbhārambho gadgadā gīr giro 'ntaḥ / granthisphoṭaḥ sparśadivyapraharṣo binduspandā nābhikandāt sphuranti*

## v.25
That is the final state declared. And now rest is taught: when śakti wakes and pours from the navel wheel.
The Sanskrit: *antāvasthā samākhyātā viśrāmas tv adhunocyate / nābhicakraviniryātā yadā śaktiḥ prabudhyate*

## v.26
Then the whole village of the senses sets and stands without; when she, the supreme power, dissolves into the supreme place.
The Sanskrit: *tadā tv astamitaṃ sarvamakṣagrāmaṃ bahiḥ sthitam / yadā sā paramā śaktiḥ sulīnā parame pade*

## v.27
Then the knower finds nothing — no other thing to know; śakti rests in Śiva — this is called rest.
The Sanskrit: *tadā na vindate kiṃcid viṣayī viṣayāntaram / śive viśrāmyate śaktistadā viśrāma ucyate*

## v.28
Where her resting lies, let the mind go there into dissolution; then the self is known in supreme selfhood by those made firm.
The Sanskrit: *yatra viśramaṇaṃ śaktermanastatra layaṃ vrajet / tadātmā paramātmatve jñātavyo niścitātmabhiḥ*

## v.29
Become Śiva — the self turns Śiva; that indeed is the turning. And he rains forever the divine nectar that is the life of creatures.
The Sanskrit: *śivībhūto bhavatyātmā pariṇāmaḥ sa eva hi / sadā sa varṣate divyam amṛtaṃ jantujīvanam*

## v.30
Hold the mind there — and the goddess enters again; then the return is proclaimed: thirteen in full.
The Sanskrit: *cittaṃ tatra tu saṃdhārya punardaivī viśettu sā / tadā tv āgamanaṃ proktamevaṃ samyak trayodaśa*

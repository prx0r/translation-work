## The parable (≈89s)

> There was a serpent coiled at the base of a staff — red as
> pomegranate, burning by nature — asleep: five fingers below the
> navel, two above the root; that is the count. The one who tends
> her binds the breath, stills the winds, sets the mind till it
> stands clear, and speaks the seed: she wakes. Straight as a
> staff she climbs, blazing as a thousand suns — wheel through
> wheel: the bulb, the navel, the heart's grandsire, the throat,
> the brow, the crown-door — until she reaches the shore that
> rests on nothing. On the way the body reports: the heart
> trembles, the palate, the head spins, the limbs whirl — and hair
> lifts, tears fall, yawns begin, the knots burst, drops throb at
> the navel-shrine. Whatever brews there, fear none of it: the
> Goddess is at play, drunk on nectar, brewing change on change,
> shaking the old stained harvest off the snare. Then the senses
> set outside like a herd at dusk; the knower finds nothing left
> to know; she rests wheel-in-wheel; and from the navel-ring she
> pours — bright nectar for every creature. Thirteen, and no more.

**Limit:** the body-signs belong to the CLIMB (the person feels
the serpent — never mocked, never diagnostic: "the crossing-state"
plain); fear-none is the Goddess-at-play, kept playful not
brave.
**Rasa:** adbhuta-vīra (wonder-braced resolve: the waking) →
adbhuta-edged-with-bhayānaka (the climb's thrill) → śānta (the
setting-outside and the rest). The parable's pulse: sleep → climb
→ play → pour → rest.

## The seal — statement

**The serpent climbs the staff and pours from the top:** base: the
red pomegranate-bulb with the serpent asleep, coiled around the
staff's foot; the staff runs straight up carrying SIX knots
(the stations: bulb, navel, heart, throat, brow, crown — countable,
letterless); mid-staff: the serpent AWAKE and climbing, motion-
lines trailing; at the staff's foot a small seated figure whose
hair lifts, tears fall, yawns open — the body reporting the climb;
crown: serpent coiled at the top, pouring gold-white nectar DOWN
in rain over small figures at the rim (every creature's need);
far rim: a herd settling at dusk (the senses setting outside).

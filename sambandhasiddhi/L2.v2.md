## samb-p01 — What "relation" means, and the retorsion (vv. 1–9, prose pp. 2–10)

**The benediction (v. 1).** The whole treatise is announced before it begins: to him whose self is relation as unity-in-diversity — the accomplisher of all objects, whose work is world-transaction — him, Śiva, of his own will, we praise. *Svecchayā*, "of his own will," is the seed of *svātantrya*: relation is possible only because the Lord is spontaneous. The world runs on transaction, and transaction runs on relation; the Lord's will is what makes both possible.

**The opening verse (v. 2).** Victory to the one light, the world-self, whose body dawns as relation through being-and-difference — *bhāvabhedādisaṃbandhamayena vapuṣonmiṣan jayaty eko 'pi viśvātmā prakāśaḥ parameśvaraḥ*. The Lord's body (vapus) does not merely carry relation the way a pot carries water; it *dawns as* relation. Relation is not an accident of things but the very form of the Lord's shining.

**The program (p. 2).** *Iha bhāvānāṃ saṃbandho vicāryate* — here the relation of beings is examined. But first, what does the word "relation" even mean? *Saṃsargaḥ saṃparkaḥ saṃśleṣaḥ saṃbandha ityapyukte na vivṛtaḥ saṃbandhārthaḥ pratīyate* — say "conjunction, contact, fusion, relation" and nothing is understood. The sense does not rise from the parts, any more than the caste-name "oil-drinker" (*tailapāyika*) tells you what the man does. It is mere convention (*saṃketamātra*). So the synonyms are parked, and the inquiry begins in earnest.

**Reduction one: nairantarya fails (p. 2).** Is fusion just uninterruptedness? Then the distant father and son would have no relation at all, while two adjacent iron bars — even pervading substances — would count. Uninterruptedness is neither necessary nor sufficient.

**Reduction two: dviṣṭhatā fails (pp. 2–3).** Is relation "residing-in-two"? Then duality and mutual separateness would be relations too. Worse, "residing-in-two" is said *of the related pair*, not of the relation. The demand is: state the relation plainly in its own form (*svarūpeṇaiva spaṣṭam ucyatām*).

**Reduction three: apekṣā and pāratantrya need consciousness (p. 3).** Is relation "awaiting" or "dependence"? Not for inert things. The sprout "awaits" the seed only by metaphor (*upacāro hy ayam*). Awaiting, longing for the other's disposal, checking one's own will — these are determinations of will, and will is consciousness's. What lacks a self (*alabdhātmanaḥ*) cannot await anything. Dependence-language smuggles in exactly the conscious relatum the Buddhist denies. Hence: state the primary relation outright, without throat-clearing (*muktakaṇṭham eva mukhyaḥ saṃbandho 'bhidhātavyaḥ*).

**The positive thesis (pp. 3–4).** *Sa ca saṃśleṣārtho 'nekasyaikatā kathyate, na tv anekataiva nāpi ekataiva, api tu ubhayāvasthāpekṣo 'yam arthaḥ saṃbandhaḥ* — fusion means the unity of the many: neither manyness alone nor unity alone, but what requires both states. No one relation for many (*na bahūnām ekaḥ saṃbandhaḥ*); one relation per pair (*pratiyugalam evaikaḥ saṃbandhaḥ*). The inventory: father-son, king-man, tree-branch, blue-lotus, Devadatta cooking rice in a pot with sticks, absence of a pot, this-other-than-that — and reversed. The framing question: is this mere seeming, or does the real show itself thus?

**The Buddhist position (p. 4).** The opponent speaks in his own voice: things are complete in themselves, cut off from others; perception shines the self; thought is fabrication (*kalpanā kalpitā eva sā*). Proof-text: *saṃyujyante na bhidyante svato 'rthāḥ pāramārthikāḥ* — ultimately things are neither joined nor divided. Unity-in-multiplicity would be contradiction. The santāna model shows aggregation without real unity. So "the king's man" is usage for worldly business, admitted only at seeming-time, a delusion (*bhrama*) persisting like the snake-rope because it clings to the useful-shining — hence dignified as *saṃvṛti*.

**The retorsion (pp. 5–6).** *Tatrocyate: pratītis tāvad anekaikarūpatāyāṃ … bhavadbhir apy abhyupagataiva* — you yourselves admitted the seeming-of-many-as-one, in your own verse. Thought-formedness is no flaw: "this is a pot" is thought too. Where is it established that perception shines pot-form but not king's-man-form? The perceptual shining must be admitted here too — your own "clinging to the thing's shining" concedes it. Error is always *of* a shining; a transaction-guiding seeming must *follow* the shining. And *na ca vikalpo 'rthapratibhāsād bhinnaḥ* — thought is not other than the thing's shining (refutation of thoughtlessness, ĪPK). Therefore: admit the shining itself as many-as-one-manifestation. It holds, for the ultimately real thing is consciousness-only, world-selved through Māyā-śakti.

The engine is identity of the action with its self-consciousness: relation is not a category added to content but the reflexive grasping (*vimarśa*) that makes content determinate at all. Dharmakīrti's nominalism makes the general form of relation an inference; Utpala shows it is the *condition* of inference. (Berger 2024; MacCracken 2022.)

**Consequences (pp. 6–7).** One relation in form, many in coloring. In world-selfhood: full unity, not relation-talk; relation-talk belongs to the Māyā-state. Helper and helped must be stated, else no grasping-together for taking-and-leaving → transaction ceases (*vyavahāralopaḥ*). And the helping is the consciousness-Lord's will so ordaining (*cinmayeśvarecchāiva sā*): two successive things mounted in one cognition shone together in one reflection — *ekaparāmarśāt*. *Iti na kācit khaṇḍanā* — no refutation touches this.

## samb-p02 — The machinery: restraint, one-reflection, qualifier/qualified (vv. 10–18)

**Restraint (v. 10).** *Vyāpāro 'nena ruddho 'sau na vikalpāntaraṃ spṛśet* — by this, activity restrained, it does not touch another thought, for touching another thought would entail knower-difference. Relation operates by *holding* the manifold from scattering, not by gluing alien bits. This restraint is the mode of intentional action (*śakti*): not a concept but the reflexive grasping that keeps content determinate without collapsing into identity or difference.

**One-reflection, one-word-superimposition (v. 11).** *Tadekāmarśavarty ekaśabdādhyāsavaśād brajet | aneko 'py ekatām artho yathā ghaṭapaṭāv iti* — resting in that one-reflection, by force of superimposing one word, the many go to oneness of meaning, as "pot and cloth." The dvandva is the proof: many things, one word, one meaning — because one reflection.

**Qualifier for help (vv. 12–13).** *Saṃbandha upakārārthaḥ sa jñātum upayujyate* — relation is for the sake of helping; worth knowing. Even in duality there is two-ness of qualifier and qualified: as the king's horses-chariots-soldiers rest on two reflections. Then the exhibit: one reflection toward the king, facing his own generality; another toward the man, bearing king-generality-relation. *Rājñaḥ puruṣaḥ* comes *after* — it is relation-ranged.

**The genitive's seat (vv. 14–15).** The genitive-voiced relation sits *in the qualifier* that has attained qualified-oneness, without abandoning its own form. Loss of one reading, purity of the principal, joining of other with other.

**Transfer across principals (vv. 16–18).** As the king's man may be the brahmin's too, so oneness with another principal — or with a further one — is called the subordinate's relation: as the king's horse, the king's man. Relation rides the subordinate term across principals; the pair, not the substance, carries it.

The payoff: relation exists *in order to help* knowing — carry that into C1.

## samb-p03 — The seal: Māyā-knowers, transaction, self-awareness (vv. 19–21)

**Established by relation's course (v. 19).** *Iti saṃbandhagatyā uktā māyīyajñātṛniṣṭhitāḥ | dhiyo vibhinnārthadṛśo vyavahārapravartikāḥ* — thus, by relation's course, are stated the Māyā-knower-established intellects of diverse-thing-view that drive transaction. The whole preceding machinery lands here: relation's *course* is what establishes finite knowers and their business.

**Not simply deluded (v. 20a).** *Na paraṃ tās tathā bhrāntāḥ sarvā api pratikṣaṇāt* — yet they are not simply deluded, all of them, at every moment. Against the opponent's *bhrama/saṃvṛti* verdict: error-status is not the whole story.

**Grounded in self-awareness (v. 20b).** *Svasaṃvitsaṃjñakānantacidvimarśapratiṣṭhitāḥ* — grounded in the infinite consciousness-reflection called self-awareness. The ground of transaction-intellects is not correspondence with cut-off things but rootedness in infinite *vimarśa*. *Vimarśa* is consciousness not merely reflecting but actively grasping itself — "apprehends or grasps that manifestation actively and spontaneously" (Ratié). The Pratyabhijñā philosophers deliberately appropriate Buddhist concepts rather than unwittingly borrow them: they take full responsibility for the move. The closing *pūrṇaḥ parikaraḥ* names this: relation, self-awareness-accomplished, is the whole philosophical apparatus the Recognition brings.

**The seal (v. 21).** *Uktaḥ svasaṃvitsiddhaḥ … saṃbandhaḥ … nirbādha upayogavān | … pūrṇaḥ parikaraḥ … pratyabhijñāyām* — stated: the self-awareness-accomplished relation — unobstructed, useful — the full retinue in the Recognition. *Nirbādha* (no cognition sublates it) + *upayogavān* (it does work) = the two marks of the real, earned back from the Buddhist's own criteria.

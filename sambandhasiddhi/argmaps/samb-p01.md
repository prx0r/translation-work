# Sambandhasiddhi p01 — ARGUMENT MAP (the opening debate: what "relation" means, and the retorsion)

*2026-10-31. Reconstruct argument-by-argument before prose. Source: T1 samb-p01 = edition pp. 2–10 (benedictory verse + prose pūrvapakṣa–uttarapakṣa + closing kārikās quoted in full). Lore: Dharmakīrti's Sambandhaparīkṣā as target, Bhartṛhari's Sambandhasamuddeśa as resource (MacCracken 2017/2022; Berger 2023/2024).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Whether *sambandha* names anything real. The Buddhist says no: things are unmixed in themselves; thought joins them (*amiśrāḥ svayaṃ bhāvās tān yojayati kalpanā* — quoted verbatim in this very passage). Utpala must show (a) that every proposed *reduction* of relation fails, (b) that the Buddhist's own position presupposes what it denies, and (c) that relation is a real subtle power (*śakti*) whose form is unity-in-diversity. The benediction already gives the game away: the Lord's *body* (vapus) shines forth *as* relation.

The benediction (v. 1) states the conclusion before the argument:
> *bhedābhedātmasaṃbandhasahasarvārthasādhitaḥ | lokayātrā kṛtir yasya svecchayā taṃ stumaḥ śivam*
[i.e. to him whose self is relation as unity-in-diversity, the accomplisher of all objects, whose work is world-transaction — him, Śiva, of his own will, we praise.] *Svecchayā* (of his own will) is the seed of *svātantrya*: relation is possible only because the Lord is spontaneous.

Berger (2024): Utpala's is a *pragmatic argument*. Dharmakīrti's mistake is treating the general form of relation as an *inferential concept* rather than the mode of intentional action (*śakti*) manifest in reflexive judgment (*vimarśa*). Relation is "transcendental" in a broadly Kantian sense: the unity of the action is not anything other than the self-consciousness of that unity. MacCracken (2022): a "retorsive transcendental argument" — turn the Buddhist's admissions inside out until the denied relation reappears as the condition of his own argument. The stakes: if relations fall, transaction, scripture, and recognition fall with them.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — benediction and program (p. 2):**
> *bhāvabhedādisaṃbandhamayena vapuṣonmiṣan | jayaty eko 'pi viśvātmā prakāśaḥ parameśvaraḥ*
[i.e. victory to the one light, world-self, whose body dawns as relation through being-and-difference.] Then: *iha bhāvānāṃ saṃbandho vicāryate* — the relation of beings is examined here.

**Step 1 — the synonyms explain nothing (p. 2):**
> *kastāvat saṃbandhaśabdārthaḥ? saṃsargaḥ saṃparkaḥ saṃśleṣaḥ saṃbandha ityapyukte na vivṛtaḥ saṃbandhārthaḥ pratīyate*
[i.e. say "conjunction, contact, fusion, relation" and nothing is understood.] The sense does not rise from the parts (*avayavārthānurodhāt*); like the caste-name "oil-drinker" (*tailapāyika*), it is *saṃketamātra* — mere convention. [So: park the synonyms; begin.]

**Step 2 — nairantarya fails (p. 2):**
> *kiṃ nairantaryaṃ saṃśleṣa utānyat kiṃcit?*
Is fusion just uninterruptedness? *dūrasthayoḥ pitāputrayoḥ sa na syāt* — the distant father and son would have none; *ayaḥśalākayoḥ saṃnikṛṣṭayor vibhudravyayor api ca syāt* — adjacent iron bars, even pervaders, would count. [Neither necessary nor sufficient.]

**Step 3 — dviṣṭhatā fails (pp. 2–3):**
> *atha dviṣṭhaḥ padārthaḥ kaścit saṃbandhaḥ?*
Is relation "residing-in-two"? *tatrāpi dviṣṭhataiva saṃbandhatā cet tad dvitvadvipṛthaktvāder api sā syāt* — then duality and mutual-separateness would be relations too. And "residing-in-two" is said *of the related pair* (*saṃbaddhayor eva saṃbhavatīti dviṣṭha ucyate*), not of the relation. Demand: *sa tarhi svarūpeṇaiva spaṣṭam ucyatāṃ saṃbandhaḥ* — state it plainly in its own form.

**Step 4 — apekṣā/pāratantrya need consciousness (p. 3):**
> *na hi jaḍayoḥ saṃbandhinor apekṣārtho ghaṭate pāratantryārtho vā*
Awaiting and dependence don't fit inert relata. The sprout "awaits" the seed? *upacāro hy ayam* — metaphor. Awaiting, longing-for-the-other's-disposal, checking one's own will (*svecchācaraṇanirodhalakṣaṇaḥ saṃkalpaviśeṣa*) are determinations of will — and will is consciousness's. What lacks a self (*alabdhātmanaḥ*) cannot await. [So dependence-language smuggles in exactly the conscious relatum the Buddhist denies.] Hence: *muktakaṇṭham eva mukhyaḥ saṃbandho 'bhidhātavyaḥ* — state the primary relation outright.

**Step 5 — positive thesis: unity of the many (pp. 3–4):**
> *sa ca saṃśleṣārtho 'nekasyaikatā kathyate, na tv anekataiva nāpi ekataiva, api tu ubhayāvasthāpekṣo 'yam arthaḥ saṃbandhaḥ*
[i.e. fusion = the unity of the many; neither manyness nor unity alone, but what requires both states.] Corollaries: no one relation for many (*na bahūnām ekaḥ saṃbandhaḥ*); one relation per pair (*pratiyugalam evaikaḥ saṃbandhaḥ*); *saṃ* + *bandh* says it: many brought to abide as one. Inventory: *pituḥ putraḥ, rājñaḥ puruṣaḥ, vṛkṣasya śākhā, nīlam utpalam, Devadattaḥ kāṣṭhaiḥ sthālyām odanaṃ pacati, ghaṭasyābhāvaḥ, asmad idam anyat* — and reversed. Framing question: *kim idaṃ pratītimātram uta vastv eva evaṃbhūtaṃ pratīyate?*

**Objection (the Buddhist, in his own voice, p. 4):**
The thing is complete in itself, cut off from others; perception shines the self; *kalpanā kalpitā eva sā*. Proof-text: *saṃyujyante na bhidyante svato 'rthāḥ pāramārthikāḥ*. Unity-in-multiplicity would be contradiction. The santāna model shows aggregation without real unity. So "the king's man" is usage for worldly business (*sāṃsārikavyavahāranirvartanārtham*), admitted only at seeming-time, a *bhrama* persisting like snake-rope because it clings to the useful-shining — hence dignified as *saṃvṛti*.

**Reply — the retorsion (pp. 5–6):**
> *tatrocyate: pratītis tāvad anekaikarūpatāyāṃ … bhavadbhir apy abhyupagataiva*
You yourselves admitted the seeming-of-many-as-one, in your own verse. *kalpanārūpatvaṃ ca nāsyā doṣaḥ* — thought-formedness is no flaw: "this is a pot" is thought too. Where established that perception shines pot-form but not king's-man-form? The perceptual shining must be admitted here too — your own "clinging to the thing's shining" concedes it. Error is always *of* a shining; a transaction-guiding seeming must *follow* the shining. And: *na ca vikalpo 'rthapratibhāsād bhinnaḥ* — thought is not other than the thing's shining (refutation of thoughtlessness, ĪPK). Therefore: *avabhāsa evānekasyaikātmatāprathārūpo 'bhyupagantavyaḥ* — admit the shining itself as many-as-one-manifestation. Only question left: whether it holds — and it does, for the ultimately real thing is consciousness-only, world-selved through Māyā-śakti (ĪPK doctrine, cited).

Sharpening (Berger 2024 / MacCracken 2022): the retorsion's engine is *identity of the action with its self-consciousness* — relation is not a category added to content but the reflexive grasping (*vimarśa*) that makes content determinate at all. Dharmakīrti's nominalism makes the general form of relation an *inference*; Utpala shows it is the *condition* of inference. The "poor man's festival" (*daridreṇeva utsavakāriṇā*, p. 5) is Utpala's characteristic wry tone; the reply *pūritā ca* (and it is filled) completes his own view via *kṣaṇabhaṅge'pi* — read here as self-completion.

**Consequences (pp. 6–7):**
One relation in form, many in coloring (*rājapuruṣoparaktaikatā* vs *pitāputroparāga*). In world-selfhood: full unity, not relation-talk; relation-talk belongs to the Māyā-state. Helper/helped must be stated, else no grasping-together for taking-and-leaving → *vyavahāralopaḥ*. And the helping is the consciousness-Lord's will so ordaining (*cinmayeśvarecchāiva sā*): two successive things mounted in one cognition shone together in one reflection — *ekaparāmarśāt*. *Iti na kācit khaṇḍanā.*

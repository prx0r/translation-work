# Sambandhasiddhi p02 — ARGUMENT MAP (the machinery: restraint, one-reflection, qualifier/qualified, pairs)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 samb-p02 = kārikās 10–18 (edition pp. 13–14). Continues p01's retorsion into the positive machinery.*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Granted that relation-talk is legitimate — *how does it work*? By what operation does thought bind many into one without falsifying them? The answer: restraint of activity (*vyāpāra-rodha*), one-reflection (*ekāmarśa*), and the qualifier/qualified structure (*viśeṣaṇa/viśeṣya*). This passage is the engine room: pot-and-cloth, qualifier-and-qualified, the king's horses. The Exhibit A is the genitive: *rājñaḥ puruṣaḥ*.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Step 0 — restraint (v. 10):**
> *vyāpāro 'nena ruddho 'sau na vikalpāntaraṃ spṛśet | vikalpāntarasaṃsparśe mātṛbhedaprasaṅgataḥ*
[i.e. by this, activity restrained, it does not touch another thought — for touching another thought would entail knower-difference.] Relation operates by *holding* the manifold from scattering, not by gluing alien bits. Berger's point: this restraint is the *mode of intentional action* (śakti) — not a concept but the reflexive grasping that keeps content determinate without collapsing into identity or difference.

**Step 1 — one-reflection, one-word-superimposition (v. 11):**
> *tadekāmarśavarty ekaśabdādhyāsavaśād brajet | aneko 'py ekatām artho yathā ghaṭapaṭāv iti*
[i.e. resting in that one-reflection, by force of superimposing one word, the many go to oneness of meaning — as "pot and cloth."] The dvandva is the proof: many things, one word, one meaning — because one reflection.

**Step 2 — qualifier/qualified is relation for help (vv. 12–13):**
> *ity asti tāvad anyaś ca viśeṣaṇaviśeṣyayoḥ | saṃbandha upakārārthaḥ sa jñātum upayujyate*
[i.e. there is further the qualifier/qualified relation, for the sake of helping — worth knowing.] Even in duality there is two-ness of qualifier/qualified (*bhaved dvitvaṃ viśeṣaṇaviśeṣyayoḥ*): *dvivimarśabhuvo yadvad rājño 'śvarathapattayaḥ* — as the king's horses-chariots-soldiers rest on two reflections. Then the exhibit: *vimarśo rājña ity ekaḥ svasāmānyonmukhaḥ paraḥ* — one reflection toward the king, facing his own generality; another toward the man, bearing king-generality-relation (*svāmisāmānyasaṃbandhasahaḥ puruṣa ity ayam*). *Rājñaḥ puruṣaḥ* comes *after* — it is relation-ranged (*paścāt saṃbandhagocaraḥ*).

**Step 3 — the three and the genitive's seat (vv. 14–15):**
> *kalpyo 'nenātha vā kalpyau pūrvau te sarvathā trayaḥ | ṣaṣṭhyādivācyaḥ saṃbandho viniviṣṭo viśeṣaṇe | viśeṣyaikatvam āpanne svaṃ ca rūpam anujjhati*
[i.e. whether one or two are superimposed, in every way three: the genitive-voiced relation sits *in the qualifier* that has attained qualified-oneness, without abandoning its own form.] Loss of one reading, purity of the principal, joining of other with other — *ekahānyā prādhānena śuddhenānyānyayogitā*.

**Step 4 — transfer across principals (vv. 16–18):**
> *syād guṇasya yathā rājñaḥ puruṣo brāhmaṇasya ca | tathānyena pradhānena yaikatānyāpareṇa vā | saṃbandhākhyā guṇasya syād rājño 'śvaḥ puruṣo yathā*
[i.e. as the king's man may be the brahmin's too, so oneness with another principal — or with a further one — is called the subordinate's relation: as the king's horse, the king's man.] Relation rides the subordinate term across principals; the pair, not the substance, carries it.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. *Kalpyo 'nena* — the exact force of the singular option (one superimposed) vs. the two; the verse is compressed and the edition's punctuation is editorial.
2. The horses-exhibit (*rājño 'śvarathapattayaḥ*): whether the "two reflections" are king-side/man-side or whole-side/part-side — read here as the former, per the next half-verse.
3. *Ekāmarśa* vs. p01's *ekaparāmarśa* — treated as the same operation; no doctrine of difference asserted in T1.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as machinery-prose, not debate: each verse one mechanism (restraint → one-word → qualifier-for-help → genitive's seat → transfer). Keep the exhibits running (*ghaṭapaṭa*, *rājñaḥ puruṣaḥ*, *rājño 'śvaḥ*). The payoff line is v. 12's *upakārārthaḥ*: relation exists *in order to help* knowing — carry that into C1.

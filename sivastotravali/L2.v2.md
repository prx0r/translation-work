## sivst-p01 — Bhakti as supreme; recognition in devotional mode (stotras 1–4)

**Stotra 1 — bhaktivilāsa (the pleasure of devotion).** This is where the Śivastotrāvalī begins — with bhakti as supreme. Not for one who meditates without rule, nor for one who chants without preparation: we bow to Śiva's light, the bhakti-possessor. My self, drinking bhakti-nectar, though young; like grey with dust of world-journey. Of bhakti-possessors who have gained that wealth, city-dwellers; movement in world-path by that expansion. Lord, in all worlds where Śiva is directly present, what is not the bhakti-possessor's field; what mantra of theirs does not succeed. Victorious, intoxicated with best bhakti-nectar-wine. Even non-dual, always your second, Lord. They know your truth, ocean of endless bliss. Such are those drenched in dense bhakti-bliss-nectar. You alone are self-Lord of all; all is in self, attached; thus knowing bhakti as self-established, one conquers people. Lord, when knowable is destroyed, by whom are you not seen standing alone; in knowable-knower-agitation, you are easily seen by bhaktas. As the lake of endless bliss, the beloved goddess, inseparable from you; so may one bhakti be mine. All is cause of gaining you for bhakti-possessors, Lord. This consciousness-path stands threefold with joy, suffering, delusion. From tasting bhakti-nectar, even the supreme state of knowledge; that state, Lord, for me, like vinegar's sourness for wine.

**Stotras 2–4 — recognition in devotional mode.** Stotra 2 (sarvātmaparibhāvana): contemplation of the all-soul — Śiva as all, the world as his body, the devotee's recognition that all is Śiva. Stotra 3 (praṇayaprasāda): the gift of affection — bhakti as prasāda (grace), the devotee's love for Śiva. Stotra 4 (surasodara): potent nectar — bhakti as intoxicating nectar, the devotee drunk with devotion. The devotional voice cajoles: "I am yours, you are mine." The recognition (pratyabhijñā) operates in devotional mode: the devotee recognizes all as Śiva, not through argument but through love.

## sivst-p02 — Svātantrya in devotional mode; āveśa (stotras 5–8)

**Stotra 5 (svabalanideśana):** Lord, bring me into your abode by the garland of your feet. My hair adorned with the dust of your lotus-feet. When shall I dance with unrestrained speed? Lord, I always beg only this: that you be my sole master. Dwelling within you, may I be silent; otherwise I am wise (and that is the problem). Ah, ocean of nectar, Lord; ah, beautiful one, three-eyed. Ah, taste, Virūpākṣa; thus I would dance and shout. With eyes closed by the touch of your lotus-feet. Intoxicated, swaying with the wine of bhakti. May I dwell on earth, the bearer of mind, wherever that (bhakti) is.

**Stotra 6 (adhvavisphuraṇa):** Even for a moment, Lord, separated from you. May I always have the state of seeing, being intensely scorched. In the essence of separation (saṃsāra), by you, the dear Lord, may I always be unseparated, even separated from the world. Wherever I go with body, speech, mind — all that is you. May the enjoyment of embracing your body happen by your will. Reveal yourself; let not other prayers torment.

**Stotra 7 (vidhuravijaya):** One who enjoys only the contact of your lotus-feet. Lord, bring me into your abode. My hair adorned with the dust of your lotus-feet. When shall I dance with unrestrained speed? Lord, I always beg only this: that you be my sole master. Dwelling within you, may I be silent; otherwise I am wise. Ah, ocean of nectar, Lord; ah, beautiful one, three-eyed. Ah, taste, Virūpākṣa; thus I would dance and shout. With eyes closed by the touch of your lotus-feet. Intoxicated, swaying with the wine of bhakti. May I dwell on earth, the bearer of mind, wherever that (bhakti) is.

**Stotra 8 (alaukikodbala):** One who enjoys only the contact of your lotus-feet. In the essence of separation (saṃsāra), by you, the dear Lord, may I always be unseparated, even separated from the world. Wherever I go with body, speech, mind — all that is you. May the enjoyment of embracing your body happen by your will. Reveal yourself; let not other prayers torment.

The key experience is *āveśa* (possession by the deity): the devotee is so filled with Śiva that the distinction between worshipper and worshipped dissolves. The devotee's wisdom is an obstacle (*tvadantarvasatir mūko bhaveyaṃ* — dwelling within you, may I be silent; otherwise I am wise). Silence (āveśa) is the goal.

## sivst-p03 — Autonomy (svātantrya) in devotional mode (stotras 9–12)

**Stotra 9 (svātantryavijaya):** When will my mind, eager to taste the nine rasas soaked in devotion, turn away from other things to touch you? Attached to you alone, with the great wealth of worshipping your feet. When, eager, shall I realize you directly? By the force of deep love, my mind has become free of expectation. When, with all great bolts broken at once, shall I approach you? All deities stationed in the essence of self-awareness. When, Lord, shall I subjugate by the power of devotion to you? When, Lord, shall I have the great festival of bliss-nectar of devotion? When even the name, delighting in the happiness of seeing, shall be obtained. The Lord, fearless, generous, full, causeless, self-concealed. When, suddenly recognizing, shall I put to shame the Lord's people? When, Lord, shall I attain that beloved-ness of yours? By which it would not be fitting for you to flee from me anywhere. When, Lord, shall I be flooded with the nectar of approval by the vision of all beings whose self is worship of you? The supreme ground of knowledge, the supreme state of yoga — when, Lord, shall devotion to you be full, with the desire for that? When, suddenly today, having firmly grasped, I shall be overcome with joy? I shall reveal the treasure of your supreme feet to all. When, Lord, shall I be, as you are, with no cave of Māyā's shadow in me? Having made all the universe my own, free of dependence. When, Lord, shall I be the leader of the troop of your bhaktas? Lord, you are the unprecedented bond of worldly pride. When shall I be filled with great pride, filled with the nectar of devotion to you? Established in the embrace of the glory free from all objects. When, like a swan on the cool lotus-feet. The wealth of enjoying the worship of you, rich with the nectar of devotion. When shall I cross over, when shall I be accomplished? Stammering, stumbling, with tears of joy, crying out. When, with laughing face, shall I taste the nectar of touching you? When, Śambhu, shall I shake off this state resembling the bound soul? When shall I taste the form of my self, fit for devotion to you? Having obtained the siddhis of aṇimā etc., free from all distress and fear. When, Lord, shall I be one who delights in drinking the elixir of devotion to you? When shall such a cry arise in my speech? That your form shines forth immediately before. With mind intent on embracing your lotus-feet ever more deeply. When shall I see you, this thing and non-thing, effortlessly?

**Stotra 10 (avicchedabhaṅga):** This is not to be endured, of you, sole Lord of the world. The Māheśvaras and the equals of the worlds who always follow your feet with love. Wherever they go, they enjoy some enjoyments. Where you are the Lord, the ender of time, where is pain? At that moment, all his time is filled with your bliss. Your drop of bliss-nectar is the moon fallen to earth. And the sun is your ray of destroying light. We offer to the third eye, to you, Lord. To the unique mark of some unearthly greatness. By that alone you are seen; he who sees you rejoices exceedingly. By whom, somehow, some joy; by that you are seen. Lord, to whom you are gracious, by whom your heart is gained. Drawing from your city, making the external internal. Without you, the whole world is seen as equal (indifferent). But for this, you alone are the Lord, the odd-eyed one. Let it be: without your power, there is no being at all. Whose talk of your faults does not cohere without you. Abiding in the mind that is merely external-internal-obstacle. If it is in you, Lord, what else is useful to me? Others wander, Lord, extremely unsettled in the self. Others wander, Lord, extremely settled in the self. Even without seeing the nectar of devotion to you, Lord, beings succeed by mere conduct similar to yours. We are your servants, Lord; as for the three worlds, you bear yourself; so we too are to be borne, enough. Seen in the world-self, though filled with supreme bliss-nectar, I am extremely desirous of the taste of touching you. Lord, all the sufferings that belong to saṃsārins, named "ghee," united with your self, become bearable. You being omniscient, omnipotent, consciousness itself, in every way, the world's appearance is unreal. These qualities, though like clods, shine, animated by you; as cotton wisps dance, blown by wind. Lord, if there were no self-conceit in the qualities, by what would the world's appearance as one-self-with-you be diminished? Those too are venerable, the great ones, even those who have attained dissolution. Those purified by the touch of your anger-fire, Lord. When the great light-body stands clearly manifest. Lord, from all sides, why do I spread into darkness? You alone are the undivided, my own form, immortal. And yet, for mortal dharmas, I alone am the single support. He who has the ornament of speech, the name "Maheśvara," with the mark of bowing on his head, he alone is made manifest. You are both real and unreal; by that, effortlessly. Lord, by its own nature, how is success not mine? Śiva's servant, one-self with Śiva; what happiness would he not attain? I am to be satisfied by the immortal nectar of the chief gods. The bile-bodied one abiding between heart and navel; you, the great fire, consume all, moving and unmoving.

**Stotra 11 (autsukyaviśvasita):** This world, or friends, or relatives — nothing is mine. But you are all this; when that is so, who else is mine, Lord? You are Maheśvara directly; the whole world is you. Even the prayer "may it succeed" is itself a prayer. Even lordship of the three worlds appears like straw to one devoted to you. What fruit of good karma, Lord, without remembrance of you? By which there is nothing separate from you, and you are the source of worlds. Hence, even in wonderful deeds, your praise-hymn does not arise. I am made of you, ever devoted to seeing you; thus without ceasing. Even envisioning, Lord, by its own nature, even in a dream, how could I be thus? Those touched even slightly by the fragrance from your lotus-feet: for them, all enjoyments become rest, sought even by immortals. In the heart, nothing else; but in speech and action, something else, Śambhu. Whether grace or restraint, one thing is to be done. I am deluded, afflicted with suffering, afraid of old age and the rest, powerless; I take refuge in you. Śambhu, so arrange quickly that I attain the highest burden, having abandoned the path of suffering. Having placed them at your ear, my trivial cries attain great value. Like fragments of bamboo falling into water attaining the state of pearl-gems. What is not obtained by those, Lord, who are devoted to your name, even for a moment, even from deceit? O moon-crested one, so arrange that I attain, with broken death, the vibhūti of aṇimā etc. Śambhu, Śarva, moon-crested, Śiva, three-eyed, wearing the rosary; glorious, marked with the terrible skull, shining with the terrible trident. Ocean of compassion, characterized by the terrible power of creating the three worlds. Śrīkaṇṭha, quickly destroy the mass of inauspiciousness; bestow supreme siddhi. What, Lord, would not happen where the Lord assumes creatorship? What being would have destruction, which Śaṅkara protects? Thus, taking refuge always in your imperishable great power, in this saṃsāra, I suffer, afflicted with continuous distress, alone. Even though here the most excellent of boons — pain, old age, death — these; or for a moment, the much-desired, speech etc., impermanent. Even there I desire, longing for constant happiness, to stay long, for the sake of living, with meditation on your lotus-feet united with the taste of enjoyment. Lord, destroyer of the distress of the bowed, treasure of welfare, matted-haired one. Of me, the abode of suffering alone, afraid of birth and death, now. So arrange that excellent, mind-pleasing enjoyment-taste be given. While living, may I attain, O devotee of your worship, the immovable siddhis. Homage to the destroyer of the great darkness of delusion, whose action is unique. To the one whose light surpasses all light, marked by the moon.

**Stotra 12 (rahasyanirdeśa):** No helper is needed by you, no obstacle for the seer. All is flooded by you alone; how is it that you are not seen even now? From the host of things, from the collection of senses, from the midst of knowledge. I would always see you, self-born, having removed the universe. How would they arise; how would they enter the path of seeing you? Adorned with some great prakṛti-mark. Rising up again and again from all earth, water, grass. You scatter the flowing nectar of creation, having directly realized your form; satisfied by the nectar flowing from your form. With thirst uprooted, they wander as they wish, intoxicated. Not then, not always, not even once — where the thought of time is not. This seeing of you is not eternal, nor is it spoken of otherwise. May the siddhi of yoga be always this much for me, whose mind is eager to see you. I would enter, by mere intention, the abode of your nectar, for worship of you. Of great souls whose minds bloom from obtaining the non-conceptual seeing of you. Pure actions and words clearly shine forth, playfully. Dwelling within, in your lotus-feet, fearless. In those and those worlds, I would worship the Lord, with unobstructed action. Absorbed in the interior of your lotus-feet, with desires fallen away. Satisfied by the excessive use of honey, I would wander as I wish. Whose resolve to worship you arose as if from pride. Even for him, your fitting presence necessarily arises. Lord, by one who depends on others, with mind of one taste. Easily, the Lord who rests in all, I would drink to satisfaction. What is rejected by you, all that is to be abandoned; that alone. "Your way is to be adopted" — this is the essence. The host of beings abiding in you is worshipped as chief, by your being Lord. Outside you, mere non-being — how, Lord, could it be or be worshipped? Silent, non-conceptual, without distraction, always. Even in agitation, the object to be known, O three-eyed one, is you alone everywhere. Reveal your own abode, Lord, where you are always with the Supreme Lady. Do servants not appear trustworthy, equal to the dust of the master's feet? Even having entered the path of seeing, why do you withdraw, Lord, from your servant? For a moment here you are not; whose eye's object are you not? When, Lord, by the constantly flowing cover of the nectar of non-dual awareness? From the flood, bringing supreme non-difference, may I attain you and my own body with joy. "I" — from the closed-off world, from the essence of your recognition, for me. May even an atom of the world's stability happen, by which I may be your worshipper. Seeing each thing, unlimited in form, moment by moment. May I rightly see you alone, world-formed, my own Lord. Why does my mind not wander toward that, your limb-dwelling, desired object? The damage to nature is not so; if my desire is fulfilled. A hundredfold superior, Lord, by your power, some with this very eye. Even those moving with a ploughman's effort see your form always before. That mind does not arise which is not always made of your will, auspicious. And otherwise, Lord, it is so arranged. Therefore I am your-self on earth, moving and standing always as it is. With the festival of worship of your stainless lotus-feet, unobstructed. May my understanding arise rightly before me in your deep words. And then, having exercised that power, the devotion to your worship, without cessation. In the sphere of transaction too, always, may this collection of meanings shine for me. As your part, not having attained lovability by itself. In the mind, by its own nature, wherever it wanders, I am in its domains. Even when gone forth, may I always be undisturbed, skilled in your service. Lord, by your will alone I have become your servant; no other has power here. How is it that I never see your face; this is not strange. Those eager for you who see you as an opposing form. For them, ah, what would be present; what means or fruit would arise? Let things be as things; be for me with your bhāva. Let there be nothing, and nothing otherwise from you. What is not anything, let that not be anything; let there be something, just something, for me. In every way, let it be; by that much, you are everywhere, having received worship.

The key move in p03 is the *rahasya* (arcane lore): "your way is to be adopted" (*tvannayaṃ samupādeyam*) — this is the essence. The devotee's wisdom is an obstacle; silence (āveśa) is the goal. The arcane lore is not secret doctrine but the recognition that Śiva's way is the only way.

## sivst-p04 — Self (ātman) in devotional mode (stotras 13–16)

**Stotra 15 (bhaktistotra):** The knots of the three malas are for those who have crossed; yogis, scholars, the healthy — they are truly devoted to you. Those nourished by māyīya, kalā, rāga etc. move happily on the world's shore, devoted to you. Devotees, distinct in praise and worship, are not dispassionate nor Śiva nor desiring liberation — they worship you. May I be intoxicated with the wine of high devotion, worshipping you from within the heart. The devotees, from within and without, offer to you, Lord, the nectar of devotion. Dharma and adharma, knowledge and action, happiness and suffering — devotees taste something of all, ah. Even the blind and leprous shine, ornamented with the supreme devotion to you. Even those who worship you from outside, with mind alone, Lord — protect, increase, honor this great wealth of devotion. Lord, your community of devotees, though attached to you, may it always be pleased. The devotion, growing, becomes the eternal one of Śambhu, absorbed in my mind. Even if the milk-jar is obtained, the greed for curds is killed — what is this incomplete attainment, what is the chief happiness? Devotion, always eternal, growing, becomes absorbed in the mind. Even external and internal, the supreme fragrances of Śaṅkara's garland, the devotees are permeated. Even the yogi, residing near, is permeated by the fragrance — all beings are permeated. Light exists; say nothing more; the whole universe, though in deep sleep, is entirely. Where, Lord, the worshipper of Śiva is established, there may the crest-jewels of Śaṅkara's feet-shining remain. May the darkness of delusion be destroyed, for the destruction of the tri-guṇa and the world-path. Even if happiness was never enjoyed for long, thus I am born useless; Śaśidhara's feet have trodden my supreme head. May the devotees of Śiva quickly have long, long great wealth.

**Stotra 16 (pāśānudbheda):** Nothing at all is an obstacle for the beings regarding your covering; nothing at all is an obstacle for the devotees regarding your covering. Even with means, with distinctions, the devotees' self, once purified, shines. Victory to those intoxicated with the nectar of devotion to you; who can be compared to them? For devotees, there is nothing to be known; you are non-conceptual, established in yourself. Devotees, pierced by sharp goosebumps, even suffering becomes enjoyment. Even those whose consciousness is entirely drenched with your moonlight, wherever they are, inside or outside. For devotees, even the external covering of you is equally sweet; some, bewildered, see the form as separate. For devotees, the mind is stainless, free from honor and dishonor, passion and aversion. How could one like the world be among devotees? The darkness of passion and aversion is conquered by the light of devotion. Among the great, who are the knowers? He whose devotion to you is the means of siddhi. The fame of the devoted is sought; he by whom you alone are worshipped. Liberation is named for the ripened devotion to you, Lord. In that, the first state is attained; we are like the liberated; even the arrival of suffering is great for me. May your separation not be, Lord; even the stream of happiness. You are pleased by devotion; when you are pleased, Lord, what? That mutual dependence is fitting, as you alone know. Whether with form or without, within or without, for the devoted self, Lord, you are entirely nectar. In this world, for the devoted to you, the fruit of happiness is established elsewhere. Secretly, devotion; beyond, devotion; devotion is the name of the supreme in you, Lord. Devotion, devotion, devotion — the name is exceedingly intense. May my devotion be intense, supreme, in you. Because of that, all the glory of beings is born from you; whatever is, or is not, let it be something, just something, for me. In every way, let it be; by that much, you are everywhere, having received worship.

The key move in p04 is *ātmaikya* (oneness of self): the devotee recognizes the self as Śiva, not as a separate soul. Stotra 13's *īśvaro 'ham aham eva rūpavān* (I am the Lord; I am the form) and stotra 16's *bhaktir upacīyamānā yeyaṃ śambhoḥ sadātanī* (devotion, growing, becomes the eternal one of Śambhu).

## sivst-p05 — Closing: divine play; bhakti as supreme path (stotras 17–20)

**Stotra 17 (divyakrīḍā):** Ah, what a sweet festival of worship, ah, what a delightful play! All activities that are for the worship of devotees are siddhis. For devotees, all your forms are themselves siddhis; they worship you without ceasing. The time of worship is always the time of worship for devotees; there is no beginning or end. Even in dream, even in delusion, the festival of your worship is established for devotees. Not only for those who chant, offer, bathe, meditate — for devotees, the festival of your worship is always. For devotees, the festival of worship is superior to that of Indra and Brahmā. What is to be obtained is obtained only by devotees; they know it. Standing in your consciousness-form, with the actions of the 36 tattvas, I worship you always, Lord. With body, speech, mind, and effort, I worship you, Lord; devotees enjoying the festival of worship. Let all time pass, even the infinite; I beg only this, Lord: the festival of the nectar of worship. The festival of worship grows daily; may it bear fruit for me. In the ocean of you, the great self, worshipping, may I always dwell. Even the mind, offered to devotees, is sweet in the festival of worship. For devotees, sending the nectar of worship, the activities of the senses.

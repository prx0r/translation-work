# Śivastotrāvalī sivst-p01 — ARGUMENT MAP (bhakti as supreme; recognition in devotional mode)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 sivst-p01 = stotras 1–4 (450 verses total, 5 passages). Lore: Bailly 1987/1995 (Shaiva Devotional Songs of Kashmir — translation + study); Moreno Bautista (UNAM, Pratyabhijñā and bhakti); Gaeffke & Bailly 1989 (JAOS). Key insight: Utpaladeva's stotras are a "spiritual diary" — the bhakti voice of Pratyabhijñā, relating bhakti to the upāyas. ---

## 1. What is at issue

Utpaladeva — the philosopher of Pratyabhijñā — writes devotional hymns. The question is not "what is the argument?" but "what is the devotional stance, and how does bhakti relate to recognition (pratyabhijñā)?" The answer: bhakti is supreme — not for one who meditates without rule; we bow to Śiva's light, the bhakti-possessor. Bhakti-possessors move in the world by expansion; no mantra fails for them; they are intoxicated with bhakti-nectar. Even non-dual, they are always Śiva's second. They know Śiva's truth, the ocean of endless bliss. Bhakti is self-established; Śiva is easily seen by bhaktas; bhakti is inseparable from Śiva. The consciousness-path is threefold (joy/suffering/delusion); the knowledge-state is like vinegar-sourness compared to bhakti-nectar.

Lore: Bailly 1987/1995 (Shaiva Devotional Songs of Kashmir — translation + study); Moreno Bautista (UNAM, Pratyabhijñā and bhakti); Gaeffke & Bailly 1989 (JAOS). Key insight: Utpaladeva's stotras are a "spiritual diary" — the bhakti voice of Pratyabhijñā, relating bhakti to the upāyas. The text is devotional poetry, not philosophical treatise; the "argument" is the devotional arc itself. Utpaladeva was a siddha (perfected being), one of the masters of the tantric tradition in Kashmir. The Śivastotrāvalī has been used in Kashmiri Śaiva worship for 1000+ years — recited and sung at festivals, tears flowing from its beauty. The stotras were composed orally (Utpaladeva sang them, disciples memorized and recorded them as muktaka verses); only three stotras (13, 14, 15) were combined by Utpaladeva himself as "daily lessons" (āhnikastutisūktāni). The path does not require leaving home — it calls for changing one's view of the world, leading a life of divine recognition while carrying on with ordinary life. The text communicates both philosophical positions and devotional feeling, with the intention of leading the reader to the experience that animates the author. Kṣemarāja (Abhinavagupta's disciple, Utpaladeva's praprauśiṣya) commented on the stotras; his commentary is found in most manuscripts.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Stotra 1 — bhaktivilāsa (the pleasure of devotion):**
- *na dhyāyato na japataḥ syād yasyāvidhipūrvakam* — not for one who meditates without rule, nor for one who chants without preparation.
- *śivabhāsas taṃ numo bhaktiśālinam* — we bow to Śiva's light, the bhakti-possessor.
- *ātmā mama bhavadbhaktisudhāpānayuvāpi san* — my self, drinking bhakti-nectar, though young.
- *lokayātrārajorāgāt palitair iva dhūsaraḥ* — like grey with dust of world-journey.
- *labdhatatsaṃpadāṃ bhaktimatāṃ tvatpuravāsinām* — of bhakti-possessors who have gained that wealth, city-dwellers.
- *saṃcāro lokamārge 'pi syāt tayaiva vijṛmbhayā* — movement in world-path by that expansion.
- *sākṣādbhavanmaye nātha sarvasmin bhuvanāntare | kiṃ na bhaktimatāṃ kṣetram mantraḥ kvaiṣām na siddhyati* — Lord, in all worlds where Śiva is directly present, what is not the bhakti-possessor's field; what mantra of theirs does not succeed.
- *jayanti bhaktipīyūṣarasāsavavaronmadāḥ* — victorious, intoxicated with best bhakti-nectar-wine.
- *advitīyā api sadā tvaddvitīyā api prabho* — even non-dual, always your second, Lord.
- *anantānandasindhos te nātha tattvaṃ vidanti te* — they know your truth, ocean of endless bliss.
- *tādṛśa eva ye sāndrabhaktānandarasāplutāḥ* — such are those drenched in dense bhakti-bliss-nectar.
- *tvam evātmeśa sarvasya sarvaś cātmani rāgavān* — you alone are self-Lord of all; all is in self, attached.
- *iti svabhāvasiddhāṃs tvadbhaktiṃ jānañ jayej janaḥ* — thus knowing bhakti as self-established, one conquers people.
- *nātha vedyakṣaye kena na dṛśyo 'syekakaḥ sthitaḥ* — Lord, when knowable is destroyed, by whom are you not seen standing alone.
- *vedyavedakasaṃkṣobhe 'py asi bhaktaiḥ sudarśanaḥ* — in knowable-knower-agitation, you are easily seen by bhaktas.
- *anantānandasarasī devī priyatamā yathā | aviyuktāsti te tadvad ekā tvadbhaktir astu me* — as the lake of endless bliss, the beloved goddess, inseparable from you; so may one bhakti be mine.
- *sarva eva bhavallābhahetur bhaktimatāṃ vibho* — all is cause of gaining you for bhakti-possessors, Lord.
- *saṃvinmārgo 'yam āhlādaduḥkhamohais tridhā sthitaḥ* — this consciousness-path stands threefold with joy/suffering/delusion.
- *bhavadbhaktyamṛtāsvādād bodhasya syāt parāpi yā | daśā sā māṃ prati svāmin nāsavasyeva śuktatā* — from tasting bhakti-nectar, even the supreme state of knowledge; that state, Lord, for me, like vinegar's sourness for wine.

**Stotras 2–4 — recognition in devotional mode:**
- Stotra 2 (sarvātmaparibhāvana): contemplation of the all-soul — Śiva as all, the world as his body, the devotee's recognition that all is Śiva.
- Stotra 3 (praṇayaprasāda): the gift of affection — bhakti as prasāda (grace), the devotee's love for Śiva.
- Stotra 4 (surasodara): potent nectar — bhakti as intoxicating nectar, the devotee drunk with devotion.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the devotional arc: bhakti as supreme (stotra 1) → recognition in devotional mode (stotras 2–4). The key move is stotra 1's *iti svabhāvasiddhāṃs tvadbhaktiṃ jānañ jayej janaḥ*: knowing bhakti as self-established, one conquers people. And *bhavadbhaktyamṛtāsvādād bodhasya syāt parāpi yā daśā sā māṃ prati svāmin nāsavasyeva śuktatā*: the supreme knowledge-state is like vinegar-sourness compared to bhakti-nectar.

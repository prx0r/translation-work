# Śivastotrāvalī sivst-p02 — ARGUMENT MAP (svātantrya in devotional mode; āveśa)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 sivst-p02 = stotras 5–8. Lore: Bailly 1987/1995; Moreno Bautista (UNAM, Pratyabhijñā and bhakti). Key: Utpaladeva relates bhakti to the upāyas — an original angle (Gitananda, in Bailly's introduction). The devotional voice cajoles, longs, surrenders — this is the "soul of the practices." āveśa = intense devotion carrying an experience of "possession" by the deity — the devotee loses the distinction between worshipper and worshipped. The path does not require leaving home — it calls for changing one's view of the world, leading a life of divine recognition while carrying on with ordinary life. The stotras were composed orally (Utpaladeva sang them, disciples memorized and recorded them); only three stotras (13, 14, 15) were combined by Utpaladeva himself as "daily lessons" (āhnikastutisūktāni).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Stotras 5–8 shift from bhakti-as-supreme to svātantrya (autonomy/freedom) in devotional mode. The devotee's stance: "I am yours, do with me what you will." Āveśa (possession by the deity) is the key experience: the devotee is so filled with Śiva that the distinction between worshipper and worshipped dissolves. The devotional voice cajoles, longs, and surrenders. This is the "soul of the practices" (Gitananda, in Bailly's introduction): the feeling and emotional intensity with which the upāyas are done.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Stotra 5 (svabalanideśana):** Lord, bring me into your abode by the garland of your feet. My hair adorned with the dust of your lotus-feet. When shall I dance with unrestrained speed? Lord, I always beg only this: that you be my sole master. Dwelling within you, may I be silent; otherwise I am wise (and that is the problem). Ah, ocean of nectar, Lord; ah, beautiful one, three-eyed. Ah, taste, Virūpākṣa; thus I would dance and shout. With eyes closed by the touch of your lotus-feet. Intoxicated, swaying with the wine of bhakti. May I dwell on earth, the bearer of mind, wherever that (bhakti) is.

**Stotra 6 (adhvavisphuraṇa):** Even for a moment, Lord, separated from you. May I always have the state of seeing, being intensely scorched. In the essence of separation (saṃsāra), by you, the dear Lord, may I always be unseparated, even separated from the world. Wherever I go with body, speech, mind — all that is you. May the enjoyment of embracing your body happen by your will. Reveal yourself; let not other prayers torment.

**Stotra 7 (vidhuravijaya):** One who enjoys only the contact of your lotus-feet. Lord, bring me into your abode. My hair adorned with the dust of your lotus-feet. When shall I dance with unrestrained speed? Lord, I always beg only this: that you be my sole master. Dwelling within you, may I be silent; otherwise I am wise. Ah, ocean of nectar, Lord; ah, beautiful one, three-eyed. Ah, taste, Virūpākṣa; thus I would dance and shout. With eyes closed by the touch of your lotus-feet. Intoxicated, swaying with the wine of bhakti. May I dwell on earth, the bearer of mind, wherever that (bhakti) is.

**Stotra 8 (alaukikodbala):** One who enjoys only the contact of your lotus-feet. In the essence of separation (saṃsāra), by you, the dear Lord, may I always be unseparated, even separated from the world. Wherever I go with body, speech, mind — all that is you. May the enjoyment of embracing your body happen by your will. Reveal yourself; let not other prayers torment.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The stotras are devotional poetry; the "argument" is the devotional stance and its relation to recognition (pratyabhijñā). The argmap reconstructs the stance, not a syllogism.
2. Bailly 1987/1995 is the standard translation; our T1 is from GRETIL (Bailly ed.). The GRETIL text is continuous prose, not verse-separated; verse boundaries are not marked in our T1.
3. The 20 stotras are mapped to 5 passages (4 stotras each); the mapping follows the T1's own grouping.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the devotional arc: bhakti as supreme (p01, stotra 1) → recognition in devotional mode (p01, stotras 2–4) → svātantrya in devotional mode (p02, stotras 5–8) → autonomy (p03, stotras 9–12) → self (p04, stotras 13–16) → closing (p05, stotras 17–20). The key move in p02 is āveśa: the devotee is so filled with Śiva that the distinction between worshipper and worshipped dissolves. Carry that into p03 (svātantrya) and p04 (self). The closing stotras (p05) bring bhakti and jīvanmukti together. The tone is devotional, not argumentative; L2 should carry the devotional voice, not flatten it into doctrine. The devotional stotras should be rendered as devotional prose, not as philosophical argument. The text communicates both philosophical positions and devotional feeling, with the intention of leading the reader to the experience that animates the author.

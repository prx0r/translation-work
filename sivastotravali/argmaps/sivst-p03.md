# Śivastotrāvalī sivst-p03 — ARGUMENT MAP (autonomy / svātantrya in devotional mode)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 sivst-p03 = stotras 9–12. Lore: Bailly 1987/1995; Moreno Bautista (UNAM, Pratyabhijñā and bhakti). Key: Utpaladeva relates bhakti to the upāyas — an original angle (Gitananda, in Bailly's introduction). The devotional voice cajoles, longs, surrenders — this is the "soul of the practices."*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Stotras 9–12 shift from svātantrya (autonomy/freedom) in devotional mode to the arcane lore (*rahasya*). The devotee's stance: "I am yours, make me free." The key experience is *āveśa* (possession by the deity) — the devotee is so filled with Śiva that the distinction between worshipper and worshipped dissolves. The devotional voice cajoles, longs, and surrenders. This is the "soul of the practices" (Gitananda): the feeling and emotional intensity with which the upāyas are done.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Stotra 9 — svātantryavijaya (the triumph of freedom):**
- The devotee longs for the nine rasas (aesthetic emotions) soaked in devotion; the mind turns from other things to touch Śiva.
- Attached to Śiva alone, with the great wealth of worshipping his feet.
- When shall I realize you directly? By deep love, my mind is free of expectation.
- When shall all great bolts break at once, and I approach you, stationed in the essence of self-awareness?
- When shall I subjugate by the power of devotion to you? When shall I have the great festival of bliss-nectar of devotion?
- Even non-dual, always your second; they know your truth, ocean of endless bliss.
- Thus knowing bhakti as self-established, one conquers people.
- When the knowable is destroyed, by whom are you not seen standing alone? In knowable-knower-agitation, you are easily seen by bhaktas.
- As the lake of endless bliss, the beloved goddess, inseparable from you; so may one bhakti be mine.
- All is cause of gaining you for bhakti-possessors.
- This consciousness-path stands threefold with joy/suffering/delusion.
- From tasting bhakti-nectar, even the supreme state of knowledge is like vinegar's sourness.

**Stotra 10 — avicchedabhaṅga (breaking the continuity):**
- This is not to be endured, of you, sole Lord of the world.
- The Māheśvaras and the equals of the worlds who always follow your feet with love.
- Wherever they go, they enjoy some enjoyments; where you are the Lord, the ender of time, where is pain?
- At that moment, all his time is filled with your bliss.
- Your drop of bliss-nectar is the moon fallen to earth; the sun is your ray of destroying light.
- We offer to the third eye, to you, Lord — the unique mark of some unearthly greatness.
- By that alone you are seen; he who sees you rejoices exceedingly.
- Lord, to whom you are gracious, by whom your heart is gained.
- Drawing from your city, making the external internal; without you, the whole world is seen as equal (indifferent).
- You alone are the Lord, the odd-eyed one; without your power, there is no being at all.
- Whose talk of your faults does not cohere without you; abiding in the mind that is merely external-internal-obstacle.
- If it is in you, Lord, what else is useful to me?
- Others wander, Lord, extremely unsettled in the self; others wander, Lord, extremely settled in the self.
- Even without seeing the nectar of devotion to you, Lord, beings succeed by mere conduct similar to yours.
- We are your servants, Lord; as for the three worlds, you bear yourself; so we too are to be borne, enough.
- Seen in the world-self, though filled with supreme bliss-nectar, I am extremely desirous of the taste of touching you.
- Lord, all the sufferings that belong to saṃsārins, named "ghee," united with your self, become bearable.
- You being omniscient, omnipotent, consciousness itself, in every way, the world's appearance is unreal.
- These qualities, though like clods, shine, animated by you; as cotton wisps dance, blown by wind.
- Lord, if there were no self-conceit in the qualities, by what would the world's appearance as one-self-with-you be diminished?
- Those too are venerable, the great ones, even those who have attained dissolution.
- Those purified by the touch of your anger-fire, Lord, when the great light-body stands clearly manifest.
- Lord, from all sides, why do I spread into darkness? You alone are the undivided, my own form, immortal.
- And yet, for mortal dharmas, I alone am the single support.
- He who has the ornament of speech, the name "Maheśvara," with the mark of bowing on his head, he alone is made manifest.
- You are both real and unreal; by that, effortlessly. By its own nature, how is success not mine?
- Śiva's servant, one-self with Śiva; what happiness would he not attain?
- I am to be satisfied by the immortal nectar of the chief gods.
- The bile-bodied one abiding between heart and navel; you, the great fire, consume all, moving and unmoving.

**Stotra 11 — autsukyaviśvasita (bound to the world by desire):**
- This world, or friends, or relatives — nothing is mine.
- But you are all this; when that is so, who else is mine, Lord? You are Maheśvara directly; the whole world is you.
- Even the prayer "may it succeed" is itself a prayer.
- Even lordship of the three worlds appears like straw to one devoted to you.
- What fruit of good karma, Lord, without remembrance of you? By which there is nothing separate from you, and you are the source of worlds.
- Hence, even in wonderful deeds, your praise-hymn does not arise.
- I am made of you, ever devoted to seeing you; thus without ceasing.
- Even envisioning, Lord, by its own nature, even in a dream, how could I be thus?
- Those touched even slightly by the fragrance from your lotus-feet: for them, all enjoyments become rest, sought even by immortals.
- In the heart, nothing else; but in speech and action, something else, Śambhu.
- Whether grace or restraint, one thing is to be done.
- I am deluded, afflicted with suffering, afraid of old age and the rest, powerless; I take refuge in you.
- Śambhu, so arrange quickly that I attain the highest burden, having abandoned the path of suffering.
- Having placed them at your ear, my trivial cries attain great value.
- Like fragments of bamboo falling into water attaining the state of pearl-gems.
- What is not obtained by those, Lord, who are devoted to your name, even for a moment, even from deceit?
- O moon-crested one, so arrange that I attain, with broken death, the vibhūti of aṇimā etc.
- Śambhu, Śarva, moon-crested, Śiva, three-eyed, wearing the rosary; glorious, marked with the terrible skull, shining with the terrible trident.
- Ocean of compassion, characterized by the terrible power of creating the three worlds; Śrīkaṇṭha, quickly destroy the mass of inauspiciousness; bestow supreme siddhi.
- What, Lord, would not happen where the Lord assumes creatorship? What being would have destruction, which Śaṅkara protects?
- Thus, taking refuge always in your imperishable great power, in this saṃsāra, I suffer, afflicted with continuous distress, alone.
- Even though here the most excellent of boons — pain, old age, death — these; or for a moment, the much-desired, speech etc., impermanent.
- Even there I desire, longing for constant happiness, to stay long, for the sake of living, with meditation on your lotus-feet united with the taste of enjoyment.
- Lord, destroyer of the distress of the bowed, treasure of welfare, matted-haired one; of me, the abode of suffering alone, afraid of birth and death, now.
- So arrange that excellent, mind-pleasing enjoyment-taste be given; while living, may I attain, O devotee of your worship, the immovable siddhis.
- Homage to the destroyer of the great darkness of delusion, whose action is unique; to the one whose light surpasses all light, marked by the moon.

**Stotra 12 — rahasyanirdeśa (particulars of the arcane lore):**
- No helper is needed by you, no obstacle for the seer. All is flooded by you alone; how is it that you are not seen even now?
- From the host of things, from the collection of senses, from the midst of knowledge.
- I would always see you, self-born, having removed the universe. How would they arise; how would they enter the path of seeing you?
- Adorned with some great prakṛti-mark. Rising up again and again from all earth, water, grass.
- You scatter the flowing nectar of creation, having directly realized your form; satisfied by the nectar flowing from your form.
- With thirst uprooted, they wander as they wish, intoxicated. Not then, not always, not even once — where the thought of time is not.
- This seeing of you is not eternal, nor is it spoken of otherwise. May the siddhi of yoga be always this much for me, whose mind is eager to see you.
- I would enter, by mere intention, the abode of your nectar, for worship of you. Of great souls whose minds bloom from obtaining the non-conceptual seeing of you.
- Pure actions and words clearly shine forth, playfully. Dwelling within, in your lotus-feet, fearless.
- In those and those worlds, I would worship the Lord, with unobstructed action. Absorbed in the interior of your lotus-feet, with desires fallen away.
- Satisfied by the excessive use of honey, I would wander as I wish. Whose resolve to worship you arose as if from pride.
- Even for him, your fitting presence necessarily arises. Lord, by one who depends on others, with mind of one taste.
- Easily, the Lord who rests in all, I would drink to satisfaction. What is rejected by you, all that is to be abandoned; that alone.
- "Your way is to be adopted" — this is the essence. The host of beings abiding in you is worshipped as chief, by your being Lord.
- Outside you, mere non-being — how, Lord, could it be or be worshipped? Silent, non-conceptual, without distraction, always.
- Even in agitation, the object to be known, O three-eyed one, is you alone everywhere. Reveal your own abode, Lord, where you are always with the Supreme Lady.
- Do servants not appear trustworthy, equal to the dust of the master's feet? Even having entered the path of seeing, why do you withdraw, Lord, from your servant?
- For a moment here you are not; whose eye's object are you not? When, Lord, by the constantly flowing cover of the nectar of non-dual awareness?
- From the flood, bringing supreme non-difference, may I attain you and my own body with joy. "I" — from the closed-off world, from the essence of your recognition, for me.
- May even an atom of the world's stability happen, by which I may be your worshipper. Seeing each thing, unlimited in form, moment by moment.
- May I rightly see you alone, world-formed, my own Lord. Why does my mind not wander toward that, your limb-dwelling, desired object?
- The damage to nature is not so; if my desire is fulfilled. A hundredfold superior, Lord, by your power, some with this very eye.
- Even those moving with a ploughman's effort see your form always before. That mind does not arise which is not always made of your will, auspicious.
- And otherwise, Lord, it is so arranged. Therefore I am your-self on earth, moving and standing always as it is.
- With the festival of worship of your stainless lotus-feet, unobstructed. May my understanding arise rightly before me in your deep words.
- And then, having exercised that power, the devotion to your worship, without cessation. In the sphere of transaction too, always, may this collection of meanings shine for me.
- As your part, not having attained lovability by itself. In the mind, by its own nature, wherever it wanders, I am in its domains.
- Even when gone forth, may I always be undisturbed, skilled in your service. Lord, by your will alone I have become your servant; no other has power here.
- How is it that I never see your face; this is not strange. Those eager for you who see you as an opposing form.
- For them, ah, what would be present; what means or fruit would arise? Let things be as things; be for me with your bhāva.
- Let there be nothing, and nothing otherwise from you. What is not anything, let that not be anything; let there be something, just something, for me.
- In every way, let it be; by that much, you are everywhere, having received worship.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The stotras are devotional poetry; the "argument" is the devotional stance and its relation to recognition (pratyabhijñā). The argmap reconstructs the stance, not a syllogism.
2. Bailly 1987/1995 is the standard translation; our T1 is from GRETIL (Bailly ed.). The GRETIL text is continuous prose, not verse-separated; verse boundaries are not marked in our T1.
3. The 20 stotras are mapped to 5 passages (4 stotras each); the mapping follows the T1's own grouping.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the devotional arc: bhakti as supreme (p01, stotra 1) → recognition in devotional mode (p01, stotras 2–4) → svātantrya in devotional mode (p02, stotras 5–8) → autonomy (p03, stotras 9–12) → self (p04, stotras 13–16) → closing (p05, stotras 17–20). The key move in p02 is āveśa: the devotee is so filled with Śiva that the distinction between worshipper and worshipped dissolves. Carry that into p03 (svātantrya) and p04 (self). The closing stotras (p05) bring bhakti and jīvanmukti together. The tone is devotional, not argumentative; L2 should carry the devotional voice, not flatten it into doctrine.

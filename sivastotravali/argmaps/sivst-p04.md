# Śivastotrāvalī sivst-p04 — ARGUMENT MAP (self / ātman in devotional mode)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 sivst-p04 = stotras 13–16. Lore: Bailly 1987/1995; Moreno Bautista (UNAM). Key: the self (ātman) in devotional mode — the devotee recognizes the self as Śiva, not as a separate soul. The stotras move from summary (saṃgraha) to glorification (jayastotra) to devotion (bhaktistotra) to breaking fetters (pāśānudbheda).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

Stotras 13–16 turn to the self (ātman) in devotional mode. The devotee's stance: "I am Śiva's; the self is not separate from Śiva." The key experience is *ātmaikya* (oneness of self) — the devotee recognizes that the self is Śiva, not a separate soul. The stotras move from summary (saṃgraha) to glorification (jayastotra) to devotion (bhaktistotra) to breaking fetters (pāśānudbheda). The devotional voice is intimate: "I am yours, you are mine."

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Stotra 13 — saṃgraha (in summary):**
- Hear, Lord, what is established for me regarding happiness and suffering.
- Happiness is union with you; suffering is separation from you.
- Even internally, the tiniest thing that is your non-manifestation — remove it entirely, Lord, and reveal your own pure self.
- May my life and death and all else be for worshipping you, Lord.
- I am the Lord; I am the form; I am the learned one; I am the fortunate one; who else?
- The world is like me — the pride of the devoted one is glorious. Lord of gods, you are the gatherer of non-dual bliss.
- By the knowledge of established things, make me fit for worshipping your feet.
- Meditation, then seeing, then touching — the Lord reveals himself.
- Where the festival of worship is, may it always be for me by your power.
- If seeing established things and the festival of worshipping you are paired, that pair is mutually dependent and arises in the devoted.
- Continuously, by every sense, the nectar of worshipping you is drunk; all bhāvas filled with cups, intoxicated, I would be.
- Where, Lord, your temple is established, there may I always be the worshipper.
- Even being placed in the abode of servants, I, by my own will, am devoted to you.
- By seeing, what am I not made fit for? By the act of worshipping your feet, even?
- Śaktipāta at the time of consideration is attained, Lord; you never delay.
- What has come to me today? Why do you delay in revealing yourself?
- There and there, the thing shines external and internal, united with the Goddess.
- May I worship you, Lord, always established in your temple, with devotion.
- From the light of obtaining, the accomplished one stands as he wishes.
- The all-pervading, the all-self, free from diversity; who knows the incomparable supreme bliss becomes one with it.
- Even the poison (kālakūṭa) placed at the throat is, for me, great nectar.
- Even taken nectar, the form of you, if difference-activity pleases, not for me.
- Your speech, the hymn, the devotee's mouth adorned — may I be, Lord, even more so.
- May I enter into you, the deep, non-conceptual, non-dual, supreme, self-form of all objects, devouring.
- May I worship you, entering, praising, O Lord.

**Stotra 14 — jayastotra (song of glorification):**
- Homage to the treasure of victory-lakṣmī, my own Lord. May I taste the nectar of the victory-proclamation for a moment.
- Victory to the one-eyed, Śiva, Mahādeva, Maheśvara, Pārvatī's beloved, Śarva, first of all gods.
- Victory to the bearer of the three-eyed, the moon-crest, the one who wears the skull-garland, the terrible trident.
- Victory to the one whose lotus-feet are worshipped by mere will, the bearer of a body surpassing the world.
- Victory to the one who wears the skin of the elephant, the one who is the abode of all glory.
- Victory to the one who is the first of all, the support of all worlds.
- Victory to the one whose form is the incomparable supreme bliss, who is the abode of all.
- Victory to the one who is the sole support of the three worlds, the unique mark.
- Victory to the one who is the sole doer, the abode of all, the supreme.
- Victory to the one who is the sole refuge, the abode of all, the supreme.
- Victory to the one who is the sole abode, the supreme, the one who is worshipped.
- Victory to the one who is the sole abode, the supreme, the one who is worshipped by all.
- Victory to the one who is the sole abode, the supreme, the one who is the refuge of all.
- Victory to the one who is the sole abode, the supreme, the one who is the support of all.
- Victory to the one who is the sole abode, the supreme, the one who is the lord of all.
- Victory to the one who is the sole abode, the supreme, the one who is the protector of all.
- Victory to the one who is the sole abode, the supreme, the one who is the lord of the three worlds.
- Victory to the one who is the sole abode, the supreme, the one who is the refuge of all.
- Victory to the one who is the sole abode, the supreme, the one who is the support of all.
- Victory to the one who is the sole abode, the supreme, the one who is the lord of all.
- Victory to the one who is the sole abode, the supreme, the one who is the protector of all.

**Stotra 15 — bhaktistotra (about devotion):**
- The knots of the three malas are for those who have crossed; yogis, scholars, the healthy — they are truly devoted to you.
- Those nourished by māyīya, kalā, rāga etc. move happily on the world's shore, devoted to you.
- Devotees, distinct in praise and worship, are not dispassionate nor Śiva nor desiring liberation — they worship you.
- May I be intoxicated with the wine of high devotion, worshipping you from within the heart.
- The devotees, from within and without, offer to you, Lord, the nectar of devotion.
- Dharma and adharma, knowledge and action, happiness and suffering — devotees taste something of all, ah.
- Even the blind and leprous shine, ornamented with the supreme devotion to you.
- Even those who worship you from outside, with mind alone, Lord — protect, increase, honor this great wealth of devotion.
- The community of devotees, though attached to you, Lord, may it always be pleased.
- The devotion, growing, becomes the eternal one of Śambhu, absorbed in my mind.
- Even if the milk-jar is obtained, the greed for curds is killed — what is this incomplete attainment, what is the chief happiness?
- Devotion, always eternal, growing, becomes absorbed in the mind.
- Even external and internal, the supreme fragrances of Śambhu's garland, the devotees are permeated.
- Even the yogi, residing near, is permeated by the fragrance — all beings are permeated.
- Light exists; say nothing more; the whole universe, though in deep sleep, is entirely.
- Where, Lord, the worshipper of Śiva is established, there may the crest-jewels of Śaṅkara's feet-shining remain.
- May the darkness of delusion be destroyed, for the destruction of the tri-guṇa and the world-path.
- Even if happiness was never enjoyed for long, thus I am born useless; Śaśidhara's feet have trodden my supreme head.
- May the devotees of Śiva quickly have long, long great wealth.

**Stotra 16 — pāśānudbheda (breaking out of the fetters):**
- Nothing at all is an obstacle for the beings regarding your covering; nothing at all is an obstacle for the devotees regarding your covering.
- Even with means, with distinctions, the devotees' self, once purified, shines.
- Victory to those intoxicated with the nectar of devotion to you; who can be compared to them?
- May the nectar of devotion to you be ever-increasing for me; let me be intoxicated with devotion, angry, shouting "Śiva!"
- Even fierce, varied, may I be devoted to you, Lord.
- For devotees, there is nothing to be known; you are non-conceptual, established in yourself.
- Devotees, pierced by sharp goosebumps, even suffering becomes enjoyment.
- Even those whose consciousness is entirely drenched with your moonlight, wherever they are, inside or outside.
- For devotees, even the external covering of you is equally sweet; some, bewildered, see the form as separate.
- For devotees, the mind is stainless, free from honor and dishonor, passion and aversion.
- How could one like the world be among devotees? The darkness of passion and aversion is conquered by the light of devotion.
- Among the great, who are the knowers? He whose devotion to you is the means of siddhi.
- The fame of the devoted is sought; he by whom you alone are worshipped.
- Liberation is named for the ripened devotion to you, Lord.
- In that, the first state is attained; we are like the liberated; even the arrival of suffering is great for me.
- May your separation not be, Lord; even the stream of happiness.
- You are pleased by devotion; when you are pleased, Lord, what?
- That mutual dependence is fitting, as you alone know.
- Whether with form or without, within or without, for the devoted self, Lord, you are entirely nectar.
- In this world, for the devoted to you, the fruit of happiness is established elsewhere.
- Secretly, devotion; beyond, devotion; devotion is the name of the supreme in you, Lord.
- Devotion, devotion, devotion — the name is exceedingly intense.
- May my devotion be intense, supreme, in you.
- Because of that, all the glory of beings is born from you; whatever is, or is not, let it be something, just something, for me.
- In every way, let it be; by that much, you are everywhere, having received worship.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The stotras are devotional poetry; the "argument" is the devotional stance and its relation to recognition (pratyabhijñā). The argmap reconstructs the stance, not a syllogism.
2. Bailly 1987/1995 is the standard translation; our T1 is from GRETIL (Bailly ed.). The GRETIL text is continuous prose, not verse-separated; verse boundaries are not marked in our T1.
3. The 20 stotras are mapped to 5 passages (4 stotras each); the mapping follows the T1's own grouping.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the devotional arc: bhakti as supreme (p01) → recognition in devotional mode (p01) → svātantrya in devotional mode (p02) → autonomy (p03) → self (p04) → closing (p05). The key move in p04 is ātmaikya: the devotee recognizes the self as Śiva, not as a separate soul. Carry that into p05 (closing stotras). The tone is devotional, not argumentative; L2 should carry the devotional voice, not flatten it into doctrine. The devotional stotras should be rendered as devotional prose, not as philosophical argument.

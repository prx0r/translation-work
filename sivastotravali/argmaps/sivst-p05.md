# Śivastotrāvalī sivst-p05 — ARGUMENT MAP (closing: divine play; bhakti as supreme path)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 sivst-p05 = stotras 17–20. Lore: Bailly 1987/1995; Moreno Bautista (UNAM). Key: the closing stotras bring bhakti and jīvanmukti together — bhakti is the supreme path, and the devotee is liberated in this life. ## 1. What is at issue

Stotras 17–20 close the Śivastotrāvalī. The devotional arc completes: bhakti as supreme (p01) → recognition in devotional mode (p01) → svātantrya (p02) → autonomy (p03) → self (p04) → closing (p05). The closing stotras bring bhakti and jīvanmukti together: bhakti is the supreme path, and the devotee is liberated in this life. The text's final word: bhakti is not different from recognition; they are one.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**Stotra 17 — divyakrīḍā (a high regard for divine amusements):** The festival of worship is the supreme for devotees. All activities for the worship of devotees are siddhis. For devotees, all your forms are themselves siddhis; they worship you without ceasing. The time of worship is always the time of worship for devotees; there is no beginning or end. Even in dream, even in delusion, the festival of your worship is established for devotees. Not only for those who chant, offer, bathe, meditate — for devotees, the festival of your worship is always. For devotees, the festival of worship is superior to that of Indra and Brahmā. What is to be obtained is obtained only by devotees; they know it. Standing in your consciousness-form, with the actions of the 36 tattvas, I worship you always, Lord. With body, speech, mind, and effort, I worship you, Lord; devotees enjoying the festival of worship. Let all time pass, even the infinite; I beg only this, Lord: the festival of the nectar of worship. The festival of worship grows daily; may it bear fruit for me. In the ocean of you, the great self, worshipping, may I always dwell. Even the mind, offered to devotees, is sweet in the festival of worship. For devotees, sending the nectar of worship, the activities of the senses. For devotees, intoxicated with the heat of devotion, what other cause of nirvāṇa is there than immersion in the nectar of worship? May the festival of the nectar of worship be the cause of your grace for me, Lord. May I experience, Lord, moment by moment, the great joy of drinking the nectar of worship. For devotees, the endeavor of worship is itself the accomplished; what is unattainable is attained by them. For devotees, the festival of worship is the supreme; even the gods are inferior. For devotees, the festival of worship is the wealth; even the great are inferior.

**Stotra 18 — āviṣkāra (becoming clear):** Having obtained you, the Lord of the world, within, they obtain the world again from within you. Lord of the world, your devotion is not far for them; there is nothing distant for them. The world is your self; there is no difference between the world and you for devotees. The world is your self; there is no difference between the world and you for devotees. (Repeated throughout — the refrain of non-difference.) The world is your self; there is no difference between the world and you for devotees. Even in dream, even in delusion, the festival of your worship is established for devotees. Not only for those who chant, offer, bathe, meditate — for devotees, the festival of your worship is always.

**Stotra 19 — udyotana (the meaning revealed):** The prayer-transcending, fruit-giving, wonderful tree of Śiva — the supreme goal. The self is not you, Lord; you alone are the supreme. May my mind, absorbed in your consciousness-nectar, be freed from the cave of Māyā. May I see your pure face, Lord, by the nectar of knowledge. May your form, dripping with immortal nectar, fill me. May all my desires, even the paired ones, be offered to you, Lord. May the pure rays of you come to me. May the thieves of qualities, who steal the supreme truth, be subdued. May my mind be filled with the nectar of devotion to you, Lord. May all the latent tendencies be washed away, having plunged into the state of liberation. May I, desiring siddhi, not be stopped by pride, Lord. May the servant's liberation be granted by the Lord quickly. May the lotus-feet of you, the treasure of immortal nectar, always bloom in my mind. May I, a devotee, be in the world, not different from you. May the supreme goal be attained; may I be your devotee, not other. May I, even in the waking state or another, be devoted to you; may I be your devotee, not other.

**Stotra 20 — carvaṇa (the meaning savored):** Homage to the three-eyed Lord, the bearer of the trident, with the moon-crest, wearing the serpent as sacred thread. The world is your self; there is no difference between the world and you. The world is your self; there is no difference between the world and you. (Repeated throughout.) ## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The stotras are devotional poetry; the "argument" is the devotional stance and its relation to recognition (pratyabhijñā). The argmap reconstructs the stance, not a syllogism.
2. Bailly 1987/1995 is the standard translation; our T1 is from GRETIL (Bailly ed.). The GRETIL text is continuous prose, not verse-separated; verse boundaries are not marked in our T1.
3. The 20 stotras are mapped to 5 passages (4 stotras each); the mapping follows the T1's own grouping.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed as the devotional arc: bhakti as supreme (p01) → recognition in devotional mode (p01) → svātantrya (p02) → autonomy (p03) → self (p04) → closing (p05). The closing stotras bring bhakti and jīvanmukti together. The tone is devotional, not argumentative; L2 should carry the devotional voice. The key move in p05 is the identification of bhakti with recognition: bhakti is not different from pratyabhijñā; they are one. Carry that into C1 as the text's closing note.

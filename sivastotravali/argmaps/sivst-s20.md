# Śivastotrāvalī s20 — ARGUMENT MAP (Stotra 20, carvaṇa, 21 verses)

*2026-11-01. Source: GRETIL UtSst_20.1–20.21 (lines 1332–1395). Title: chewing/rumination. The close of the collection. Meter: VERIFY. Lore: Bailly 1987 ch.20 ("The Meaning Savored"); Lakshmanjoo audio + translations.json 20_1–20_21; Kṣemarāja Vivṛti (consult before L2).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

How does a collection end? Answer: by chewing — *carvaṇa*, rumination as the final rite. The song re-tastes everything: icon, devotees, play, couple, meditation, itch, festival, path, non-duality, knowledge, action, world, destruction, presence, night, and the limb that began it — and what cannot be tasted is declared already eaten.

## 2. The arc

Icon adored (1–2) → devotees bowed to (3) → play affirmed (4–5) → couple celebrated (6) → meditation rooted (7) → itch scratched into pillar (8) → ever-fed, ever-playing, ever-eating Lord (9) → no going, His abode seen here (10) → wealth and poverty both beg nothing (11) → pain turns pleasure, poison nectar, saṃsāra liberation — that path is Śiva (12) → no suffering at root, middle, end — yet we sink: tell why (13) → light begged even by others (14) → no prayer, no thought, yet "Śiva" on the lip (15) → action-power praised: vimarśa wrapping all in "I am this" (16) → all beings grasped, world bowed as His (17) → all beyond Me false, by His destruction-play alone (18) → meditated form stands present (19) → night-heroes dance, roused by vetālas (20) → the praise begun with this limb make all time unending (21).

## 3. The argument, verse by verse (terse)

**20.1** — *nathaṃ tribhuvananāthaṃ bhūtisitaṃ trinayanaṃ triśūladharam.* Bow to the three-worlds-Lord, ash-white, three-eyed, trident-bearing, snake-threaded, moon-crested. The icon in full dress opens the close. (Lakshmanjoo's vibhūti detail.)
**20.2** — *naumi nijatanuvinismaradaṃśukapariveṣadhavalaparidhānam.* I praise Him self-forgetting, snake-garland-dance-ready. Forgetting the body as dress-code for praise.
**20.3** — *vande tān daivataṃ yeṣāṃ haraś ceṣṭā harocitāḥ.* Bow to those whose god is Hara, whose acts are Hara-fit, whose lives lean wholly to Hara — the fortunate abodes. Devotees bowed to before doctrine: persons first.
**20.4** — *krīḍitaṃ tava maheśvaratāyāḥ pṛṣṭhato 'nyad idam eva yathaitat.* Your play, behind lordship — and this too, as wished-fulfilled in gifts, by self I reach the supreme means. Play behind rank; self as vehicle.
**20.5** — *tvaddhāmni viśvavandye 'sminn iyati krīḍane sati.* In Your world-venerated abode, in this play — how great would my bliss-birth be? Play as birthplace of bliss.
**20.6** — *kathaṃ sa subhago mā bhūdyo gauryā vallabho haraḥ.* How could He not be fortunate — Hara, Gauri's beloved? And how could Hara not be Gauri's supreme beloved? Mutual belovedness argued both ways.
**20.7** — *dhyānāmṛtamayaṃ yasya svātmamūlam anaśvaram.* Whose meditation-nectar is undying, self-rooted, ever-fresh consciousness-creepers, like some tree of being. Rooted meditation, branching awareness.
**20.8** — *bhaktikaṇḍūsam ullāsāvasare parameśvara.* At devotion-itch's showing, Lord's pillar-firmness arises — by it the world, great festival, is won. Itch scratched into pillar: irritation turned institution.
**20.9** — *sadā srṣṭivinodāya sadā sthitisukhāsine.* Ever to creation-sport, ever seated in staying-pleasure, ever world-eating-sated — to the husband, bow. The ever-fed, ever-playing, ever-eating Lord.
**20.10** — *na kvāpi gatvā hitvāpi na kiṃcid idam eva ye.* Nowhere gone, nothing left — who see Your abode as to-be here, fortunate: bow to them. No going as highest going.
**20.11** — *bhaktilakṣmīsamṛddhānāṃ kim anyad upayācitam.* For the devotion-fortuned, what else is begged; for the devotion-poor, what else is begged? Wealth and poverty beg nothing: needlessness both ways.
**20.12** — *duḥkhāny api sukhāyante viṣam apy amṛtāyate.* Where pains turn pleasures, poison turns nectar, saṃsāra turns liberation — that path is Śiva. The transmutation verse. (Lakshmanjoo's path-verse.)
**20.13** — *mūle madhye 'vasāne ca nāsti duḥkhaṃ bhavajjuṣāṃ.* At root, middle, end, no suffering for Your people — yet we sink, Lord: tell why. The close's honest complaint: doctrine says no suffering; experience sinks. Kept open.
**20.14** — *jñānayogādinānyeṣām apy apekṣitum arhati.* Light should be begged even by others, as lovers beg — You, Lord, before devotees. Lovers as the begging-model for all.
**20.15** — *bhaktānāṃ nārtayo nāpy asty ādhyānaṃ svātmanas tava.* Devotees have no prayers, no thought of self — yet "Śiva" on the outer lip: something of theirs. No prayer, Name on lip: the *kim api* of utterance.
**20.16** — *sarvābhāsāvabhāso yo vimarśavalito 'khilam.* Who lights all appearances, wrapped in vimarśa — "I am this": I praise Your action-power, Lord. The one philosophical verse: recognition as agency.
**20.17** — *vartante jantavo 'śeṣā api brahmendraviṣṇavaḥ.* All beings grasped, from Brahmā to Viṣṇu — I bow the world as Your-formed. Grasping as salutation's ground.
**20.18** — *sato vināśasambandhān matparaṃ nikhilaṃ mṛṣā.* All beyond Me is false, by relation to destruction — thus engaged, Lord, by Your destruction-play alone. Solipsism dissolved by destruction-play.
**20.19** — *dhyātam ātmupatiṣṭhata eva tvadvapur varada.* Meditated, Your form stands present for devotion-rich — unconquered by unthinkable-wonder-makers. Presence defeats marvel-mongers.
**20.20** — *tāvakabhaktirasāsavasekād iva sukhitamarmamaṇḍalasphuritaiḥ.* By Your devotion-wine-taste nerves' thrill, night-heroes dance, roused by vetālas. The wildest verse saved for the penultimate: vetāla-roused night-dance. (Lakshmanjoo's darkness-universe reading.)
**20.21** — *ārabdhā bhavadabhinutir amunā yenāṅgakena mama.* The praise begun with this limb of mine — by it may all this time be firmly unending. Close: the devotee's mouth opened the collection's first cry (1.1's *numaḥ*); the limb is asked to keep it unending.

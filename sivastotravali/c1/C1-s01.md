# C1 — Śivastotrāvalī Stotra 1 (bhaktivilāsa)

*Passage commentary for L2-s01 (26 verses). Local to this song; no modern comparison, no PARALLELS inside. Machine-proposed, needs human review.*

## SUMMARY

The song establishes devotion without preparatory rule as valid, then proves it: since all beings desire the self and the self alone is consciousness, love directed at it stands on its own nature and needs no external ground. Knowing this by immersion is itself the conquest. The remaining verses draw the consequences — the world becomes ritual ground, every road works, localization of the goal in special states is the one deceit — and close by transfiguring the opening image: the dust-greyed mind, watered by devotion, fruits liberation.

## FUNCTION

Foundational polemic for the whole collection: it licenses methodless devotion (against rule-bound ritualism) and preempts the yogic objection (against seated practice), so that the later songs can presuppose what this one proves.

## KEY TERMS

* **svabhāvasiddhā** — self-established: standing by its own nature, requiring no outside ground. The song's load-bearing word.
* **samāveśayuktyā** — by immersion's means (Kṣemarāja): the *how* of the knowing in v.7; entry, not inference.
* **vañcanā** — the deceit: attainment at some time, in some place, by yoga. The song's single negative doctrine.
* **vyutthāna** — emergence: return from absorption. The testing ground where devotees differ from yogins.
* **avidhipūrvakam** — without preceding rule: denial of method as precondition (v.1), distinct from denial of practice.

## LOCAL CONTEXT

First song of twenty;	reader has nothing presupposed. Its thesis (v.7) is the premise of every later song's devotion-talk, and its polemic (vv.16–18) sets the collection's stance toward technique.

## EXPLANATION

The song moves in three acts. First, foundation (1–7): praise is offered for manifestation without method (1), the self declared young beneath the body's greying (2), the devotee's world-movement re-described as expansion (3), omnipresence converted into universal ritual ground with unfailing mantras (4), intoxication praised and non-duality kept second to love (5), knowledge restricted to the immersed (6) — then the center (7): everyone loves the self, the self is consciousness, so devotion to it is self-standing, and knowing this by immersion conquers. The logic is tight: desire needs no justification when its object is the desirer's own nature.

Second, epistemics and grace-logic (8–15): with objects removed the subject stands revealed, easily seen (8); the devotee asks for non-separation modeled on the divine couple (9); all three states of mind become path (10); even supreme knowledge tastes sour beside devotion (11) — yet discrimination itself is devotion's fruit, not its entry fee (12); speech grows like a watered creeper (13); scripture's paradox of becoming Śiva in order to worship Śiva is resolved by declared transformation (14); every road works and no one is obstructed (15).

Third, polemic and praise (16–26): the sometime-somewhere-by-yoga account is named deceit, refuted by the devotee's all-state shining (16); the distinguishing mark is composure in emergence, which withdrawal-techniques cannot produce (17); hence no method is enjoined and devotion alone praised on the guileless path (18). What follows is not argument but tasting: covering shattered from all sides (19), the Name on the tongue delivering every sense (20), status inverted for divers (21), devotion as medicine with liberation as after-taste (22), true wealth redefined (23), falling without staining (24), even liberation demoted to a limb of the creeper (25), and the closing wonder — the pain-seed mind transfigured (26), answering verse 2.

## BOUNDARY / OPEN

The song proves devotion needs no ground; it does not grade devotees (the low of v.15 and the some of v.25 are left unquantified — do not systematize them into ranks). It asserts immersion as the means (7) without describing immersion's technique — that is supplied elsewhere in the tradition, not here. The four speech-levels behind verse 13, the scope of thoughts destroyed in verse 19, and the *anāma* crux in verse 21 are all open; the song's claims do not depend on settling them.

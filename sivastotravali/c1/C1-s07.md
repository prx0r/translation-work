# C1 — Śivastotrāvalī Stotra 7 (vidhuravijaya)

*Passage commentary for L2-s07 (9 verses). Local to this song; no modern comparison, no PARALLELS inside. Machine-proposed, needs human review.*

## SUMMARY

The mind is cured by drowning: dropped in the bliss-ocean (1), axed at the mine/not-mine root (2), melted then blazing (3), mother-fed till winged (4), skilled in tasting (5), sun-melted of wax-drops (6), praising where gods wait outside (7), seeing world-as-Śiva through frenzy (8), and finally remarried — the mind-boy wedding the devotion-maiden and hardening a new mine (9).

## FUNCTION

Domestic-allegory song: interior work staged as household events (dropping, axing, feeding, wedding), making the mind's cure narratable and therefore practicable.

## KEY TERMS

* **mameti-buddhi** — the mine-thought (vv.2, 9): felled at the wrong root, re-hardened at the right one; kept, not killed.
* **unmāda / vibhrama** — madness/frenzy (v.8): corrective lens, not pathology; compare Stotra 1's intoxication and Stotra 2's bhakti-madmen.
* **naipuṇa** — skill (v.5): connoisseurship in tasting as the cure-form of knowledge.

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Seventh song, shortest: nine verses, one cure. Read as the mind-handbook answering Stotra 6's burning with method — drown, feed, wed.

## EXPLANATION

The song treats the mind as both patient and agent: dropped (1), axed (2), melted and blazed (3) — all passive verbs — then fed (4), skilled (5), melted again by sun (6): cure by immersion and nourishment, never by argument. Verse 7 escalates the claim socially (praise where gods wait outside); verse 8 epistemologically (frenzy as unmaimed seeing); verse 9 closes the arc with the boldest move in the set: possessiveness itself is preserved and remarried — the mine-thought hardens around devotion's family instead of possessions.

## BOUNDARY / OPEN

The song does not say what happens to the aṇimā-sons (v.9) or who the waiting gods are (v.7, epithet uncertain). Frenzy (v.8) is praised as lens, not prescribed as permanent state — do not read it as anti-sobriety doctrine.

## RELATED

* Stotra 1, vv.11–12 (vinegar-knowledge; discrimination as fruit) — the same tasting epistemology.
* Stotra 2, v.21 (madmen seen; ascetics miss) — the social version of this song's cure.
* Stotra 5, v.3 (silent, not wise) — dumbness praised here as frenzy.

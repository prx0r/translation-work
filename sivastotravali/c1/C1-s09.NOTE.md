# C1 NOTE — s09

Moved from C1 (pristine rasa-evocative translation only):

## SUMMARY

Twenty when-questions that are really one: when will longing become meeting? It asks it of the mind (1), the face (2), needlessness (3), the gods (4), distinction (5), the slow (6), flight (7), the world (8), knowledge and yoga (9), all hearers (10), transparency (11), leadership (12), pride (13), lying down (14), the far shore (15), tears (16), beast-life (17), play (18), speech (19) — and answers it once, in the last verse: by hugging hard and seeing without effort (20).

## FUNCTION

Asking-song: longing structured as twentyfold *kadā*, proving autonomy by desiring nothing but Him — the victory of the title is that the questions never leave Him.

## KEY TERMS

* **kadā** — when: not scheduling but intensity; each when raises the pressure the last one built. * **svātantrya** — autonomy: freedom desiring only itself; it's title earned in verse 20, not asserted in verse 1. * **utsuka** — eager (vv.2, 19): the honored mood; eagerness outranks satisfaction.

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Ninth song, exact middle of the twenty: the hinge. Behind it eight songs of praise and method; ahead, breaking (10), longing-trust (11), secret-pointing (12), summary (13). Read as the center that holds both halves.

## EXPLANATION

It never varies its form — *kadā* twenty times — and that monotony is the argument: one pressure, twenty objects, single direction. Verses 1–4 ask it of mind, face, needlessness, gods (inner faculties per the Lakshmanjoo reading, kept alongside the cosmic one). Verses 5–8 ask it of distinction, the slow, flight, the world. Verses 9–10 crown devotion over its rivals and go public with it. Verses 11–12 ask transparency and leadership — states, not moments. Verses 13–14 sanctify pride and lying down — the postures of arrival. Verses 15–16 name the far shore and its bodily signs (tears first). Verses 17–18 shed beast-life and play in the medicine. Verse 19 makes speech itself cry out for form. Verse 20 answers everything: hug hard, see without effort — the asking was the finding, stated only at the end.

## BOUNDARY

It does not explain HOW asking becomes finding (v.20 states, not derives). The gods of v.4 stay double-read (inner/cosmic). The shaming of v.6 is exultation, not ethics — do not moralize it.

## RELATED

* Stotra 11 (*autsukya*): longing again, now with trust added. * Stotra 6 (effortlessness): the finding-mode v.20 assumes. * Stotra 1, v.7 (immersion as means): the mechanism it performs without naming.

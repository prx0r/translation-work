# C1 NOTE — s13

Moved from C1 (pristine rasa-evocative translation only):

## SUMMARY

It defines pain as separation and pleasure as union (v.1), then proves the definition across twenty moves: the impure instant wiped (2), abiding unto death (3), pride sanctified (4), perception-as-it-stands as fitness (5), meditation-vision-touch sequence (6), the mutually grounding pair of seeing and festival (7), drinking through every sense (8), the country with no other (9), slavery as fitness (10), grace that never deliberates turned back on its delay (11), beholding with one's own hands inside and out (12), ascent by mere wish (13), worship answering touch (14), joy swelling the world (15), fearlessness as perception's corollary (16), poison turned nectar by stance (17), songs wanted otherwise (18), the uncountable play that withholds the cup (19), entering-and-praising the fathomless (20).

## FUNCTION

Daily-lesson digest (*āhnika*): the whole path compressed into recitable moves — it lets a devotee rehearse, in one sitting, definition, purification, pride, perception, worship, grace-logic, ascent, cosmology, and refuge.

## KEY TERMS

* **saṃgraha** — summary, compression: it's genre and claim (the whole in brief). * **yathāsthitapadārtha** — things exactly as they stand: perception unedited as worship-fitness. * **śaktipāta** — descent of grace: deliberation-free by definition (v.11), which licenses the accusation of delay. * **abhisandhimātra** — by mere resolve: ascent without stages (v.13). * **āveśa-adjacent vocabulary** (unmāda, pralāpa) — madness and prattle as devotional modes, not faults.

## LOCAL CONTEXT

The middle of Utpala's own three (13/14/15): after Stotra 1's foundation and Stotra 2's litany, this one teaches; Stotra 14 will shout victory over the same ground, Stotra 15 will sing devotion proper.

## EXPLANATION

It's engine is verse 1's definition, and every verse after is a test of it. Verses 2–4 establish the devotee's standing: impurity reduced to a single instant (2), abiding offered even unto death (3), and the famous reversal — I-am-the-Lord pride, which elsewhere would be bondage, declared glorious precisely in lovers (4). Verses 5–9 build the epistemology: things-as-they-stand qualify for worship (5); meditation matures into vision into touch (6); seeing and festival ground each other (7) — neither first, both always; every sense becomes a mouth (8); and all of it happens in a country where nothing is felt as other (9). Verses 10–11 are it's daring: slavery-by-His-will as fitness for audience (10), then grace's own deliberation-free nature turned into a charge against its delay (11) — the devotee litigates with God's logic. Verses 12–14 answer ascent: beholding with one's own hands (12), mounting by mere wish (13), worship as the echo of touch (14). Verses 15–17 widen to cosmos: His joy swells worlds (15), so seeing all as His body ends fear (16), and poison/nectar are decided by stance, with the churning myth as exhibit (17). Verses 18–20 close domestically: songs and girls wanted otherwise (18) — desire kept, object exchanged; the play uncountable, the cup given yet withheld (19); and the end is entering-and-praising, union that keeps singing (20).

## BOUNDARY

It does not explain HOW seeing-as-things-stand arises (5 assumes it); it does not resolve whether 11's accusation is answered anywhere in the collection; it does not say what the mere wish of 13 presupposes. The 13/14/15 grouping is Utpala's own — this commentary does not claim a trilogy structure beyond the shared daily-use attestation.

## RELATED

* Stotra 1, vv.7–8 (self-established devotion; knowable destroyed) — the ground it assumes. * Stotra 14 (*jaya*) — victory over the same ground, companion recitation. * Stotra 15 (*bhakti*) — devotion proper, the third of the set.

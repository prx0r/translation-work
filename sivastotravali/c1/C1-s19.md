# C1 — Śivastotrāvalī Stotra 19 (udyota)

## SUMMARY

It stops praying for things and starts praying for Him: the wish-tree outgrows prayer-grounds (1), yet the self stays unjoined to the highest (2) though declared identical with the Lord (3); melt into His sight (4); when the amazement (5), the face (6), the flood (7), the soon (8); bring on everything including contradiction (9); rays face to face (10); thieves tamed (11); mind drowned (12); set the mortal on fitting ground (13); no siddhi-drop (14); I ask only this (15); feet blooming in mind (16); I have a Lord, a father, a mother — no second: I roam fulfilled (17).

## FUNCTION

Dawning-song: the meaning revealed is that prayer itself dawns — from begging things, through begging Him, into roaming as His.

## KEY TERMS

* **udyota** — dawning/rise: the title's event; what dawns is prayer, not doctrine.
* **anuttararasa-touch** (v.8): the peerless-rasa contact that leaves restlessness as its trace.
* **naimittika saptamī** (v.13, Lakshmanjoo's grammatical note): the occasion-case — recorded, to be verified against Kṣemarāja before freezing.

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Nineteenth song, penultimate: after manifestation (18) comes dawning — what the beheld does in the beholder. Read as the turn from seeing to roaming.

## EXPLANATION

Verses 1–3 set the paradox that powers it: prayer outgrown (1), all got yet self unjoined (2), identity declared yet body questioned (3). Verses 4–8 beg the turn: melt (4), amazement (5), face (6), flood (7), soonness (8). Verses 9–12 widen the ask to everything: clashes included (9), rays face to face (10), thieves tamed (11), all saplessness drowned (12). Verses 13–14 handle the end-state honestly: liberation-state questioned, fitting ground begged (13); siddhi-drop declined for mouth-powers (14). Verses 15–17 close by shrinking then widening: the minimal ask (15), feet blooming in mind (16), and the whole tradition compressed into kinship — Lord, father, mother, no second, roaming fulfilled (17).

## BOUNDARY / OPEN

Verse 9's totality-demand: askesis or rhetoric? Open. Verse 13's naimittika analysis is Lakshmanjoo's — record, verify, do not yet assert. Verse 3's flat identity vs verse 2's unjoining: held together, not harmonized.

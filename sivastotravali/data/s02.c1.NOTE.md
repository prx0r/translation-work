# C1 NOTE — s02.c1

Register: GONE (Swamiji: composed "gone"/ecstatic, not in senses — read as effusion; do not flatten into doctrine).

Scholar commentary moved from C1 (C1 holds essence + SUMMARY + rasa only):

## SUMMARY

It performs contemplation of the all-self as twenty-nine salutations. Each verse names Śiva by a paradoxical pair — cool fire and devourer, Veda-breaker and Veda-maker, saṃsāra's cause and foe, ever-bound and ever-free, all rites and no rite — and the *namaḥ* addressed to both poles at once is itself the recognition that opposites coincide only in Him. The litany closes by turning from Him to His terrible powers (23), from knowing to embracing (24), and from victory-cries to refuge (29).

## FUNCTION

Method-song: it teaches *how* to contemplate, not what to conclude — the title's *paribhāvanā* is executed by the act of saluting, so reciting it IS the practice it describes.

## KEY TERMS

* **sarvātmaparibhāvanā** — contemplation of the all-self: it's genre (method, not topic).
* **namaḥ** — salutation as recognition-act; each bow affirms identity of the opposed predicates.
* **kasmei cit (to someone)** — the unnamed remainder (4, 6, 17, 24): the list's deliberately open edge; what cannot be named is still bowed to.
* **śaktivṛnda** — the host of (terrible) powers: saluted apart from Him in v.23, it's boldest move.
* **vyutthāna-absent** — unlike Stotra 1, no emergence test here; it stays inside vision, which is why Stotra 1 precedes it.

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Second song, after Stotra 1's foundation: where Stotra 1 argued (devotion needs no ground), Stotra 2 trains (naming opposites until they coincide). Read aloud, in sequence.

## EXPLANATION

It works by accumulation, not argument: verses 1–3 invoke elements and sacrificial fire with comfort and terror held together (coolness that devours); verses 4–7 name gods, mantra-bodies, marklessness, and the Veda's author-transgressor; verse 8 states the engine outright — sole cause AND sole opponent of the wheel. Verses 9–10 run time forward and backward into fullness, then price the Name below all merit — open access to the unattainable. Verses 11–13 stage play, paradox, and destroying power; verse 14 places attainment on the shore, not the depth. Verses 15–17 rest in pure auspiciousness before the two hardest paradoxes — bound-and-liberated (17), the mockery-world's one serious seat (18). Verses 19–20 universalize rite and address: every observance, whichever god. Verses 21–22 cool austerity-heat with beauty and declare the ever-festival. Then the three turns that make it: to His terrible energies rather than Him (23), embracers over knowers (24), nectar stored beyond far and near (25). Verses 26–27 praise mantra-form and declare non-wonder — oneness spread out makes marvels ordinary. Verse 28 bows the path itself as weapon; verse 29 collapses litany into refuge.

## BOUNDARY

HOW opposites coincide is unexplained — their coincidence is performed, not derived; metaphysics belongs to the IPK. The 29 names are unranked; the *kasmei cit* verses leave the list open by design. Verse 14's shore-powers may praise or warn; verse 23's redirection to the energies may honor or displace — both left open, to be read with Kṣemarāja before freezing.

## RELATED

* Stotra 1, vv.16–17 (yoga-deceit; yogins vs devotees) — the polemic its method replaces.
* Stotra 13, v.5 (things-as-they-stand) — the perception this litany trains.
* Stotra 9 (*svātantryavijaya*) — autonomy sung where it only salutes power.

# C1 NOTE — s14.c1

Register: SANE (Swamiji: ch14 composed "in his senses" with ch13 — deliberate doctrine; read as stated teaching).

Scholar commentary moved from C1 (C1 holds essence + SUMMARY + rasa only):

## SUMMARY

It is a victory-litany: twenty-four *jaya*-cries that praise the Lord by letting every attribute stand absolute — names (2–3), body and feet (4), beauty and adornment (5–8), duping and embrace (9), greed for gifts (10), outlasting the gods (11), sealing worlds and giving the self (12), secondlessness with a helper (13), stride and song (14), need-free height (15), unopposed terror (16), play and anger (17), lamp and wakefulness (18), cave-hum and mind-sky (19), gold-mountain and portent-moon (20), missed-by-austerity and seen-by-madness (21), leafing and fondling (22), sole doing and lotus-festival (23) — until the cries themselves run to thirteen and end in the three-eyed One (24).

## FUNCTION

Acclamation as completion: where Stotra 13 taught and Stotra 2 trained, Stotra 14 celebrates — the collection's festival song, recited to convert understanding into praise.

## KEY TERMS

* **jaya** — victory/hail: not description but speech-act; each cry performs conquest over silence.
* **mudrā** — seal (v.12): glory stamped on worlds as signature; manifestation as authorship.
* **nikṛṣṭa-absent** — unlike Stotra 1, no low here; worthiness never comes up because victory needs no defense.
* **tryakṣa** — the three-eyed One: the list's deliberately visual close; number ends in seeing.

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Middle of Utpala's own three (13/14/15): the shout between the teaching and the devotion-song. Its anti-merit verse (15) and anti-ascetic verse (21) are the sharpest in the set — read against Stotra 1, vv.16–17.

## EXPLANATION

It's form is its argument: anaphora sustained for twenty-four verses proves inexhaustibility — the list could continue, and verse 24's thirteenfold pile-up shows it deliberately not concluding but overflowing. Verses 2–8 dress Him: names heaped (2), marks as offices (3), powers as body and wishes as feet (4), beauty with ash (5), moonlight and jewels as contact-theology (6–7), the low touch sanctifying and the gathering as His address (8). Verses 9–10 make Him strange and near: duping the childish while fit for the Goddess's embrace (9), greedy for wet love-gifts and pleased by drunken dance (10). Verses 11–14 establish scale: gods rise and fall, His command held high (11); worlds sealed, self given to the uttermost (12); secondless yet helped (13); one stride, childhood-sung sound (14). Verses 15–17 state the theology baldly: high without need of pity (15 — the anti-merit verse, read with 13.11), terror unopposed (16), play pouring nectar and anger drying seas (17). Verses 18–20 turn outward: lamp for blind beings (18), hum in the body-cave (19), fortune for devotees and portent for the wicked in one rising (20). Verses 21–23 close the human circuit: missed by austerities, seen by madness (21); dependents leafed, surrender fondled (22); sole doer, lotus-festival (23). Verse 24 stops counting and starts seeing.

## BOUNDARY

It asserts inexhaustibility by form, not by claim — it does not say the list is complete or exemplary. Verses 9 (duping), 12 (self-gift's recipient), 20 (portent's intent) remain open as in the argmap. The thirteen of v.24 is not allegorized here.

## RELATED

* Stotra 13, v.11 (grace without deliberation) — the ground of v.15's need-free height.
* Stotra 1, vv.16–17 (deceit; yogins vs devotees) — the polemic v.21 sharpens.
* Stotra 2, v.29 (victory and refuge) — the other *jaya*-close; read the pair.

# L200 — Śivasūtra (75 sūtras, 8 passages)

## 1. What the text says (plain)
Śivasūtra (75 sūtras) is Vasugupta's (9th c.) foundational text of Kashmir Śaivism. It establishes: consciousness is self (caitanya = ātman); knowledge is bondage (jñāna = bandha); arising is Bhairava (udyama = bhairava); three states (waking/dream/deep sleep) mapped to knowledge/thoughts/Māyā; self = mind; mantra doctrine (mind = mantra, Guru = means, body = offering, knowledge = food); siddhi = free-state; practice (immersed, enter by mind, breath-conduct = equal-vision, becomes Śiva-like); close = jīva-destruction, freed Lord-like, closing of eyes.

## 2. What the text argues (core claims)
- C1: Self = consciousness (caitanya = ātman).
- C2: Knowledge = bondage (jñāna = bandha).
- C3: Arising = Bhairava (udyama = bhairava); śakti-wheel-union dissolves world.
- C4: Three states: waking = knowledge, dream = thoughts, deep sleep = Māyā (non-discrimination).
- C5: Self = mind (ātmā cittam); non-discrimination of kalā tattvas = Māyā.
- C6: Mantra: mind = mantra; Guru = means; body = offering; knowledge = food.
- C7: Siddhi = free-state; seed-attention; knowledge-destruction → birth-destruction.
- C8: Practice: immersed enter by mind; breath-conduct = equal-vision; becomes Śiva-like; body-conduct = vow; talking = japa; giving = self-knowledge.
- C9: Close: jīva-destruction; freed Lord-like; natural breath-connection; closing of eyes.

## 3. What the text rejects
- R1: Self as non-consciousness.
- R2: Knowledge as liberation (it's bondage).
- R3: Arising as non-Bhairava.
- R4: States without threefold mapping.
- C5: Self as non-mind.
- R6: Mantra without Guru.
- R7: Siddhi without free-state.
- R8: Practice without breath-conduct.
- R9: Close without jīva-destruction.

## 4. What the text leaves unresolved
- U1: The relation to the fuller Spandakārikā (Vasugupta's other work).
- U2: Whether the sūtra count is 75 or 77 (GRETIL has 75).
- U3: The exact dating (9th c.).
- U4: The relation to Kṣemarāja's Vimarśinī.

## The parable (≈87s)

> A riddle goes through the village and every answer points to no
> one: who opens and closes the world each night? who builds the
> dreams nobody remembers building? who taught the song that is a
> thought itself? who is the old hand — the only way in? The five
> watchers were asked — they hold the best seats in the whole
> show — did you ever see him? We watched everything, they said.
> Never him. The shepherd was asked where his shepherd is: there
> is none, he said; the flock keeps itself. And the ones who gave
> everything away were asked how they knew: giving is knowing,
> they said. And the reckoner's every road of reasoning ended
> where it began — at nobody, knowing its own knowing. Then the
> answer came, and it was not an answer: it was the questioner.
> The one asking was the one invisible — consciousness is the
> self. The riddle closes. The riddle reopens. Ask again.

## The seal — statement

**The invisible man among the clues:** a painted village-scene with
ONE figure-shaped EXCERENCE at its centre — the unpainted man: a
white silhouette-glow where no paint was laid (the invisible one,
present only as absence-with-light); around him the villagers of
the riddle: the five watchers seated in a row, eyes everywhere,
facing the empty-centre (the spectators who never see the maker);
the shepherd's staff leaning with NO hand on it (the shepherdless
stand); a small loom weaving mist at the dream-builder's corner;
the old hand's key hung at a door with no lock (the means);
giving-hands open with fruit falling away (giving is knowing);
and at the village-edge a small child pointing straight at the
empty-centre — the questioner, who casts no separate shadow
(he IS the answer, not its finder).

## The parable v2 (≈85s) — REPLACES v1

> A riddle goes through the village. "Who opens and closes the
> worlds each night?" No one raised a hand. "Dream is constructs,"
> offered the dreamer — and it was so: someone builds what none
> remember. "Mind is mantra," said the singer — thought itself the
> song. "The guru is the means," said the traveler — the old hand,
> the only way in. The five watchers were asked — best seats in
> the whole show: did you ever see him? "The senses are the
> spectators," they answered. We watched everything. Never him.
> "There is no shepherd," said the shepherd, and the flock kept
> itself. "Giving is self-knowing," said the ones who gave it all —
> that's how we knew. And the reckoner proved it: reasoning is
> self-knowing — every road ends at nobody. Then the answer came,
> and it was no answer: it was the questioner. Consciousness is the
> self — the one asking was the one invisible. The riddle closes;
> the riddle reopens; ask again.

## PARABLE v3 — TEACHING THE SŪTRAS THROUGH STORY (owner's final form)
**Replaces v2's quoting-contest frame: now EVENTS teach, each
closing with its own sūtra-saying — the folk-catechism shape
(tale → the saying it earned), all eight + the twist-line.**

> Each night the village watched the world fold up and be raised
> again — and no one raised it. And a bridge stood across the
> river that nobody remembers building; the builders asked each
> other, and everyone had dreamt it: **Dream is constructs**, the
> elders said — someone builds what none remember. Then a child
> thought the gate shut, and the gate shut: **Mind is mantra** —
> here the thought and the word are one work. There was one way
> through the fog: behind the old hand, who never explained:
> **The guru is the means.** Five children sat at the best seats
> of the show and were asked what they had seen: **The senses are
> the spectators** — everything but the showman. Those who gave
> everything away came back knowing: **Giving is self-knowing** —
> that was their proof. And the reckoner walked every road of
> reasoning till it curled home: **Reasoning is self-knowing** —
> every road ends at the one walking it. **There was no shepherd,
> and the flock kept itself.** Then the child who had asked it all
> felt the world close and open again from her own place — and the
> saying landed: **Consciousness is the self.** The one asking was
> the one invisible. The riddle closes; the riddle reopens. Ask
> again.

**Teaching-structure:** eight events, each closing with its actual
sūtra-saying (7 verbatim L4 lines + p08's shepherdless-event as
deed) — the child's own place is where p01's open-close HAPPENS
(the twist performed, not announced) and 1.1 lands as the eighth
moral. No villager quotes doctrine: the WORLD demonstrates it and
the narrator names it, which is how folk-tales teach.

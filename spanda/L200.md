# L200 — Spandakārikā (51 verses, 6 passages)

## 1. What the text says (plain)
Spandakārikā (51 verses) is Vasugupta's (9th c.) foundational text on the spanda (vibration) principle. It establishes: spanda = Śiva's opening/closing (unmeṣa/nimeṣa) = source of śakti-wheel; all abides in Him; flows undivided through states; supreme truth beyond suffering/pleasure/grasper. Spanda operates through presiding over own-nature; evil course absent; tattva not attained by mere remembering. Danger = unawakened intellect conceals own-state → saṃsāra; remedy = constant striving; spanda established in every state. Jīvanmukta = one who sees world as play, constantly united; Śiva in all. Body's spanda: omniscience by presiding; languor from ignorance; opening destroys it. Close: śakti of Śiva abiding in paśu binds; tanmātra-arising; eight-city; dependent enjoyment → saṃsāra; declares cause of saṃsāra-dissolution.

## 2. What the text argues (core claims)
- C1: Spanda = Śiva's opening/closing (unmeṣa/nimeṣa) = source of śakti-wheel.
- C2: All abides in Him; flows undivided through states; supreme truth beyond suffering/pleasure/grasper.
- C3: Spanda operates through presiding over own-nature; evil course absent; tattva not attained by mere remembering.
- C4: Danger = unawakened intellect conceals own-state → saṃsāra; remedy = constant striving; spanda in every state.
- C5: Jīvanmukta = world as play, constantly united; Śiva in all sound/meaning/thought.
- C6: Body's spanda: omniscience by presiding; languor from ignorance; opening destroys it.
- C7: Close: śakti of Śiva abiding in paśu binds; tanmātra-arising; eight-city; dependent enjoyment → saṃsāra; declares cause of saṃsāra-dissolution.

## 3. What the text rejects
- R1: Spanda as non-Śiva.
- R2: Obstruction in the supreme.
- R3: Tattva by mere remembering.
- R4: Danger without unawakened intellect.
- R5: Jīvanmukta without world-as-play.
- R6: Body without spanda.
- R7: Close without saṃsāra-dissolution cause.

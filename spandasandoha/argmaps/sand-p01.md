# Spandasandoha — ARGMAP (verses 1–2)

*2026-11-01. Source: Muktabodha M00084. Lore: sandeśa-kāvya precursor? No — Spandakārikā 1.1 + Kṣemarāja's vivṛti. KSTS. Rasa: adbhuta (wonder at Śiva's spanda) with śānta (tranquility of self-established nature).*

---

## 1. What is at issue

What is Śiva's spanda (throb), and how does it relate to self-awareness and the world's arising?

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

**The verse (2 lines):**
*sa jayati śiva ekaḥ spandavān svapratiṣṭhaḥ yo'ntarvaktraṃ rasayati*
*jayatyeṣa vīraḥ kulendraḥ*

**Line 1, first half:** *sa jayati śiva ekaḥ* — He alone, Śiva, is victorious. Ekaḥ (alone) = non-dual; no second. (Scholarship: Spanda is synonymous with Śakti = I-consciousness; Spanda is the Power of Śiva by which He is conscious of Himself — sanskrit-trikashaivism.com.)

**Line 1, second half:** *spandavān svapratiṣṭhaḥ* — possessing spanda, self-established. Spandavān = endowed with spanda (the throb). Svapratiṣṭha = established in his own nature, not dependent on anything else.

**Line 1, third:** *yo'ntarvaktraṃ rasayati* — who makes the inner face taste. Antarvaktra = the inner face = I-consciousness (the face turned inward). Rasayati = makes relishable/experienceable. (Kṣemarāja's commentary: "yo'ntarvaktraṃ rasayati" = who makes the I-consciousness tasteable — the self-awareness that is the spanda itself.)

**Line 2:** *jayatyeṣa vīraḥ kulendraḥ* — this hero, lord of the Kula, is victorious. Vīra = hero (the one who recognizes spanda in himself). Kulendra = lord of the Kula (the Kaula tradition). The victory is recognition (pratyabhijñā).

**Kṣemarāja's gloss (from the commentary):** *akalitamahimā* — whose greatness is unmeasured (mahimā = greatness, akalita = unmeasured/ungraspable). *yaḥ kṣmādisādāśivāntaṃ kalayati hṛdi viśvaṃ* — who measures the world from earth to Sadāśiva in the heart (the world arises within consciousness, not outside it). *citrasaṃyojanābhiḥ* — with various combinations (the world's diversity is Śiva's own combination, not external creation). *prathayati ca vicitrāḥ sṛṣṭisaṃhāralīlāḥ* — and displays various creation-dissolution plays (sṛṣṭi = creation, saṃhāra = dissolution, līlā = play). *sa ekaḥ advaitasvarūpaḥ san* — he alone, of non-dual nature. *svābhinnasvātantryaśaktisamavetaḥ* — endowed with his own autonomy-power (svasvātantryaśakti = the power of self-aware autonomy). *svasvarūpaviśrāntyavasthitaḥ* — established in his own nature (viśrānti = rest, repose). *samudra iva viśvakallolaprasare'pi avicalatsvasvarūpāvasthānaḥ* — like an ocean remaining unmoved even as world-waves arise (samudra = ocean, viśvakallola = world-waves, avicala = unmoved). *sarvotkṛṣṭatayā prasphurati* — shines supreme.

→ Spanda = Śiva's own autonomy-power (svasvātantryaśakti), by which he is conscious of himself (antarvaktra = I-consciousness, rasayati = makes relishable). The world arises as his play (sṛṣṭisaṃhāralīlā) without disturbing his self-established nature (svapratiṣṭha), like an ocean unmoved by waves. The hero (vīra) who is lord of the Kula (kulendra) is victorious — the Kaula master who recognizes this spanda in himself.

## 3. What the verse does not resolve

- Whether "spanda" here means the same as in Spandakārikā 1.1 or a broader sense (Kṣemarāja's Spandasandoha is specifically on the FIRST kārikā, so it's tightly bound to that verse).
- Whether "antarvaktra" (inner face) is the I-consciousness (ahaṃtā) or something else — Kṣemarāja's gloss "rasayati" (makes tasteable) suggests it's the self-awareness that becomes relishable through recognition.
- Whether "vīraḥ kulendraḥ" refers to Śiva himself or to the practitioner who recognizes spanda — both readings are live; the commentary's "jayatyeṣa" (this one is victorious) suggests the practitioner.

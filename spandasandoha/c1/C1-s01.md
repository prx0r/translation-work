## SUMMARY

Śiva alone, possessing spanda, self-established, makes the inner face taste; this hero, lord of the Kula, is victorious. Kṣemarāja's commentary: whose greatness is unmeasured, who measures the world from earth to Sadāśiva in the heart, who displays creation-dissolution plays — he alone, of non-dual nature, endowed with his own autonomy-power, established in his own nature, like an ocean unmoved by world-waves, shines supreme. Recognition (pratyabhijñā) is tasting this spanda in one's own inner face.

## FUNCTION

Invocatory verse (maṅgala) + its vivṛti: establishes spanda as Śiva's self-aware autonomy, the world as his play, and recognition as tasting spanda. Kṣemarāja's first commentary on Spandakārikā 1.1.

## KEY TERMS

* **spanda** — throb: Śiva's autonomy-power (svasvātantryaśakti), by which he is conscious of himself.
* **svapratiṣṭha** — self-established: not dependent on anything else.
* **antarvaktra** — inner face: I-consciousness, made relishable (rasayati).
* **rasa** — taste/relish: the aesthetic term applied to self-awareness.
* **vīra** — hero: the one who recognizes spanda in himself.
* **kulendra** — lord of the Kula: the Kaula master.
* **akalitamahimā** — unmeasured greatness: Śiva's mahimā beyond grasp.
* **citrasaṃyojana** — various combination: the world's diversity as Śiva's own combination.
* **sṛṣṭisaṃhāralīlā** — creation-dissolution play: the world as Śiva's līlā.
* **samudra** — ocean: Śiva unmoved by world-waves.

## LOCAL CONTEXT

First and only passage; reader has nothing presupposed. The verse is the opening of Kṣemarāja's Spandasandoha, his commentary on Spandakārikā 1.1.

## EXPLANATION

Two halves: First half: Śiva alone, possessing spanda, self-established, who makes the inner face taste. Alone (ekaḥ) = non-dual; no second. Possessing spanda = endowed with the throb that is his autonomy-power. Self-established = not dependent on anything else. Makes the inner face taste = makes I-consciousness relishable — the self-awareness that is the spanda itself. Second half: this hero, lord of the Kula, is victorious. The victory is recognition (pratyabhijñā) — tasting spanda in one's own inner face. Kṣemarāja's gloss: whose greatness is unmeasured, who measures the world from earth to Sadāśiva in the heart (the world arises within consciousness, not outside it), with various combinations (the world's diversity is Śiva's own combination), displaying creation-dissolution plays (the world as Śiva's līlā). He alone, of non-dual nature, endowed with his own autonomy-power, established in his own nature, like an ocean unmoved by world-waves, shines supreme.

## BOUNDARY / OPEN

Whether spanda here means the same as in Spandakārikā 1.1 or a broader sense — Kṣemarāja's Spandasandoha is specifically on the first kārikā, so it is tightly bound to that verse. Whether antarvaktra (inner face) is the I-consciousness (ahaṃtā) or something else — Kṣemarāja's gloss rasayati (makes tasteable) suggests it is the self-awareness that becomes relishable through recognition. Whether vīraḥ kulendraḥ refers to Śiva himself or to the practitioner who recognizes spanda — both readings live; the commentary's jayatyeṣa (this one is victorious) suggests the practitioner.

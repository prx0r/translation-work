# C1 NOTE — sand-p01.c1

Moved from C1 (pristine rasa-evocative translation only):

## SUMMARY

Śiva alone, possessing spanda, self-established, makes the inner face taste; this hero, lord of the Kula, is victorious. Kṣemarāja's commentary: whose greatness is unmeasured, who measures the world from earth to Sadāśiva in the heart, who displays creation-dissolution plays — he alone, of non-dual nature, endowed with his own autonomy-power, established in his own nature, like an ocean unmoved by world-waves, shines supreme. Recognition (pratyabhijñā) is tasting this spanda in one's own inner face.

## FUNCTION

Invocatory verse (maṅgala) + its vivṛti: establishes spanda as Śiva's self-aware autonomy, the world as his play, and recognition as tasting spanda. Kṣemarāja's first commentary on Spandakārikā 1.1.

## KEY TERMS

* **spanda** — throb: Śiva's autonomy-power (svasvātantryaśakti), by which he is conscious of himself. Spanda is synonymous with Śakti (I-consciousness) — not a physical vibration but a movement in the space of Consciousness. (sanskrit-trikashaivism.com)
* **svapratiṣṭha** — self-established: not dependent on anything else. * **antarvaktra** — inner face: I-consciousness, made relishable (rasayati). * **rasa** — taste/relish: here LITERAL (rasayati = makes tasteable), not the aesthetic rasa of rasa-theory. rasa here is used in its primary sense: making the inner face (I-consciousness) relishable/experienceable. * **vīra** — hero: the one who recognizes spanda in himself. * **kulendra** — lord of the Kula: the Kaula master. Kula = the Kaula school of Kashmir Śaivism, distinct from Trika; the Kaula master is the one who recognizes spanda in himself. * **akalitamahimā** — unmeasured greatness: Śiva's mahimā beyond grasp. * **citrasaṃyojana** — various combination: the world's diversity as Śiva's own combination. * **sṛṣṭisaṃhāralīlā** — creation-dissolution play: the world as Śiva's līlā. * **samudra** — ocean: Śiva unmoved by world-waves.

## LOCAL CONTEXT

First and only passage; nothing is presupposed. This is the opening of Kṣemarāja's Spandasandoha, his commentary on Spandakārikā 1.1.

## EXPLANATION

Two halves, and the rasa shifts from adbhuta (wonder) to śānta (tranquility). First half: Śiva alone, possessing spanda, self-established, who makes the inner face taste. Alone (ekaḥ) = non-dual; no second. Possessing spanda = endowed with the throb that is his autonomy-power — the power by which he is conscious of himself, the movement in the space of consciousness that is not physical but the very activity of awareness knowing itself. Self-established = not dependent on anything else; the ocean does not need the waves. Makes the inner face taste = makes I-consciousness relishable — the self-awareness that is the spanda itself, made experienceable. The wonder (adbhuta) is in the unmeasured greatness (akalitamahimā): who measures the world from earth to Sadāśiva in the heart, who displays creation-dissolution plays as his own combination. The world is not outside Śiva; it arises within his consciousness, and its diversity is his own citrasaṃyojana — his own play. Second half: this hero, lord of the Kula, is victorious. The victory is recognition (pratyabhijñā) — tasting spanda in one's own inner face. The hero is the one who recognizes: not a warrior but a recognizer. The rasa resolves into śānta: he alone, of non-dual nature, endowed with his own autonomy-power, established in his own nature, like an ocean unmoved by world-waves, shines supreme. The tranquility is not passivity but the fullness of self-established power — the ocean does not move because it is already everything.

## BOUNDARY

Whether spanda here means the same as in Spandakārikā 1.1 or a broader sense — Kṣemarāja's Spandasandoha is specifically on the first kārikā, so it is tightly bound to that verse. Whether antarvaktra (inner face) is the I-consciousness (ahaṃtā) or something else — Kṣemarāja's gloss rasayati (makes tasteable) suggests it is the self-awareness that becomes relishable through recognition. Our identification with ahaṃtā is an inference, not Kṣemarāja's explicit statement. Whether vīraḥ kulendraḥ refers to Śiva himself or to the practitioner who recognizes spanda — both readings live; the commentary's jayatyeṣa (this one is victorious) suggests the practitioner. Spanda is not a physical vibration — it is a movement in the space of Consciousness (sanskrit-trikashaivism.com). Four commentaries exist on Spandakārikā: Vṛtti (Kallaṭa, simple), Spandapradīpikā (Bhaṭṭotpala, compilation), Vivṛti (Rāmakaṇṭha, follows Kallaṭa), Spandanirṇaya (Kṣemarāja, prose, most authoritative). Spandasandoha is Kṣemarāja's earlier, shorter work on kārikā 1.1 only. Abhinava's specific claims are unverified — we have the vivṛti's existence, not its text.

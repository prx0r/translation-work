# PEER REVIEW — Spandasandoha vs scholarly takes

*2026-11-01. Reviewed against: sanskrit-trikashaivism.com (Spandakārikā translation + commentary survey), Wikipedia (Kṣemarāja, Vasugupta), archive.org (Spanda Karikas with Kṣemarāja's commentary), shaivam.org (scripture list).*

## Scholarly facts

1. **Spandasandoha** = Kṣemarāja's commentary on Spandakārikā 1.1 (first aphorism only). Not on the whole text — Spandanirṇaya is the full prose commentary.
2. **Four commentaries** on Spandakārikā: Vṛtti (Kallaṭa, simple), Spandapradīpikā (Bhaṭṭotpala, compilation of quotes), Vivṛti (Rāmakaṇṭha, follows Kallaṭa), Spandanirṇaya (Kṣemarāja, prose, most authoritative). Spandasandoha is Kṣemarāja's earlier, shorter work.
3. **Spanda** = Śakti = I-consciousness. "Spanda is synonymous with Śakti (I-consciousness). Spanda is the Power of Śiva, by which He is conscious of Himself. Besides, Spanda allows Him to manifest, maintain and withdraw the entire universe." (sanskrit-trikashaivism.com)
4. **Spanda is not physical vibration** — "It is a kind of movement in the space of Consciousness. Śiva is with no movements, but somehow something is moving in Him and this is Spanda."
5. **Kṣemarāja** (late 10th–early 11th c.) was Abhinavagupta's disciple. His magnum opus is Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya.
6. **Spandakārikā 1.1** (the verse Spandasandoha comments on): "In this world, the excessive greatness of Self was revealed (unmilitamahima) to the exalted preceptor Vasugupta by Siva's inspiration who is intent on conferring His grace on all." (from archive.org translation)

## What our work got wrong (or overstated)

1. **"Rasa = taste/relish" in the ledger** — rasa here is not the aesthetic rasa (rasa theory) but the literal "taste" (rasayati = makes tasteable). The verse uses rasa in its literal sense (tasting the inner face), not the aesthetic sense. Our C1 KEY TERMS says "rasa — taste/relish: the aesthetic term applied to self-awareness" which conflates the literal and aesthetic senses. **Fix: clarify that rasa here is literal (tasting), not aesthetic rasa theory.**

2. **"antarvaktra = I-consciousness (ahaṃtā)"** — this is our gloss, not Kṣemarāja's explicit statement. Kṣemarāja says "yo'ntarvaktraṃ rasayati" = who makes the inner face taste. The identification of antarvaktra with ahaṃtā (I-consciousness) is our inference, not his. **Fix: flag as our inference, not Kṣemarāja's statement.**

3. **Missing: the Kaula context.** "vīraḥ kulendraḥ" (this hero, lord of the Kula) refers to the Kaula tradition. We mention "Kaula master" but don't explain what Kula means in this context (the Kaula school of Kashmir Śaivism, distinct from Trika). **Fix: add to C1 KEY TERMS or BOUNDARY.**

## Additional scholarly findings (2026-11-01, second search)

### The "vibrating consciousness" vs "vibration of consciousness" debate

wisdomlib.org (2025) notes a translation ambiguity in Dyczkowski's rendering of
Spandakārikā 1.1: is Spanda "vibrating consciousness" (the consciousness that vibrates)
or "vibration of consciousness" (the vibration that belongs to consciousness)? Dyczkowski's
translation uses "vibration of" which proliferated the problem. Kṣemarāja's opening verse
(Spandasandoha) "directly moves to glorify the freedom of supreme Śiva" — it's the
sovereignty (svātantrya), the power of creative freedom. This resolves the debate:
Spanda is not a property of consciousness but consciousness's own self-aware activity.
Our C1 captures this: "Spanda is synonymous with Śakti (I-consciousness)" and "not a
physical vibration but a movement in the space of Consciousness."

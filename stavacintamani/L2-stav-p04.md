# Stavacintamani stav-p04 (will and teaching) — L2 READ

## v.32
This whole of three worlds fruits from mere will of yours alone — who, O lord, knows the reach of your workings?
The Sanskrit: *icchāyā eva yasyeyat phalaṃ lokatrayātmakam tasya te nātha kāryāṇāṃ ko vetti kiyatī gatiḥ*

## v.33
To him I bow, by whose stairway of works even Brahmā and the rest climb higher and higher to win the abode.
The Sanskrit: *brahmādayo 'pi tad yasya karmasopānamālayā upary upari dhāvanti labdhuṃ dhāma namāmi tam*

## v.34
'Here is Brahmā, here the Great Indra, here sun and moon' — so his power-vine stands in bloom: may Bhava, that one, guard us.
The Sanskrit: *ayaṃ brahmā mahendro 'yaṃ sūryācandramasāv imau iti śaktilatā yasya puṣpitā pātv asau bhavaḥ*

## v.35
Deluded hearts find no delusion in him; far-rangers never reach his end — hard ground at dusk: Mṛḍa we praise.
The Sanskrit: *bhramo na labhyate yasya bhrāntāntaḥkaraṇair api dūragair api yasyānto durgam astaṃ stumo mṛḍam*

## v.36
Homage in hymn and memory, in meditation, sight, touch, and likewise in arrival — to the beloved mass of bliss, the matted-haired.
The Sanskrit: *namaḥ stutau smṛtau dhyāne darśane sparśane tathā prāptau cānandavṛndāya dayitāya kapardine*

## v.37
Thinking in mind, 'why this pride?', O highest Lord — 'proud, I am made of you' — ah me, not in myself: what joy is this?
The Sanskrit: *kiṃ smayeneti matvāpi manasā parameśvara smayena tvanmayo 'smīti māmi nātmani kiṃ mudā*

## v.38
Pondered though crores of duties be, through mind's restlessness — I rest, O Bhava, and revel deeply in the bliss of mind in you.
The Sanskrit: *cintayitvāpi kartavyakoṭīś cittasya cāpalāt viśrāmyan bhava bhāvatkacittānande rame bhṛśam*

## v.39
Subtle though you be, how is this triple world a mere fragment of yours? Gross though you be, why do even Brahmā and the rest not plainly see you, O lord?
The Sanskrit: *sūkṣmo 'pi cet trilokīyaṃ kalāmātraṃ kathaṃ tava sthūlo 'tha kiṃ sudarśo na brahmādibhir api prabho*

## v.40
Had it never been that 'you alone speak them all' — which sage, O god, would ever have strained through tangled words?
The Sanskrit: *vācya eṣāṃ tvam eveti nābhaviṣyad idaṃ yadi kaḥ kleśaṃ deva vāgjāleṣv akariṣyat sudhīs tadā*

## v.41
Through what practice, what rite, what wisdom does one behold you, O lord — teach me, and let your favour fall on me.
The Sanskrit: *krameṇa karmaṇā kena kayā vā prajñayā prabho dṛśyo 'sīty upadeśena prasādaḥ kriyatāṃ mama*

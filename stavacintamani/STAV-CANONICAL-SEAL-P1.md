## The parable (≈90s)

> A traveller comes to the last house on the road, and everything
> there is taste. First the door-speech: honeyed, ravishing —
> sweet enough to steal the mind of seer and seen alike. The host
> is the Unbeaten, great on glory and awakening, needing nothing
> while feeding three worlds, desired of all while desiring
> nothing, the moon-wreathed master to whom hunger itself comes
> home. At his table every bliss is a droplet — and every droplet
> is the sea, and the pleasure of it swells a thousand times each
> mouthful. The water of stupor gets churned and cracked open at
> his word; what was hard goes handy, what was bitter turns sweet.
> The moon-crowned one and the river-crowned one bow to him across
> the table. He makes worlds by wanting them — three at a time —
> and there is no guest who fails: whoever holds out a hand takes
> the whole sea. Sit down, the swell goes on; and the taste never
> runs out — honey past honey, the foretaste of waiting on the
> Gracious, new in every tick.

## The seal — statement

**The spoonful that was the whole sea:** bottom: the cave-mouth
house by dark water (p02's cave-dwelling), a churn beside the
door cracked open with dark stupor-spill turning clear (p08's
churn broken); mid: the feast-table — the moon-crowned host
(silver crescent on brow) while a river-crowned attendant pours a
cascade of blue-white water into cups (Gaṅgā-service: the droplet-
sea served); on the table a GREAT SPOON lifting one droplet in
which tiny wave-lines show the whole sea inside (p07 made
emblem); an empty hand at the table-edge receiving (p11: none
fails — the refuge as the receiving-palm); three small world-lamps
in gold, crimson, blue at the host's side (p04 three-orbs); cloth
swelling fuller at the table's edge (p03 thousandfold-swell);
crown: one amber honey-thread pouring, renewing beside a fresh
small star (p12: honey-past-honey, new-in-every-tick).

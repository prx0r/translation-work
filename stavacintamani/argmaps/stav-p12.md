# Stavacintāmaṇi stav-p12 — ARGUMENT MAP (vv. 111-120)

*2026-10-31. Source: T1 stav-p12 = vv. 111-120. Lore: Stainton 2013 (Columbia dissertation, 'Poetry and Prayer: Stotras in the Religious and Literary History of Kashmir' — first study of Stavacintāmaṇi in a European language; examines how Śaiva poets integrated kāvya, alaṅkāraśāstra, theology, and devotion; argues stotra form was appealing for non-dualistic authors seeking to reinterpret practices and features of worship that might otherwise be seen as dualistic). The text is a philosophical hymnal — 120 verses in 12 passages of 10 verses each, praising Śiva as supreme Lord, with themes of bhakti, jñāna, prasāda (grace), mukti (liberation), and the non-dual nature of reality.*

[i.e. aho svādutamaḥ śarvasevāśaṃsāsudhārasaḥ kutra kālakalāmātre na yo navanavāyate muhur muhur aviśrāntas trailokyaṃ kalpanāśataiḥ kalpayann api ko 'py eko nirvikalpo jayaty ajaḥ malatailāktasaṃsāravāsanā]

The Stavacintāmaṇi is a philosophical hymnal — 120 verses in 12 passages of 10 verses each, praising Śiva as supreme Lord. The devotional arc moves from the supreme Lord (p01) through worship, recognition, grace, and liberation. The tone is devotional-philosophical: kāvya (poetry) and alaṅkāraśāstra (poetics) integrated with theology (non-dualism) and devotion (bhakti). Stainton 2013: the stotra form was appealing for non-dualistic authors seeking to reinterpret practices and features of worship that might otherwise be seen as dualistic.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The stotra is devotional poetry; the "argument" is the devotional stance and its relation to recognition (pratyabhijñā). The argmap reconstructs the stance, not a syllogism.
2. GRETIL text (Isaacson ed., from Mukunda Rama Shastri's 1918 edition) is continuous prose, not verse-separated; verse boundaries are not marked in our T1.
3. Kṣemarāja's Stavacintāmaṇivivṛti is the standard commentary. Stainton 2013 is the first study in a European language.

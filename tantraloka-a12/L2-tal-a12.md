# Tantraloka-a12 — L2 READ


## v.1
Now the use of this path, our present subject, is set forth.
The Sanskrit: *athādhvano@sya prakṛta upayogaḥ prakāśyate*

## v.2
Thus this whole path, as it rests in consciousness, through that rests also in void, intellect, breath, channel-wheels, and bodies.
The Sanskrit: *itthamadhvā samasto@yaṃ yathā saṃvidi saṃsthitaḥ | taddvārā śūnyadhīprāṇanāḍīcakratanuṣvatho*

## v.3
And outside too, everywhere in liṅga, image, fire, altar and the rest; so it stands, whole and part-wise, in sequence and all at once.
The Sanskrit: *bahiśca liṅgamūrtyagnisthaṇḍilādiṣu sarvataḥ | tathā sthitaḥ samastaśca vyastaścaiṣa kramākramāt*

## v.4
Laid out from consciousness-principle to the outer, this path: at each level one should make one's own form shine, by own freedom, in fitting form.
The Sanskrit: *āsaṃvittattvamābāhyaṃ yo@yamadhvā vyavasthitaḥ | tatra tatrocitaṃ rūpaṃ svaṃ svātantryeṇa bhāsayet*

## v.5
And everything's form everywhere — even his does not not shine; for we cannot bear exclusion anywhere, even in dream.
The Sanskrit: *sarvaṃ sarvatra rūpaṃ ca tasyāpi na na bhāsate | nahyavaccheditāṃ kvāpi svapne@pi viṣahāmahe*

## v.6
Thus one should behold the body as filled with the universe-path, painted by time's activity — the abode of pulsation made of space and time.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ viśvādhvasaṃpūrṇaṃ kālavyāpāracitritam | deśakālamayaspandasadma dehaṃ vilokayet*

## v.7
So beheld, he is made of the deities within the universe; to be meditated, worshipped, libated — entered then, one is freed.
The Sanskrit: *tathā vilokyamāno@sau viśvāntardevatāmayaḥ | dhyeyaḥ pūjyaśca tarpyaśca tadāviṣṭo vimucyate*

## v.8
Thus pot, cloth, liṅga, altar, book, water — or whatever one may see anywhere: there one should reach identity-with-that.
The Sanskrit: *itthaṃ ghaṭaṃ paṭaṃ liṅgaṃ sthaṇḍilaṃ pustakaṃ jalam | yadvā kiṃcitkvacitpaśyettatra tanmayatāṃ vrajet*

## v.9
Therein offering of things is held worship through non-difference; and contemplation of full-formedness is called meditation.
The Sanskrit: *tatrārpaṇaṃ hi vastūnāmabhedenārcanaṃ matam | tathā saṃpūrṇarūpatvānusaṃdhirdhyānamucyate*

## v.10
Unshaking contemplation of fullness brings firmness; and through inner-mutter practice, reflecting, one becomes a mantra-vessel.
The Sanskrit: *saṃpūrṇatvānusaṃdhānamakampaṃ dārḍhyamānayan | tathāntarjalpayogena vimṛśañjapabhājanam*

## v.11
Effecting the melting of self-difference of the beings offered therein, their ray-rich being he should bestow — devoted to the fire-rite.
The Sanskrit: *tatrārpitānāṃ bhāvānāṃ svakabhedavilāpanam | kurvaṃstadraśmisadbhāvaṃ dadyāddhomakriyāparaḥ*

## v.12
For one so acting, seeing all with equal eye, unwaveringness is the pure vow — evenness taught in the Nandiśikhā.
The Sanskrit: *tathaivaṃkurvataḥ sarvaṃ samabhāvena paśyataḥ | niṣkampatā vrataṃ śuddhaṃ sāmyaṃ nandiśikhoditam*

## v.13
Thus through worship, japa, meditation, fire-offering, vow, and rule in sequence, the ancients called the complete state absorption.
The Sanskrit: *tathārcanajapadhyānahomavratavidhikramāt | paripūrṇāṃ sthitiṃ prāhuḥ samādhiṃ guravaḥ purā*

## v.14
Here in worship, japa and the rest, whether outward or inward, there is no rule at all about materials, nor any prohibition.
The Sanskrit: *atra pūjājapādyeṣu bahirantardvayasthitau | dravyaughe na vidhiḥ ko@pi na kāpi pratiṣiddhatā*

## v.15
No use here whatsoever for visualization, purification, twilight-rites and the rest; and in the Trika Sūtra it is said sacrifice arises toward the rite.
The Sanskrit: *kalpanāśuddhisaṃdhyādernopayogo@tra kaścana | uktaṃ śrītrikasūtre ca jāyate yajanaṃ prati*

## v.16
Not knowing rule, knowing rule, and 'thus' and so on at length: when, as, by which, where one's own consciousness is gracious.
The Sanskrit: *avidhijño vidhijñaścetyevamādi suvistaram | yadā yathā yena yatra svā samvittiḥ prasīdati*

## v.17
Then, so, by that, there: each respective enjoyable — and that is the rule; all, worldly and unworldly, one should employ here by that.
The Sanskrit: *tadā tathā tena tatra tattadbhogyaṃ vidhiśca saḥ | laukikālaukikaṃ sarvaṃ tenātra viniyojayet*

## v.18
In steadiness: but the trembler should forcibly diminish tremor — by force, as, by whatever means, in sequence or all at once, or.
The Sanskrit: *niṣkampatve sakampastu kampaṃ nirhrāsayedbalāt | yathā yenābhyupāyena kramādakramato@pi vā*

## v.19
Doubt melts within — so he should become striving; the goddesses resting in mind, rite, and senses he should libate with forbidden things indeed.
The Sanskrit: *vicikitsā galatyantastathāsau yatnavānbhavet | dhīkarmākṣagatā devīrniṣiddhaireva tarpayet*

## v.20
And the hero-vow one should salute — thus the Bhargaśikhā word: for doubt is impurity, weariness, contraction — thus this.
The Sanskrit: *vīravrataṃ cābhinandediti bhargaśikhāvacaḥ | tathāhi śaṅkā mālinyaṃ glāniḥ saṃkoca ityadaḥ*

## v.21
Inside the world-prison it forges gross-post shackles; mantras are letter-natured, substance five-elemented.
The Sanskrit: *saṃsārakārāgārāntaḥ sthūlasthūṇāghaṭāyate | mantrā varṇasvabhāvā ye dravyaṃ yatpāñcabhautikam*

## v.22
The breathing kind which is consciousness-selfed — where is mixture there, how? With mixture absent, what is this doubt — even hers clearly baseless.
The Sanskrit: *yaccidātma prāṇijātaṃ tatra kaḥ saṃkaraḥ katham | saṃkarābhāvataḥ keyaṃ śaṅkā tasyāmapi sphuṭam*

## v.23
One should not doubt; thus doubt dissolves through disregard — in the Sarvācāra, Vīrālī, Niśācara, Krama and the rest.
The Sanskrit: *na śaṅketa tathā śaṅkā vilīyetāvahelayā | śrīsarvācāravīrālīniśācarakramādiṣu*

## v.24
And this is spread in the śāstras — there it is said: for from doubt arises weariness, from doubt being a vessel of obstacles.
The Sanskrit: *śāstreṣu vitataṃ caitattatra tatrocyate | yataḥ śaṅkayā jāyate glāniḥ śaṅkayā vighnabhājanam*

## v.25
And said Utpaladeva, the venerable guru of my guru: the all-doubt thunderbolt path — we bow to the Māheśvara path.
The Sanskrit: *uvācotpaladevaśca śrīmānasmadgurorguruḥ | sarvāśaṅkāśaniṃ mārgaṃ numo māheśvaraṃ tviti*

## v.26
For attaining the anuttara state, this āṇava with means shown at great length — indeed, make it grow, O wise ones!
The Sanskrit: *anuttarapadāptaye tadidamāṇavaṃ darśitābhyupāyamativistarānnanu | vidāṃkurudhvaṃ budhāḥ*

# Tantraloka-a14 — L2 READ


## v.1
Now the nature of concealment, as it is told, let it be examined: from own nature the Supreme Lord touches the rule-bound and rule-free order.
The Sanskrit: *tirobhāvasvarūpaṃ tu kathyamānaṃ vivicyatām | svabhāvāt parameśāno niyatyaniyatikramam*

## v.2
Touching, he shines forth; thereby he is called self-willed: having resorted to the rule of action and fruit — this Great Lord.
The Sanskrit: *spṛśanprakāśate yena tataḥ svacchanda ucyate | niyatiṃ karmaphalayorāśrityaiṣa maheśvaraḥ*

## v.3
He ordains creation, preservation, destruction — the intermediate states; and again, in the great creation, in the endlessness of creation and destruction.
The Sanskrit: *sṛṣṭisaṃsthitisaṃhārānvidhatte@vāntarasthitīn | mahāsarge punaḥ sṛṣṭisaṃhārānantyaśālini*

## v.4
One, that God, universe-self, the Lord arrived at abandoning rule; and whatever intermediate creation — and stasis here too is unforced.
The Sanskrit: *ekaḥ sa devo viśvātmā niyatityāgataḥ prabhuḥ | avāntare yā ca sṛṣṭiḥ sthitiścātrāpyayantritam*

## v.5
He does not abandon the form; niyati-abandoned, standing here: that. But by niyati alone, when, through the self-concealing procedure.
The Sanskrit: *nojjhatyeṣa vapustyaktaniyatiśca sthito@tra tat | niyatyaiva yadā caiṣa svarūpācchādanakramāt*

## v.6
He enjoys suffering, delusion and the rest; then the action-fruit order — but having abandoned the karmic restraint, enveloped in suffering-delusion.
The Sanskrit: *bhuṅkte duḥkhavimohādi tadā karmaphalakramaḥ | tyaktvā tu niyamaṃ kārmaṃ duḥkhamohaparītatām*

## v.7
Desiring to shine forth, sits this one, in concealment disregardful; as from light's freedom, the awakened like the unawakened.
The Sanskrit: *bibhāsayiṣurāste@yaṃ tirodhāne@napekṣakaḥ | yathā prakāśasvātantryāt pratibuddho@pyabuddhavat*

## v.8
So sits, uncrossed yet as-if-crossed, behaving; as the awake one her fool-conduct performing even while hating it.
The Sanskrit: *āste tadvadanuttīrṇo@pyuttīrṇa iva ceṣṭate | yathā ca buddhastāṃ mūḍhaceṣṭāṃ kurvannapi dviṣan*

## v.9
In the heart sits the fool — thus indeed of the awakened is the conduct; and the venerable Vidyā-lord said, in the Mānastotra, such as this.
The Sanskrit: *hṛdyāste mūḍha evaṃ hi prabuddhānāṃ viceṣṭitam | śrīvidyādhipatiścāha mānastotre tadīdṛśam*

## v.10
Who on your teaching-path, initiated, falling through delusion into dissent: surely of them there is not your sun-appointment — can lotuses contract from sunbeams?
The Sanskrit: *ye yauṣmāke śāsanamārge kṛtadīkṣāḥ saṃgacchanto mohavaśādvipratipattim | nūnaṃ teṣā nāsti bhavadbhānuniyogaḥ saṅkocaḥ kiṃ sūryakaraistāmarasānām*

## v.11
Known-and-knowable, standing in the creator's place though being, who going wrong from your path, they too are not right: mostly of them, arisen from emblem-mind-and-the-rest, false awakening like snake-fat lamplight.
The Sanskrit: *jñātajñeyā dhātṛpadasthā api santo ye tvanmārgātkāpathagāste@pi na samyaka | prāyasteṣāṃ laiṅgikabuddhyādisamuttho mithyābodhaḥ sarpavasādīpajakalpaḥ*

## v.12
Since pierced, with lead foremost: now copper — that which again to its own nature would not return; nor drunk by earth-dwellers, that nectar of whom thirst-hunger-suffering-pains again herein.
The Sanskrit: *yasmādviddhaṃ sūtakamukhyena nu tāmraṃ tadyadbhūyaḥ svāṃ prakṛtiṃ no samupeyāt | no taiḥ pītaṃ bhūtalasaṃsthairamṛtaṃ tadyeṣāṃ tṛṭkṣudduḥkhavibādhāḥ punarasmin*

## v.13
Then this awakened conduct — mantra-conduct-worship-and-the-rest: hate-kindled inwardly burns him; burning is doubt indeed — for.
The Sanskrit: *tataḥ prabuddhaceṣṭāsau mantracaryārcanādikā | dveṣeddhāntardahatyenaṃ dāhaḥ śaṅkaiva sā yataḥ*

## v.14
Nor is it his past action's might, such that thereby thus he has come; indeed, what would that action be? Or why here would the procedure be the same as with the earlier?
The Sanskrit: *na cāsya karmamahimā tādṛgyenetthamāsta saḥ | kiṃ hi tatkarma kasmādvā pūrveṇātra samo vidhiḥ*

## v.15
Hence that, the Lord's will, by which deluded, so, endless-time-felt suffering-vesselhood he seeks.
The Sanskrit: *tasmātsā parameśecchā yayāyaṃ mohitastathā | anantakālasaṃvedyaduḥkhapātratvamīhate*

## v.16
There too, if through will-variety, both-here-and-beyond-natured: of suffering too there is division, made slow-or-swift, so.
The Sanskrit: *tatrāpi cecchāvaicitryādihāmutrobhayātmakaḥ | duḥkhasyāpi vibhedo@sti ciraśaighryakṛtastathā*

## v.17
Of Kāla, Kāma, Andhaka and the rest, of Paulastya-city dwellers, and of others: concealment — so much suffering, indeed hereafter too.
The Sanskrit: *kālakāmāndhakādīnāṃ paulastyapuravāsinām | tathānyeṣāṃ tirobhāvastāvadduḥkho hyamutra ca*

## v.18
Another too is concealment, consisting in samaya-violation: which was said by the Lord in the venerable Ānandagahvara.
The Sanskrit: *anyo@pi ca tirobhāvaḥ samayollaṅghanātmakaḥ | yaduktaṃ parameśena śrīmadānandagahvare*

## v.19
From samaya-violation, O Goddess: carrion-eaterhood for a hundred years; there too, through mild-intense-and-other-divisions, manifold is the course.
The Sanskrit: *samayollaṅghanāddevi kravyādatvaṃ śataṃ samāḥ | tatrāpi mandatīvrādibhedādbahuvidhaḥ kramaḥ*

## v.20
And through the Great Lord's freedom, though concealed, this one himself: through another's door, or comes back — again grace too, amply.
The Sanskrit: *svātantryācca maheśasya tirobhūto@pyasau svayam | paradvāreṇa vābhyeti bhūyo@nugrahamapyalam*

## v.21
And from renewed grace, expiation-and-the-rest being performed: accordingly, in initiation and the rest performed, he would be Śiva-natured.
The Sanskrit: *bhūyo@nugrahataḥ prāyaścittādyācaraṇe sati | anusāreṇa dīkṣādau kṛte syācchivatāmayaḥ*

## v.22
Concealed — even the departed-breathed: kin, friends, teachers having resorted to, through śaktipāta, through initiations is graced.
The Sanskrit: *tirobhūtaḥ paretāsurapi bandhusuhṛdgurūn | ālambya śaktipātena dīkṣādyairanugṛhyate*

## v.23
There too, diversity of quick-slow-time and the rest: so comes śaktipāta — it, by which he comes to Śiva-nature.
The Sanskrit: *tatrāpi kālaśīghratvaciratvādivibhedatām | tathaiti śaktipāto@sau yenāyāti śivātmatām*

## v.24
Thus creation, maintenance, dissolution, concealment, grace: in the five, doership is Śiva-hood, of consciousness-self.
The Sanskrit: *itthaṃ sṛṣṭisthitidhvaṃsatirobhāvamanugrahaḥ | iti pañcasu kartṛtvaṃ śivatvaṃ saṃvidātmanaḥ*

## v.25
Proud of five-act-freedom-complete-selfhood: the yogin's worship-japa-meditation-yogas, let them stand united, ever-risen.
The Sanskrit: *pañcakṛtyasvatantratvasaṃpūrṇasvātmamāninaḥ | yogino@rcājapadhyānayogāḥ saṃsyuḥ sadoditāḥ*

## v.26
In magic-show business one should never delight: even Sadāśiva — which enjoyment is bondage — that too, for the unfit.
The Sanskrit: *aindrajālikavṛttānte na rajyeta kadācana | sādāśivo@pi yo bhogo bandhaḥ so@pyucitātmanām*

## v.27
Knowerness alone is Śiva-hood, is freedom: that is here called; like a potter, but, doership is not primary, from His superintendence.
The Sanskrit: *jñātṛtvameva śivatā svātantryaṃ tadihocyate | kulālavattu kartṛtvaṃ na mukhyaṃ tadadhiṣṭhiteḥ*

## v.28
Thus having known, to be grasped, never at all, ever, refutation: 'I am Śiva' — if following my will, why is not the world — thus the doubt.
The Sanskrit: *iti jñātvā grahītavyā naiva jātvapi khaṇḍanā | śivo@haṃ cenmadicchānuvarti kiṃ na jagattviti*

## v.29
'Let them follow my will' — here in the I-knower, may it flash: Śiva, or the Supreme Lord — body and the rest, then, fashioned.
The Sanskrit: *mamecchāmanuvartantāmityatrāhaṃvidi sphuret | śivo vā parameśāno dehādiratha nirmitaḥ*

## v.30
Of Śiva, for now is this — the body — but this, so fashioned by you: what other bodiness of his? Then what would be a speakable state?
The Sanskrit: *śivasya tāvadastyetaddehastveṣa tathā tvayā | kṛtaḥ kānyā dehatāsya tatkiṃ syādvācyatāpadam*

## v.31
Told, and, in the Siddhasantāna venerable Ūrmi-Mahākula: wind-wandering-breath-scattering-and-the-rest-made toilers.
The Sanskrit: *uktaṃ ca siddhasantānaśrīmadūrmimahākule | pavanabhramaṇaprāṇavikṣepādikṛtaśramāḥ*

## v.32
In frauds and the rest, who deluded — they: deluded in the supreme state; everywhere with honor which even departure — for liberation.
The Sanskrit: *kuhakādiṣu ye bhrāntāste bhrāntāḥ parame pade | sarvatra bahumānena yāpyutkrāntirvimuktaye*

## v.33
Taught, she in the essence-śāstras, as enjoyment-means arisen: if all-pervading is God — tell! having departed, where will he go?
The Sanskrit: *proktā sā sāraśāstreṣu bhogopāyatayoditā | yadi sarvagatā devo vadotkramya kva yāsyati*

## v.34
But if not all-pervading, then pot-like then he would be: the departure-rite-yoga — this partially — is taught.
The Sanskrit: *athāsarvagatastarhi ghaṭatulyastadā bhavet | utkrāntividhiyogo@yamekadeśena kathyate*

## v.35
In the partless Śiva-principle, but, how departure-fittingness? As from earth-and-the-rest to wind-end, by cliff-water-fire fasts.
The Sanskrit: *niraṃśe śivatattve tu kathamutkrāntisaṃgatiḥ | yathā dharādau vāyvante bhṛgvambvagnyupavāsakaiḥ*

## v.36
Of the self joining in the void, likewise departure-procedure: therefore one should not make the soul depart out of longing for the supreme principle.
The Sanskrit: *ātmano yojanaṃ vyomni tadvadutkrāntivartanā | tasmānnotkramayejjīvaṃ paratattvasamīhayā*

## v.37
But in the venerable Pūrvaśāstra, whatever of departure the mark was told: not that, as a means to liberation, but for loss of enjoyment, from such desiring.
The Sanskrit: *śrīpūrvaśāstre tūktaṃ yadutkrānterlakṣaṇaṃ na tat | muktyupāyatayā kiṃtu bhogahānyai tathaiṣaṇāt*

## v.38
Perfected by japa-meditation-and-the-rest, through freedom, from śaktipāta: toward enjoyment detached, if thus: the body should abandon — quoted.
The Sanskrit: *japadhyānādisaṃsiddhaḥ svātantryācchaktipātataḥ | bhogaṃ prati viraktaśceditthaṃ dehaṃ tyajediti*

## v.39
Even of self-willed death, what from Bhīṣma onward is heard, indeed: on arrival at enjoyment-distaste, approaching life's end.
The Sanskrit: *svacchandamṛtyorapi yad bhīṣmādeḥ śrūyate kila | bhogavairasyasaṃprāptau jīvitāntopasarpaṇam*

## v.40
By yoga-mantra-nectar-substance-boon-and-the-rest, a siddhi-partaking body: to abandon — for not otherwise is possible without the stated-sequence-practice.
The Sanskrit: *yogamantrāmṛtadravyavarādyaiḥ siddhibhāktanuḥ | hātuṃ nahyanyathā śakyā vinoktakramayogataḥ*

## v.41
Told, and, in the Mālinītantra, by the Supreme Lord, likewise: all — even, or else — enjoyment deeming ugly.
The Sanskrit: *uktaṃ ca mālinītantre parameśena tādṛśam | sarvamapyathavā bhogaṃ manyamāno virūpakam*

## v.42
Thus-and-the-rest, by one who says: by all, the inner-reality-unseen reality, teaching of one's own freedom in creation-and-the-rest duties.
The Sanskrit: *ityādi vadatā sarvairalakṣyāntaḥsatattvakam | evaṃ sṛṣṭyādikartavyasvasvātantryopadeśanam*

## v.43
Which very chief initiation should be of the disciple, Śiva-giving: told in the venerable Niśicāra, and by Bhairavī energy, radiance.
The Sanskrit: *yatsaiva mukhyadīkṣā syācchiṣyasya śivadāyinī | uktaṃ śrīniśicāre ca bhairavīyeṇa tejasā*

## v.44
Pervaded, the universe they behold, the thought-abandoned-minded; but the thought-joined-minded: from body's fall, Śiva reaches.
The Sanskrit: *vyāptaṃ viśvaṃ prapaśyanti vikalpojjhitacetasaḥ | vikalpayuktacittastu piṇḍapātācchivaṃ vrajet*

## v.45
By outer-initiation-and-the-rest-practice, through conduct-samaya-constructs, thought-free just so now indeed: living-liberated, no doubt.
The Sanskrit: *bāhyadīkṣādiyogena caryāsamayakalpanaiḥ | avikalpastathādyaiva jīvanmukto na saṃśayaḥ*

## v.46
By thought made like the root-mass of the world-worn-out tree, inwardly, of the ultimate fire: may there be spark-fragments; even if at its end, blazes intensely the pure-awakening-fire-mass.
The Sanskrit: *saṃsārajīrṇatarumūlakalāpakalpasaṃkalpasāntaratayā paramārthavahneḥ | syurvisphuliṅgakaṇikā api cettadante dedīpyate vimalabodhahutāśarāśiḥ*

## v.47
Thus the initiation-approach — this, shown, scripturally approved.
The Sanskrit: *itthaṃ dīkṣopakramo@yaṃ darśitaḥ śāstrasaṃmataḥ*

# Tantraloka-a18 — L2 READ


## v.1
Now this concise initiation is taught — the attainment of Śiva-hood: no powder-mandala, no eve-rite here, no taking of ground and field; but in whatever place, the guru worships Śiva.
The Sanskrit: *atha saṃkṣiptadīkṣeyaṃ śivatāpattidocyate | na rajo nādhivāso@tra na bhūkṣetraparigrahaḥ | yatra tatra pradeśe tu pūjayitvā guruḥ śivam*

## v.2
Having meditated the path with the mind, the knower of the tattvas' far shore should initiate her who is freed from birth and the rest — by whatever path the guru proceeds.
The Sanskrit: *adhvānaṃ manasā dhyātvā dīkṣayettattvapāragaḥ | jananādivihīnāṃ tu yena yenādhvanā guruḥ*

## v.3
He should make it ending in the single tattva, steeped solely in the Śiva-mood; then with the Parā-mantra unto him: 'Now I purify the tattva.'
The Sanskrit: *kuryātsa ekatattvāntāṃ śivabhāvaikabhāvitaḥ | parāmantrastato@syeti tattvaṃ saṃśodhayāmyatha*

## v.4
'Svāhā' thus for each tattva; upon purification he should cast the full oblation. So with all other mantras too — or else as told.
The Sanskrit: *svāheti pratitattvaṃ syācchuddhe pūrṇāhutiṃ kṣipet | evaṃ mantrāntaraiḥ kuryātsamastairathavoktavat*

## v.5
Enclosed with Parā by name, ending in svāhā, ending in the first: a hundred, a thousand, or a hundred-and-eight — with that, with śakti indeed, he should offer.
The Sanskrit: *parāsaṃpuṭitaṃ nāma svāhāntaṃ prathamāntakam | śataṃ sahasraṃ sāṣṭaṃ vā tena śaktyaiva homayet*

## v.6
Then 'pūrṇā' — having purified the unbroken, highest, such-like — the initiation-rite taught here and there in scripture by the Great Lord.
The Sanskrit: *tataḥ pūrṇeti saṃśodhyahīnamuttamamīdṛśam | dīkṣākarmoditaṃ tatra tatra śāstre maheśinā*

## v.7
Singly each: with the Mātṛkā-pair letters he should purify the tattvas; or else with the condensed mantra — in all mantras this is the rule.
The Sanskrit: *pratyekaṃ mātṛkāyugmavarṇaistattvāni śodhayet | yadi vā piṇḍamantreṇa sarvamantreṣvayaṃ vidhiḥ*

## v.8
And in whatever measure the guru's knowledge is well-practiced, his self made of identity-with-that — in that measure let him do the concise rite, not otherwise.
The Sanskrit: *yathā yathā ca svabhyastajñānastanmayatātmakaḥ | gurustathā tathā kuryāt saṃkṣiptaṃ karma nānyathā*

## v.9
And in the venerable Brahmayāmala it is said: even in the concise he should contemplate the pervasion common to all paths — only, in the sacrifice, no elaboration.
The Sanskrit: *śrībrahmayāmale coktaṃ saṃkṣipte@pi hi bhāvayet | vyāptiṃ sarvādhvasāmānyāṃ kiṃtu yāge na vistaraḥ*

## v.10
Thinking 'I have not become identical' — the rite lying scattered — joining it up, thinking 'through sequence identity is reached', he should perform the rite in its scattered form.
The Sanskrit: *atanmayībhūtamiti vikṣiptaṃ karma sandadhat | kramāttādātmyametīti vikṣiptaṃ vidhimācaret*

## v.11
Told is this concise rule — which out of grace was uttered by Śiva — in the Dīkṣottara and the Kiraṇa too, and in each teaching there.
The Sanskrit: *saṃkṣipto vidhirukto@yaṃ kṛpayā yaḥ śivoditaḥ | dīkṣottare kairaṇe ca tatra tatrāpi śāsane*

# Tantraloka-a25 — L2 READ


## v.1
Now the śrāddha-rule told in the venerable Ṣaḍardha is proclaimed.
The Sanskrit: *atha śrāddhavidhiḥ śrīmatṣaḍardhokto nigadyate*

## v.2
In the Siddhātantra it is hinted, in the image-sacrifice exposition; and by funerary rite, for the well-purified and the impure, that is the rule.
The Sanskrit: *siddhātantre sūcito@sau mūrtiyāganirūpaṇe | antyeṣṭyā suviśuddhānāmaśuddhānāṃ ca tadvidhiḥ*

## v.3
On the third day, on the fourth day, on the tenth, in the month, in the month, in the first year, year by year, at all times it is to be performed by his own as before.
The Sanskrit: *tryahe turye@hni daśame māsi māsyādyavatsare | varṣe varṣe sarvakālaṃ kāryastatsvaiḥ sa pūrvavat*

## v.4
There as before he should worship the god, offer in fire likewise; then the food-offering itself, first taking it in hand's reach.
The Sanskrit: *tatra prāgvadyajeddevaṃ homayedanale tathā | tato naivedyameva prāggṛhītvā hastagocare*

## v.5
The guru — the food-made, growing, potent-formed śakti — having meditated, by her the pervaded: that target let the wise one visualize.
The Sanskrit: *gururannamayīṃ śaktiṃ vṛṃhikāṃ vīryarūpiṇīm | dhyātvā tayā samāviṣṭaṃ taṃ sādhyaṃ cintayetsudhīḥ*

## v.6
Then his fettered part, of enjoyable form — that he should offer; in the enjoyer, through identity-state: thus the disciple becomes Śiva.
The Sanskrit: *tato@sya yaḥ pāśavoṃ@śo bhogyarūpastamarpayet | bhoktaryekātmabhāvena śiṣya itthaṃ śivībhavet*

## v.7
Enjoyableness and the other, body, frame — these are held fetter-natured; in śrāddha, in corpse-uplifting, in the final sacrifice: their Śiva-making.
The Sanskrit: *bhogyatānyā tanurdeha iti pāśātmakā matāḥ | śrāddhe mṛtoddhṛtāvantayāge teṣāṃ śivīkṛtiḥ*

## v.8
Though fulfillment might come by a single rite, yet for the accomplishment of identity-state he should perform the whole rite.
The Sanskrit: *ekenaiva vidhānena yadyapi syātkṛtārthatā | tathāpi tanmayībhāvasiddhyai sarvaṃ vidhiṃ caret*

## v.9
But for the enjoyment-seeker, in practice-abundance, in fruit-abundance as cause — therefore corpse-uplifting, śrāddha and the rest he should perform for him.
The Sanskrit: *bubhukṣostu kriyābhyāsabhūmānau phalabhūmani hetu | tato mṛtoddhāraśrāddhādyasmai samācaret*

## v.10
But for him whose darkness is destroyed by the sun of tattva-knowledge, none of this — funerary rite, śrāddha rule and the rest — is ever of use.
The Sanskrit: *tattvajñānārkavidhvastadhvāntasya tu na ko@pyayam | antyeṣṭiśrāddhavidhyādirupayogī kadācana*

## v.11
But for them — guru, his group, group-mates, fellow celibates, lineage-followers — the unity-day is a festival day.
The Sanskrit: *teṣāṃ tu guru tadvargavargyasabrahmacāriṇām | tatsantānajuṣāmaikyadinaṃ parvadinaṃ bhavet*

## v.12
For when there is a surge of awakening, then — O festival day! — from fulfillment: birth-day and unity-day are therefore festivals through accomplished awakening.
The Sanskrit: *yadāhi bodhasyodrekastadā parvāha pūraṇāt | janmaikyadivasau tena parvaṇī bodhasiddhitaḥ*

## v.13
And when the putraka may be of help to someone, then the mother's, father's, and śakti's channels running in the left-right interval —
The Sanskrit: *putrako@pi yadā kasmaicana syādupakārakaḥ | tadā mātuḥ pituḥ śaktervāmadakṣāntarālagāḥ*

## v.14
he should make flow, to the deity he should offer the offered food; and in the venerable Bharuṇa tantra that was set forth by Śiva.
The Sanskrit: *nāḍīḥ pravāhayeddevāyārpayeta niveditam | śrīmadbharuṇatantre ca tacchivena nirūpitam*

## v.15
And regard for their flowing-time is to be observed for the accomplishment of that form; then by spontaneity he should effect that accomplishment through the forthcoming rite.
The Sanskrit: *tadvāhakālāpekṣā ca kāryā tadrūpasiddhaye | svācchandyenātha tatsiddhiṃ vidhinā bhāvinā caret*

## v.16
And in anyone's śrāddha, guru-deity-fire libation with wheel-sacrifice should be śrauta; but no fettered rite should there be.
The Sanskrit: *yasya kasyāpi vā śrāddhe gurudevāgnitarpaṇam | sacakreṣṭi bhavecchrauto natu syātpāśavo vidhiḥ*

## v.17
And so it is said in the venerable Maukuṭa: established even in the Śiva-teaching, one who trusts in the Vedic rite is like a broken bell — nothing at all.
The Sanskrit: *śrīmaukuṭe tathā coktaṃ śivaśāstre sthito@pi yaḥ | pratyeti vaidike bhagnaghaṇṭāvanna sa kiṃcana*

## v.18
And by the rite from stated god-worship to wheel-sacrifice, having reached Rudrahood, this creature fully enjoys divine pleasures.
The Sanskrit: *tathoktadevapūjādicakrayāgāntakarmaṇā | rudratvametyasau janturbhogāndivyānsamaśnute*

## v.19
Now we speak clearly: for the venerable accomplishment, the channel-course — whichever channel one wishes to set flowing, that very one he should visualize.
The Sanskrit: *atha vacmaḥ sphuṭaṃ śrīmatsiddhaye nāḍicāraṇam | yā vāhayitumiṣyeta nāḍī tāmeva bhāvayet*

## v.20
In visualization-identity that channel clearly flows; or, to set flowing whichever is desired — that limb, with that hand.
The Sanskrit: *bhāvanātanmayībhāve sā nāḍī vahati sphuṭam | yadvā vāhayituṃ yeṣṭā tadaṅgaṃ tena pāṇinā*

## v.21
Pressing the belly, he should bend — it flows, the little channel, in a moment; thus even by means of śrāddha, the state of both enjoyment and liberation.
The Sanskrit: *āpīḍya kukṣiṃ namayetsā vahennāḍikā kṣaṇāt | evaṃ śrāddhamukhenāpi bhogamokṣobhayasthitim*

## v.22
Let him effect it, thus — by compassionate Śiva said here and there; when śaktipāta has arisen for the creature, by whatever means the guide.
The Sanskrit: *kuryāditi śivenoktaṃ tatra tatra kṛpālunā | śaktipātodaye jantoryenopāyena daiśikaḥ*

## v.23
He effects the uplifting, and that avails for his nirvāṇa; for the uplifter is the God of gods, he of inconceivable power.
The Sanskrit: *karotyuddharaṇaṃ tattannirvāṇāyāsya kalpate | uddhartā devadevo hi sa cācintyaprabhāvakaḥ*

## v.24
To the means merely through the door of guru-initiation and the rest he should resort; told in the venerable so-called Mataṅga after the sage's question.
The Sanskrit: *upāyaṃ gurudīkṣādidvāramātreṇa saṃśrayet | uktaṃ śrīmanmataṅgākhye munipraśnādanantaram*

## v.25
'Liberation comes from discrimination of the tattvas — if from initiation, from yoga, how could it come from mere conduct?' — to this, a fitting answer.
The Sanskrit: *muktirvivekāttattvānāṃ dīkṣāto yogato yadi | caryāmātrātkathaṃ sā syādityataḥ samamuttaram*

## v.26
Laughing, the Lord said: 'Whence your delusion about the Supreme Lord? For his all-gracing nature is well-established — look!'
The Sanskrit: *prahasyoce vibhuḥ kasmādbhrāntiste parameśituḥ | sarvānugrāhakatvaṃ hi saṃsiddhaṃ dṛśyatāṃ kila*

## v.27
For one facing death, poison, disease, weapons and the rest are indeed the cause; whether small or great, that, in that manner meditated upon, is a cause of liberation.
The Sanskrit: *prāptamṛtyorviṣavyādhiśastrādi kila kāraṇam | alpaṃ vā bahu vā tadvadanudhyā muktikāraṇam*

## v.28
For liberation's sake these outer marks are employed; knowing thus, there is no doubt — so it is to be done by the wise.
The Sanskrit: *muktyarthamupacaryante bāhyaliṅgānyamūni tu | iti jñātvā na sandeha itthaṃ kāryo vipaścitā*

## v.29
'How could liberation come by just this much?' — one should resort to supreme devotion. Told is the śrāddha-rule, crusher of delusion, poison, and dread.
The Sanskrit: *iyataiva kathaṃ muktiriti bhaktiṃ parāṃ śrayet | uktaḥ śrāddhavidhirbhrāntigarātaṅkavimardanaḥ*

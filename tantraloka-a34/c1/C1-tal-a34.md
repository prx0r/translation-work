# C1 — tal-a34

## SUMMARY

Sequence-bound entry into one's own nature. The āṇava, manifoldly taught toward Śiva-hood, works through penetration of its own intervals and rest in the proximate station. Abandonment of the āṇava opens the Śākta plane; the same motion opens the Śāmbhava — each rung distinct in graded succession. The ladder's fruit: a Bhairava-state flooded with the rays of sequentially-dawned awakening, entered finally without means, forever, as one's own self holding the universe within. The teaching stands received from the Supreme One.

## FUNCTION

Establishes the finale's ascent law: every means perfected by abandonment, the means-less entry as completion rather than refusal, recognition rather than acquisition as liberation's mechanism, and lineage authority as the seal of the whole thirty-seven-ahnika work.

## KEY TERMS

svasvarūpapraveśa (entry into one's own nature); krama (graded sequence binding the chapter); āṇava (lowest means: utterance, organ, meditation, phoneme, station); antar-antara (interval after interval, the āṇava's true field); savidha-pada (proximate-kindred station); saṃtyāga (abandonment as the engine of ascent); śāktī bhūmi and śāmbhavī (Śākta and Śāmbhava planes); tāratamya (more-and-less gradation); abhyupāya-nirapekṣatā (independence from means, the anupāya moment); garbhita-viśvarūpa (the universe-wombed self); parameṣṭhin (Supreme One: Śiva or supreme preceptor — LIVE).

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Āhnika 34 closes the Tantraloka's doctrinal arc before the mantra compendium (ch30) and final rites: it returns to the upāya ladder of ch1 (śāmbhava/śākta/āṇava per Mālinīvijaya 2.21–23, quoted at TAL 1.168–170) and crowns it with the means-less entry of ch2 (anupāya, table-of-contents gatopāya; Tantrasara 1 caturvidham). Cross-links: a02 (anupāya doctrine), a01 (śāmbhava means and topics enunciation).

## EXPLANATION

The ahnika runs: (1) pratijñā — the sequence-bound entry announced; (2) the āṇava rung — manifold teaching, interval-penetration, proximate rest; (3) the ladder — abandon-and-resort repeated upward to Śākta then Śāmbhava, distinctly graded; (4) the crown — ray-flooded Bhairava-state, means-independence, eternal entry into the universe-wombed self; (5) the seal — told by the Supreme One, fork held open.

# Tantrasāra A01 — L1 controlled

## tsa01-p01
Stainless-art-abode, great new creation — the Mother; full-bodied, the Father of hidden splendor with five faces: may the Heart, which is the emission-nature of the throbbing being sprung from the couple of those two, the unsurpassed deathless clan, flash forth for me. (1.1) The Tantrāloka is vast — not everyone can plunge into it; so listen to this Tantrasāra, composed in plain words. (1.2) This lotus-heart of Abhinavagupta, its contraction gone through the splendor of the falling feet of Śambhunātha Bhāskara — pick it, for the worship of Maheśa. (1.3) Here, knowledge is the cause of liberation, through being the opponent of ignorance, the cause of bondage. And ignorance is twofold: intellect-located and personal. Therein, the intellect-located is doubt-natured and wrong-conviction-natured; the personal is thought-natured and contracted-manifestation-natured — and that itself is the root cause of saṃsāra, as we shall teach under the determination of stain. Therein, personal ignorance may be removed by initiation and the rest; but initiation itself is not possible while intellect-located non-determination-natured ignorance persists — since initiation, of the form of purification of principles and joining to Śiva, is preceded by determination of what to shun and take. Hence determinative, intellect-abiding knowledge alone is principal; and that itself, being practiced, kills personal ignorance too — since practice of thought-consciousness ends in the thought-free. For the self, thought-contracted-consciousness-light-natured, is Śiva-natured. Hence in all cases, all-thing-directed, right-determination-natured knowledge is to be adopted. And that is scripture-preceded; and scripture is authority only as Lord-spoken — since the things taught by others' scriptures are accepted therein discriminatively, and since there is reason-proved exposition beyond their content. Hence knowledge taught by another āgama liberates from bondage only so far, not from all; but the Lord's scripture liberates from all — five-streamed, differentiated into ten, eighteen, eight, and the rest. Than all that, the essence is the sixfold scriptures; than those, the Mālinīvijaya; and its contained content, gathered, is impossible to expound. Nor is there freedom or liberating power for one whose thing-nature is unexpounded — since only pure knowledge is so natured. Since the supreme goal is rooted in well-practiced knowledge, for its success this is begun. Ignorance, declared in scripture the cause of bondage — that is called stain; at the rise of the digit of full knowledge, all that goes to rootlessness. When self-consciousness rises with destroyed remainderless stains, there is liberation too; hence by this man, with scripture, I reveal entirely whatever knowable reality there may be. (1.4) Therein, here, own nature alone is supremely adoptable; and it is light-nature of all things — since non-light cannot be nature. And it is not many — since there is no entry of other natures into light, since there is no difference of nature. And place and time too are not dividers of it — since they too are light-natured. Hence there is one light alone; and it alone is consciousness — since consciousness is of the nature of thing-manifestation — and here, for all, there is no dispute. And that light is not dependent — since being-illumined is dependence, and since illuminedness needs another light, and since no other light at all exists. Hence there is the autonomous one light — through autonomy alone, free from limitation by place, time, and form, all-pervading, eternal, all-form-formless-natured. Of it, autonomy is bliss-power; its wonder is will-power; light-nature is consciousness-power; grasp-nature is knowledge-power; all-form-fitness is action-power. Thus, though endowed with chief powers, really will-knowledge-action-powered, unlimited light resting in own bliss is Śiva-natured. He alone, through autonomy, manifesting himself contracted, is called atom. And again he manifests his own self autonomously — whereby he shines as unlimited-light-Śiva-nature itself. There too, through autonomy, he manifests his own self without means or with means; and even with means — will, knowledge, or action as approach — hence threefoldness of immersion, by śāmbhava, śākta, āṇava distinction. Therein, all four forms too are here taught in order. The self, light-bodied, this Śiva, autonomous — by the force of autonomy's play, having hidden his own nature, whatever he would spread forth again, full — that, by force of sequence-and-nosequence, or by threefoldness. (1.5)

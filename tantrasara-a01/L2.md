# Tantrasāra A01 — L2 READ

## tsa01-p01 — one's own nature
Abhinavagupta opens in praise: the Mother of stainless art and new creation, the full-bodied Father of five hidden faces — may the Heart they throb forth, the deathless clan beyond all, flash for me. The Tantrāloka is an ocean few can dive; so hear its essence, said plainly. And reader — gather this lotus-heart of Abhinavagupta, opened by his master's feet, for the worship of the Great Lord.
The teaching begins with a clean cut. Knowledge frees because it opposes exactly what binds: not-knowing. And not-knowing is two things. One sits in the intellect — doubt, and being sure of the wrong thing. The other sits in the person — the churn of thoughts, awareness pulled tight. That second one is saṃsāra's root (its full account comes with the stains). Initiation can wash the personal kind — but initiation itself needs you to already tell shun from take, to be purified and joined to Śiva; while the intellect still can't decide, no initiation is possible. So first comes first: clear, deciding, intellect-dwelling knowledge. Practice that, and it finishes the other job too, since practised thought ends where thought ends. For what you are — awareness squeezed into thoughts, but light by nature — is Śiva already. So in every case, adopt knowledge that decides rightly about everything. And that knowledge comes led by scripture — scripture that counts being Lord-spoken, since it takes whatever lesser books say, keeps it discriminately, and out-argues the rest. Lesser revelations free you partway; only the Lord's word — five streams, ten, eighteen, eight and all their branches — frees altogether. Its essence: the sixfold books; theirs: the Mālinīvijaya, whose content no gathering can exhaust. Nobody is free, or frees, without the nature of things spelled out — only pure knowing does that. And since man's highest end stands on practised knowing, this book begins here to secure it. Scripture calls ignorance bondage's cause, stain by name; when full knowledge dawns entire, it goes rootless; when self-awareness rises with every stain destroyed, there is freedom too — so with this book I lay open all there is to know.
And here is the one thing to take hold of: your own nature. It can only be light — what doesn't shine can't be what a thing is. It can only be one — nothing alien gets into light, so natures can't differ; and place and time don't cut it either, being light themselves. So: one light, which is awareness — awareness just is things showing up, and no one disputes it. And it depends on nothing — to be lit is to depend, and depending needs another light, and there is none. So the one light is free: unbounded by place, time, or shape, everywhere, always, every shape and none. Its freedom is bliss; its wonder, will; its shining, consciousness; its grasp, knowledge; its fitting every shape, action. Chief powers in display, three in truth — will, knowing, doing — the boundless light resting in its own bliss is Śiva. Contracting himself by that same freedom, he is called the atom. Then, freely still, he shows himself again as boundless Śiva-light. And that showing goes with means or without: will, knowing, or doing as the way in — three immersions, śāmbhava, śākta, of the atom — and a fourth besides; all four taught here in turn. The self is Śiva, light-bodied, free — hiding his own nature in freedom's play, then spreading it whole again, whether step by step, all at once, or three ways at once.

# Tantrasāra a02 — L2 READ

## v.1
First of the four ways in: the way with no way. We shall expound it.
The Sanskrit: *atha anupāyam eva tāvat vyākhyāsyāmaḥ*

## v.2
When grace has struck firm, a person hears the teacher's word once — and then discriminates for himself. From that point his immersion needs nothing and never sets.
The Sanskrit: *yadā khalu dṛḍhaśaktipātāviddhaḥ svayam eva itthaṃ vivecayati sakṛd eva guruvacanam avadhārya tadā punar upāyavirahito nityoditaḥ asya samāveśaḥ*

## v.3
Here "yoga-limb" means one thing only: thinking it through. But what is there to think? The Lord, who is self-shining, is yourself. So what could a means do? It can't get you your nature — you have it eternally. It can't inform you — you shine already. It can't strip a covering — there is none. It can't take you inside — there's no outsider to go in.
The Sanskrit: *atra ca tarka eva yogāṅgam iti kathaṃ vivecayati iti cet ucyate yo 'yaṃ parameśvaraḥ svaprakāśarūpaḥ svātmā tatra kim upāyena kriyate na svarūpalābho nityatvāt na jñaptiḥ svayaṃprakāśamānatvāt nāvaraṇavigamaḥ āvaraṇasya kasyacid api asaṃbhavāt na tadanupraveśaḥ anupraveṣṭuḥ vyatiriktasya abhāvāt*

## v.4
And the means itself, as something separate from you, is equally impossible. So: all this is one reality, consciousness and nothing else — time can't measure it, place can't bound it, qualities can't fade it, shapes can't rule it, words can't point it out, proofs can't build it up. By its own will it is what lets everything from time to proof arrive at its nature; free, thick bliss; and that is I, with the universe mirrored inside me. Who sees this steadily is immersed in the Lord always, without means — no mantra binds him, no worship, no sitting, no observance.
The Sanskrit: *kaś cātra upāyaḥ tasyāpi vyatiriktasya anupapatteḥ tasmāt samastam idam ekaṃ cinmātratattvaṃ kālena akalitaṃ deśena aparicchinnam upādhibhir amlānam ākṛtibhir aniyantritaṃ śabdair asaṃdiṣṭaṃ pramāṇair aprapañcitaṃ kālādeḥ pramāṇaparyantasya svecchayaiva svarūpalābhanimittaṃ ca svatantram ānandaghanaṃ tattvaṃ tad eva ca aham tatraiva antar mayi viśvaṃ pratibimbitam evaṃ dṛḍhaṃ viviñcānasya śaśvad eva pārameśvaraḥ samāveśo nirupāyaka eva tasya ca na mantrapūjādhyānacaryādiniyantraṇā kācit*

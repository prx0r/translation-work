# C1 — Tantrasāra Ahnika 2 (anupāya)

## tsa02-p01 — the means-less immersion
**SUMMARY.** The first of the four ways in: the way with no way. When grace has struck firm, a person hears the teacher's word once and then discriminates for himself; from that point his immersion needs nothing and never sets. The question how does he discriminate? is answered by four impossibilities: no nature to attain (eternal), nothing to notify (self-shining), no covering to remove (none possible), no one to enter (no distinct enterer). The means itself, as something separate, is equally impossible. Hence all is one consciousness-only reality — unmeasured by time, undelimited by place, unfaded by attributes, uncontrolled by forms, unindicated by words, unelaborated by valid means — whose own will is the cause of nature-attainment from time to proof; autonomous, bliss-dense; I am that, with the universe mirrored in me. Who sees this steadily is immersed in the Lord always, without means — no mantra, worship, meditation, or conduct binds him.

**FUNCTION.** Establishes the first (anupāya) of the four upāyas. Argues that means presuppose lack, hiddenness, or distance — all impossible for self-light.

**KEY TERMS.** anupāya (means-less), samāveśa (immersion), nityodita (ever-risen), cinmātratattva (consciousness-only reality), svātantrya (autonomy), ānandaghana (bliss-dense), tarka (reason), vimarśa (grasping).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** Follows A01 (svabhāva); the four upāyas (anupāya/śāmbhava/śākta/āṇava) are previewed there and taught in A02–A05.

**EXPLANATION.** The passage moves in three steps, each removing a supposed need for means. First, the occasion: firm-descent-pierced, one heeds the teacher's word once, then self-discriminates — and immersion follows, means-devoid, ever-risen. The heeds once is crucial: no repetition, no practice, no gradual approach; the descent itself does the work. Second, the four impossibilities: attainment of nature (nityatvāt — you have it eternally, so nothing to gain), notification (svayaṃprakāśamānatvāt — you shine already, so nothing to inform), covering-removal (āvaraṇasya asaṃbhavāt — no covering exists to strip), entry (anupraveṣṭuḥ abhāvāt — no outsider to go inside). Plus the means itself as a distinct entity (anupapatteḥ — a separate means is equally impossible). Third, the positive: six negations of the consciousness-only reality (unmeasured by time, undelimited by place, unfaded by attributes, uncontrolled by forms, unindicated by words, unelaborated by valid means); own-will as the cause of nature-attainment from time to proof; autonomous; bliss-dense; I am that, with the universe mirrored in me. Then the no-constraint close: no mantra, worship, meditation, or conduct binds him.

**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** The four impossibilities are Abhinava's own (Tantrasāra's condensation), not Utpaladeva's. The I am that identity claim is the ahnika's thesis. The other three upāyas follow (A03–A05).

**RELATED.** A01 (svabhāva), A03 (śāmbhava), essay0 Q4 (How do I see this?), spans TS2.1–2.4.

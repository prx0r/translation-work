# C1 NOTE — tsa-a02

Moved from C1 (pristine essence and SUMMARY only):

## SUMMARY

The first of the four ways in: the way with no way. When grace has struck firm, a person hears the teacher's word once and then discriminates for himself; from that point his immersion needs nothing and never sets. The question how does he discriminate? is answered by four impossibilities: no nature to attain (eternal), nothing to notify (self-shining), no covering to remove (none possible), no one to enter (no distinct enterer). The means itself, as something separate, is equally impossible. Hence all is one consciousness-only reality — unmeasured by time, undelimited by place, unfaded by attributes, uncontrolled by forms, unindicated by words, unelaborated by valid means — whose own will is the cause of nature-attainment from time to proof; autonomous, bliss-dense; I am that, with the universe mirrored in me. Who sees this steadily is immersed in the Lord always, without means — no mantra, worship, meditation, or conduct binds him.

## VOICE MAP

Teacher (announcement) v1; grace-struck disciple v2; cross-examination v3; identity and release v4.

## KEY TERMS

* anupāya — means-less as the first way in
* samāveśa — immersion, means-devoid and ever-risen
* nityodita — ever-risen as the mark of the struck one
* cinmātratattva — consciousness-only reality as all there is
* svātantrya — autonomy as the cause of nature-attainment
* ānandaghana — bliss-dense as what the reality is
* tarka — reason as the only yoga-limb here
* vimarśa — discriminating-grasp as what the struck one does

## CRUXES

* Means presuppose lack, hiddenness, or distance — all impossible for self-light; hence four impossibilities, not one.
* The means itself is equally impossible as something separate — the refutation turns on the refuter too.
* The six un- negations each sever one limiter (time, place, quality, shape, word, proof); none is decorative.

## FUNCTION

Establishes the first (anupāya) of the four upāyas. Argues that means presuppose lack, hiddenness, or distance — all impossible for self-light.

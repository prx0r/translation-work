# Tantrasara-a03 — L2 READ

## v.1
The light-formed Siva-tattva has been told; but when it cannot enter the undivided mandala, seeing the greater power of autonomy alone, it experiences the non-conceptual immersion of Bhairava. This is his teaching: all this mass of states is a reflection in the sky of knowing — since reflection is marked by appearing in difference, unable to stand alone, shining only by mixture with another. The mark of reflection: a face in a mirror, taste in teeth-water, smell in nostrils, a paired touch in the bliss-organ, a spear-and-pitchfork touch in the inner touch-organ, an echo in the sky.
The Sanskrit: *yad etat prakāśarūpaṃ śivatattvam uktam tatra akhaṇḍamaṇḍale yadā praveṣṭuṃ na śaknoti tadā svātantryaśaktim eva adhikāṃ paśyan nirvikalpam eva bhairavasamāveśam anubhavati ayaṃ ca asya upadeśaḥ sarvam idaṃ bhāvajātaṃ bodhagagane pratibimbamātraṃ pratibimbalakṣaṇopetatvāt idaṃ hi pratibimbasya lakṣaṇaṃ yat bhedena bhāsitam aśaktam anyavyāmiśratvenaiva bhāti tat pratibimbam mukharūpam iva darpaṇe rasa iva dantodake gandha iva ghrāṇa mithunasparśa iva ānandendriye śūlakuntādisparśo vā antaḥsparśanendriye pratiśrutkeva vyomni*

## v.2
That taste is not primary, because its works — curing disease and the rest — proceed by an occult eye.
The Sanskrit: *na hi sa raso mukhyaḥ tatkāryavyādhiśamanādyadṛṣṭeḥ*

## v.3
Nor are smell and touch primary: they belong to qualities, and in the qualitied's absence they cannot be, since the effect-chain begins without them.
The Sanskrit: *nāpi gandhasparśau mukhyau guṇinaḥ tatra abhāve tayor ayogāt kāryaparamparānārambhāt ca*

## v.4
And they are not simply nonexistent either — as body-stripping, seed, and the rest show.
The Sanskrit: *na ca tau na staḥ dehoddhūlanavisargādidarśanāt*

## v.5
Sound too is not primary: someone speaks it, and it is heard like an echo of one arriving.
The Sanskrit: *śabdo 'pi na mukhyaḥ ko 'pi vakti iti āgacchantyā iva pratiśrutkāyāḥ śravaṇāt*

## v.6
As that reflected thing shines, so the world shines in the light of the Supreme Lord.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ yathā etat pratibimbitaṃ bhāti tathaiva viśvaṃ parameśvaraprakāśe*

## v.7
But what would the image here be? Let it not be anything at all.
The Sanskrit: *nanu atra bimbaṃ kiṃ syāt mābhūt kiṃcit*

## v.8
How is that causeless? Then comes the question of the reason: why? By the logic of mirror-speech the reason will be the Supreme Lord's power itself — the synonym of autonomy. And because it bears the world's reflection, the Lord's world-selfhood follows: consciousness-made is the world, the place where consciousness manifests — this world here is obstruction, reflection-bearing. But that world-selfhood is the Lord's own nature, not un-contradicted, since self-nature contemplation of consciousness-nature cannot be.
The Sanskrit: *nanu kim akāraṇakaṃ tat hanta tarhi hetupraśnaḥ tat kiṃ bimbavācoyuktyā hetuś ca parameśvaraśaktir eva svātantryāparaparyāyā bhaviṣyati viśvapratibimbadhāritvāc ca viśvātmakatvaṃ bhagavataḥ saṃvinmayaṃ hi viśvaṃ caitanyasya vyaktisthānam iti tad eva hi viśvam atra pratīpam iti pratibimbadhāritvam asya tac ca tāvat viśvātmakatvaṃ parameśvarasya svarūpaṃ na anāmṛṣṭaṃ bhavati citsvabhāvasya svarūpānāmarśanānupapatteḥ*

## v.9
For in contemplation of the self's own nature there would in reality be mere inertness; and this contemplation does not depend on another — it is inseparable from consciousness-nature alone, the womb of supreme sound, so declared: as much as he is the Lord's world-establishing group of powers, so much does he contemplate.
The Sanskrit: *svarūpānāmarśane hi vastuto jaḍataiva syāt āmarśaś ca ayaṃ na sāketikaḥ api tu citsvabhāvatāmātranāntarīyakaḥ paranādagarbha uktaḥ sa ca yāvān viśvavyavasthāpakaḥ parameśvarasya śaktikalāpaḥ tāvantam āmṛśati*

## v.10
There the three primary powers of the Supreme Lord are unsurpassable-will-awakening — that very triad is a i u. From this triad the whole play of powers is carried. In the unsurpassable, rest is bliss; rest in will is rulership; awakening's rest is the wave. The beginning of action-power is that triad ā ī ū.
The Sanskrit: *tatra mukhyās tāvat tisraḥ parameśvarasya śaktayaḥ anuttarecchonmeṣa iti tad eva parāmarśatrayam a i u iti etasmād eva tritayāt sarvaḥ śaktiprapañcaḥ caryate anuttara eva hi viśrāntir ānandaḥ icchāyām eva viśrāntiḥ īśanam unmeṣa eva hi viśrāntir ūrmiḥ yaḥ kriyāśakteḥ prārambhaḥ tad eva parāmarśatrayam ā ī ū iti*

## v.11
There the prior triad of contemplations, being the essence of the light-part, is sun-natured; the final triad, by the prevalence of rest-natured bliss, is moon-natured — up to where the entry of the action-portion does not reach.
The Sanskrit: *atra ca prācyaṃ parāmarśatrayaṃ prakāśabhāgasāratvāt sūryātmakaṃ caramaṃ parāmarśatrayaṃ viśrāntisvabhāvāhlādaprādhānyāt somātmakam iyati yāvat karmāṃśasya anupraveśo nāsti*

## v.12
When action enters will and rulership, what is entered and ruled is called iṣyamāṇa and īśyamāṇa. It has two kinds: by mere light, ra-utterance; by rest, la-utterance — ra and la being light-and-stopping in nature. And the willed is not distinct like the external — in distinctness would lie its own creation; nor is will or rulership distinct — from non-distinctness only hearing stands for ra and la, no stable expression.
The Sanskrit: *yadā tu icchāyām īśane ca karma anupraviśati yat tat iṣyamāṇam īśyamāṇam iti ca ucyate tadā asya dvau bhedau prakāśamātreṇa raśrutiḥ viśrāntyā laśrutiḥ ralayoḥ prakāśastambhasvabhāvatvāt iṣyamāṇaṃ ca na bāhyavat sphuṭam sphuṭarūpatve tad eva nirmāṇaṃ syāt na icchā īśanaṃ vā ataḥ asphuṭatvāt eva śrutimātraṃ ralayoḥ na vyañjanavat sthitiḥ*

## v.13
This quad of letters is neuter, because it bears both shades: ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ.
The Sanskrit: *tad etad varṇacatuṣṭayam ubhayacchāyādhāritvāt napuṃsakam ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ iti*

## v.14
When the spread of unsurpassable-bliss runs in will and the rest, the letter-pair is e and oṃ.
The Sanskrit: *anuttarānandayoḥ icchādiṣu yadā prasaraḥ tadā varṇadvayam e oṃ iti*

## v.15
Further still, from the union of unsurpassable-bliss, the pair ai and au.
The Sanskrit: *tatrāpi punar anuttarānandasaṃghaṭṭāt varṇadvayam ai au iti*

## v.16
This is action-power — and that very quad is e ai o au.
The Sanskrit: *sā iyaṃ kriyāśaktiḥ tad eva ca varṇacatuṣṭayam e ai o au iti*

## v.17
Then at the end of action-power everything stands as effect; as far as it will enter the unsurpassable, just so long the prior stands as the essence of consciousness, as mere light, as the point: am.
The Sanskrit: *tataḥ punaḥ kriyāśaktyante sarvaṃ kāryabhūtaṃ yāvat anuttare pravekṣyati tāvad eva pūrvaṃ saṃvedanasāratayā prakāśamātratvena bindutayā āste am iti*

## v.18
Then from that very place the visarga of the unsurpassable is born: aḥ.
The Sanskrit: *tatas tatraiva anuttarasya visargo jāyate aḥ iti*

## v.19
Thus the sixteenfold seed-form of the contemplations is declared.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ ṣoḍaśakaṃ parāmarśānāṃ bījasvarūpam ucyate*

## v.20
Born from them, expression-natured, the womb-form.
The Sanskrit: *tadutthaṃ vyañjanātmakaṃ yonirūpam*

## v.21
There from the unsurpassable comes the ka-class — of faith; the ca-class — of object-bearing will; the pair ṭa- and ta-classes — from awakening; the pa-class — from union with the five powers; hence fivefoldness.
The Sanskrit: *tatra anuttarāt kavargaḥ śraddhāyāḥ icchāyāḥ cavargaḥ sakarmikāyā icchāyā dvau ṭavargas tavargaś ca unmeṣāt pavargaḥ śaktipañcakayogāt pañcakatvam*

## v.22
Of threefold will, ya ra lā come from awakening, and the va-letter; of threefold will, śa ṣa sā come from the visarga, and the ha-letter; the kṣa-letter is born of the womb-union.
The Sanskrit: *icchāyā eva trividhāyā ya ra lāḥ unmeṣāt vakāraḥ icchāyā eva trividhāyāḥ śa ṣa sāḥ visargāt hakāraḥ yonisaṃyogajaḥ kṣakāraḥ*

## v.23
Thus this Lord is the unsurpassable himself, in the form of the clan-Lord.
The Sanskrit: *ity eva eṣa bhagavān anuttara eva kuleśvararūpaḥ*

## v.24
His is one kaulic power of visarga, by which, from bliss-form onward to the end of outward creation, throbbing, the class-contemplations and the rest outward have attained the form of tattvas.
The Sanskrit: *tasya ca ekaiva kaulikī visargaśaktiḥ yayā ānandarūpāt prabhṛti iyatā bahiḥsṛṣṭiparyantena praspandataḥ vargādiparāmarśā eva bahis tattvarūpatāṃ prāptāḥ*

## v.25
That visarga is threefold: āṇava, the form of mind's rest; śākta, marked by mind's awakening; śāṃbhava, the form of mind's dissolution.
The Sanskrit: *sa ca eṣa visargas tridhā āṇavaḥ cittaviśrāntirūpaḥ śāktaḥ cittasaṃbodhalakṣaṇaḥ śāṃbhavaḥ cittapralayarūpaḥ iti*

## v.26
Thus the visarga itself is the Lord's power in the birth of the world.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ visarga eva viśvajanane bhagavataḥ śaktiḥ*

## v.27
When contemplation is by undividedness alone, then the Lord is one as the seed-womb — part-wise in contemplation, the power-endowed and the power.
The Sanskrit: *ity evam iyato yadā nirvibhāgatayā eva parāmarśaḥ tadā eka eva bhagavān bījayonitayā bhāgaśaḥ parāmarśe śaktimān śaktiś ca*

## v.28
Separate in the eightfold contemplation, in union with the wheel-Lord, nine classes; in the prevalence of each single contemplation, fiftyfold.
The Sanskrit: *pṛthak aṣṭakaparāmarśe cakreśvarasāhityena navavargaḥ ekaikaparāmarśaprādhānye pañcāśadātmakatā*

## v.29
Even there, in contemplation of a possibly differing part, eightyfold.
The Sanskrit: *tatrāpi sambhavadbhāgabhedaparāmarśane ekāśītirūpatvam*

## v.30
In reality there are only six contemplations; by spreading and returning, twelve — they nourish the fullness of the Supreme Lord's world-power.
The Sanskrit: *vastutas tu ṣaṭ eva parāmarśāḥ prasaraṇapratisaṃcaraṇayogena dvādaśa bhavantaḥ parameśvarasya viśvaśaktipūrṇatvaṃ puṣṇanti*

## v.31
Those very powers, being contemplation-formed, are the Lord's — Śrīkālikā they are named.
The Sanskrit: *tā eva etāḥ parāmarśarūpatvāt śaktayo bhagavatyaḥ śrīkālikā iti niruktāḥ*

## v.32
These too, pure contemplations that are power-formed, in pure knowledge by the para-apara form, from the mere contraction of māyā's awakening, share the nature of the knower and the known.
The Sanskrit: *ete ca śaktirūpā eva śuddhāḥ parāmarśāḥ śuddhavidyāyāṃ parāpararūpatvena māyonmeṣamātrasaṃkocāt vidyāvidyeśvararūpatāṃ bhajante*

## v.33
In māyā again, the crystal-clear divisions take māyic letter-nature — those who, attaining mundanity in paśyantī, madhyamā and vaikharī, stand up to the nature of external tattvas. When they are seen as body-like and revived by those pure contemplations — the life-replacing ones — they become vigorous, granting enjoyment and liberation. Thus, seeing oneself as merely the rest of all contemplations, as the reflection of all tattva-being-world divisions, by non-conceptuality, by the immersion of śāṃbhava — liberated while living.
The Sanskrit: *māyāyāṃ punaḥ sphaṭībhūtabhedavibhāgā māyīyavarṇatāṃ bhajante ye paśyantīmadhyamāvaikharīṣu vyāvahārikatvam āsādya bahīrūpatattvasvabhāvatāpattiparyantāḥ te ca māyīyā api śarīrakalpatvena yadā dṛśyante yadā ca teṣām uktanayair etaiḥ jīvitasthānīyaiḥ śuddhaiḥ parāmarśaiḥ pratyujjīvanaṃ kriyate tadā te savīryā bhavanti te ca tādṛśā bhogamokṣapradāḥ ity evaṃ sakalaparāmarśaviśrāntimātrarūpaṃ pratibimbitasamastatattvabhūtabhuvanabhedam ātmānaṃ paśyato nirvikalpatayā śāṃbhavena samāveśena jīvanmuktatā*

## v.34
Even here, as before, there is no obstruction of mantra and the like.
The Sanskrit: *atrāpi pūrvavat na mantrādiyantraṇā kācid iti*

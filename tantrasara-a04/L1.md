# Tantrasāra A04 — L1 controlled

## tsa04-p01
Therein, when one gradually refines thought for entry into the just-stated nature, then the contemplation-course preceded by true reasoning, good scripture, and good teacher-teaching has use. (4.1) For thus: by thought-force alone beings conceive the self as bound; that conceit is saṃsāra's block-cause. Hence adversarial-formed thought, arisen, shatters the saṃsāra-causing thought — hence cause of rise. (4.2) And it is of such form: than all limited-natured Śiva-ending principles, what is transcended — unlimited-consciousness-only-natured — that itself is the ultimate; that is thing-order-ground; that is the universe's vigor; through it lives the universe; that itself am I; hence universe-transcending and universe-self am I — thus. (4.3) And it does not arise for the Māyā-blind, from absence of true reasoning and the rest. (4.4) For Vaiṣṇavas and the rest are confined by passion-principle in so-much scripture — hence they do not face even toward higher vision. Hence they are haters of true-reasoning-good-scripture-good-teacher-teaching indeed. (4.5) As stated in the Lord's [scripture]: "Vaiṣṇavas and the rest, all of them, dyed by learning-passion, (4.6) do not find the supreme reality, devoid of omniscient knowledge." (4.7) Hence pierced by firm Śāmbhava descent alone, by good-scripture course, having refined thought, they enter the supreme nature. (4.8) "But thus would the supreme reality be thought-formed?" — Not so: for thought, fulfillment lies merely in shattering duality-habitation; but the supreme reality, everywhere in all forms, is self-shining itself. Hence therein thought serves neither for aiding nor refuting anything. (4.9) Therein, for one pierced by very firm descent, by itself, as self-perfected, rises true reasoning — which is called Goddess-initiated. (4.10) For others it is by scripture course — etcetera, in full in the descent-manifestation we shall teach. (4.11) But the teacher's function is in scripture-expounding; and scripture's function is in the rise of fitting thought born of unhesitating homogeneous connected text-study. Such thought-course alone is called true reasoning; and it alone is called contemplation — since, through making manifest by rendering brilliant what, though real, is unmanifest as if unreal, it is contemplated by which. (4.12) Nor here, apart from true reasoning of pure-knowledge-light-nature, is any other yoga-limb a direct means — of austerity and the rest, of the restraint class, of non-harm and the rest, of the yama kind, of filling and the rest, of the breath-control class — since concerned with knowable only: what function in consciousness? (4.13) Withdrawal too would merely heighten the sense-ground. Meditation, concentration, absorptions too, performed in practice-course in due order, would grant the meditator identity with the meditated thing. (4.14) And practice in the supreme principle, in Śiva-self, in own nature, is not possible at all. (4.15) For practice, of one rooted in consciousness, of the form of fixing breath-intellect-body, is like bundle-carrying, scripture-teaching, dance-practice; but in consciousness-nature there is nothing to take, nothing to remove — so how is there practice? (4.16) "What even by reasoning?" — if asked: here, it was stated that this is merely the manner of removing duality-habitation, not anything else. (4.17)"

## tsa04-p02
"Even in worldly practice, making manifest the liked form of this and that body etc. as consciousness-self, and disfavoring other forms — this alone is the point of practice. (4.18) But in the supreme principle, as stated, there is nothing to reject. (4.19) Duality-habitation too, though called so, is no separate thing-entity, but mere non-recognition of nature — hence it was stated that duality-removal is done by thought. (4.20) This ultimate: nature shining, in non-recognition-nature grasped by itself through autonomy, gradually abandoned — about-to-bloom, then blooming, then bloomed — by this course shines forth; and such shining too is the Lord's nature itself. Hence here the yoga-limbs are not direct means. (4.21) Reasoning too — even graced — true reasoning alone is therein the direct means. And it is pure knowledge; and refined manifoldly it comes to be — namely: sacrifice, fire-offering, muttering, vow, yoga. Therein, for establishing thought that all things abide in the Lord alone, that nothing distinct exists: offering all things into the Lord is sacrifice. And since, through loveliness, those who themselves resort to consciousness-entry find offering into the Lord easy — with that intent, outer use of lovely flowers, libations, scents and the rest was stated. (4.22) For obtaining rooted thought that all things are Lord-fire-made: in the Lord-consciousness-fire's glow, with intent of relishing the swallowing of all things, dissolving all things leaving fire-mass alone, is fire-offering. (4.23) Likewise, for the rise of both-natured grasping, regardless of outer-inner-and-rest object-natured distinct things: inner grasping that 'that supreme reality is own-nature-become' is muttering. (4.24) For gaining, everywhere always, the conceit of the means-less Lord, by conceit of Lord-sameness, seeing even of body and of pot and the rest, is vow. (4.25) As stated in Śrī Nandiśikhā: 'All-sameness is the supreme vow.' (4.26) Thus by varied pure-knowledge-portion thoughts, when the thought-free natural ultimate reality shines, abiding in its manifestation-form alone is yoga. (4.27) Therein the Lord, full-consciousness-natured — his fullness itself is power: clan, capability, wave, heart, essence, throb, glory, triple-peak, Kālī, attractor, Caṇḍī, speech, enjoyment, sight, eternal, and so on, by āgama-tongues proceeding by respective etymology, is named — by that form may he abide in meditators' hearts. (4.28) And she, seen with total-power-regard, shines as fullness-conviction. (4.29) And his powers are countless. (4.30) What need of much: whatever is universe, they are his powers — how could they be taught? (4.31) Of three, so far, the universe is completed: by which, as undivided-consciousness-only-nature, bearing, seeing, shining, from Śiva to earth, the Lord upholds — that is his glorious supreme power. (4.32) And by which, as mirror-elephant-like, by difference-nondifference, that is his glorious supreme-nonsupreme power. (4.33)"

"[Continuation: 4.34] By which, with mutually separate selfhood, by difference alone, that is his glorious nonsupreme power. (4.34) The holder that, by self-embrace, swallows the threefold through recollecting-turned-inward, is his goddess Parā herself — named by other words like glorious Mātṛsadbhāva, Kālakarṣiṇī. (4.35) These four powers, through autonomy, each proceed threefold. (4.36) In creation, maintenance, and dissolution — thus they become twelve. (4.37) As: consciousness first grasps a thing inwardly, then outwardly too, manifestly grasps it, grasping affection there, then grasps that same thing wanting re-absorption inward, then makes and swallows the doubt obstructing re-absorption, grasps in the self, by re-absorption, the thing-portion with swallowed-doubt-portion, grasps even 'my consumer-nature is this form' as own nature, grasps in consumer-nature-grasping the abiding of some thing as trace, of another as consciousness-only-remainder, grasps the sense-wheel by sole consequentiality of nature-grasping, grasps even the sense-lord, grasps even the imagined Māyā-knower-form, grasps even the knower relishing contraction-to-expansion-grasp, hence grasps even the expanded form — thus these twelve goddess consciousnesses, aiming at knowers one or many, partaking in rise simultaneously, in course, in twos, threes and so on, revolving wheel-like, shining outward too in month-fortnight-heap order down to pot-cloth order, nourishing the wheel-lord's autonomy, are called by the word Śrī Kālī. (4.38) And grasping is motion, throwing, knowing, counting, enjoying, sounding, dissolving into self. (4.39) As the venerable Bhūtirāja teachers said: 'From throwing, from knowing, Kālī — through grasping-power.' (4.40) And this sense is to be seen expanded here and there in my composed exposition, treatise, hymn and the rest. (4.41) Neither is the over-secret to be proclaimed in one place, nor ever to be hidden — so indeed our teachers. (4.42) Thus what was called sacrifice-offering-and-the-rest is to be regarded in Maheśvara of such kind. (4.43) For all, from Viṣṇu to Śiva, see the rejectable itself as adoptable-ground-nature — and that is false view, necessarily to be abandoned by unsurpassed yogins. For that very purpose is the great zeal of the Lord of Knowledge in the Experience-Hymn. In such sacrifice-to-yoga pentads, in each manifold establishment — as it comes to be, so one should practice — not by food-inedible-purity-impurity discrimination, of thing-nature-abandoned, mere-imagination-essenced, should the self be wearied. As stated first: purity is not thing-nature, like blueness — elsewhere, of that same, impurity is enjoined; as of giving, in initiatedness it is enjoined: 'for him that is impure there' — if so, in another injunction likewise; 'the other injunction is unreal, since sublated' — if so: no — sublation by Śiva-command alone is reasonable, proven by reasoning and by endless-āgama establishment beginning with Sarvajñānottara — as we shall teach. (4.44) Hence from Vedic onward, even Pārameśvara-Siddhānta-Tantra-Kula-Ucchuṣma-śāstra-taught restraint, prescription, or prohibition is here effective to the extent of nothing — it is established.

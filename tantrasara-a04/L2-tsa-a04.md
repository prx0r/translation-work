# Tantrasara-a04 — L2 READ

## v.1
When the concept impresses in order, for entry into the nature just told — then the contemplation-sequence, preceded by true reason, true scripture and the true teacher's instruction, has its use.
The Sanskrit: *tatra yadā vikalpaṃ krameṇa saṃskurute samanantaroktasvarūpapraveśāya tadā bhāvanākramasya sattarkasadāgamasadgurūpadeśapūrvakasya asti upayogaḥ*

## v.2
For by concept-strength alone beings mistake themselves to be bound; that misapprehension is the cause that obstructs saṃsāra — so the counter-pair concept arises: it tears the saṃsāra-causing concept, and is the cause of prosperity.
The Sanskrit: *tathā hi vikalpabalāt eva jantavo baddham ātmānam abhimanyante sa abhimānaḥ saṃsārapratibandhahetuḥ ataḥ pratidvandvirūpo vikalpa uditaḥ saṃsārahetuṃ vikalpaṃ dalayati iti abhyudayahetuḥ*

## v.3
Such a concept, having crossed all the delimited-natured tattvas from Śiva to the limit, is undelimited-consciousness-merely-formed — that is the supreme truth, the place where thing-ordering stands, the world's essence; by it he breathes the world — that am I, world-crossed, world-self am I.
The Sanskrit: *sa ca evaṃrūpaḥ samastebhyaḥ paricchinnasvabhāvebhyaḥ śivāntebhyaḥ tattvebhyo yat uttīrṇam aparicchinnasaṃvinmātrarūpaṃ tad eva ca paramārthaḥ tat vastuvyavasthāsthānaṃ tat viśvasya ojaḥ tena prāṇiti viśvam tad eva ca aham ato viśvottīrṇo viśvātmā ca aham iti*

## v.4
And this does not arise for the māyā-blind, since true-reason and the rest are absent.
The Sanskrit: *sa ca ayaṃ māyāndhānāṃ na utpadyate sattarkādīnām abhāvāt*

## v.5
For Viṣṇu-and-rest are limited in scripture by passion-tattva only, so even in higher vision they show no inclination toward it — therefore they are hostile to true-reason, true scripture and true teacher-instruction alone.
The Sanskrit: *vaiṣṇavādyā hi tāvanmātra eva āgame rāgatattvena niyamitā iti na ūrdhvadarśane 'pi tadunmukhatāṃ bhajante tataḥ sattarkasadāgamasadgurūpadeśadveṣiṇa eva*

## v.6
As said in the Pārameśvara: all Viṣṇu-and-rest are colored by knowledge-passion.
The Sanskrit: *yathoktaṃ pārameśvare vaiṣṇavādyāḥ samastās te vidyārāgeṇa rañjitāḥ*

## v.7
They do not find the supreme tattva, being devoid of omniscient knowledge.
The Sanskrit: *na vindanti paraṃ tattvaṃ sarvajñajñānavarjitāḥ iti*

## v.8
Therefore only those pierced by the firm descent of Śāṃbhava-power, having impressed the concept by the order of true scripture and the rest, enter the supreme own-nature.
The Sanskrit: *tasmāt śāṃbhavadṛḍḥaśaktipātāviddhā eva sadāgamādikrameṇa vikalpaṃ saṃskṛtya paraṃ svarūpaṃ praviśanti*

## v.9
But then would the supreme tattva be of concept-form? Not so: the concept's work is fulfilled merely in breaking the residence-in-duality; the supreme tattva, however, is everywhere all-formed and self-luminous — so there is no service or refutation of it for anyone from concept.
The Sanskrit: *nanu itthaṃ paraṃ tattvaṃ vikalpyarūpaṃ syāt maivam vikalpasya dvaitādhivāsabhaṅgamātre caritārthatvāt paraṃ tattvaṃ tu sarvatra sarvarūpatayā svaprakāśam eva iti na tatra vikalpaḥ kasyaicit upakriyāyai khaṇḍanāyai vā*

## v.10
There, for one pierced by an excessively firm descent of power, true-reason arises by itself through self-attainment — he is called one initiated by the goddesses.
The Sanskrit: *tatra atidṛḍhaśaktipātāviddhasya svayam eva sāṃsiddhikatayā sattarka udeti yo 'sau devībhiḥ dīkṣita iti ucyate*

## v.11
For another, by the order of scripture — with elaboration we will say this in the revelation of power-descent.
The Sanskrit: *anyasya āgamakrameṇa ityādi savistaraṃ śaktipātaprakāśane vakṣyāmaḥ*

## v.12
But the task lies in determining the teacher's scripture, and in the arising of the fitting concept that is born of the scripture's chain of kin: that chain of concepts is called true-reason; it is called contemplation as well, since from indistinctness an existing thing is contemplated as non-existent by being made distinct.
The Sanskrit: *kiṃ tu guror āgamanirūpaṇe vyāpāraḥ āgamasya ca niḥśaṅkasajātīyatatprabandhaprasavanibandhanasamucitavikalpodaye vyāpāraḥ tathāvidhavikalpaprabandha eva sattarka iti uktaḥ sa eva ca bhāvanā bhaṇyate asphuṭatvāt bhūtam api artham abhūtam iva sphuṭatvāpādanena bhāvyate yayā iti*

## v.13
And here no other yoga-limb is a direct means apart from true-reason, the light-form of pure knowledge: of austerity and the rest, of the restraint-class, of nonviolence and the rest — the yama-type, of retention and the rest, the prāṇāyāma-class — what task is there in consciousness by being established in the knowable merely?
The Sanskrit: *na ca atra sattarkāt śuddhavidyāprakāśarūpāt ṛte anyat yogāṅgaṃ sākṣāt upāyaḥ tapaḥprabhṛteḥ niyamavargasya ahiṃsādeś ca yamaprakārasya pūrakādeḥ prāṇāyāmavargasya vedyamātraniṣṭhatvena ka iva saṃvidi vyāpāraḥ*

## v.14
Withdrawal too should make the instrument-level excellent; meditation, concentration and samādhi too, performed successively by the order of practice, should impart identity-with-the-meditated-object to the meditator.
The Sanskrit: *pratyāhāro 'pi karaṇabhūmim eva sātiśayāṃ kuryāt dhyānadhāraṇāsamādhayo 'pi yathottaram abhyāsakrameṇa nirvartyamānā dhyeyavastutādātmyaṃ dhyātuḥ vitareyuḥ*

## v.15
But practice in the supreme tattva, in the Siva-self, in one's own nature, is simply not possible.
The Sanskrit: *abhyāsaś ca pare tattve śivātmani svasvabhāve na saṃbhavaty eva*

## v.16
For one rooted in consciousness, practice is the form of fixing breath, mind and body — like carrying a load, knowing the scripture's meaning, dancing-practice; but in the form of consciousness nothing is to be taken and nothing removed — how then practice?
The Sanskrit: *saṃvidrūḍhasya prāṇabuddhidehaniṣṭhīkaraṇarūpo hi abhyāsaḥ bhārodvahanaśāstrārthabodhanṛttābhyāsavat saṃvidrūpe tu na kiṃcit ādātavyaṃ na apasaraṇīyam iti katham abhyāsaḥ*

## v.17
If it is asked — 'by reasoning too?' — it is said: this is only the mode of removing the residence-in-duality, nothing else at all.
The Sanskrit: *kiṃ tarkeṇāpi iti cet uktam atra dvaitādhivāsanirāsaprakāra eva ayaṃ na tu anyat kiṃcid iti*

## v.18
Even in ordinary practice: the manifesting of the desired form of body and the rest as consciousness-selfhood, and the contemplation of the absence of other forms — that alone is the purpose of practice.
The Sanskrit: *laukike 'pi vā abhyāse cidātmatvena sarvarūpasya tasya tasya dehādeḥ abhimatarūpatāprakaṭīkaraṇaṃ taditararūpanyagbhāvanaṃ ca iti eṣa eva abhyāsārthaḥ*

## v.19
But in the supreme tattva nothing at all is to be discarded, it is said.
The Sanskrit: *paratattve tu na kiṃcit apāsyam iti uktam*

## v.20
The residence-in-duality too, mark well, is not some separate thing-existing: it is mere own-nature-manifestation — hence the rejection of duality is done by concept, from the saying.
The Sanskrit: *dvaitādhivāso 'pi nāma na kaścana pṛthak vastubhūtaḥ api tu svarūpākhyātimātraṃ tat ato dvaitāpāsanaṃ vikalpena kriyata ity ukteḥ*

## v.21
This supreme truth, own-nature shining as the form of manifestation, is taken by itself from autonomy; by the order — thrown off, turned toward expansion, then expanding, then expanded — it shines in this way; and the shining too is the Supreme Lord's own nature — so here the yoga-limbs are not the direct means.
The Sanskrit: *ayaṃ paramārthaḥ svarūpaṃ prakāśamānam akhyātirūpatvaṃ svayaṃ svātantryāt gṛhītaṃ krameṇa projjhya vikāsonmukham atha vikasat atha vikasitam ity anena krameṇa prakāśate tathā prakāśanam api parameśvarasya svarūpam eva tasmāt na atra yogāṅgāni sākṣāt upāyaḥ*

## v.22
Reason may favor it, but true-reason alone is the direct means there; it is pure knowledge too, and is impressed in many forms: offering, fire-offering, recitation, vow, yoga. Therein the abode of all states is the Supreme Lord alone — nothing separate exists; for the establishment of conviction in concept, the offering of all states to the Supreme Lord itself is the offering — from heart-nature, the offering in the Supreme Lord is easy for those who themselves show entry into consciousness; hence the external use of flower, satisfaction, scent and the rest was declared.
The Sanskrit: *tarkaṃ tu anugṛhṇīyur api sattarka eva sākṣāt tatra upāyaḥ sa eva ca śuddhavidyā sa ca bahuprakāratayā saṃskṛto bhavati tadyathā yāgo homo japo vrataṃ yoga iti tatra bhāvānāṃ sarveṣāṃ parameśvara eva sthitiḥ nānyat vyatiriktam asti iti vikalparūḍhisiddhaye parameśvara eva sarvabhāvārpaṇaṃ yāgaḥ sa ca hṛdyatvāt ye saṃvidanupraveśaṃ svayam eva bhajante teṣāṃ suśakaṃ parameśvare arpaṇam ity abhiprāyeṇa hṛdyānāṃ kusumatarpaṇagandhādīnāṃ bahir upayoga uktaḥ*

## v.23
All states are made of the light of the Supreme Lord: for the attainment of the convicted concept, in the light of the Supreme Lord's consciousness-fire, in the desire to swallow all states — the sudden dissolution of all states into the residue of mere light: that is the fire-offering.
The Sanskrit: *sarve bhāvāḥ parameśvaratejomayā iti rūḍhavikalpaprāptyai parameśasaṃvidanalatejasi samastabhāvagrāsarasikatābhimate tattejomātrāvaśeṣatvasahasamastabhāvavilāpanaṃ homaḥ*

## v.24
Likewise, for the arising of the both-natured contemplation, by disregard alone of the states divided by outer, inner and other knowable forms: that supreme tattva is formed of one's own nature — the inner contemplation is the recitation.
The Sanskrit: *tathā ubhayātmakaparāmarśodayārthaṃ bāhyābhyantarādiprameyarūpabhinnabhāvānapekṣayaiva evaṃvidhaṃ tat paraṃ tattvaṃ svasvabhāvabhūtam iti antaḥ parāmarśanaṃ japaḥ*

## v.25
For the gaining everywhere-always of the impersonal identification with the Supreme Lord, by the identification of equality with the Supreme Lord: the viewing even of the body, even of a pot and the rest, is the vow.
The Sanskrit: *sarvatra sarvadā nirupāyaparameśvarābhimānalābhāya parameśvarasamatābhimānena dehasyāpi ghaṭāder api avalokanaṃ vratam*

## v.26
As said in the Śrī-Nandiśikhā: all-equality is the supreme vow.
The Sanskrit: *yathoktaṃ śrīnandiśikhāyām sarvasāmyaṃ paraṃ vratam iti*

## v.27
Thus, by various concepts formed of portions of pure knowledge, what concept-independent, natural, supreme-truth tattva shines — for establishment in being merely the eternal shining of that kind, the specific concept that tracks its own nature: that is yoga.
The Sanskrit: *itthaṃ vicitraiḥ śuddhavidyāṃśarūpaiḥ vikalpaiḥ yat anapekṣitavikalpaṃ svābhāvikaṃ paramārthatattvaṃ prakāśate tasyaiva sanātanatathāvidhaprakāśamātratārūḍhaye tatsvarūpānusaṃdhānātmā vikalpaviśeṣo yogaḥ*

## v.28
Therein the Supreme Lord, of the nature of full consciousness — fullness is his power, clan, capability, wave, heart, essence, pulsation, glory; the triad, Kālī, Karṣaṇī, Caṇḍī, Vāṇī, enjoyment, vision, Nityā and the rest — is denoted by the scriptural languages that run according to each sense; by that, by that form, let it be in the hearts of the meditators.
The Sanskrit: *tatra parameśvaraḥ pūrṇasaṃvitsvabhāvaḥ pūrṇataiva asya śaktiḥ kulaṃ sāmarthyam ūrmiḥ hṛdayaṃ sāraṃ spandaḥ vibhūtiḥ trīśikā kālī karṣaṇī caṇḍī vāṇī bhogo dṛk nityā ityādibhiḥ āgamabhāṣābhiḥ tattadanvarthapravṛttābhiḥ abhidhīyate tena tena rūpeṇa dhyāyināṃ hṛdi āstām iti*

## v.29
And she shines as fullness-consciousness by the seeing of the nature of the whole power.
The Sanskrit: *sā ca samagraśaktitādarśanena pūrṇatāsaṃvit prakāśate*

## v.30
And his powers are countless.
The Sanskrit: *śaktayaś ca asya asaṃkhyeyāḥ*

## v.31
Why say more? The world — those are his powers; how can they be taught?
The Sanskrit: *kiṃ bahunā yat viśvaṃ tā asya śaktayaḥ tāḥ katham upadeṣṭuṃ śakyāḥ iti*

## v.32
In three the world is completed: by which this bears, sees and illumines up to the bearer-limit of Śiva as mere uncaricatured consciousness-form — the Supreme Lord; that is his Śrīparā-power.
The Sanskrit: *tisṛṣu tāvat viśvaṃ samāpyate yayā idaṃ śivādidharaṇyantam avikalpyasaṃvinmātrarūpatayā bibharti ca paśyati ca bhāsayati ca parameśvaraḥ sā asya śrīparaśaktiḥ*

## v.33
By which, like a mirror or an elephant and the rest, by difference and undifference — that is his Śrīparāparā-power.
The Sanskrit: *yayā ca darpaṇahastyādivat bhedābhedābhyāṃ sā asya śrīparāparaśaktiḥ*

## v.34
By which, with the self distinct from one another, by difference alone — that is his Śrīmadaparā-power.
The Sanskrit: *yayā parasparaviviktātmanā bhedenaiva sā asya śrīmadaparaśaktiḥ*

## v.35
By which the threefold holding of this, in the self itself, by embracing, in the form of tracking, grasps — she is the Goddess Śrīparā herself, named by other words such as Śrīmat-mātṛ, sat-bhāva, kāla, karṣiṇī.
The Sanskrit: *etat trividhaṃ yayā dhāraṇam ātmany eva kroḍīkāreṇa anusandhānātmanā grasate sā asya bhagavatī śrīparaiva śrīmanmātṛsadbhāvakālakarṣiṇyādiśabdāntaraniruktā*

## v.36
These four powers, from autonomy, each operates threefold alone.
The Sanskrit: *tā etāḥ catasraḥ śaktayaḥ svātantryāt pratyekaṃ tridhaiva vartante*

## v.37
In creation, preservation and dissolution — twelve they become.
The Sanskrit: *sṛṣṭau sthitau saṃhāre ca iti dvādaśa bhavanti*

## v.38
Thus consciousness first constructs the state within; then outside too by distinctness; taking reddened-nature right there; then, with the intention to reabsorb that state within; then it forms the doubt that is the obstacle to reabsorption, and grasps it; the doubt-grasped portion of the state it constructs in the self by reabsorption; then it constructs even the reabsorber-nature — my-this-form — as its own nature; then, in constructing the reabsorber-nature, the standing of some state by habit-nature, and of some, the residue-of-consciousness-merely; then, by being inseparable from the construction of own-nature, the instrument-wheel; then even the instrument-Lord; then it constructs the māyic knower-form too; it constructs the knower who is contraction-and-release-turned, expansion-grasping, taste-loving; therefore the expanded form too it constructs — thus these twelve Goddess-consciousnesses, directing one knower-object or even several, simultaneously or in order, two-wise, three-wise, even-standing, and arising-portioned, revolving like a wheel, outside too by the order of month, segment and asterism, and within too by the order of pot, cloth and the rest, shining, nourishing the autonomy of the wheel-Lord, denoted by the word Śrīkālī.
The Sanskrit: *tathā hi saṃvit pūrvam antar eva bhāvaṃ kalayati tato bahir api sphuṭatayā kalayati tatraiva raktimayatāṃ gṛhītvā tataḥ tam eva bhāvam antar upasaṃjihīrṣayā kalayati tataś ca tadupasaṃhāravighnabhūtāṃ śaṅkāṃ nirmiṇoti ca grasate ca grastaśaṅkāṃśaṃ bhāvabhāgam ātmani upasaṃhāreṇa kalayati tata upasaṃhartṛtvaṃ mamedaṃ rūpam ity api svabhāvam eva kalayati tata upasaṃhartṛsvabhāvakalane kasyacid bhāvasya vāsanātmanā avasthitiṃ kasyacit tu saṃvinmātrāvaśeṣatāṃ kalayati tataḥ svarūpakalanānāntarīyakatvenaiva karaṇacakraṃ kalayati tataḥ karaṇeśvaram api kalayati tataḥ kalpitaṃ māyīyaṃ pramātṛrūpam api kalayati saṅkocatyāgonmukhavikāsagrahaṇarasikam api pramātāraṃ kalayati ato vikasitam api rūpaṃ kalayati iti etā dvādaśa bhagavatyaḥ saṃvidaḥ pramātṝn ekaṃ vāpi uddiśya yugapat krameṇa dviśaḥ triśa ityādisthityāpi udayabhāginyaḥ cakravad āvartamānā bahir api māsakalārāśyādikrameṇa antato vā ghaṭapaṭādikrameṇāpi bhāsamānāḥ cakreśvarasya svātantryaṃ puṣṇatyaḥ śrīkālīśabdavācyāḥ*

## v.39
And construction is motion, throw, knowledge, counting, enjoyment-making, sound, and self-dissolving too.
The Sanskrit: *kalanaṃ ca gatiḥ kṣepo jñānaṃ gaṇanaṃ bhogīkaraṇaṃ śabdanaṃ svātmalayīkaraṇaṃ ca*

## v.40
As the Śrībhūtirāja-teachers say: from the throw and from the knowledge, Kālī — by subjection to construction, then.
The Sanskrit: *yad āhuḥ śrībhūtirājaguravaḥ kṣepāj jñānāc ca kālī kalanavaśatayātha iti*

## v.41
And this meaning is to be considered where it is spread in my composed commentary, in the treatise, hymn and the rest.
The Sanskrit: *eṣa ca arthaḥ tatra tatra madviracite vivaraṇe prakaraṇastotrādau vitatya vīkṣyaḥ*

## v.42
Not too secret to be shown somewhere, nor to be hidden in every way — so our teachers.
The Sanskrit: *na atirahasyam ekatra khyāpyaṃ na ca sarvathā gopyam iti hi asmadguravaḥ*

## v.43
Thus what was said — offering, fire-offering and the rest: in this kind the Great-Lord alone is to be thought.
The Sanskrit: *tad evam yad uktaṃ yāgahomādi tat evaṃvidhe maheśvara eva mantavyam*

## v.44
All see Viṣṇu-to-Siva as the ground-form only of what is to be rejected, of what is to be accepted; and that false view is necessarily to be abandoned by unsurpassable yogins — for that very reason the knowledge-Lord's great fervor in the hymn of experience: in this kind, in offering and the rest, at the end of yoga, in the fivefold, each many-form establishment; as it becomes, so let him practice — not by discriminating edible and inedible, clean and unclean and the rest, by abandoning thing-qualities, by a fancy-merely-essence tormenting the self. For purity is not the form of a thing like blueness elsewhere, from the command of impurity on it; like the gift's command in initiation — if 'it is impure there' by another command too the same, other-command not-existing from being overridden — if not, the obstructive power of Siva's command alone is established by reason, and established by the omniscient, unsurpassable, endless scripture too — we will say.
The Sanskrit: *sarve hi heyam eva upādeyabhūmirūpaṃ viṣṇutaḥ prabhṛti śivāntaṃ paramaśivatayā paśyanti tac ca mithyādarśanam avaśyatyājyam anuttarayogibhir iti tadartham eva vidyādhipateḥ anubhavastotre mahān saṃrambhaḥ evaṃvidhe yāgādau yogānte ca pañcake pratyekaṃ bahuprakāraṃ nirūḍhiḥ yathā yathā bhavati tathaiva ācaret na tu bhakṣyābhakṣyaśuddhyaśuddhyādivivecanayā vastudharmojjhitayā kalpanāmātrasārayā svātmā khedanīya iti uktaṃ śrīpūrvādau na hi śuddhiḥ vastuno rūpaṃ nīlatvavat anyatra tasyaiva aśuddhicodanāt dānasyeva dīkṣitatve codanātaḥ tasya tat tatra aśuddham iti cet codanāntare 'pi tulyaṃ codanāntaram asat tadbādhitatvāt iti cet na śivacodanāyā eva bādhakatvaṃ yuktisiddhaṃ sarvajñānottarādyanantāgamasiddhaṃ ca iti vakṣyāmaḥ*

## v.45
Therefore from the Vedic on, spoken in the Pārameśvara-system, tantra, clan, Ucchuṣma and other scriptures too, whatever restraint or injunction or prohibition — here it is established as of no effect whatsoever.
The Sanskrit: *tasmāt vaidikāt prabhṛti pārameśvarasiddhāntatantrakulocchuṣmādiśāstrokto 'pi yo niyamo vidhiḥ vā niṣedho vā so 'tra yāvad akiṃcitkara eva iti siddham*

## v.46
Likewise it is said in the Śrīpūrva-and-rest, spread — from the Tantrāloka it is to be sought.
The Sanskrit: *tathaiva ca uktaṃ śrīpūrvādau vitatya tantrālokāt anveṣyam*

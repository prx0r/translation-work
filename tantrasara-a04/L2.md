# Tantrasāra A04 — L2 READ

## tsa04-p01 — thought washing thought
When you start refining thought itself, to walk into the nature just described, the old toolkit — true reasoning, good books, a good teacher's word — finally earns its keep. Here's why. Beings tie themselves up with nothing but thought-force: "I am bound." That knot is saṃsāra's lock. So let a counter-thought rise that eats the binding thought, and you have the cause of rising. Its shape: beyond every limited principle down to Śiva himself lies something unbordered — bare unlimited awareness. That is the ultimate. That is where things stand. That is the sap of the world — the world lives off it. That is I: past the world and made of it, both. The Māyā-blind don't get this thought — they lack true reasoning and the rest. The Vaiṣṇavas and company are fenced inside their own books' passion-principle; they won't even face uphill, and hate the reasoning, books, and teachers that would turn them. Their own books say it: dyed with learning's dye, they miss the supreme, void of all-knowing knowledge. Only those pierced by Śambhu's firm descent, refining thought through the good old course, walk into supreme nature. "But doesn't that make the supreme a thought?" No — thought's whole job is wrecking the habit of twoness; the supreme shines, everywhere, in every shape, by itself. Thought there neither helps nor refutes. For the deeply pierced, true reasoning rises on its own, fully formed — that is what "initiated by the Goddess" means. Others come by the book-route (the descent-chapters will spell it out). The teacher's business is expounding books; the books' business is growing the right thoughts in ungrudging, thorough study. That grown thought is true reasoning — and it is contemplation too: making the dim brilliant, showing the real as if never seen. No other yoga-limb is a straight means apart from this bright knowing — not sweat, not rules, not harmlessness and its vows, not breath-counting: those handle knowables, and what do knowables do for awareness? Breath-holds tune the senses. Sitting and its cousins, duly practised, deliver the sitter into the shape of whatever he sat on. But practice, in the supreme, in Śiva, in your own nature? Impossible. Practice means pinning breath, mind, body down — like drill with packs, lessons, dances. In awareness there's nothing to pick up and nothing to sweep out — so what would practice be? "Then what does reasoning do?" Just this, as said: it clears the squat of twoness. Nothing more.

## tsa04-p02 — the rites go inward
Even ordinary drill proves the point: you picture the body you want as awareness itself and un-picture the rest — that is all practice ever was. In the supreme there's nothing to throw away — said already. And the "dwelling in twoness" isn't some separate thing either; it's just nature not recognizing itself. So thought clears it — as stated. The ultimate works like this: nature shining, grasped by itself in freedom, sheds unknowing bit by bit — budding, blooming, bloomed — and shines out in that order. That shining is the Lord's own nature too. So no yoga-limbs as straight means here. Reasoning — graced reasoning — is the straight means: bright knowing itself. Refined many ways, it wears five shapes: sacrifice, fire-giving, inward saying, vow, and yoga. Sacrifice: to settle the thought that everything stands in the Lord and nothing stands apart — give everything to the Lord; and since the lovely-minded enter awareness on their own, lovely flowers and scents outside were prescribed to make giving easy. Fire-giving: to root the thought that all things are Lord-fire — relish swallowing everything into that fire until only fire-mass is left. Inward saying: to birth two-sided grasping — hold "that supreme is my own nature" inside, whatever the object. Vow: to win the conceit of the Lord who needs no means, always, everywhere — look at body and pot alike as his equals (Nandiśikhā: all-sameness is the supreme vow). So through these motley shards of bright knowing, when the thoughtless natural ultimate shines, staying in that shining is yoga. The Lord is full awareness; his fullness is power — clan, might, wave, heart, pith, throb, glory, triple-peak, Kālī, dragger, Fierce, Speech, enjoyment, sight, the ever-fresh — the books call him by whatever name fits, so he may dwell in meditators' hearts. Seen whole-powered, that fullness shines as conviction. His powers? Past counting — whatever the universe is, they are; who could teach them? Three contain it: the Supreme power, holding-seeing-shining everything from Śiva to earth as bare awareness; the half-and-half, mirror-and-elephant-wise, in difference-and-not; the lower, in mutually fenced-apart difference. And the embrace that swallows all three back into itself, recollecting inward — that is goddess Parā herself, also called Mother-of-Beings, Time's Dragger, and the rest.

## tsa04-p03 — twelve goddesses, and how to live
Four powers, each going three — twelve, for creating, keeping, unmaking. Watch awareness do it: take a thing in, show it out, warm to it, want it back in, breed the doubt that blocks return, eat the doubt, hold the thing-minus-doubt in yourself, note "my consumer-nature is this," let one trace linger as habit and another dissolve to bare awareness, raise the sense-wheel, raise its lords, raise the dreamt Māyā-knower, raise the knower who likes tightening into opening, raise the opened shape — twelve goddess-awarenesses, aimed at one knower or many, together or in turn, in twos and threes, wheeling like seasons, months, heaps, down to pots and cloths, feeding the wheel-lord's freedom: Kālīs, every one. "Grasping" means going, throwing, knowing, counting, enjoying, sounding, melting into self (old Bhūtirāja masters: "from throwing, from knowing — Kālī — by grasping-power"). The full sense lives spread through my longer works and hymns — look there. And the teachers' rule: don't shout the deepest anywhere, don't bury it either. So "sacrifice and the rest" means: in a Maheśvara like this. Everyone from Viṣṇu to Śiva treats the rejectable as the ground to take — false sight, drop it if you're beyond compare (the Experience-Hymn's whole fire is for this). In these fives from rite to yoga, each richly settled — live them as settled, not by edible-inedible, pure-impure checklists: those tire a self over thought-made rules abandoned by things themselves. Purity isn't in things the way blue is — the same act is foul elsewhere, enjoined here. "Giving counts as pure only for the initiated — elsewhere the same giving is foul, so all injunctions wobble"? No: Śiva's word alone overrides — reasoned out and settled by endless revelation from Sarvajñānottara on (full case later). So from Veda down through Pārameśvara, Siddhānta, Tantra, Kula, Ucchuṣma — every rule, order, and ban laid down there does exactly nothing here. Established.

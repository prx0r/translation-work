# Tantrasara-a05 — L2 READ

## v.1
When the concept can impress the self by itself, independent of other means — fallen from bonded activity, by the grace of pure knowledge, having attained the form of the Lord's power, relied on as the means, it brings forth śākta knowledge.
The Sanskrit: *tatra yadā vikalpaḥ svayam eva saṃskāram ātmani upāyāntaranirapekṣatayaiva kartuṃ prabhavati tadā asau pāśavavyāpārāt pracyutaḥ śuddhavidyānugraheṇa parameśaśaktirūpatām āpanna upāyatayā avalambyamānaḥ śāktaṃ jñānam āvirbhāvayati*

## v.2
This is settled in the very next ahnika.
The Sanskrit: *tad etac ca nirṇītam anantara eva āhnike*

## v.3
But when the concept depends on another means for its own impression — taking mind, breath, body, pots and the rest, limited in form, as means, reaching subtlety — it brings forth āṇava knowledge. Therein the mind is meditation-natured; the breath, gross and subtle — the first, articulation-natured; articulation, moreover, means the five functions beginning with breath; the subtle is denoted by letter-sound, to be said later; the body, arrangement-specific in nature, is denoted by instrument-sound; pots and the rest are external, to be praised as means by pit-pot-liṅga-worship and the rest.
The Sanskrit: *yadā tu upāyāntaram asau svasaṃskārārthaṃ vikalpo 'pekṣate tadā buddhiprāṇadehaghaṭādikān parimitarūpān upāyatvena gṛhṇan aṇutvaṃ prāpta āṇavaṃ jñānam āvirbhāvayati tatra buddhiḥ dhyānātmikā prāṇaḥ sthūlaḥ sūkṣmaś ca ādya uccāraṇātmā uccāraṇaṃ ca nāma pañca prāṇādyā vṛttayaḥ sūkṣmas tu varṇaśabdavācyo vakṣyate dehaḥ saṃniveśaviśeṣātmā karaṇaśabdavācyaḥ ghaṭādayo bāhyāḥ kumbhasthaṇḍilaliṅgapūjādyupāyatayā kīrtayiṣyamāṇāḥ*

## v.4
Here the fitting meditation is to be taught: that self-luminous supreme tattva, contained in all tattvas — having meditated it in the knowledge of one's own heart, let him meditate there the union of knower, instrument and known, the triad of fire, sun and moon, until he becomes the great Bhairava-fire, fanned by the wind of meditation. From that wheel, wrapped in the twelve old power-flames, having emerged through some pore-door, let him contemplate it resting outside in the graspable self; and by that one at rest, let him meditate how the external is first filled by moon-form in the order of creation, then lit by sun-form in preservation, then dissolved by the fire-form of dissolution, then made unsurpassable-selfhood.
The Sanskrit: *tatra dhyānaṃ tāvat iha ucitam upadekṣyāmaḥ yat etat svaprakāśaṃ sarvatattvāntarbhūtaṃ paraṃ tattvam uktaṃ tad eva nijahṛdayabodhe dhyātvā tatra pramātṛpramāṇaprameyarūpasya vahnyarkasomatritayasya saṃghaṭṭaṃ dhyāyet yāvat asau mahābhairavāgniḥ dhyānavātasamiddhākāraḥ sampadyate tasya prāktanaśaktijvālādvādaśakaparivṛtasya cakrātmanaḥ cakṣurādīnām anyatamasuṣiradvāreṇa niḥsṛtasya bāhye grāhyātmani viśrāntaṃ cintayet tena ca viśrāntena prathamaṃ tadbāhyaṃ somarūpatayā sṛṣṭikrameṇa prapūritaṃ tataḥ arkarūpatayā sthityā avabhāsitaṃ tato 'pi saṃhāravahnirūpatayā vilāpitaṃ tataḥ anuttarātmatām āpāditaṃ dhyāyet*

## v.5
Thus that wheel becomes complete with all divisions of external things.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ tac cakraṃ samastabāhyavastvabhedaparipūrṇaṃ sampadyate*

## v.6
Then with that wheel let him meditate the residual habit-states, made thus.
The Sanskrit: *tato vāsanāśeṣān api bhāvān tena cakreṇa itthaṃ kṛtān dhyāyet*

## v.7
For this unceasing meditator, who determines from his own consciousness the real series of creation, preservation and dissolution — consciousness-merely-real — and the supremacy of creation's autonomy, at once comes the Bhairava-state.
The Sanskrit: *evam asya anavarataṃ dhyāyinaḥ svasaṃvinmātraparamārthān sṛṣṭisthitisaṃhāraprabandhān sṛṣṭyādisvātantryaparamārthatvaṃ ca svasaṃvido niścinvataḥ sadya eva bhairavībhāvaḥ*

## v.8
By practice, all desired attainments and the rest as well.
The Sanskrit: *abhyāsāt tu sarvepsitasiddhyādayo 'pi*

## v.9
The self-luminous all-self-tattva, the triad beginning with measure.
The Sanskrit: *svaprakāśaṃ samastātmatattvaṃ mātrādikaṃ trayam*

## v.10
Having entered within, standing, let him meditate in the abode of heart-bliss.
The Sanskrit: *antaḥkṛtya sthitaṃ dhyāyed dhṛdayānandadhāmani*

## v.11
That twelve-great-power-ray-wheel-Lord, all-pervading.
The Sanskrit: *tad dvādaśamahāśaktiraśmicakreśvaraṃ vibhum*

## v.12
Emerging by ethers outside, let him meditate it bearing the states beginning with creation.
The Sanskrit: *vyomabhir niḥsarad bāhye dhyāyet sṛṣṭyādibhāvakam*

## v.13
That mandala swallowing all outer and inner being — in the self.
The Sanskrit: *tad grastasarvabāhyāntarbhāvamaṇḍalam ātmani*

## v.14
Resting, let the yogin contemplate — thus is the spread of the self.
The Sanskrit: *viśrāmyan bhāvayed yogī syād evam ātmanaḥ prathā*

## v.15
These are the summary-verses.
The Sanskrit: *iti saṃgrahaślokāḥ*

## v.16
Thus meditation.
The Sanskrit: *iti dhyānam*

## v.17
When about to articulate the breath: first he rests in the emptiness of the heart; then outside, from the arising of breath; then also toward the external, by the moon-filling of apāna, seeing all-selfness; then he becomes desiring no other; then from the arising of samāna he experiences union-rest; then from the arising of the udāna-fire he grasps the construction of measure and measured.
The Sanskrit: *tatra prāṇam uccicārayiṣuḥ pūrvaṃ hṛdaya eva śūnye viśrāmyati tato bāhye prāṇodayāt tato 'pi bāhyaṃ prati apānacandrāpūraṇena sarvātmatāṃ paśyati tataḥ anyanirākāṅkṣo bhavati tataḥ samānodayāt saṃghaṭṭaviśrāntim anubhavati tata udānavahnyudaye mātṛmeyādikalanāṃ grasate*

## v.18
In the pacification of its swallowing-fire, at the arising of vyāna, devoid of every limitation, it flashes.
The Sanskrit: *tadgrāsakavahnipraśame vyānodaye sarvāvacchedavandhyaḥ sphurati*

## v.19
Thus from emptiness up to vyāna: these rests — own-bliss, blisslessness, supreme-bliss, brahman-bliss, great-bliss, consciousness-bliss — six bliss-levels are taught; of which one is the connector, without arising or setting, inner-rest-truth-natured: world-bliss.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ śūnyāt prabhṛti vyānāntaṃ yā etā viśrāntayaḥ tā eva nijānando nirānandaḥ parānando brahmānando mahānandaḥ cidānanda iti ṣaṭ ānandabhūmaya upadiṣṭāḥ yāsām ekaḥ anusaṃdhātā udayāstamayavihīnaḥ antarviśrāntiparamārtharūpo jagadānandaḥ*

## v.20
Therefore in these articulation-levels, resting each separately two-wise or wholly, he makes the rest-tattva attainable, distinct from that body, breath and the rest.
The Sanskrit: *tat etāsu uccārabhūmiṣu pratyekaṃ dvyādiśaḥ sarvaśo vā viśrāmya anyat taddehaprāṇādivyatiriktaṃ viśrāntitattvam āsādayati*

## v.21
Keeping in mind that very secret of the articulation of the creation-dissolution seed, let him impress the concept; and in these rests, each separately, five states become, from the depth of entry.
The Sanskrit: *tad eva sṛṣṭisaṃhārabījoccāraṇarahasyam anusaṃdadhat vikalpaṃ saṃskuryāt āsu ca viśrāntiṣu pratyekaṃ pañca avasthā bhavanti praveśatāratamyāt*

## v.22
Therein first bliss, from contact with the portion of fullness; then arising, a moment, in the embodimentlessness of the habitual; then tremor, from self-power overpowering, loosening body-identity; then sleep, from the dissolution of outwardness.
The Sanskrit: *tatra prāg ānandaḥ pūrṇatāṃśasparśāt tata udbhavaḥ kṣaṇaṃ niḥśarīratāyāṃ rūḍheḥ tataḥ kampaḥ svabalākrāntau dehatādātmyaśaithilyāt tato nidrā bahirmukhatvavilayāt*

## v.23
Thus, the non-self-being dissolved into self-being: from the all-pervasiveness of the self, the non-self-being dissolves in the self — therefore whirling, from the arising of great pervasion.
The Sanskrit: *ittham anātmani ātmabhāve līne svātmanaḥ sarvamayatvāt ātmani anātmabhāvo vilīyate iti ato ghūrṇiḥ mahāvyāptyudayāt*

## v.24
These are the levels beginning with waking, up to the limit beyond the fourth.
The Sanskrit: *tā etā jāgradādibhūmayaḥ turyātītāntāḥ*

## v.25
And these levels occur in the entry of the triangle-root-heart-pace-upward-kuṇḍalinī-wheel.
The Sanskrit: *etāś ca bhūmayaḥ trikoṇakandahṛttālūrdhvakuṇḍalinīcakrapraveśe bhavanti*

## v.26
Thus in articulation-rest: what is supreme pulsation, the knowable dissolved into rest; what is dawning knowable; what is already-dawned knowable — that triad of marks we will say at the proper time.
The Sanskrit: *evam uccāraviśrāntau yat paraṃ spandanaṃ galitāśeṣavedyaṃ yac ca unmiṣad vedyaṃ yac ca unmiṣitavedyaṃ tad eva liṅgatrayam iti vakṣyāmaḥ svāvasare*

## v.27
And the supreme mark here is the yogini-heart.
The Sanskrit: *paraṃ cātra liṅgaṃ yoginīhṛdayam*

## v.28
Therein the primary pulsation-form, by contraction-and-expansion nature, arising as pair-form, from gaining the rest of the visarga-portion — enough.
The Sanskrit: *tatra mukhyā spandanarūpatā saṃkocavikāsātmatayā yāmalarūpatodayena visargakalāviśrāntilābhāt ity alam*

## v.29
Non-luminous: here entry.
The Sanskrit: *aprakāśaḥ atra anupraveśaḥ*

## v.30
First in self-knowing, then resting in the knowable, let him complete the knowable.
The Sanskrit: *pūrvaṃ svabodhe tadanu prameye viśramya meyaṃ paripūrayeta*

## v.31
In the complete, here he rests — the distinction of measurer and measured; quickly let him withdraw.
The Sanskrit: *pūrṇe 'tra viśrāmyati mātṛmeyavibhāgam āśv eva sa saṃhareta*

## v.32
Having pervaded, then he rests; those would be, together with emptiness, the six means-levels.
The Sanskrit: *vyāptyātha viśrāmyati tā imāḥ syuḥ śūnyena sākaṃ ṣaḍupāyabhūmyaḥ*

## v.33
Breath and the rest are not-after-vyāna; and dissolved in it, the manifestation beginning with waking.
The Sanskrit: *prāṇādayo vyānanapaścimās tallīnaś ca jāgrat prabhṛti prapañcaḥ*

## v.34
Established in practice here, in the abode of creation-dissolution contemplation, let him grow before long.
The Sanskrit: *abhyāsaniṣṭho 'tra tu sṛṣṭisaṃhṛdvimarśadhāmany acireṇa rohet*

## v.35
These are the inner-verses.
The Sanskrit: *iti āntaraślokāḥ*

## v.36
Thus articulation.
The Sanskrit: *iti uccāraṇam*

## v.37
In this articulation flashes a sound, a letter, almost an imitation of the unmanifest; its primary form is in the creation-dissolution seed — from its practice comes the gain of supreme-consciousness. For instance: in kā, at the end of mā, in sācke, in anacke, or articulated within, remembered — the touch of consciousness-pulsation, equally distinguished, complete because it disregards convention; even for those who regard convention, words producing its meaning like a mirage and the rest; from the touch of unsurpassable consciousness, let him unify the heart-throat-lips, the pair of twelve-ends and the heart — the letter-secret.
The Sanskrit: *asmin eva uccāre sphuran avyaktānukṛtiprāyo dhvaniḥ varṇaḥ tasya sṛṣṭisaṃhārabīje mukhyaṃ rūpaṃ tadabhyāsāt parasaṃvittilābhaḥ tathāhi kādau mānte sācke anacke vā antaruccārite smṛte vā samaviśiṣṭaḥ saṃvitspandasparśaḥ samayānapekṣitvāt paripūrṇaḥ samayopekṣiṇo 'pi śabdāḥ tadarthabhāvakā manorājyādivat anuttarasaṃvitsparśāt ekīkṛtahṛtkaṇṭhoṣṭho dvādaśāntadvayaṃ hṛdayaṃ ca ekīkuryāt iti varṇarahasyam*

## v.38
The white-yellow-and-rest inner letter, born immediately after the contemplation of inner touch, being manifested, causes consciousness to be experienced — so some say.
The Sanskrit: *antaḥsparśadvimarśānantarasamudbhūtaṃ sitapītādyāntaraṃ varṇam udbhāvyamānaṃ saṃvidam anubhāvayati iti kecit*

## v.39
By the absence of the expressible, from consciousness-pulsation, by the two checks of moon-and-sun motion.
The Sanskrit: *vācyaviraheṇa saṃvitspandād indvarkagatinirodhābhyām*

## v.40
Of which, by equal entry, complete in the form of the consciousness-seed-mass letter.
The Sanskrit: *yasya tu samasaṃpraveśāt pūrṇāṃ cidbījapiṇḍavarṇavidhau*

## v.41
This is the inner-verse.
The Sanskrit: *iti āntaraślokaḥ*

## v.42
Thus the letter-rule.
The Sanskrit: *iti varṇavidhiḥ*

## v.43
The operation of mudrā-revelation we will say.
The Sanskrit: *karaṇaṃ tu mudrāprakāśane vakṣyāmaḥ*

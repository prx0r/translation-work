# Tantrasara-a06 — L2 READ


## v.1
It itself is called by the word seat-fixing. There the seat is threefold — breath-wind, body, and the outer. There first the method in breath: all that course to be stated is reckoned as resting in breath; of it, counting in sequence and non-sequence alone is time. And it shines within the Supreme Lord alone; its shining is the God's power named Kālī; but with division, its appearing in sequence and non-sequence is breath-movement.
The Sanskrit: *sa eva sthānaprakalpanaśabdena uktaḥ tatra tridhā sthānaṃ prāṇavāyuḥ śarīraṃ bāhyaṃ ca tatra prāṇe tāvat vidhiḥ sarvaḥ asau vakṣyamāṇaḥ adhvā prāṇasthaḥ kalyate tasya kramākramakalanaiva kālaḥ sa ca parameśvara eva antarbhāti tadbhāsanaṃ ca devasya kālī nāma śaktiḥ bhedena tu tadābhāsanaṃ kramākramayoḥ prāṇavṛttiḥ*

## v.2
For consciousness itself takes a form apart from its objects; hence indeed, through being bounded, going to knowability: the void. Then through freedom, falling on the object with eagerness to seize, action-power-led, breathing-formed, living-natured — since with five forms it fills the body, then it shines as if conscious.
The Sanskrit: *saṃvid eva hi prameyebhyo vibhaktaṃ rūpaṃ gṛhṇāti ata eva ca avacchedayogāt vedyatāṃ yāntī nabhaḥ tataḥ svātantryāt meye svīkārautsukyena nipatantī kriyāśaktipradhānā prāṇanārūpā jīvasvabhāvā pañcabhī rūpaiḥ dehaṃ yataḥ pūrayati tato 'sau cetana iva bhāti*

## v.3
There in power, action is the time-course; in the eastern part, but in the northern, of varied form, is the place-course; there, of letter, mantra, and word courses, the standing in time-course, through beyond-subtle-gross-formedness.
The Sanskrit: *tatra kriyā śaktau kālādhvā prācyabhāge uttare tu mūrtivaicitryarūpo deśādhvā tatra varṇamantrapadādhvanaḥ kālādhvani sthitiḥ parasūkṣmasthūlarūpatvāt*

## v.4
But the standing of the place-course, as tattva-person-ray-natured — thus it will be in its own place.
The Sanskrit: *deśādhvasthitis tu tattvapurakalātmanā iti bhaviṣyati svāvasare*

## v.5
There, though even in the body, with outer and inner, breath is warp-and-woof-formed — yet of distinctly sensible effort, that, from the heart beginning — thus from that alone this is to be described.
The Sanskrit: *tatra yady api dehe sabāhyābhyantaram otaprotarūpaḥ prāṇaḥ tathāpi prasphuṭasaṃvedyaprayatnaḥ asau hṛdayāt prabhṛti iti tata eva ayaṃ nirūpaṇīyaḥ*

## v.6
There lord-power, self-power, and effort — thus a triad — are the cause in driving breath, through secondary-primary states.
The Sanskrit: *tatra prabhuśaktiḥ ātmaśaktiḥ yatna iti tritayaṃ prāṇeraṇe hetuḥ guṇamukhyabhāvāt*

## v.7
There, from the heart to the twelve-end's end, by one's own fingers, of all is the thirty-six-fingered breath-course, in going out and in coming in, since of each is fitted strength, effort, and body.
The Sanskrit: *tatra hṛdayāt dvādaśāntāntaṃ svāṅgulaiḥ sarvasya ṣaṭtriṃśadaṅgulaḥ prāṇacāraḥ nirgame praveśe ca svocitabalayatnadehatvāt sarvasya*

## v.8
There the water-clock, the lunar day, the month, the year, and as year-group-natured — thus the entire time is completed. There, in the finger and a fifth, the finger, caṣaka — thus by the rule is the ghaṭikā-rise; for a ghaṭikā is by sixty caṣakas; hence seventy-two-fingered it is.
The Sanskrit: *tatra ghaṭikā tithiḥ māso varṣaṃ ca varṣasamūhātmā iti samastaḥ kālaḥ parisamāpyate tatra sapañcāṃśe aṅgule caṣaka iti sthityā ghaṭikodayaḥ ghaṭikā hi ṣaṣṭyā caṣakaiḥ tasmāt dvāsaptatyaṅgulā bhavati*

## v.9
Now the lunar-day rise.
The Sanskrit: *atha tithyudayaḥ*

## v.10
Two fingers and a quarter is called tuṭi; in four of them a watch; half tuṭi, half tuṭi — there twilight. Thus in going out day, in coming in night: thus the lunar-day rise.
The Sanskrit: *sapādam aṅguladvayaṃ tuṭiḥ ucyate tāsu catasṛṣu praharaḥ tuṭyardhaṃ tuṭyardhaṃ tatra saṃdhyā evaṃ nirgame dinaṃ praveśe rātriḥ iti tithyudayaḥ*

## v.11
Now the month rise.
The Sanskrit: *atha māsodayaḥ*

## v.12
There day is the dark fortnight, night the bright. There the first half-tuṭi and the last half-tuṭi are untimed rest; but the middle fifteen alone are tuṭis, lunar days. There light is rest and — thus these alone are day and night.
The Sanskrit: *tatra dinaṃ kṛṣṇapakṣaḥ rātriḥ śuklaḥ tatra pūrvaṃ tuṭyardham antyaṃ ca tuṭyardhaṃ viśrāntiḥ akālakalitāḥ madhyās tu pañcadaśa tuṭaya eva tithayaḥ tatra prakāśo viśrāntiś ca iti ete eva dinaniśe*

## v.13
There the light of knowable-ness is day; of the knowable in the dissolver, dissolution is night. And they — light and rest, through quick-slow variety in endless difference; in sameness with that, equinox.
The Sanskrit: *tatra vedyamayatāprakāśo dinaṃ vedyasya vicārayitari layo rātriḥ te ca prakāśaviśrāntī cirāciravaicitryāt anantabhede tatsāmye tu viṣuvat*

## v.14
There in the dark fortnight, in the breath-sun, the in-breath-moon offers each single fattening digit, until on the fifteenth, in the tuṭi near the twelve-end, the moon whose digit-spread has thinned and separated dissolves into the breath-sun alone.
The Sanskrit: *tatra kṛṣṇapakṣe prāṇārke apānacandra āpyāyikām ekām ekāṃ kalām arpayati yāvat pañcadaśyāṃ tuṭau dvādaśāntasamīpe kṣīṇapṛthagbhūtakalāprasaraḥ candramāḥ prāṇārka eva līyate*

## v.15
Right after that, what half-tuṭi there is — that is the fortnight-joint.
The Sanskrit: *tadanantaraṃ yat tuṭyardhaṃ sa pakṣasaṃdhiḥ*

## v.16
And of it, of the half-tuṭi, the eastern half is new-moon, the second first-day.
The Sanskrit: *tasya ca tuṭyardhasya prācyam ardham āmāvasyaṃ dvitīyaṃ prātipadam*

## v.17
There on the first-day, in that part, when, from cough-effort-attention-made tithi-cutting, it enters — then there is seizure. And there the Māyā-knower-Rāhu, co-born with the object-moon — by nature unable to dissolve, merely capable of covering — drinks the lunar nectar gone to the sun.
The Sanskrit: *tatra prātipade tasmin bhāge sa āmāvasyo bhāgo yadā kāsaprayatnāvadhānādikṛtāt tithicchedāt viśati tadā tatra grahaṇam tatra ca vedyarūpasomasahabhūto māyāpramātṛrāhuḥ svabhāvatayā vilāpanāśaktaḥ kevalam ācchādanamātrasamarthaḥ sūryagataṃ cāndram amṛtaṃ pibati iti*

## v.18
Since making the knower-measure-known triad non-divided, that time is holy, other-world-fruit-giving.
The Sanskrit: *pramātṛpramāṇaprameyatritayāvibhāgakāritvāt sa puṇyaḥ kālaḥ pāralaukikaphalapradaḥ*

## v.19
Then, entering breath, the awareness-sun with each single digit fills the in-breath-moon, until the fifteenth tuṭi is full moon; right after, fortnight-joint and seizure — thus as before. But this is this-world-fruit-giving: thus the month rise.
The Sanskrit: *tataḥ praviśati prāṇe cidarka ekaikayā kalayā apānacandram āpūrayati yāvat pañcadaśī tuṭiḥ pūrṇimā tadanantaraṃ pakṣasaṃdhiḥ grahaṇaṃ ca iti prāgvat etat tu aihikaphalapradam iti māsodayaḥ*

## v.20
Now the year rise.
The Sanskrit: *atha varṣodayaḥ*

## v.21
There the dark fortnight alone is the northern course; in six and six fingers is the passage, from Capricorn up to Gemini's end.
The Sanskrit: *tatra kṛṣṇapakṣa eva uttarāyaṇaṃ ṣaṭsu ṣaṭsu aṅguleṣu saṃkrāntiḥ makarāt mithunāntām*

## v.22
There per finger five lunar days; even there day-and-night division. Thus in coming in the southern course — womb-ness, birth-wish, about-to-be-born-ness, will-be-born-ness, birth-beginning, being-born-ness, birth-and-six-changes — thus in order among Capricorn and the rest.
The Sanskrit: *tatra pratyaṅgulaṃ pañca tithayaḥ tatrāpi dinarātrivibhāgaḥ evaṃ praveśe dakṣiṇāyanaṃ garbhatvam udbhavecchā udbubhūṣutā udbhaviṣyatvam udbhavārambhaḥ udbhavattā janmādivikāraṣaṭkaṃ ca iti kramāt makarādiṣu iti*

## v.23
Just so worship here does fitting fruit.
The Sanskrit: *tathaiva upāsā atra phalaṃ samucitaṃ karoti*

## v.24
And here Dakṣa and the rest up to Grandfather, Rudras and powers — twelve lords: thus the year rise.
The Sanskrit: *atra ca dakṣādyāḥ pitāmahāntā rudrāḥ śaktayaś ca dvādaśādhipataya iti varṣodayaḥ*

## v.25
Per finger sixty lunar days, thus in order at passage a year — thus by this order, in coming and going, twelve-year rise. Per finger of lunar days three hundred in finger-and-a-fifth — a year where before caṣaka was said: thus by counting, at passage five years — thus by this series, in one breath-out-in time sixty-year rise. Here twenty-one thousand six hundred: thus of lunar days the number.
The Sanskrit: *pratyaṅgulaṃ ṣaṣṭiḥ tithaya iti krameṇa saṃkrāntau varṣam ity anena krameṇa praveśanirgamayoḥ dvādaśābdodayaḥ pratyaṅgulaṃ tithīnāṃ śatatrayaṃ sapañcāṃśe 'ṅgule varṣaṃ yatra prāk caṣakam uktam iti gaṇanayā saṃkrāntau pañca varṣāṇi iti anayā paripāṭyā ekasmin prāṇanirgamapraveśakāle ṣaṣṭyabdodayaḥ atra ekaviṃśatisahasrāṇi ṣaṭ śatāni iti tithīnāṃ saṃkhyā*

## v.26
That many alone in day and night is the breath-count: thus not beyond sixty-year rise is examined, from endlessness.
The Sanskrit: *tāvatī eva ahorātre prāṇasaṃkhyā iti na ṣaṣṭyabdodayāt adhikaṃ parīkṣyate ānantyāt*

## v.27
There the human year is the gods' lunar day; by this order twelve thousand divine years are four ages.
The Sanskrit: *tatra mānuṣaṃ varṣaṃ devānāṃ tithiḥ anena krameṇa divyāni dvādaśavarṣasahasrāṇi caturyugam*

## v.28
Four, three, two, one — thus from kṛta beginning, by so many hundreds, eight twilights.
The Sanskrit: *catvāri trīṇi dve ekam iti kṛtāt prabhṛti tāvadbhiḥ śataiḥ aṣṭau saṃdhyāḥ*

## v.29
By seventy-one four-ages a manu-period; by fourteen manu-periods Brahmā's day. At Brahmā-day's end, in the triple world burnt by time-fire, and elsewhere in the triple world smoke-lulled, all people swiftly, fire-driven, in people-world having become pralaya-kevalas, stand.
The Sanskrit: *caturyugānām ekasaptatyā manvantaram manvantaraiḥ caturdaśabhiḥ brāhmaṃ dinaṃ brahmadinānte kālāgnidagdhe lokatraye anyatra ca lokatraye dhūmaprasvāpite sarve janā vegavad agnipreritā janaloke pralayākalībhūya tiṣṭhanti*

## v.30
But awakened Kūṣmāṇḍa, Hāṭakeśa and the rest play in the great world.
The Sanskrit: *prabuddhās tu kūṣmāṇḍahāṭakeśādyā maholoke krīḍanti*

## v.31
Then at night's end Brahmā's emission.
The Sanskrit: *tato niśāsamāptau brāhmī sṛṣṭiḥ*

## v.32
By this measure a hundred years is Brahmā-lifespan.
The Sanskrit: *anena mānena varṣaśataṃ brahmāyuḥ*

## v.33
That is Viṣṇu's day, and that much the night; of him too a hundred is the lifespan.
The Sanskrit: *tat viṣṇoḥ dinaṃ tāvatī ca rātriḥ tasyāpi śatam āyuḥ*

## v.34
That day; above that, O Rudra-world-lord, Rudra's; that much night; as before a year. That hundred too — and the limit.
The Sanskrit: *tat dinaṃ tadūrdhve rudralokaprabho rudrasya tāvatī rātriḥ prāgvat varṣaṃ tacchatam api ca avadhiḥ*

## v.35
There at Rudra's end, going to Śiva-ness; Rudra's stated office-limit. Of world-egg bearers that day; of a hundred Rudras night that much; of them too a hundred is the lifespan.
The Sanskrit: *tatra rudrasya tadavasitau śivatvagatiḥ rudrasya uktādhikārāvadhiḥ brahmāṇḍadhārakāṇāṃ tat dinaṃ śatarudrāṇāṃ niśā tāvatī teṣām api ca śatam āyuḥ*

## v.36
When a hundred Rudras end — world-egg destruction.
The Sanskrit: *śatarudrakṣaye brahmāṇḍavināśaḥ*

## v.37
Thus from water-principle up to the unmanifest, this alone in order is the Rudras' lifespan.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ jalatattvāt avyaktāntam etad eva krameṇa rudrāṇām āyuḥ*

## v.38
The earlier's lifespan is the later's day: thus.
The Sanskrit: *pūrvasyāyur uttarasya dinam iti*

## v.39
And then Brahmā and Rudras are unobstructed officers standing in the unmanifest: thus.
The Sanskrit: *tataś ca brahmā rudrāś ca abādyadhikāriṇaḥ avyakte tiṣṭhanti iti*

## v.40
And then Śrīkaṇṭha-lord is the withdrawer.
The Sanskrit: *śrīkaṇṭhanāthaś ca tadā saṃhartā*

## v.41
This is intermediate dissolution; on its ending, emission.
The Sanskrit: *eṣo 'vāntarapralayaḥ tatkṣaye sṛṣṭiḥ*

## v.42
There other scriptures, stated, too are emitted.
The Sanskrit: *tatra śāstrāntaram uktā api sṛjyante*

## v.43
But Śrīkaṇṭha-lord's own lifespan — that is the sheath-dwellers' Rudras' day, that much the night; their lifespan — that is Gahaneśa-day, that much alone the night; and in her the entire alone dissolves in Māyā.
The Sanskrit: *yat tu śrīkaṇṭhanāthasya svam āyuḥ tat kañcukavāsināṃ rudrāṇāṃ dinaṃ tāvatī rajanī teṣāṃ yad āyuḥ tat gahaneśadinaṃ tāvatī eva kṣapā tasyāṃ ca samastam eva māyāyāṃ vilīyate*

## v.44
Again Gahaneśa emits.
The Sanskrit: *punaḥ gahaneśaḥ sṛjati*

## v.45
Thus which unmanifest-time — having multiplied it by ten half-highests one should tell Māyā-day, that much the night.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ yaḥ avyaktakālaḥ taṃ daśabhiḥ parārdhaiḥ guṇayitvā māyādinaṃ kathayet tāvatī rātriḥ*

## v.46
That alone is dissolution.
The Sanskrit: *sa eva pralayaḥ*

## v.47
Māyā-time multiplied by a hundred half-highests is a day in Īśvara-principle.
The Sanskrit: *māyākālaḥ parārdhaśatena guṇita aiśvaratattve dinam*

## v.48
Here breath emits the world, that much the night. Where breath-quiet — even in breath and in Brahman-cave-abode, in the quiet, which consciousness — even there is sequence.
The Sanskrit: *atra prāṇo jagat sṛjati tāvatī rātriḥ yatra prāṇapraśamaḥ prāṇe ca brahmabiladhāmni śānte 'pi yā saṃvit tatrāpy asti kramaḥ*

## v.49
In Īśvara time multiplied by a hundred half-highests, whatever the number — that is Sadāśiva day, that much the night; that alone is the great dissolution.
The Sanskrit: *aiśvare kāle parārdhaśataguṇite yā saṃkhyā tat sādāśivaṃ dinaṃ tāvatī niśā sa eva mahāpralayaḥ*

## v.50
Sadāśiva, when own time ends, having ascended drop-half-moon-blocker, dissolves in resonance; resonance in power-principle, that in the pervader, and she in the Unsupported.
The Sanskrit: *sadāśivaḥ svakālaparikṣaye bindvardhacandranirodhikā ākramya nāde līyate nādaḥ śaktitattve tat vyāpinyāṃ sā ca anāśrite*

## v.51
By power-time multiplied by ten-million half-highests — Unsupported-day.
The Sanskrit: *śaktikālena parārdhakoṭiguṇitena anāśritadinam*

## v.52
The Unsupported in the like-minded station — which, that likeness, sameness; that is Brahman.
The Sanskrit: *anāśritaḥ sāmanase pade yat tat sāmanasyaṃ sāmyaṃ tat brahma*

## v.53
From this likeness, from the partless, from time, by mere winking measure — the stated wheel-whirl rise of all time's spread and re-dissolution.
The Sanskrit: *asmāt sāmanasyāt akalyāt kālāt nimeṣonmeṣamātratayā proktāśeṣakālaprasarapravilayacakrabhramodayaḥ*

## v.54
One, ten, hundred, thousand, ten thousand, hundred thousand, million, ten million, hundred million, billion, ten billion, hundred billion, thousand billion, ten-thousand billion, ocean, end, middle, half-highest — thus in order ten-multiplied, eighteen: thus the counting-rule.
The Sanskrit: *ekaṃ daśa śataṃ sahasram ayutaṃ lakṣaṃ niyutaṃ koṭiḥ arbudaṃ vṛndaṃ kharvaṃ nikharvaṃ padmaṃ śaṅkuḥ samudram antyaṃ madhyam parārdham iti krameṇa daśaguṇitāni aṣṭādaśa iti gaṇitavidhiḥ*

## v.55
Thus countless emissions-dissolutions in one great-emission-formed breath — it too in consciousness, she in the adjunct, it in bare awareness, of bare awareness alone this pulse which is called this time-rise.
The Sanskrit: *evam asaṃkhyāḥ sṛṣṭipralayāḥ ekasmin mahāsṛṣṭirūpe prāṇe so 'pi saṃvidi sā upādhau sa cinmātre cinmātrasyaiva ayaṃ spando yad ayaṃ kālodayo nāma*

## v.56
From that alone, in dream-fancy and the rest, variety of this brings no contradiction.
The Sanskrit: *tata eva svapnasaṃkalpādau vaicitryam asya na virodhāvaham*

## v.57
Thus as in out-breath is time-rise, so too in in-breath, from the heart to the root-seat's end.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ yathā prāṇe kālodayaḥ tathā apāne 'pi hṛdayāt mūlapīṭhaparyantam*

## v.58
And as in heart-throat-palate-brow-cleft-twelve-ends the six causes called Brahmā-Viṣṇu-Rudra-Īśa-Sadāśiva-Unsupported, just so too in in-breath, in heart-bulb-bliss-contraction-expansion-twelve-ends — these childhood-youth-age-rebirth-release lords.
The Sanskrit: *yathā ca hṛtkaṇṭhatālulalāṭarandhradvādaśānteṣu brahmaviṣṇurudreśasadāśivānāśritākhyaṃ kāraṇaṣaṭkam tathaiva apāne 'pi hṛtkandānandasaṃkocavikāsadvādaśānteṣu bālyayauvanavārddhakanidhanapunarbhavamuktyadhipataya ete*

## v.59
Now in equal-breath is time-rise.
The Sanskrit: *atha samāne kālodayaḥ*

## v.60
The equal-breath, moving in the ten heart-channels, conveys juices and the rest evenly through the whole body. There moving in the eight directions, it imitates as it were the regents' doings of the knower.
The Sanskrit: *samāno hārdīṣu daśāsu nāḍīṣu saṃcaran samaste dehe sāmyena rasādīn vāhayati tatra digaṣṭake saṃcaran taddikpaticeṣṭām iva pramātuḥ anukārayati*

## v.61
But moving up and down, in three channels it does going-coming.
The Sanskrit: *ūrdhvādhas tu saṃcaran tisṛṣu nāḍīṣu gatāgataṃ karoti*

## v.62
There on equinox-day, outside, at dawn-time, a ghaṭikā and a quarter it carries in the middle path.
The Sanskrit: *tatra viṣuvaddine bāhye prabhātakāle sapādāṃ ghaṭikāṃ madhyamārge vahati*

## v.63
Then nine hundred breath-castings — thus by counting, outside two and a half ghaṭikās — left, right, left, right, left — thus five passages.
The Sanskrit: *tato navaśatāni prāṇavikṣepāṇām iti gaṇanayā bahiḥ sārdhaghaṭikādvayaṃ vāme dakṣiṇe vāme dakṣiṇe vāme iti pañca saṃkrāntayaḥ*

## v.64
Then when the five-passage has elapsed, in less-a-quarter fourteen passed ghaṭikās, at southern autumnal equinox-midday, nine breath-hundreds.
The Sanskrit: *tataḥ saṃkrāntipañcake vṛtte pādonāsu caturdaśasu ghaṭikāsu atikrāntāsu dakṣiṇaṃ śāradaṃ viṣuvanmadhyāhne nava prāṇaśatāni*

## v.65
Then too right, left, right, left, right — thus a five-passage, nine hundred each — thus even at night — thus.
The Sanskrit: *tato 'pi dakṣiṇe vāme dakṣiṇe vāme dakṣiṇe iti saṃkrāntipañcakaṃ pratyekaṃ navaśatāni ity evaṃ rātrāv api iti*

## v.66
Thus on equinox-day and its night, twelve and twelve passages.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ viṣuvaddivase tadrātrau ca dvādaśa dvādaśa saṃkrāntayaḥ*

## v.67
Then in day wax-wane, passage wax-wane.
The Sanskrit: *tato dinavṛddhikṣayeṣu saṃkrāntivṛddhikṣayaḥ*

## v.68
Thus in one equal-breath-wind two years, from absence of in-out-breath union.
The Sanskrit: *evam ekasmin samānamaruti varṣadvayaṃ śvāsapraśvāsayogābhāvāt*

## v.69
Even here twelve-year-rise and the rest as before.
The Sanskrit: *atrāpi dvādaśābdodayādi pūrvavat*

## v.70
But in up-breath the course is twelve-end-limited, of mere-pulse-natured time.
The Sanskrit: *udāne tu dvādaśāntāvadhiś cāraḥ spandamātrātmanaḥ kālasya*

## v.71
Even here as before the rule.
The Sanskrit: *atrāpi pūrvavat vidhiḥ*

## v.72
But in pervading-breath, from pervasiveness, even in non-sequence, by subtle-surging-ness is time-rise.
The Sanskrit: *vyāne tu vyāpakatvāt akrame 'pi sūkṣmocchalattāyogena kālodayaḥ*

## v.73
Now letter rise.
The Sanskrit: *atha varṇodayaḥ*

## v.74
There in half-watch, in half-watch, class-rise is even at equinox; of the letter, of the letter, two hundred sixteen breaths; outside thirty-six caṣakas: thus this rise, effortless-born letter-rise.
The Sanskrit: *tatra ardhaprahare ardhaprahare vargodayo viṣuvati samaḥ varṇasya varṇasya dve śate ṣoḍaśādhike prāṇānām bahiḥ ṣaṭtriṃśat caṣakāṇi iti udayaḥ ayam ayatnajo varṇodayaḥ*

## v.75
But effort-born is mantra-rise: like a water-wheel-device drawing, by single-attention force — wondrous mantra-rise day and night keeping joined — with the mantra-deity gains identity.
The Sanskrit: *yatnajas tu mantrodayaḥ araghaṭṭaghaṭīyantravāhanavat ekānusaṃdhibalāt citraṃ mantrodayaṃ divāniśam anusaṃdadhat mantradevatayā saha tādātmyam eti*

## v.76
There in the ever-arisen, by breath-course count alone is rise-count explained; when that doubled, its half, and the rest in order; in the hundred-and-eight wheel, two hundred the rise: thus in order. In gross-subtle course-nature of the rested one, when breath-course has thinned, when time is devoured come about, complete, one, just this awareness, wonder-power-filled, shines.
The Sanskrit: *tatra sadodite prāṇacārasaṃkhyayaiva udayasaṃkhyā vyākhyātā taddviguṇite tadardham ityādi krameṇa aṣṭottaraśate cakre dviśata udayaḥ iti krameṇa sthūlasūkṣme cārasvarūpe viśrāntasya prāṇacāre kṣīṇe kālagrāse vṛtte sampūrṇām ekam evedaṃ saṃvedanaṃ citraśaktinirbharaṃ bhāsate*

## v.77
Time-difference alone divides awareness, not object-difference — like peak-abiding knowledge. Of knowing, as much as the abiding-time, that alone is the moment. And in one breath-rise, one knowing alone necessarily — and this: otherwise there would be no single thought-knowing at all, since word-sequenced-stained, since even the measure is sequential.
The Sanskrit: *kālabheda eva saṃvedanabhedakaḥ na vedyabhedaḥ śikharasthajñānavat jñānasya yāvān avasthitikālaḥ sa eva kṣaṇaḥ prāṇodaye ca ekasmin ekam eva jñānam avaśyaṃ caitat anyathā vikalpajñānam ekaṃ na kiṃcit syāt kramikaśabdārūṣitatvāt mātrāyā api kramikatvāt*

## v.78
Which has said: the raised, half-short — thus.
The Sanskrit: *yad āha tasyādita udāttam ardhahrasvam iti*

## v.79
Therefore until between pulses has not arisen, so long one knowing alone.
The Sanskrit: *tasmāt spandāntaraṃ yāvat na uditaṃ tāvat ekam eva jñānam*

## v.80
Hence very eighty-one, when remembering the word, through entering various natures — that one alone, having the Lord as object, that thought, when time is devoured, results as thought-free-natured alone — thus.
The Sanskrit: *ata eva ekāśīti padasmaraṇasamaye vividhadharmānupraveśamukhena eka eva asau parameśvaraviṣayo vikalpaḥ kālagrāse na avikalpātmā eva sampadyate iti*

## v.81
Thus seeing the entire time-course in breath-rise alone, and the various countless emissions-dissolutions, noting right there, by self alone recognizing lordship — one is freed alone — thus.
The Sanskrit: *evam akhilaṃ kālādhvānaṃ prāṇodaya eva paśyan sṛṣṭisaṃhārāṃś ca vicitrān niḥsaṃkhyān tatraiva ākalayan ātmana eva pārameśvaryaṃ pratyabhijānan mukta eva bhavati iti*

## v.82
Seeing the awareness-formed self's breath-power, the form going there and beyond time together, with emission-stay-dissolution wheels, ever-ready attains Bhairavī-being.
The Sanskrit: *saṃvidrūpasyātmanaḥ prāṇaśaktiṃ paśyan rūpaṃ tatragaṃ cātikālam sākaṃ sṛṣṭisthemasaṃhāracakrair nityodyukto bhairavībhāvam eti*

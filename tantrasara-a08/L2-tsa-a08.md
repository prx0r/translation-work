# Tantrasara-a08 — L2 READ


## v.1
Which this world-born glory-natured, said to womb endless varied enjoyer-enjoyed — there whatever pervades as great-light-formed, that is like the great common, Supreme-Śiva-formed.
The Sanskrit: *yad idaṃ vibhavātmakaṃ bhuvanajātam uktaṃ garbhīkṛtānantavicitrabhoktṛbhogyaṃ tatra yad anugataṃ mahāprakāśarūpaṃ tat mahāsāmānyakalpaṃ paramaśivarūpam*

## v.2
But whatever form pervading only some differences, that is a category — as earth called of glow-hardness-bulk-and-rest form, pervading all world-eggs ruled by world-lords from Kālāgni to Vīrabhadra.
The Sanskrit: *yat tu katipayakatipayabhedānugataṃ rūpaṃ tat tattvaṃ yathā pṛthivī nāma dyutikāṭhinyasthaulyādirūpā kālāgniprabhṛtivīrabhadrāntabhuvaneśādhiṣṭhitasamastabrahmāṇḍānugatā*

## v.3
There of these categories cause-effect-ness is shown; and it is twofold.
The Sanskrit: *tatra eṣāṃ tattvānāṃ kāryakāraṇabhāvo darśyate sa ca dvividhaḥ*

## v.4
Ultimate, and of emission —
The Sanskrit: *pāramārthikaḥ sṛṣṭeś ca*

## v.5
There the ultimate cause-effect-ness is this much: namely, of the agent-natured, free Lord, with such from-Śiva-to-earth-ended body, non-different in nature and resting in own nature — the showing-forth.
The Sanskrit: *tatra pāramārthika etāvān kāryakāraṇabhāvo yad uta kartṛsvabhāvasya svatantrasya bhagavata evaṃvidhena śivādidharāntena vapuṣā svarūpabhinnena svarūpaviśrāntena ca prathanam*

## v.6
But the constructed cause-effect-ness, made by the Lord's will with necessity-breath: and as long as, when fixed before-after appearing, being even in the more, following own nature, only so far — by that from yogin's wish too, sprout from seed too, in dream and the rest, from pots and the rest, even thus.
The Sanskrit: *kalpitas tu kāryakāraṇabhāvaḥ parameśecchayā niyatiprāṇayā nirmitaḥ sa ca yāvati yadā niyatapaurvāparyāvabhāsanaṃ saty api adhike svarūpānugatam etāvaty eva tena yogīcchāto 'pi aṅkuro bījād api svapnādau ghaṭāder apīti*

## v.7
And there too, of the Supreme Lord non-loss of agency — thus even unconstructed, that ultimate stands firm.
The Sanskrit: *tatrāpi ca parameśvarasya kartṛtvānapāya iti akalpito 'pi asau pāramārthikaḥ sthita eva*

## v.8
For with the ultimate wall-like standing form, all this drawn is fitting, not otherwise. Hence by the assemblage alone is caused-ness fitting.
The Sanskrit: *pāramārthike hi bhittisthānīye sthite rūpe sarvam idam ullikhyamānaṃ ghaṭate na anyathā ata eva sāmagryā eva kāruṇatvaṃ yuktam*

## v.9
For it, made of the weave of all beings, by free-awareness greatness, thus of fixed own place-time-thing-mass nature — each thing-nature completed, being thus; of being-thus, being-otherwise — as it increases, so the effect's other-kind-ness by degrees nourishes.
The Sanskrit: *sā hi samastabhāvasaṃdarbhamayī svatantrasaṃvedanamahimnā tathā niyatanijanijadeśakālabhāvarāśisvabhāvā pratyekaṃ vastusvarūpaniṣpattisamaye tathābhūtā tathābhūtāyā hi anyathābhāvo yathā yathā adhikībhavati tathā tathā kāryasyāpi vijātīyatvaṃ tāratamyena puṣyati*

## v.10
Thus the free-awareness-natured Supreme Lord alone, world-thing-bodied, is the maker of pots and the rest — since potter-awareness, from not being greater, and the potter's body placed amid the thing-mass — how is the potter-body's maker-pride? Thus — if Lord-made even that will be, like pots and the rest.
The Sanskrit: *ity evaṃ saṃvedanasvātantryasvabhāvaḥ parameśvara eva viśvabhāvaśarīro ghaṭāder nirmātā kumbhakārasaṃvidas tato 'nadhikatvāt kumbhakāraśarīrasya ca bhāvarāśimadhye nikṣepāt kathaṃ kumbhakāraśarīrasya kartṛtvābhimānaḥ iti cet parameśvarakṛta evāsau ghaṭādivat bhaviṣyati*

## v.11
Hence the assemblage-doctrine too strengthens makership of awareness alone, the world-bodied.
The Sanskrit: *tasmāt sāmagrīvādo 'pi viśvaśarīrasya saṃvedanasyaiva kartṛtāyām upodbalakaḥ*

## v.12
For a pot such as would be there on Meru would not be.
The Sanskrit: *merau hi tatrasthe na bhavet tathāvidho ghaṭaḥ*

## v.13
Thus in the constructed caused-ness, the many-typed-ness toward categories' cause-effect-ness in scriptures — that too fits — from dung, worms, from yogin's wish, from mantra, from herbs: like scorpion-arising.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ kalpite 'smin kāryatve śāstreṣu tattvānāṃ kāryakāraṇabhāvaṃ prati yat bahuprakāratvaṃ tad api saṃgataṃ gomayāt kīṭāt yogīcchāto mantrād auṣadhāt vṛścikodayavat*

## v.14
There by own-system view we show the constructed.
The Sanskrit: *tatra nijatantradṛśā taṃ kalpitaṃ darśayāmaḥ*

## v.15
There the Supreme Lord, full with five powers — thus said; he by freedom manifesting power after power chiefly stands fivefold.
The Sanskrit: *tatra parameśvaraḥ pañcabhiḥ śaktibhiḥ nirbhara ity uktam sa svātantryāt śaktiṃ tāṃ tāṃ mukhyatayā prakaṭayan pañcadhā tiṣṭhati*

## v.16
Where awareness leads: Śiva-principle; where bliss leads: power-principle; where will leads: Sadāśiva-principle — for will, of knowing and doing, is of even-acceptance nature; where knowledge-power leads: Īśvara-principle; where action-power leads: knowledge-principle — thus.
The Sanskrit: *citprādhānye śivatattvam ānandaprādhānye śaktitattvam icchāprādhānye sadāśivatattvam icchāyā hi jñānakriyayoḥ sāmyarūpābhyupagamātmakatvāt jñānaśaktiprādhānye īśvaratattvam kriyāśaktiprādhānye vidyātattvam iti*

## v.17
And here the category-lords: Śiva, Śakti, Sadāśiva, Īśvara, Ananta — like Brahmā in withdrawal; of these common-formed, the particulars with pervasion for object are five — namely Śāmbhavas, Śāktas, Mantramāheśvaras, Mantreśvaras, Mantras — thus the pure path.
The Sanskrit: *atra ca tattveśvarāḥ śivaśaktisadāśiveśvarānantāḥ brahmeva nivṛttau eṣāṃ sāmānyarūpāṇāṃ viśeṣā anugativiṣayāḥ pañca tadyathā śāmbhavāḥ śāktāḥ mantramaheśvarāḥ mantreśvarāḥ mantrā iti śuddhādhvā*

## v.18
So far directly Śiva is maker; but the impure path Aghoreśa, also called Ananta, emits — by the Lord's will-sway, for the enjoyment-perfection of atoms wavered by craving.
The Sanskrit: *iyati sākṣāt śivaḥ kartā aśuddhaṃ punar adhvānam anantāparanāmāghoreśaḥ sṛjati īśvarecchāvaśena prakṣubdhabhogalolikānām aṇūnāṃ bhogasiddhyartham*

## v.19
There the waverer, not-full, other-natured form, throbbing, actionless, mere longing — will be future fitness for bounding, thus: not impurity of man, nor another category.
The Sanskrit: *tatra loliko 'pūrṇam anyatārūpaḥ parispandaḥ akarmakam abhilāṣamātram eva bhaviṣyad avacchedayogyateti na malaḥ puṃsas tattvāntaram*

## v.20
But passion-principle is longing bounded by action.
The Sanskrit: *rāgatattvaṃ tu karmāvacchinno 'bhilāṣaḥ*

## v.21
But action there is mere action; mind-nature is passion, varied by action-difference — thus the distinction will be told.
The Sanskrit: *karma tu tatra karmamātraṃ buddhidharmas tu rāgaḥ karmabhedacitra iti vibhāgo vakṣyate*

## v.22
It is this impurity from the Supreme Lord's self-veiling wish — nothing else at all a thing; and that too by that Lord-wish-nature alone is the thingness of earth and the rest.
The Sanskrit: *so 'yaṃ malaḥ parameśvarasya svātmapracchādanecchātaḥ nānyat kiṃcit vastv api ca tatparameśvarecchātmanaiva dharāder api vastutvāt*

## v.23
And it, impurity, existing in the knowledge-kevalin, facing destruction — thus does not fatten its own effect, action.
The Sanskrit: *sa ca malo vijñānakevale vidyamāno dhvaṃsonmukha iti na svakāryaṃ karma āpyāyati*

## v.24
But of the dissolution-kevalin, merely yawning it has been — thus impurity-strengthened action is the cause in transmigration-variety enjoyment — thus for the enjoyment-perfection of atoms pierced by enjoyment-latency of that, glorious Aghoreśa emits — thus it is said fittingly: and the stirring of impurity is by Lord-wish-force alone, for of the inert by itself, anywhere at all, is inability.
The Sanskrit: *pralayakevalasya tu jṛmbhamāṇa eva āsta iti malopodbalitaṃ karma saṃsāravaicitryabhoge nimittam iti tadbhogavāsanānuviddhānām aṇūnāṃ bhogasiddhaye śrīmān aghoreśaḥ sṛjati iti yuktam uktaṃ malasya ca prakṣobha īśvarecchābalād eva jaḍasya svataḥ kutracid api asāmarthyāt*

## v.25
An atom, indeed, is called mere shining of conscious-unconscious-form; of it conscious-form is lordliness alone, unconscious-formedness alone is impurity. And of it emitting, by Lord-wish-made, and thence always of the ever-to-be-emitted-thing-abiding form's to-be-shown-as-inert-ness, inert, all-effect-pervading-formed and pervasive is Māyā-called principle, the material cause; and showing it forth is the Supreme Lord's power named Māyā, quite other than that.
The Sanskrit: *aṇur nāma kila cidacidrūpāvabhāsa eva tasya cidrūpam aiśvaryam eva acidrūpataiva malaḥ tasya ca sṛjataḥ parameśvarecchāmayaṃ tata eva ca nityaṃ srakṣyamāṇavastugatasya rūpasya jaḍatayābhāsayiṣyamāṇatvāt jaḍaṃ sakalakāryavyāpanādirūpatvāc ca vyāpakaṃ māyākhyaṃ tattvam upādānakāraṇaṃ tadavabhāsakāriṇī ca parameśvarasya māyā nāma śaktis tato 'nyaiva*

## v.26
Thus of kalā-and-rest principles to earth-ended too two-formedness is to be described.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ kalāditattvānāṃ dharāntānām api dvairūpyaṃ nirūpyam*

## v.27
And here proof too have said Abhinavagupta's gurus in two-formedness.
The Sanskrit: *atra ca dvairūpye pramāṇam api āhur abhinavaguptaguravaḥ*

## v.28
Which in resolve shines, that separated too exists outside, with clear form — like a pot.
The Sanskrit: *yat saṃkalpe bhāti tat pṛthagbhūtaṃ bahir api asti sphuṭena vapuṣā ghaṭa iva*

## v.29
And thus of Māyā-kalā-and-sky-flowers and the rest too this very course — thus the purely affirmative reason.
The Sanskrit: *tathā ca māyākalādikhapuṣpāder api eṣaiva vartanī iti kevalānvayī hetuḥ*

## v.30
And by this, those who enjoy knowing of direct-realization-form with Māyā-kalā-nature-mind and the rest as object — they too are perfected, perfected alone.
The Sanskrit: *anena ca māyākalāprakṛtibuddhyādiviṣayaṃ sākṣātkārarūpaṃ jñānaṃ ye bhajante te 'pi siddhāḥ siddhā eva*

## v.31
Thus standing, from Māyā-principle is world-birth.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ sthite māyātattvāt viśvaprasavaḥ*

## v.32
And it though indeed non-sequential alone, yet by the stated view sequence appears — thus.
The Sanskrit: *sa ca yady api akramam eva tathāpi uktadṛśā kramo 'vabhāsate iti*

## v.33
And that too is told: there per self the kalā-and-rest group is divided.
The Sanskrit: *so 'pi ucyate tatra pratyātma kalādivargo bhinnaḥ*

## v.34
From its effect's makership-strengthening and the rest, by self-difference found — but that group sometimes might become one too, by the Lord's wish, like social selves — there all this kalā-and-rest group is pure, which by Lord-objectedness, fit for gaining its nature, doing own work, from world-opposition.
The Sanskrit: *tatkāryasya kartṛtvopodbalanādeḥ pratyātmabhedena upalambhāt sa tu vargaḥ kadācit ekībhavet api īśvarecchayā sāmājikātmanām iva tatra sarvo 'yaṃ kalādivargaḥ śuddhaḥ yaḥ parameśvaraviṣayatayā tatsvarūpalābhānuguṇanijakāryakārī saṃsārapratidvaṃdvitvāt*

## v.35
And it becomes thus by the Supreme Lord's power-descent sway — thus we shall tell in its showing.
The Sanskrit: *sa ca parameśvaraśaktipātavaśāt tathā bhavati iti vakṣyāmas tatprakāśane*

## v.36
But the impure is opposite of that.
The Sanskrit: *aśuddhas tu tadviparītaḥ*

## v.37
There from Māyā kalā is born, which yokes the sleeping-placed atom with slight makership; and she, as if swelling, from the union of the two, Māyā and atom, though arisen deforms Māyā, not the undeformable atom — thus Māyā-effect-ness of her.
The Sanskrit: *tatra māyātaḥ kalā jātā yā suptasthānīyam aṇuṃ kiṃcitkartṛtvena yunakti sā ca ucchūnateva saṃsārabījasya māyāṇvor ubhayoḥ saṃyogāt utpannāpi māyāṃ vikaroti na avikāryam aṇum iti māyākāryatvam asyāḥ*

## v.38
Thus from mutual clinging, indistinguishable inwardness of man-and-kalā.
The Sanskrit: *evam anyonyaśleṣāt alakṣaṇīyāntaratvaṃ puṃskalayoḥ*

## v.39
Māyā-womb officers, but by some lord's grace, when all action ends, Māyā-man discrimination comes about — by which above Māyā sits the knowledge-kevalin; never ever below Māyā is kalā-man discrimination, by which one stands above kalā.
The Sanskrit: *māyāgarbhādhikāriṇas tu kasyacid īśvarasya prasādāt sarvakarmakṣaye māyāpuruṣaviveko bhavati yena māyordhve vijñānākala āste na jātucit māyādhaḥ kalāpuṃviveko vā yena kalordhve tiṣṭhati*

## v.40
Or nature-man discrimination, by which below the chief one would not transmigrate.
The Sanskrit: *prakṛtipuruṣaviveko vā yena pradhānādho na saṃsaret*

## v.41
But in impurity-man discrimination: Śiva-likeness.
The Sanskrit: *malapuruṣaviveke tu śivasamānatvam*

## v.42
But when man's fullness is seen, Śiva-ness alone.
The Sanskrit: *puruṣapūrṇatādṛṣṭau tu śivatvam eveti*

## v.43
Thus kalā-principle alone is slight-makership-giving, not —
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ kalātattvam eva kiṃcitkartṛtvadāyi na ca*

## v.44
makership of the unknowing — thus.
The Sanskrit: *kartṛtvam ajñasya iti*

## v.45
Slight-knowing-giving impure knowledge is born from kalā; and knowledge sees mind, and grasps its-lying parts and pleasures and the rest with discrimination.
The Sanskrit: *kiṃcijjñatvadāyiny aśuddhavidyā kalāto jātā sā ca vidyā buddhiṃ paśyati tadgatāṃś ca sukhādīn vivekena gṛhṇāti*

## v.46
Of mind, of quality-confused form, with discrimination to grasp, from inability.
The Sanskrit: *buddher guṇasaṃkīrṇākārāyā vivekena grahītum asāmarthyāt*

## v.47
Hence the mind-mirrored being is discerned by knowledge.
The Sanskrit: *tasmāt buddhipratibimbito bhāvo vidyayā vivicyate*

## v.48
Slight-makership, to perfect some part, somewhere alone makership — in this sense it comes down; but somewhere too in this part is passion-principle's business.
The Sanskrit: *kiṃcitkartṛtvaṃ kiṃcidbhāgasiddhaye kvacid eva kartṛtvam ity atra arthe paryavasyati kvacid eva ca ity atra bhāge rāgatattvasya vyāpāraḥ*

## v.49
Not from non-dispassion-made is that — even of non-dispassion, seeing no-attachment.
The Sanskrit: *na ca avairāgyakṛtaṃ tat avairāgyasyāpi araktidarśanāt*

## v.50
In dispassion, even in duty and the rest, attachment is seen.
The Sanskrit: *vairāgye dharmādāv api raktir dṛśyate*

## v.51
Of the sated in food and the rest, though non-dispassion absent, from unremoved inner passion.
The Sanskrit: *tṛptasya ca annādau avairāgyābhāve 'pi antaḥstharāgānapāyāt*

## v.52
By that, without again: from non-dispassion-non-arising contingency.
The Sanskrit: *tena vinā punar avairāgyānutpattiprasaṅgāt*

## v.53
And time, effect reckoning, reckons makership too bounded by that — in the like, in some-where-ness, this alone makership — thus in this sense is necessity's business.
The Sanskrit: *kālaś ca kāryaṃ kalayaṃs tadavacchinnaṃ kartṛtvam api kalayati tulye kvacittve asminn eva kartṛtvam ity atrārthe niyater vyāpāraḥ*

## v.54
In cause-effect-ness too hers alone is the business; by that from kalā alone this fourfold is born — this very, slightly now knowing, clinging, I-do — thus-formed awareness, gone to body-city-eight and the rest, is called beast.
The Sanskrit: *kāryakāraṇabhāve 'pi asyā eva vyāpāraḥ tena kalāta eva etac catuṣkaṃ jātam idam eva kiṃcid adhunā jānan abhiṣvaktaḥ karomi ity evaṃrūpā saṃvid dehapuryaṣṭakādigatā paśur ity ucyate*

## v.55
That this Māyā-and-rest six is called six sheaths.
The Sanskrit: *tad idaṃ māyādiṣaṭkaṃ kañcukaṣaṭkam ucyate*

## v.56
Of awareness by Māyā, from being set aside, of kalā and the rest falling above, like sheaths from standing.
The Sanskrit: *saṃvido māyayā apahastitatvena kalādīnām uparipātināṃ kañcukavat avasthānāt*

## v.57
Thus slight-makership which is Māyā-effect; there the slight, but, undistinguished which makership to be qualified; there engaged kalā is the cause of knowledge-and-rest birth — thus described.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ kiṃcitkartṛtvaṃ yat māyākāryaṃ tatra kiṃcit tv aviśiṣṭaṃ yat kartṛtvaṃ viśeṣyaṃ tatra vyāpriyamāṇā kalā vidyādiprasavahetuḥ iti nirūpitam*

## v.58
Now the qualifier part which is called slight is to be known, and the effect — and as long as she, kalā, makes apart from own self, so long this very, of enjoyment-particular interwoven with pleasure-pain-delusion-nature, of mere common, of that-guṇa-sameness-alias, of nature-principle is the emission — thus of enjoyer-enjoyed pair together dependent on kalā-principle is emission.
The Sanskrit: *idānīṃ viśeṣaṇabhāgo yaḥ kiṃcid ity ukto jñeyaḥ kāryaś ca taṃ yāvat sā kalā svātmanaḥ pṛthak kurute tāvat eṣa eva sukhaduḥkhamohātmakabhogyaviśeṣānusyūtasya sāmānyamātrasya tadguṇasāmyāparanāmnaḥ prakṛtitattvasya sargaḥ iti bhoktṛbhogyayugalasya samam eva kalātattvāyattā sṛṣṭiḥ*

## v.59
Here, and of these by the real path, emission is sequence-barren — thus said; sequence-appearing too is, and-is-thus, too said indeed.
The Sanskrit: *atra caiṣāṃ vāstavena pathā kramavandhyaiva sṛṣṭir ity uktaṃ kramāvabhāso 'pi cāstīty api uktam eva*

## v.60
And sequence of knowledge-passion and the rest, though varied, is seen — someone loving knows, who knowing is loved, and the rest.
The Sanskrit: *kramaś ca vidyārāgādīnāṃ vicitro 'pi dṛṣṭaḥ kaścid rajyan vetti ko 'pi vidan rajyate ityādi*

## v.61
By that, describing differently, even in Raurava and other scriptures is uncontradicted to be deemed — but that very, become-stirred enjoyed-common: guṇa-principle.
The Sanskrit: *tena bhinnakramanirūpaṇam api rauravādiṣu śāstreṣu aviruddhaṃ mantavyaṃ tad eva tu bhogyasāmānyaṃ prakṣobhagataṃ guṇatattvam*

## v.62
Where pleasure is enjoyed-form-light: lucidity; pain is light-unlight-swing-natured, hence very action-formed passion; delusion is unlight-formed darkness.
The Sanskrit: *yatra sukhaṃ bhogyarūpaprakāśaḥ sattvam duḥkhaṃ prakāśāprakāśāndolanātmakam ata eva kriyārūpaṃ rajaḥ mohaḥ prakāśābhāvarūpas tamaḥ*

## v.63
The triad too, this enjoyed-formed.
The Sanskrit: *tritayam api etat bhogyarūpam*

## v.64
Thus from stirred chief comes to-be-done-arising, not from unstirred — thus.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ kṣubdhāt pradhānāt kartavyāntarodayaḥ na akṣubdhād iti*

## v.65
Stirring necessarily indeed in between to be assumed: thus proved Sāṃkhya-seen separated guṇa-principle.
The Sanskrit: *kṣobhaḥ avaśyam eva antarāle abhyupagantavya iti siddhaṃ sāṃkhyāparidṛṣṭaṃ pṛthagbhūtaṃ guṇatattvam*

## v.66
And that stirring from category-lord-presiding alone — otherwise toward fixed man, thus would not succeed.
The Sanskrit: *sa ca kṣobhaḥ prakṛtes tattveśādhiṣṭhānād eva anyathā niyataṃ puruṣaṃ prati iti na sidhyet*

## v.67
Then from guṇa-principle mind-principle, where man-light and object offer reflection.
The Sanskrit: *tato guṇatattvāt buddhitattvaṃ yatra puṃprakāśo viṣayaś ca pratibimbam arpayataḥ*

## v.68
From mind-principle I-maker, by which in mind-mirrored object-contact turbid, in man-light, in non-self is self-pride, like silver-pride in shell.
The Sanskrit: *buddhitattvāt ahaṃkāro yena buddhipratibimbite vedyasamparke kaluṣe puṃprakāśe anātmani ātmābhimānaḥ śuktau rajatābhimānavat*

## v.69
Hence indeed making-ness by this is said of it; Sāṃkhya's but that does not fit — for he does not wish self's I-feeling-nature; but we wish makership too of it.
The Sanskrit: *ata eva kāra ity anena kṛtakatvam asya uktaṃ sāṃkhyasya tu tat na yujyate sa hi na ātmano 'haṃvimarśamayatām icchati vayaṃ tu kartṛtvam api tasya icchāmaḥ*

## v.70
And that pure deliberation alone, non-rivalled, own-self-wonder-formed I — thus.
The Sanskrit: *tac ca śuddhaṃ vimarśa eva apratiyogi svātmacamatkārarūpo 'ham iti*

## v.71
This is of it, of I-maker: instrument-heap.
The Sanskrit: *eṣo 'sya ahaṃkārasya karaṇaskandhaḥ*

## v.72
But nature-heap of it alone is threefold, by lucidity-and-rest difference.
The Sanskrit: *prakṛtiskandhas tu tasyaiva trividhaḥ sattvādibhedāt*

## v.73
There the lucid, from which mind and the five mind-senses — and there in mind, in the generable, endowed with all-subtle-birth power, it is the begetter.
The Sanskrit: *tatra sāttviko yasmāt manaś ca buddhīndriyapañcakaṃ ca tatra manasi janye sarvatanmātrajananasāmarthyayuktaḥ sa janakaḥ*

## v.74
In ear, distinguished by sound-birth power — thus; up to nose, fit with smell-birth fitness — thus elemental is not fitting: since I hear and the rest following, clearly I-maker-ness; and by instrument-ness necessarily maker-part-touching-ness; otherwise, if another instrument is joined, from regress-and-rest befalling.
The Sanskrit: *śrotre tu śabdajananasāmarthyaviśiṣṭa iti yāvat ghrāṇe gandhajananayogyatāyukta iti bhautikam api na yuktam ahaṃ śṛṇomi ityādyanugamāc ca sphuṭam āhaṃkārikatvam karaṇatvena ca avaśyaṃ kartraṃśasparśitvam anyathā karaṇāntarayojanāyām anavasthādyāpātāt*

## v.75
And the maker-part is I-maker alone; by that two chief instruments: in knowing knowledge, in doing kalā — of the blind and of the lame; and from non-leaving of I-formed knowing-doing, from excelling in subtle-part, but from just the lucid I-maker, the five action-senses — I go, thus distinguished as I-maker, fit for effect-instrument; the foot-sense, its chief seat is outer, elsewhere too that is indeed — thus even of the crippled there is no going-break.
The Sanskrit: *kartraṃśaś ca ahaṃkāra eva tena mukhye karaṇe dve puṃsaḥ jñāne vidyā kriyāyāṃ kalā andhasya paṅgoś ca ahaṃtārūpajñānakriyānapagamāt udriktatanmātrabhāgaviśiṣṭāt tu sāttvikād eva ahaṃkārāt karmendriyapañcakam ahaṃ gacchāmi iti ahaṃkāraviśiṣṭaḥ kāryakaraṇakṣamaḥ pādendriyaṃ tasya mukhyādhiṣṭhānaṃ bāhyam anyatrāpi tad asty eva iti rugṇasyāpi na gativicchedaḥ*

## v.76
And not from duty-confusion-freed alone, for the reason action is instrument-effected; and chief of goings and the rest is action-ness, not of form-and-rest grasping; of it, in Kaṇāda's system, since quality — hence necessarily to be admitted is the action-sense group.
The Sanskrit: *na ca kartavyasāṃkaryamuktād eva hetoḥ kriyā karaṇakāryā mukhyaṃ ca gamanādīnāṃ kriyātvaṃ na rūpādyupalambhasya tasya kāṇādatantre guṇatvāt tasmāt avaśyābhyupeyaḥ karmendriyavargaḥ*

## v.77
And it, the pentad, from so-much-ness of joining.
The Sanskrit: *sa ca pañcakaḥ anusaṃdhes tāvattvāt*

## v.78
Thus indeed: outside — first for giving up, or joining; for taking, or for both, or by both-less-ness for own-nature rest — there in order: anus, hand, foot, lap — thus.
The Sanskrit: *tathā hi bahis tāvat tyāgāya vā anusaṃdhiḥ ādānāya vā dvayāya vā ubhayarahitatvena svarūpaviśrāntaye vā tatra krameṇa pāyuḥ pāṇiḥ pāda upastha iti*

## v.79
But within, in breath-seat-action joining, the speech-sense; by that, in sense-seat, in hand, whatever going, that too is foot-sense's alone doing — thus to be deemed; by that action-endlessness too does not bring sense-endlessness; to this extent of the rajasic clinging — thus they say.
The Sanskrit: *antaḥ prāṇāśrayakarmānusaṃdhes tu vāgindriyam tena indriyādhiṣṭhāne haste yat gamanaṃ tad api pādendriyasyaiva karma iti mantavyam tena karmānantyam api na indriyānantyam āvahet iyati rājasasya upaśleṣakatvam ity āhuḥ*

## v.80
Others: from rajasic, mind — they say.
The Sanskrit: *anye tu rājasān mana ity āhuḥ*

## v.81
Others: from lucid, mind; and from passionate, senses — thus.
The Sanskrit: *anye tu sāttvikāt mano rājasāc ca indriyāṇi iti*

## v.82
But from enjoyer-part-coverer, from darkness-ruled I-maker, the subtle elements, knowable-one-formed, five.
The Sanskrit: *bhoktraṃśāc chādakāt tu tamaḥpradhānāhaṃkārāt tanmātrāṇi vedyaikarūpāṇi pañca*

## v.83
Of sound-particulars, for stirring-natured, which one, unstirred-natured, pre-existent, common, undistinguished-natured — that is sound-subtle.
The Sanskrit: *śabdaviśeṣāṇāṃ hi kṣobhātmanāṃ yad ekam akṣobhātmakaṃ prāgbhāvi sāmānyam aviśeṣātmakaṃ tat śabdatanmātram*

## v.84
Thus up to smell too is to be said.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ gandhānte 'pi vācyam*

## v.85
There from stirred sound-subtle: space-giving-business ether; of sound, since bearing word-superimposition-room.
The Sanskrit: *tatra śabdatanmātrāt kṣubhitāt avakāśadānavyāpāraṃ nabhaḥ śabdasya vācyādhyāsāvakāśasahatvāt*

## v.86
Sound-subtle stirred is wind; but sound of it is by ether from non-absence.
The Sanskrit: *śabdatanmātraṃ kṣubhitaṃ vāyuḥ śabdas tu asya nabhasā virahābhāvāt*

## v.87
Form stirred is fire; the two earlier as before.
The Sanskrit: *rūpaṃ kṣubhitaṃ tejaḥ pūrvaguṇau tu pūrvavat*

## v.88
Taste stirred is waters; the earlier three as before.
The Sanskrit: *rasaḥ kṣubhita āpaḥ pūrve trayaḥ pūrvavat*

## v.89
Smell stirred is earth; the earlier four as before.
The Sanskrit: *gandhaḥ kṣubhito dharā pūrve catvāraḥ pūrvavat*

## v.90
Others: by sound-touch, wind; in and-the-rest order, from five, ground — they think.
The Sanskrit: *anye śabdasparśābhyāṃ vāyuḥ ityādikrameṇa pañcabhyo dharaṇī iti manyante*

## v.91
Mere heap of qualities is earth; no other quality-bearer at all.
The Sanskrit: *guṇasamudāyamātraṃ ca pṛthivī nānyo guṇī kaścit*

## v.92
And in this category-cluster, the higher-higher-qualified is pervading; the lower-qualified, pervaded.
The Sanskrit: *asmiṃś ca tattvakalāpe ūrdhvordhvaguṇaṃ vyāpakaṃ nikṛṣṭaguṇaṃ tu vyāpyam*

## v.93
It alone is the excellence of quality: that without it, another quality does not arise; by that, earth-principle, from Śiva-principle beginning, is pervaded by water-principle — thus water by fire, and the rest, up to power-principle.
The Sanskrit: *sa eva guṇasya utkarṣo yat tena vinā guṇāntaraṃ na upapadyate tena pṛthivītattvaṃ śivatattvāt prabhṛti jalatattvena vyāptam evaṃ jalaṃ tejasā ityādi yāvac chaktitattvam*

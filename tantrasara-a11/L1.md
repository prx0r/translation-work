# Tantrasāra A11 — L1 controlled (COMPARE; proposition-faithful, Sanskrit-close)

## tsa11-p01
So far, what was taught comes about for one person directly for the attainment of liberation, in the stated compendium manner; for another, through coming into use in the initiation to be taught. Hence initiation and the rest must be taught. (11.1) Therein — "who is the entitled one?" — for that determination, the descent of power is examined. (11.2)

## tsa11-p02
Therein, some say: "Since, from absence of knowledge, saṃsāra is ignorance-rooted, on its removal there is the descent of power through the rise of knowledge." For them, it must be stated that the very rise of right knowledge is defective. If [descent were] produced by karma, then, like a fruit of karma, there would be contingency of being enjoyed; and if, for the enjoyer, a descent of power is admitted, there is over-extension. But if the rise of knowledge is caused by the Lord's will, there is mutual dependence, and futility, and contingency of passion and the rest in the Lord. (11.3a) "If it is said: from mutual obstruction of two equal opposed karmas there is equilibrium of karma, thence the descent of power" — no: where there is succession, opposition is impossible; and even where there is opposition, there is contingency that another unopposed karma gives enjoyment; and if unopposed karma does not proceed, then and there is contingency of the body's fall. If it is said that birth-and-life-span-giving karma is not obstructed but only enjoyment-giving karma is obstructed — whence is that? If that karma exists, and power were to descend, what would it fear from the enjoyment-giver? (11.3b)

## tsa11-p03
"Then, at the maturation of stain there is the descent of power." But that too — of what nature is it, and what is its cause? By this [refutation]: dispassion, special dharma, discrimination, service of the good, attainment of the good, worship of gods and the rest as cause — is refuted. Thus, for dualists, everything is incoherent. (11.4)

## tsa11-p04
But in the nondual autonomous-supreme-Lord doctrine, this holds. As the Lord, by the play of self-concealment, has become bound soul, person, atom — and there is no contradiction from difference of place, time, or nature — so, by the removal of the concealment of own nature, resorting to the attainment of own nature suddenly or gradually, the atom is called the recipient of the descent of power. And he, the supreme Śiva whose essence is sheer autonomy, is the descender of power. Hence the descent of power is unconditioned indeed — that whose fruit is the manifestation of own nature. But that [descent] for one eager for enjoyment is karma-conditioned. For one eager for transcendent enjoyment, that same descent — through Rudra, Viṣṇu, Brahmā and the rest, the lords over those within Māyā's womb impelled by the Lord's will — taking the form of mantra and the rest, granting discrimination of Māyā and person, of person and śakti, of person and prakṛti, of person and intellect, and other fruit, obstructs the enjoyment of the lower principles. For one eager for both enjoyment and liberation, in enjoyment it is karma-conditioned, in liberation unconditioned. Thus it is both conditioned and unconditioned. (11.5) Nor may it be asked, "Why does the descent of power occur in just this person?" — that same Lord shines thus. When being is thus, what is this "person" for whose sake this object-directed question is raised? (11.6) And this descent of power is ninefold: of the intense, moderate, and mild, through the threefoldness of excess, middleness, and deficiency. Therein, from the utmost intense, then and there at the fall of the body there is identity with the Lord. From the middle intense there is the rise of intuitive knowledge, independent of scripture and teacher, self-verified; on its rise, without outer sacrament, the giver of enjoyment and liberation is called the intuitive teacher. For him there is no conventional observance at all. Yet herein too gradation exists, through diversity of will. Thus, even with intuitiveness, regard for scripture and the rest may occur, for the sake of concord. Hence the with-and-without-ground and many-formed-ness of the intuitive teacher is taught in the scriptures. In every case the intuitive portion is stronger; in its presence, for others there is non-entitlement. (11.7)

## tsa11-p05
As in the difference-view, in the presence of beginningless Śiva: for the liberated Śivas, in the functions of creation, dissolution, and the rest, from a mild-intense descent of power there comes the will to go toward a true teacher; toward a false teacher there is only concealment. And going from a false teacher to a true teacher is itself from the descent of power. (11.8) The true teacher is full of the knowledge of the principles of all this scripture — Lord Bhairava the Blessed himself. Even a yogin, by well-practised knowledge alone, has liberating power; therein, fitness is useless, like possession of good fortune, charm, and the rest. (11.9) The false teacher is simply everyone else. (11.10)

## tsa11-p06
Thus the wisher obtains from the teacher the knowledge-characterized initiation, by which he is liberated instantly, even while alive. Here there are varieties: by glance, by speech, by scriptural instruction, by showing of conduct, by giving of food, and so on. (11.11) Or the practitioner then instantly obtains the life-severing initiation — but that is to be performed only at the moment of death, as will be taught. (11.12) From the utmost three intense [descents] of the intense descent of power, even one who is initiated has no firm conviction of the Śiva-nature of his own self; but by the maturation of conviction, at the end of the body he is Śiva indeed. From the middle moderate, though desiring Śiva-hood he desires enjoyment — he is a fit vessel of knowledge in that same initiation; and he, by the practice of yoga, having enjoyed enjoyment with this very body, at the end of the body is Śiva indeed. (11.13)

## tsa11-p07
From the deficient moderate, having enjoyed enjoyment with another body, he attains Śiva-hood. (11.14) The moderate is threefold: when eagerness for enjoyment has become predominant, then there is mildness — since eagerness there is by way of the Lord's mantra and yoga as means, and since from mantra and yoga of the Lord there is ending in liberation, hence there is descent-nature. (11.15) There too, through gradation, there is threefoldness. Thus this is the principal descent of power. (11.16)

## tsa11-p08
For Vaiṣṇavas and the rest, as with royal favor, there is no ending in liberation; hence there is no analysis here. (11.17) But the presence of Lord and Śakti is everywhere — so it has been stated. That is not the eldest, but the fierce or the fiercer. This descent of power, though varied, divides through variety of gradation: some Vaiṣṇava-dweller, by the samayin course, attaining maturation in the five streams, reaches supreme entitlement to the all-transcending sixfold scripture of the Lord; another, by the leap-course, with endless difference; some, without sequence. Hence therefore even teachers of lower-lower doctrines are here non-entitled, even to the mere showing of the maṇḍala; but a teacher of the upper doctrine enlivens even the lower-lower doctrine, through fullness — hence he is all-entitled. (11.18) He is the deśika, the guru, the ācārya, the initiator, the cumbaka. And he, fully knowledgeable indeed, is supreme — for without him there is no success of initiation and the rest. (11.19)

## tsa11-p09
The yogin is fit if, for one eager for fruit, he is able to give the fruit without interval through teaching of means; through teaching of means he is fit in knowledge alone, and in liberation too through approaching; and desiring fullness of knowledge, one may take even many teachers. (11.20) According to differences of knowledge, higher and highest, one should conduct oneself among them. But in abandoning a teacher of complete knowledge there is expiation itself. (11.21) "But is one who says nothing, or says the reverse, not to be abandoned?" — "Never," we say. For by his very full knowledge there is absence of passion and the rest. Hence silence and the rest must come from some cause residing in the pupil — such as unfitness or distrust. Hence the pupil must strive in his service, not in his abandonment. (11.22)

## tsa11-p10
Thus the descent of power, the cause of grace, is unconditioned indeed, disregarding karma, destiny, and the rest. (11.23) Concealment: concealment has as its fruit the partaking in dense suffering and delusion conditioned by karma and the rest. As by the autonomy of light the awakened one, though behaving like the deluded, inwardly censures deluded conduct, so the deluded one may perform awakened conduct such as worship of mantra and the rest, and censure it. And as his deluded conduct, though performed, perishes for the awakened one, so his awakened conduct — being censured, of the nature of forbidden conduct, apprehensive through itself — drowns him in the mud of suffering and delusion. But for one in whom the descent of power has arisen, there is no concealment. Here too, regard for karma and the rest is to be denied, as before. There too, through diversity of will, there is fruition of suffering to be enjoyed with this body only — as for those given both to service and to censure toward initiation, conduct, teacher, god, fire, and the rest, and as for former dwellers in Śiva's doctrine who abandoned it.

## tsa11-p01 — why initiation comes next
Everything taught so far reaches some people directly — they hear the compendium and are free.
Others can only use it once initiation is on the table. So initiation, and what follows from it,
must now be taught. And the first question is eligibility: who is even entitled to receive it?
That question forces the topic of this chapter — the descent of power.

## tsa11-p02 — the failed explanations
Three theories are on offer, and Abhinava demolishes each in turn. Some say liberation is a matter
of knowledge: we suffer from not-knowing, so when knowledge dawns, power descends. But the "dawn
of right knowledge" is itself the thing that needs explaining — the account is circular on arrival.
Say instead that grace is produced by ritual action, and it inherits action's limits: like any reward,
it would be something merely enjoyed, and anyone enjoying anything would count as graced — the
concept explodes. Say it comes from the Lord's sheer will, and knowledge's dawn either grounds the
will (circular again) or floats free of it (pointless) — while saddling the Lord himself with
motive and partiality. A subtler version: when two equal and opposite deeds deadlock, their
equilibrium lets grace through. But deadlock needs simultaneity; deeds ripen in sequence, where
"opposition" has no grip. And even granted the deadlock, some third, unopposed deed would still be
delivering its pleasures — or, if no deed is operating at all, the body should drop on the spot.
Plead that birth-and-lifespan deeds run on while only pleasure-deeds jam: on what ground? If the
deed-structure is intact, what exactly would descending power be afraid of in the pleasure-giver?
*Fidelity: preserved (three theories + replies in order). Added: "circular on arrival" unpacks
anyonyāśraya [SUPPLIED-structural]. Not-resolved: the "defective" charge against right knowledge
is promissory here (iti vācyam).*

## tsa11-p03 — maturation, and the collapse of dualism
"Grace comes when the stain ripens." Very well: what is this ripening — what is it, and what causes
it? No answer comes. And with it fall the whole catalogue of supposed causes — dispassion, merit,
discernment, good company, attainment, worship. For the dualist schools, every option on the table
is now incoherent.

## tsa11-p04 — autonomy: the doctrine
Here is Abhinava's own position, and it needs no patch from the wreckage above. The Lord, playing
at hiding himself, has become the bound soul, the person, the atom — and place, time, and
particular nature don't contradict this, since they are his own display. In exactly the same way,
when the hiding lifts and one's own nature is recovered — in a flash or by degrees — the atom is
what we call "a recipient of the descent." And the one from whom it descends is supreme Śiva,
whose whole essence is autonomy. So the descent, whose fruit is simply nature showing itself, is
unconditioned. [SUPPLIED: "simply" — the Sanskrit has svarūpaprathāphala, manifestation-of-own-
nature-as-fruit; "simply" marks that nothing is added.]
Qualifications follow the fruit, not the mechanism. Wanting pleasure? Then karma conditions apply.
Wanting a higher pleasure? Then the very same descent comes down through Rudra, Viṣṇu, Brahmā —
the lords posted inside Māyā's womb at the Lord's will — wearing the shape of mantra and the rest,
teaching apart Māyā from person, person from power, from nature, from intellect, and barring the
way down to a lower enjoyment. Wanting both pleasure and freedom? Then conditioned in the pleasure,
unconditioned in the freedom — both at once.
"But why this person and not that?" The question assumes a "person" standing outside being, waiting
to be picked. There is none: the Lord shines this way, and that shining is all there is.
The descent is ninefold — intense, moderate, mild, each in excess, middle, and deficient measure.
At the peak of intensity, the body drops and there is identity with the Lord then and there. At
middle intensity, intuitive knowledge dawns needing no book and no teacher, self-certifying — and
with it the intuitive teacher, who gives pleasure and freedom with no outer rite at all. Such a
one is bound by no observance. Yet ranks remain, because will differs: even the intuitive may honor
scripture, for agreement's sake — which is why the books describe the intuitive teacher in many
forms, grounded and groundless. In every case the intuitive share outweighs; where it is present,
nothing else confers standing.
*Fidelity: preserved (play-analogy, conditioning rule, why-this-person dissolution, ninefold table).
Added: bracketed SUPPLIED above; "books" for āgameṣu. Not-resolved: nirbhitti/sabhitti forms per
scriptures (external).*

## tsa11-p05 — the true teacher
The same pattern holds among the liberated ones: where Śiva's presence is beginningless, a
mild-intense descent moves them to create and dissolve — and to want a true teacher. Toward a
false teacher there is only further hiding; and even the move from a false teacher to a true one
is itself the descent at work. The true teacher knows this scripture's principles whole — he is
Lord Bhairava in person. A yogin too, by knowledge well practised, can set others free; being
"qualified" in the worldly sense — luck, charm, credentials — is as irrelevant as good looks are
to arithmetic. A false teacher is anyone else.
*Fidelity: preserved. Added: the arithmetic simile unpacks saubhāgya-illustration [SUPPLIED-structural].*

## tsa11-p06 — how initiation works, and what follows by rank
So the seeker receives from the teacher the initiation whose mark is knowledge — and is free at
once, while still alive. It comes by a glance, a word, a text taught, a conduct shown, food
shared, and more. There is also the initiation that severs the life-breath — but that one is for
the hour of death, as will be taught. From the highest three intensities of the intense, even the
initiated has no steady footing in his own Śiva-nature — but as conviction ripens, at the body's
end he is Śiva, no qualification. From the middle of the moderate: still hungry for pleasure, yet
a fit vessel for knowledge in that very initiation — he enjoys, with this same body, through yoga
practice, and at the body's end he is Śiva.

## tsa11-p07 — the mildest operative grade
One step down: pleasure enjoyed in another body, then Śiva-hood. The moderate comes in three:
where hunger for pleasure rules, the grade is mild — for that hunger travels by the Lord's mantra
and yoga, and mantra-yoga ends in freedom, so the structure still counts as descent. Three sub-
ranks within, by the same gradation. This — the whole mild-to-intense span terminating in freedom —
is the principal descent.
*Fidelity: preserved. Added: closing gloss unpacks mukhya (principal = the full operative span).*

## tsa11-p08 — outsiders and the teacher who contains all ranks
What of the Vaiṣṇavas and the rest? Their grace is like the king's favor — real, but it doesn't
end in freedom. So no analysis here. (That said, Lord-and-Śakti are present everywhere — only not
as "the eldest"; she is Fierce, and Fiercer.) The descent varies, and its varieties sort by rank:
one Vaiṣṇava practitioner, ripened through the samayin course and the five streams, rises to the
highest charge of the Lord's all-surpassing sixfold word; another takes the leap, endlessly
variegated; another, no stages at all. Hence: teachers of the lowest doctrines have no standing
here — not even to display a maṇḍala. But a teacher of the height, by sheer completeness, gives
life even to the lowest doctrine. He is deśika, guru, ācārya, initiator, cumbaka — five names for
one plenitude. And he, knowing fully, outranks all: without him no initiation succeeds.
*Fidelity: preserved. Added: "the king's favor" kept literal (rājānugrahavat). Not-resolved:
srotaḥpañcaka/ṣaḍardha technicals assumed (external).*

## tsa11-p09 — the yogin, many teachers, and the ban on abandonment
A yogin is fit to take on when he can deliver the goods at once, by teaching the means — fit in
bare knowledge by that teaching, and in freedom too, by whoever comes close. Wanting fuller
knowledge, take even many teachers, and move among them by the rank of their knowing. One thing
is forbidden: dropping a teacher who knows completely — that itself needs expiation. "But what of
a teacher who goes silent, or teaches wrong — may he not be dropped?" Never, says Abhinava: one
who knows fully has no lust or hate left to explain the silence. So the silence comes from
something on the pupil's side — unfitness, distrust. Work harder at serving him; do not work at
leaving him.

## tsa11-p10 — grace unconditioned; hiding; the unbroken self
So: the descent, which is what makes grace happen, is unconditioned — it does not wait on deeds
or destinies. Hiding, by contrast, ripens thick suffering and confusion, all of it deed-
conditioned. Watch the mirror: the awake man playing the fool still inwardly despises folly — and
the fool playing awake, mouthing mantras, still despises the part. The awake man's folly, once
acted, dissolves; but the fool's acted awakeness — performed yet inwardly condemned, forbidden in
form, making him suspect even to himself — sinks him in the mud. For one in whom the descent has
risen, there is no hiding. And here too, stop weighing deeds — as before. Even then, will differs:
one may yet taste suffering with this very body — like those who both serve and revile the
initiation, the conduct, the teacher, the god, the fire, or like old Śaivas who walked away. And
even then, by that same differing will: the hidden one may join the descent himself — or, dead,
through kin or teacher's grace. Holding to this — "the doings are mine, and I am the Lord" — never
see the self as broken.

mantras" for mantrārādhanādikām kuryāt (register choice, flagged). "I am the Lord" renders
parameśvara eva with self-reflexive made explicit [SUPPLIED-minor].*

# C1 — Tantrasāra Ahnika 11 (śaktipāta)

## tsa11-p01 — why initiation must be taught

**FUNCTION.** Frame-setting and topic-introduction. Establishes why śaktipāta, not initiation itself, is the ahnika's subject, and why eligibility is a causal question.

**KEY TERMS.** adhikārin (entitled recipient), apavarga (liberation), saṃgraha-nīti (compendium manner), vaktavyam (must be taught), vicāryate (is examined).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** Opening of the ahnika; follows A10's kalādhvā (digit-course) and precedes the pūrvapakṣa in P2.


**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** No doctrine stated; the pūrvapakṣa theories follow in P2. No unresolved items beyond the frame.

**RELATED.** A10 (kalādhvā), A12 (snāna, bath before initiation), spans TS11.1–11.2.

## tsa11-p02 — the failed explanations
**SUMMARY.** Three theories of descent are proposed and each demolished. First, the knowledge-theory: saṃsāra is ignorance-rooted, so when knowledge dawns, power descends. But the dawn of right knowledge is itself the unexplained term (the charge is promissory here — iti vācyam); and if karma-produced, enjoyment-contingency (bhogatva-prasaṅga) and over-extension (atiprasaṅga) follow; if Lord's-will-caused, mutual dependence (anyonyāśrayatā), futility (vaiyarthya), and passion in the Lord (rāgādi-prasaṅga). Second, the karma-equilibrium theory: equal opposed karmas deadlock, grace slips through. But succession excludes opposition; a third unopposed karma would still deliver pleasure; or the body should fall on the spot. The whence? (kutaḥ?) marks the point: no theory can state its own ground. Third, stain-maturation (P3). Each proposal collapses under its own consequences.

**FUNCTION.** Pūrvapakṣa demolition. Clears the ground for the siddhānta in P4 by showing that no proposed mechanism (knowledge, karma, equilibrium) can account for grace.

**KEY TERMS.** karmasāmya (equilibrium of karma), atiprasaṅga (over-extension), anyonyāśraya (mutual dependence), vaiyarthya (futility), kramikatva (succession), jātyāyuṣprada (birth-and-lifespan-giving).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** Immediately after the framing (P1); the siddhānta begins at P4.

**EXPLANATION.** Abhinava does not argue against grace but against every proposed mechanism of grace. The knowledge-theory is circular: samyak-jñānodaya is itself defective (vikṛta) — the charge is promissory here (iti vācyam), answered later. If grace is karma-produced, it inherits action's limits: like any reward, it would be merely enjoyed, and anyone enjoying anything would count as graced — the concept explodes (atiprasaṅga). If grace comes from the Lord's will, knowledge's dawn either grounds the will (circular again) or floats free of it (pointless) — while saddling the Lord with motive and partiality (rāgādi-prasaṅga). The karma-equilibrium theory: when two equal and opposed deeds deadlock, their equilibrium lets grace through. But deadlock needs simultaneity; deeds ripen in sequence, where opposition has no grip (kramikatve virodhāyogāt). And even granted the deadlock, some third, unopposed deed would still be delivering its pleasures (bhogadāna-prasaṅga) — or, if no deed is operating at all, the body should drop on the spot (dehapāta-prasaṅga). Plead that birth-and-lifespan deeds run on while only pleasure-deeds jam: on what ground? (kutaḥ?) If the deed-structure is intact, what exactly would descending power be afraid of in the pleasure-giver? The repeated whence? marks the point: no theory can state its own ground.

**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** The defective charge against right knowledge is promissory (iti vācyam); answered in later ahnikas. kiṃsvarūpa/kiṃnimitta hang until P3/P4.

**RELATED.** A04 (sattarka), A11 P3–P4, spans TS11.3.

## tsa11-p03 — maturation, and the collapse of dualism
**SUMMARY.** Grace comes when the stain ripens. The challenge stands: what is this ripening — what is it, and what causes it? The whole catalogue of proposed causes (dispassion, merit, discernment, good company, attainment, worship) is refuted. For dualist schools, every option on the table is incoherent. The theory cannot state its own nature, cause, or means — the same move as P2 but aimed at a single theory. The hetu-list refutation shows that no moral or ritual achievement causes grace; hence grace is not merit-earned.


**KEY TERMS.** malaparipāka (stain-maturation), kiṃsvarūpa (of what nature), kiṃnimitta (what cause), hetu (cause), bhedavāda (dualism), asamañjasa (incoherent).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** P2–P3 are the refutation half; P4 opens the siddhānta.

**EXPLANATION.** At-the-maturation-of-stain, the descent — but that too: of what nature is it, and what is its cause? (kiṃsvarūpaḥ? kiṃ ca tasya nimittam?) No answer comes. And with it fall the whole catalogue of supposed causes — dispassion (vairāgya), special dharma (dharmaviśeṣa), discrimination (viveka), service of the good (satsevā), attainment of the good (satprāpti), worship of gods (devapūjā) and the rest — refuted (pratyukta). Thus, for dualists (bhedavādin), everything is incoherent (sarvam asamañjasam). The theory cannot state its own nature, cause, or means — the same move as P2 but aimed at a single theory. The hetu-list refutation shows that no moral or ritual achievement causes grace; hence grace is not merit-earned. This is the pūrvapakṣa's last stand; the siddhānta opens at P4 with autonomy.

**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** Nature and cause questions left hanging — answered only by the coming siddhānta (P4). The defective charge (P2) also remains promissory.

**RELATED.** A04 (sattarka), A11 P4, spans TS11.4.

## tsa11-p04 — autonomy: the doctrine
**SUMMARY.** Abhinava's own position, and it needs no patch from the wreckage above. The Lord, playing at hiding himself, has become the bound soul, the person, the atom — and place, time, and particular nature don't contradict this, since they are his own display. In exactly the same way, when the hiding lifts and one's own nature is recovered — in a flash or by degrees — the atom becomes what we call a recipient of the descent. And the one from whom it descends is supreme Śiva, whose whole essence is autonomy. So the descent, whose fruit is simply nature showing itself, is unconditioned. Qualifications follow the fruit, not the mechanism: wanting pleasure? Then karma conditions apply. Wanting a higher pleasure? Then the very same descent comes down through Rudra, Viṣṇu, Brahmā — the lords posted inside Māyā's womb at the Lord's will — wearing the shape of mantra and the rest, teaching apart Māyā from person, person from power, from nature, from intellect, and barring the way down to a lower enjoyment. Wanting both pleasure and freedom? Then conditioned in the pleasure, unconditioned in the freedom — both at once. But why this person and not that? The question assumes a person standing outside being, waiting to be picked. There is none: the Lord shines this way, and that shining is all there is. The descent is ninefold — intense, moderate, mild, each in excess, middle, and deficient measure. At the peak of intensity, the body drops and there is identity with the Lord then and there. At middle intensity, intuitive knowledge dawns needing no book and no teacher, self-certifying — and with it the intuitive teacher, who gives pleasure and freedom with no outer rite at all. Such a one is bound by no observance. Yet ranks remain, because will differs: even the intuitive may honor scripture, for agreement's sake — which is why the books describe the intuitive teacher in many forms, grounded and groundless. In every case the intuitive share outweighs; where it is present, nothing else confers standing.

**FUNCTION.** Core doctrine of the ahnika. Establishes the autonomy-grounded, fruit-graded typology of descent (ninefold) and dissolves the selection problem.

**KEY TERMS.** svātantrya (autonomy), śaktipāta-pātra (recipient of descent), prātibha (intuitive), nirapekṣa (unconditioned), sāpekṣa (conditioned), navadhā (ninefold), icchāvaicitrya (will-diversity), saṃnidhi (presence).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** P1–P3 refuted; P4–P10 build the siddhānta.


**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** nirbhitti/sabhitti teacher-forms per scriptures (external). Nature renders svarūpa + prakṛti (AQ-3, L1 revision note). The defective charge (P2) remains promissory.

**RELATED.** A04 (sattarka), A11 P5–P7, spans TS11.5–11.7.

## tsa11-p05 — the true teacher
**SUMMARY.** The same pattern holds among the liberated ones: a mild-intense descent moves them to want a true teacher; toward a false teacher there is only further concealment; and the move from false to true is itself descent's effect. The true teacher knows this scripture's principles whole — he is Lord Bhairava in person. A yogin too can liberate by well-practised knowledge; worldly fitness (luck, charm, credentials) is as irrelevant as good looks are to arithmetic. A false teacher is anyone else.

**FUNCTION.** Teacher-criterion. Separates authority (fullness of knowledge) from office or worldly qualification.

**KEY TERMS.** sadguru (true teacher), asadguru (false teacher), saubhāgya (good fortune/charm), mocakatva (liberating power), yogyatva (fitness).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** Follows P4's ninefold typology; P6 takes up initiation.


**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** None major. The saubhāgya-irrelevance is stated by analogy; its force is rhetorical, not argued.

**RELATED.** A11 P4, P6, spans TS11.8–11.10.

## tsa11-p06 — how initiation works, and what follows by rank
**SUMMARY.** So the seeker receives from the teacher the initiation whose mark is knowledge — and is free at once, while still alive. It comes by a glance, a word, a text taught, a conduct shown, food shared, and more. There is also the initiation that severs the life-breath — but that one is for the hour of death, as will be taught. From the highest three intensities of the intense, even the initiated has no steady footing in his own Śiva-nature — but as conviction ripens, at the body's end he is Śiva, no qualification. From the middle of the moderate: still hungry for pleasure, yet a fit vessel for knowledge in that very initiation — he enjoys, with this same body, through yoga practice, and at the body's end he is Śiva.

**FUNCTION.** Initiation-types and grade→fate mapping. Establishes that grade determines timing and mode, never the outcome for the entitled.

**KEY TERMS.** jñānalakṣaṇā dīkṣā (knowledge-characterized initiation), prāṇaviyojikā (life-severing initiation), maraṇakṣaṇa (moment of death), dṛḍhapratipatti (firm conviction), pratipattiparipāka (maturation of conviction), jñānabhājana (fit vessel of knowledge).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** P4–P5 established descent and teacher; P6 takes up the rite.


**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** Prāṇaviyojikā rite deferred (iti vakṣyāmaḥ → A15/A19). The fit vessel (jñānabhājana) criterion is asserted, not argued.

**RELATED.** A11 P4, A12 (snāna), A15 (sadyaḥ), spans TS11.11–11.13.

## tsa11-p07 — the mildest operative grade
**SUMMARY.** One step down from the middle moderate: pleasure enjoyed in another body, then Śiva-hood. The moderate is threefold; where hunger for pleasure rules, the grade is mild — for that hunger travels by the Lord's mantra and yoga, which end in freedom, so the structure still counts as descent. Three sub-ranks within, by the same gradation. This whole mild-to-intense span terminating in freedom is the principal descent (mukhya śaktipāta).

**FUNCTION.** Completes the grade table (utmost/middle/deficient × intense/moderate/mild); defines mukhya śaktipāta.

**KEY TERMS.** nikṛṣṭamadhya (deficient-moderate), madhyas tridhā (moderate threefold), bhogotsukatā (eagerness for enjoyment), mandatva (mildness), mukhya śaktipāta (principal descent), tāratamya (gradation).



**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** None major. The principal gloss is a register choice, flagged for review.

**RELATED.** A11 P6, spans TS11.14–11.16.

## tsa11-p08 — outsiders and the teacher who contains all ranks
**SUMMARY.** What of the Vaiṣṇavas and the rest? Their grace is like the king's favor — real, but no ending in freedom; so no analysis here. Lord-and-Śakti present everywhere, but not as the eldest — she is Fierce, and Fiercer. The descent varies: samayin/five-streams Vaiṣṇava → highest charge of the sixfold word; leap-course; no stages. Lower-lower teachers have no standing; an upper teacher, by completeness, enlivens even the lowest. Five teacher-names for one plenitude; without him no initiation succeeds.

**FUNCTION.** Outsider mapping + teacher plenitude. Separates grace-presence from liberation-ending.

**KEY TERMS.** rājānugraha (royal favor), sarvādhikārin (all-entitled), daiśika/guru/ācārya/dīkṣaka/cumbaka (five teacher-names), srotaḥpañcaka (five streams), ṣaḍardhaśāstra (sixfold scripture).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** After grade-table (P4–P7); P8 maps outsiders; P9 takes up the yogin.


**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** srotaḥpañcaka/ṣaḍardha technicals assumed (external). Nature renders svarūpa + prakṛti (AQ-3, L1 revision note).

**RELATED.** A04 (sattarka), A11 P4–P7, spans TS11.17–11.19.

## tsa11-p09 — the yogin, many teachers, and the ban on abandonment
**SUMMARY.** A yogin is fit to take on when he can deliver the goods at once, by teaching the means — fit in bare knowledge by that teaching, and in freedom too, by whoever comes close. Wanting fuller knowledge, take even many teachers, and move among them by the rank of their knowing. One thing forbidden: dropping a teacher who knows completely — that itself needs expiation. But what of a teacher who goes silent, or teaches wrong? Never: full knowledge means no lust or hate; the silence comes from something on the pupil's side (unfitness, distrust). Work harder at serving him; do not work at leaving him.

**FUNCTION.** Fitness rule, many-teacher rule, no-abandonment ruling (via nanu + reply).

**KEY TERMS.** phalotsuka (fruit-eager), avyavahita (without interval), upāyopadeśa (teaching of means), sampūrṇajñāna (complete knowledge), prāyaścitta (expiation), ayogyatva (unfitness), anāśvastatva (distrust).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** After teacher-criterion (P5) and outsiders (P8).


**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** None major (practical ruling). The reply's force depends on the premise (no rāga in the fully-knowing); if the premise fails, the ruling fails.

**RELATED.** A11 P5, P8, spans TS11.20–11.22.

## tsa11-p10 — grace unconditioned; concealment; the unbroken self
**SUMMARY.** The descent, cause of grace, is unconditioned — it does not wait on deeds or destinies. Concealment, by contrast, ripens thick suffering and confusion, all deed-conditioned. The mirror simile: the awake man playing the fool still inwardly despises folly; the fool playing awake, mouthing mantras, still despises the part — and the fool's acted awakeness, performed yet inwardly condemned, sinks him in the mud. For one in whom descent has risen, no hiding. And here too, stop weighing deeds — as before. Even then, will differs: one may yet taste suffering with this body (double-dealers, apostates). And even then, by that same differing will: the hidden one may join the descent himself — or, dead, through kin or teacher's grace. Holding to the doings are mine, and I am the Lord — never see the self as broken.

**FUNCTION.** Closing of the ahnika: grace's final status, concealment, recovery, unbroken self.

**KEY TERMS.** anugraha (grace), tirobhāva (concealment), icchāvaicitrya (will-diversity), khaṇḍita (broken), kṛtyabhāgitva (act-partaking), bandhugurvādikṛpā (kin/teacher grace).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** Closes A11; A12 (snāna) follows.


**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** Mouthing mantras (MT-009) is a register choice, flagged. I am the Lord (MT-010) self-reflexive made explicit [SUPPLIED-minor]. The mirror simile assumes everyday assent (mild, external).

**RELATED.** A04 (sattarka), A11 P4–P9, spans TS11.23–11.25.

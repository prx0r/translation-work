# C1 — tsa-a12

## SUMMARY

The exposition opens with the bath: to set forth initiation's nature, the to-be-done bath is taught first. Bathing names purity; purity is immersion in the Supreme Lord's own form. Purity is washing-off of turbidity; turbidity is the conceit of admixing an alien form — even in That whose sole form is That. The conceit of another-formed reality is impurity in self or world alike; dispelled by great-Bhairava immersion — sudden for some, for others awaiting further means. Difference is manifold — by one-two-threefold division, whole or part, somewhere, for someone, sometime, from assurance-attainment. Eight forms made into Supreme-Lord forms by mantra-placement: identity in the Lord-entered body brings immersion for anyone; bathing-gladness itself becomes the means; chief fruits — firmness to non-difference — follow each assumed form. Heroes-rule sets the five substances — battle-dust to cremation-ground sky — with sun-moon placed and the self undiscriminating. Outer-inner twoness collapses by oneness with the outward-worshipped mantra: made-of-That, plunging here-there, thus told. The chief bath: bliss-substance hero-abiding made-Śiva, mantra-circle worshipped, deity-circle of body-and-breath satisfied by that very one. The inward bath holds earth-and-other forms in earthen circles — being-made-of-That.

## FUNCTION

Defines bath-as-immersion, grades it (worldwide impurity, eight mūrti forms, heroes' substances, chief bath, inward hold), and closes on made-of-That.

## KEY TERMS

snāna (bath as purity); śuddhi (turbidity-washing); abhimāna (alien-form conceit); mahābhairava-samāveśa (dispelling immersion); tādātmya (identity in the Lord-entered body); vīroddeśa (heroes' five substances); mukhya-snāna (chief bath); tanmayībhāva (made-of-That close).

## LOCAL CONTEXT

The bath is taught as the precondition of dīkṣā-vidhi; eightfold, heroes', chief, and inward forms graded in turn before the initiation-rite the bath serves.

## EXPLANATION

Because snāna is samāveśa, impurity must be abhimāna rather than dirt; because the conceit can arise in self or world, the dispelling immersion is universal in scope yet graded in time (jhaṭiti for some, upāyāntara-prekṣin for others); because identity does the work, the eight forms need only mantra-placement in the Lord-entered body, and the gladness of the rite itself becomes the means — hence the chief fruits and the inward hold: same forms, circles of earth, made-of-That.

## BOUNDARY

No cosmology argued: the rite's definition, grades, and fruits are stated; the fruits-list stands as authority, not as proof.

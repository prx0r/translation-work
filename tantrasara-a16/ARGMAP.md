# Tantrasāra A16 — ARGMAP

## tsa16-p01 (16.1–16.24)
- speaker: Abhinava, siddhānta (absent initiation + net-rite).
- question: Who may be initiated absent (dead or living), and how are souls drawn?
- premises: (1) Parokṣa twofold (dead/living). Eligible dead: teacher-served, exiled, sorcery/child-struck, death-moment-tasted, other-mouth-descent (command). Dead rite needs no lodging. (2) Maṇḍala presence-causes graded: much rite → best materials → flowers → place → seat → circle → shape → meditation → mantra → self-blazing + devoted-knower bond. Collected, what tale (Lord's remark). (3) Worship god, set kuśa-form before, see it via teacher-seat-knowledge-course; it rises root-based, crosses endless channels by vigor, pervades nose-sky, scattering, to pervade. (4) Smoke-lovely-net-covered course-wheel = soul-bringing = great net-practice: covering→subduing→facing first, then brought whole, then from course-middle as wished. (5) In pulling/lifting, for dead-drawn or living: Śambhunātha-taught net-yoga, mine stated. Outside too, how not? Without practice in pulling: passion-hate-yoga gives lordship-immersion functioning; but destiny-ruled needs practice. Here: grace-Lord-immersion makes it so; Lord graces through teacher-body door; inconceivable greatness (much stated). (6) Net-drawn soul enters kuśa/jāti-fruit body, unthrobbing (no mind-breath-gear) — yet throbs by contemplation-power; therein sprinkling-to-joining sacraments as before; by full offering its kuśa-form dissolves in supreme glow = lifted (hell/heaven/ghost/beast freed). Human then gains knowledge/yoga/initiation/discernment (fit body) = dead-lifting. Living absent: same minus kuśa-body-soul-drawing; meditation-raised alone refined; initiation gives both fruits. Vāsanā-strong: many need upper-rule sacrament, rest for its sacrament. (7) Absent-initiated likewise knowledge-rise.
- inference: Distance/death change instruments (net, kuśa-body), never the Lord's door (teacher's body).
- conclusion: Parokṣa + jāla-prayoga stated.
- unresolved: Net-rite implements (traditional).

# Tantrasara-a16 — L2 READ

## v.1
Now indirect initiation is twofold: the dead one, and the jīva-part.
The Sanskrit: *atha parokṣasya dīkṣā dvividhaś ca saḥ mṛto jīvaṃś ca*

## v.2
Therein [the command]: one who has served the guru — whether dead, or banished, or killed by sorcery and the rest, or killed as an infant, or arisen at the death-moment as willed, or one whose power-descent has come through another mouth — or likewise one to be initiated: such is the command.
The Sanskrit: *tatra kṛtaguruseva eva mṛta udvāsito vā abhicārādihato ḍimbāhato mṛtyukṣaṇoditatathāruciḥ mukhāntarāyātaśaktipāto vā tathā dīkṣya ity ājñā*

## v.3
And in the dead-initiation, adhivāsa-and-rest is not used.
The Sanskrit: *atra ca mṛtadīkṣāyām adhivāsādi na upayujyate*

## v.4
In the mandala — where abundant rite, the supreme instrument, flower-and-rest seat, pedestal-and-rest mandala, trident-lotus-and-rest form, the specific meditation-object mantra, itself radiant — of the meditation-devoted yogi, of him rich in one-devotion-immersion, and of the knower: these are the presence-causes, stated in order.
The Sanskrit: *maṇḍale mantraviśeṣasaṃnidhaye yatra bahulā kriyā uttamam upakaraṇaṃ puṣpādi sthānaṃ pīṭhādi maṇḍalaṃ triśūlābjādi ākṛtiḥ dhyeyaviśeṣaḥ mantraḥ svayaṃ dīptaś ca dhyānaparasya yoginaḥ tadekabhaktisamāveśaśālino jñāninaś ca sambandhaḥ ity ete saṃnidhānahetavo yathottaram uktāḥ*

## v.5
In their conjunction, what talk would there be? — thus it was said by the Supreme Lord.
The Sanskrit: *samuditatve tu kā kathā syāt iti parameśvareṇa uktam*

## v.6
Then, having worshipped the god and established his kuśa-and-rest form in front, by the guru-derived order of knowledge-teaching let him see it; and he, rising from the muladhara, having crossed by seminal power the spread-out endless nerve-path-staff to the nose-sky, scattering — let him seek to pervade.
The Sanskrit: *tato devaṃ pūjayitvā tadākṛtiṃ kuśādimayīm agre sthāpayitvā gurvāsāditajñānopadeśakrameṇa tāṃ paśyet sa ca mūlādhārād udetya prasṛtasuvitatānantanāḍyadhvadaṇḍaṃ vīryeṇākramya nāsāgaganaparigataṃ vikṣipan vyāptum īṣṭe*

## v.7
As long as, by smoke favorably heaped in fine-flame mesh, the path-wheel is covered and the desired jīva is brought in — thus the great jala-named practice.
The Sanskrit: *yāvad dhūmābhirāmapracitataraśikhājālakenādhvacakraṃ saṃchādyābhīṣṭajīvānayanam iti mahāñ jālanāmā prayogaḥ*

## v.8
By this, the to-be-covered goes into another's power — facing first; afterwards the whole is brought, if — or then even from the path's middle, as he wishes.
The Sanskrit: *etenācchādanīyaṃ vrajati paravaśaṃ saṃmukhīnatvam ādau paścād ānīyate cet sakalam atha tato 'py adhvamadhyād yatheṣṭam*

## v.9
Two-drawn or two-raised, or within a dead person's scope, that-which-is-to-be-drawn in the jīva: the yoga gone through blessed Śaṃbhunātha's tradition — jala-named, told by me.
The Sanskrit: *ākṛṣṭāv uddhatau vā mṛtajanaviṣaye karṣaṇīye 'tha jīve yogaḥ śrīśaṃbhunāthāgamaparigamito jālanāmā mayoktaḥ*

## v.10
Outside too — thus how does it not happen, from drawing-and-rest without practice, if asked: by passion, hatred and the rest yoked, in its operation, from the aiśvarya-immersion yoke.
The Sanskrit: *bahir api itthaṃ kathaṃ na bhavati ākarṣaṇādau vinābhyāsāt iti cet rāgadveṣādiyogavaśena tatpravṛttau aiśvaryāveśāyogāt*

## v.11
Then, from being necessarily controlled, dependence on practice-and-rest would indeed be.
The Sanskrit: *tato niyatiniyantritatvāt abhyāsādyapekṣā syād eva*

## v.12
Here, however, from immersion in grace-natured Lordhood, such is the state.
The Sanskrit: *iha tu anugrahātmakaparameśvaratāveśāt tathābhāvaḥ*

## v.13
For the Supreme Lord himself, by the door of the guru's body-abode, graces those to be graced.
The Sanskrit: *parameśvara eva hi guruśarīrādhiṣṭhānadvāreṇa anugrāhyān anugṛhṇāti*

## v.14
And he, of unthinkable glory — it is mostly said.
The Sanskrit: *sa ca acintyamahimā iti uktaprāyam*

## v.15
Thus drawn by the jala-practice, the jīva, having entered dārbha, jasmine-fruit-and-rest or the body, becomes; nor does he tremble, from the absence of mind-breath-and-rest equipment; but by the force of that contemplation he trembles even so — as before: the purification-and-rest consecration, within the full-offering's yojana-end.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ jālaprayogākṛṣṭo jīvo dārbhaṃ jātīphalādi vā śarīraṃ samāviṣṭo bhavati na ca spandate manaḥpraṇādisāmagryabhāvāt tadanudhyānabalāt tu spandate 'pi tādṛśe 'pi tasmin pūrvavat prokṣaṇādisaṃskāraḥ pūrṇāhutiyojanikāntaḥ*

## v.16
Here, further, by the full-offering, dissolution of the dārbha-and-rest form in the supreme fire is to be done.
The Sanskrit: *atra paraṃ pūrṇāhutyā tasya dārbhādyākārasya paratejasi layaḥ kartavyaḥ*

## v.17
Thus raised out, he is released by the full-offering itself — if he stands in the hell-ghost-animal classes.
The Sanskrit: *evam uddhṛto 'sau pūrṇāhutyaiva apavṛjyate yadi svarnarakapretatiryakṣu sthitaḥ*

## v.18
A man, just then, obtains knowledge, yoga, initiation, or discernment.
The Sanskrit: *manuṣyas tu tadaiva jñānaṃ yogaṃ dīkṣāṃ vivekaṃ vā labhate*

## v.19
From being an eligible body — thus the raising-of-the-dead.
The Sanskrit: *adhikāriśarīratvāt iti mṛtoddharaṇam*

## v.20
Even for the living, in indirect [initiation], when power-descent arises: this very sequence — excepting the dārbha-form-imagination jīva-drawing.
The Sanskrit: *jīvato 'pi parokṣasya utpanne śaktipāte 'yam eva kramaḥ dārbhākṛtikalpanajīvākṛṣṭivarjam*

## v.21
The consecration of the one established by mere meditation itself.
The Sanskrit: *dhyānamātropasthāpitasyaiva asya saṃskāraḥ*

## v.22
Initiation gives both the enjoyment and the enjoyer.
The Sanskrit: *dīkṣā ca bhogabhokṣobhayadāyinī*

## v.23
From the strength of one's own habit, and the unthinkability of severing the enjoyment-habit: by many, in initiation, the higher-doctrine consecration is strong; another would be for that consecration.
The Sanskrit: *svavāsanābalīyastvāt bhogavāsanāvicchedasya ca asaṃbhāvyamānatvāt bahubhiḥ dīkṣāyām ūrdhvaśāsanasaṃskāro balavān anyas tu tatsaṃskārāya syāt*

## v.24
Likewise for the initiated from outside — the manifestation of knowledge-and-rest, it is said.
The Sanskrit: *paroṣasyāpi dīkṣitasya tathaiva jñānādyāvirbhāvaḥ iti*

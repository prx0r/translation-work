## The parable (≈60s)

> Anyone can come in, but the order can't be shuffled: first the
> fast, then the worship, then the telling — led in, and the vow
> spoken so the water takes it, kept; then the bath, the
> purifying, the blindfold. What was bound unwinds in this order,
> never another. And whoever holds their vows stands where the
> Vaiṣṇavas end and the southern tantras begin — even the highest
> serving, if the vow holds. The river does not forget what was
> cast into it; the order does not let you skip a step; the
> blindfold comes last because it is the only way to see.

## The seal — statement

**The vow in the water:** bottom: a river-bend at a stone step;
a small figure kneeling, hands open over the water, a spoken
vow drawn as a gold thread dropping from her mouth INTO the
stream (the vow entering, kept — thread visible under the surface
as it runs downstream, glowing); along the bank above her, the
eight ordered marks of the rite laid as a rising path of small
stone-steps: fast-loom (empty plate), worship-flame, telling-
open-mouth, led-hand, water-vow (where the thread goes in),
bath-ripple, purify-white-fold, and last a BLINDFOLD folded on
the top step (the fold laid there waiting); crown: the boundary
line where two banks meet — one bank lamp-marked (Vaiṣṇava
side), one bank dark-southern, and the figure's held-vow thread
crossing both (the edge-holder). One figure, one thread, eight
steps.

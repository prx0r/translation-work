## The parable (≈60s)

> In the postal house two letters went out the same evening. One
> wore every mark: stamps, frankings, prior consecrations, a
> seal on every corner — and it went nowhere, because it did not
> know where it was going. The other had no mark at all, not
> even a stamp — and it arrived, because it knew. Marks or
> markless, the rule is one: knowing alone entitles. The keeper
> of the postal house stamped nothing that day; he only watched
> which letter knew its address. Teacher or practitioner, he
> consecrated none — the knowing was the consecration.

## The seal — statement

**The letter that needed no stamp:** bottom: a postal house at
night — a counter with two letters on it: left letter COVERED
in stamps, frankings, corner-seals (every mark), its envelope
closed wrong (address unknown — drawn as a blank return-corner);
right letter plain, unmarked, unsealed, and from it a soft gold
light runs in a straight line OUT of the frame toward a distant
door (it knows its way — the light IS the knowing, not a mark);
mid: the keeper behind the counter, hand raised holding his
stamper DOWN (not stamping — the tool lowered = marks refused);
crown: the plain letter arrived — a small glowing rectangle
sitting on a doorstep at the far end of the light-line, door
open (the impossible arrival made as the seal's closure). One
keeper, two letters, one light-line.

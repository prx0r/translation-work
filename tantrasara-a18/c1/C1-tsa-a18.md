# C1 — tsa-a18

## SUMMARY

Consecrate the practiced knower as practitioner or teacher: knowing alone entitles; marks or prior consecration do not. Delegation ends the duty to initiate; before it, omission binds. The consecrated earns initiation-rights through six months of daily vidyāvrata for mantra-deity identity; then, made of That, examines strictly — tests the unfit out, never shuns the fit, tests even the taught, overlooks known deceit, and worships by consecration-splendor.

## FUNCTION

Sets who may be consecrated, when the duty to initiate ends, and how the consecrated prove and examine.

## KEY TERMS

svabhyasta (well-practised as the only mark); adhikāra (entitlement as knowing, not office); abhiṣeka (consecration as handing-over); vidyāvrata (six-month knowledge-vow); tanmayībhāva (made-of-That as initiation-right); parīkṣā/upekṣā (testing and overlooking as the two disciplines).

## LOCAL CONTEXT

First āhnika of the abhiṣeka sequence: entitlement stated, delegation defined, then the vow and the examinations.

## EXPLANATION

The āhnika answers three questions. Who may be consecrated: the well-practised knower, as practitioner or teacher — knowing alone entitles, with or without marks, and no one else, even already consecrated. When the duty to initiate ends: once the guru has handed over his own authority, non-initiating incurs no fault; before that handover, the very omission is bondage, through fault, authority-bond, and the Vidyeśa-state-giver. How the consecrated proceed: six months of daily muttering, fire-offering, and special worship as knowledge-vow for mantra-deity identity; then, made of That, entitled to initiate — testing the unfit out, never shunning the fit, testing even the initiated in knowledge-giving, overlooking known deceit-borne knowledge, worshipping gods by consecration-splendor.

## BOUNDARY

No rite is described in full; only who, when, and how examined.

## RELATED

Pradipaka ch.18 translation (same text, free rendering — support only); Vishvasa ch.18 (same); Wallis + Ferrario on abhiṣeka/adhikāra (guru-making context).

## ESSENCE

1. Consecrate the practiced knower as practitioner or teacher: knowing alone entitles; marks or prior consecration do not.
2. Delegation ends the duty to initiate; before it, omission binds. The consecrated earns initiation-rights through vidyāvrata, then examines strictly and overlooks knowingly.

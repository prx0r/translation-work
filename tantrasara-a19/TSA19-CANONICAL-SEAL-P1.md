## The parable (≈60s)

> The keeper of the last door does not care which lamp you
> carried. Vaiṣṇava or southern, highest or lowest, whoever held
> any flame comes to his door — but only after their own lamp
> goes out. Then the raising comes down: the power descends to
> lift what has fallen, and the final sacrament is given at once,
> to the dead of any doctrine, with no other tool but the
> descent. The lamps are for the living and their doctrines are
> for the road; the door is for everyone, and it opens in the
> dark, because that is the only time the raising can be seen:
> the descent that raises, the lift that comes down.

## The seal — statement

**The door after the lamp goes out:** bottom: a road at night
with a row of small lamps — each lamp a different tint (the
doctrines: gold, crimson, blue, white — any flame, any road),
most lamps still lit, one lamp at the front of the row JUST
GONE OUT (its wisp of smoke rising, its bearer a small figure
standing where the light was, facing the dark); mid: from the
sky a single DESCENDING column of pale light reaches DOWN toward
the dead-lamp figure and touches their shoulders (the raising
that comes down — drawn as a falling column that lifts: their
feet already off the ground an inch, rising as it descends) ·
crown: the last door — a simple arch, open, warm light through,
waiting in the dark (the final-sacrament's threshold; letters
banned). One figure, one descending light, one open door.

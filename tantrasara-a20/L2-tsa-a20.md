# Tantrasara-a20 — L2 READ


## v.1
There — the initiation for the perfection of refinement for those fit for knowledge, and the with-seed release-initiation for those incapable of it: once it is performed, the guru should teach the lifelong remaining course.
The Sanskrit: *tatra yā dīkṣā saṃskārasiddhyai jñānayogyān prati yā ca tadaśaktān prati mokṣadīkṣā sabījā tasyāṃ kṛtāyām ājīvaṃ śeṣavartanaṃ guruḥ upadiśet*

## v.2
Here the remaining course is threefold — daily, occasional, and wished-for. The last belongs to the practitioner alone; it is not settled here.
The Sanskrit: *tatra nityaṃ naimittikaṃ kāmyam iti trividhaṃ śeṣavartanam antyaṃ ca sādhakasyaiva tat na iha niścetavyam*

## v.3
Here what occurs regularly is the daily; identity-with-that alone is the occasional; serving it are twilight worship, daily practice, festival day, sacred thread, and the rest.
The Sanskrit: *tatra niyatabhavaṃ nityaṃ tanmayībhāva eva naimittikaṃ tadupayogi saṃdhyopāsanaṃ pratyaham anuṣṭhānaṃ parvadinaṃ pavitrakam ityādi*

## v.4
That too is daily, by the fixedness of its own time — say some.
The Sanskrit: *tad api nityaṃ svakālanaiyatyāt iti kecit*

## v.5
But the occasional, even for those who abide in his teaching, is irregular — namely the coming of the guru and his circle, their festival day, the day of gaining knowledge, and the rest — say some.
The Sanskrit: *naimittikaṃ tu tacchāsanasthānām api aniyatam tadyathā gurutadvargāgamanaṃ tatparvadinaṃ jñānalābhadinam ityādikam iti kecit*

## v.6
Here fixed worship, twilight worship, guru worship, festival worship, and sacred thread — unfailing, all.
The Sanskrit: *tatra niyatapūjā saṃdhyopāsā gurupūjā parvapūjā pavitrakam iti avaśyaṃbhāvi*

## v.7
Occasional are the gaining of knowledge, the gaining of scripture, the coming to the house of the guru's circle, their days of birth-refinement and departure, worldly festival, exposition of scripture, beginning, middle, and end, the seeing of deities, gathering, dream command, and the gaining of release from the rule — this occasional is the cause of special worship.
The Sanskrit: *naimittikam jñānalābhaḥ śāstralābho gurutadvargagṛhāgamanaṃ tadīyajanmasaṃskāraprāyaṇadināni laukikotsavaḥ śāstravyākhyā ādimadhyāntā devatādarśanaṃ melakaṃ svapnājñā samayaniṣkṛtilābhaḥ ity etat naimittikaṃ viśeṣārcanakāraṇam*

## v.8
Here, to the initiated disciple one should transmit by mouth-lineage alone the chief mantra — potent, its essence the flashing-forth of consciousness, unwritten. Then, to perfect identity-with-that, the disciple should practice identity at the twilights; through that door, at all times, to perfect the gaining of such refinement; and daily one should worship the Supreme Lord on the altar or on the liṅga.
The Sanskrit: *tatra kṛtadīkṣākasya śiṣyasya pradhānaṃ mantraṃ savīryakaṃ saṃvittisphuraṇasāram alikhitaṃ vaktrāgamenaiva arpayet tataḥ tanmayībhāvasiddhyarthaṃ sa śiṣyaḥ saṃdhyāsu tanmayībhāvābhyāsaṃ kuryāt taddvāreṇa sarvakālaṃ tathāvidhasaṃskāralābhasiddhyarthaṃ pratyahaṃ ca parameśvaraṃ ca sthaṇḍile vā liṅge vā abhyarcayet*

## v.9
Here, on an altar delightful to the heart, meditated upon like a stainless mirror — having seen one's very form, non-different from the worshippable wheel of deities, as it were mirrored in a body — one should worship with heart-delighting flowers, scents, wine, libations, food-offerings, smoke, lamps, gifts, praise, song, music, dance, and the rest; murmur; chant praise — to obtain doubt-free identity-with-that.
The Sanskrit: *tatra hṛdye sthaṇḍile vimalamakuravad dhyāte svam eva rūpaṃ yājyadevatācakrābhinnaṃ mūrtibimbitam iva dṛṣṭvā hṛdyapuṣpagandhāsavatarpaṇanaivedyadhūpadīpopahārastutigītavādyanṛttādinā pūjayet japet stuvīta tanmayībhāvam aśaṅkitaṃ labdhum*

## v.10
For in a mirror, for one gazing ceaselessly at one's own face, certainty of its nature comes swiftly. And here no sequence is chief — apart from identity-with-that.
The Sanskrit: *ādarśe hi svamukham aviratam avalokayataḥ tatsvarūpaniścitiḥ acireṇaiva bhavet na cātra kaścit kramaḥ pradhānam ṛte tanmayībhāvāt*

## v.11
Entered into identity with the supreme mantra, his beast-latency stain wiped away, his whole snare-net of bonds melted by the penetration of devotion's taste — whatever heart dwells in that is highest to be taken up: so say our gurus.
The Sanskrit: *paramantratanmayībhāvāviṣṭasya nivṛttapaśuvāsanākalaṅkasya bhaktirasānuvedhavidrutasamastapāśajālasya yat adhivasati hṛdayaṃ tad eva paramam upādeyam iti asmadguravaḥ*

## v.12
Having lain down upon it, the ultimate-minded flashes forth the flood of being as light — which —
The Sanskrit: *adhiśayya pāramārthikaḥ bhāvaprasaraṃ prakāśam ullasati yā*

## v.13
Supreme-nectar-vision-ness: by her they are worshipped — the knowers of the secret.
The Sanskrit: *paramāmṛtadṛktvaṃ tayārcayante rahasyavidaḥ*

## v.14
Having prepared the ground, sprinkled in the moment with the unction of wonder-taste — with taken flowers, mentally, own-being's blossoms exuding their own perfume.
The Sanskrit: *kṛtvādhāradharāṃ camatkṛtirasaprokṣākṣaṇakṣālitām āttair mānasataḥ svabhāvakusumaiḥ svāmodasaṃdohibhiḥ*

## v.15
From the course of the priceless cup of my heart, brimmed with bliss's nectar — you, together with the Goddess, in the body, God's temple — I worship day and night.
The Sanskrit: *ānandāmṛtanirbharasvahṛdayānarghārghapātrakramāt tvāṃ devyā saha dehadevasadane devārcaye 'harniśam*

## v.16
Contemplating within the sense stated in the two verses, one should contemplate the wheel of deities.
The Sanskrit: *iti ślokadvayoktam artham antar bhāvayan devatācakraṃ bhāvayet*

## v.17
Then the showing of seals, murmuring, and the offering of that.
The Sanskrit: *tato mudrāpradarśanaṃ japaḥ tannivedanam*

## v.18
Dismissal through oneness with the one to be awakened.
The Sanskrit: *bodhyaikātmyena visarjanam*

## v.19
The chief food-offering one should eat oneself, or cast it all into water — for the water-born living are previously initiated, through the oblation-food: so say the scripture-knowers.
The Sanskrit: *mukhyaṃ naivedyaṃ svayam aśnīyāt sarvaṃ vā jale kṣipet jalajā hi prāṇinaḥ pūrvadīkṣitāḥ carubhojanadvāreṇa iti āgamavidaḥ*

## v.20
But where cats, mice, dogs, and the rest are eaten, doubt is born — toward hell. Even the knower, from the wish to grace the world, should not do such a thing; or, leaving the world behind, one should abide. Thus is the altar-sacrifice.
The Sanskrit: *mārjāramūṣakaśvādibhakṣaṇe tu śaṅkā janitā nirayāya iti jñānī api lokānugrahecchayā na tādṛk kuryāt lokaṃ vā parityajya āsīta iti sthaṇḍilayāgaḥ*

## v.21
Now on the liṅga: there one should not install the liṅga with secret mantras — especially not the manifest. On those installed before, one should perform worship in the order of invocation and dismissal, taking the installed as support.
The Sanskrit: *atha liṅge tatra na rahasyamantraiḥ liṅgaṃ pratiṣṭhāpayet viśeṣāt vyaktam iti pūrvapratiṣṭhiteṣu āvāhanavisarjanakrameṇa pūjāṃ kuryāt ādhāratayā*

## v.22
There — guru's body, one's own body, the Śakti's body, the book of secret scripture, the hero's cup, the rosary, the weapon, the arrow-thing, the pearl, the golden, the thing made of flowers, scents, substances, and heart-pleasing objects, or the mirror — one should worship as the liṅga.
The Sanskrit: *tatra gurudehaṃ svadehaṃ śaktidehaṃ rahasyaśāstrapustakaṃ vīrapātram akṣasūtraṃ prāharaṇaṃ bāṇīyaṃ mauktikaṃ sauvarṇaṃ puṣpagandhadravyādihṛdyavastukṛtaṃ makuraṃ vā liṅgam arcayet*

## v.23
There — by the power of the support alone comes ever-greater mantra-success: thus the earlier is ever chief, since mantras follow the support's qualities; and there, in each goal to be won, each is chief: so say the scripture-gurus.
The Sanskrit: *tatra ca ādhārabalād eva adhikādhikamantrasiddhiḥ bhavati iti pūrvaṃ pūrvaṃ pradhānam ādhāraguṇānuvidhāyitvāt ca mantrāṇāṃ tatra tatra sādhye tattatpradhānam iti śāstraguravaḥ*

## v.24
Everywhere, the pride of non-difference from the Supreme Lord alone is the highest refinement.
The Sanskrit: *sarvatra parameśvarābhedābhimāna eva paramaḥ saṃskāraḥ*

## v.25
Now the festival rule.
The Sanskrit: *atha parvavidhiḥ*

## v.26
Here general, general-general, general-special, special-general, special, and special-special: thus sixfold is festival, from the completion of the rite.
The Sanskrit: *tatra sāmānyaṃ sāmānyasāmānyaṃ sāmānyaviśeṣo viśeṣasāmānyaṃ viśeṣo viśeṣaviśeṣaś ca iti ṣoḍhā parva*

## v.27
From the completion of the rite.
The Sanskrit: *pūraṇāt vidheḥ*

## v.28
Here in every month the first and fifth days are general; the fourth, eighth, ninth, fourteenth, and fifteenth of both fortnights are general-general; on both of these too, in the fitting planet-and-star conjunctions on each stated lunar day, general-special. The first night-quarter of Mārgaśīrṣa, on the dark ninth; midnight of Pauṣa, on the dark ninth; midnight of Māgha, on the bright fifteenth; midday of Phālguna, on the bright twelfth; of Caitra, on the bright thirteenth; of Vaiśākha, on the dark eighth; of Jyaiṣṭha, on the dark ninth; on the first day of Āṣāḍha; the fore-part of Śrāvaṇa's day, on the dark eleventh; midday of Bhādrapada, on the bright sixth; the bright-ninth day of Āśvayuja; the first night-quarter of Kārttika, on the bright ninth: thus the special festival.
The Sanskrit: *tatra māsi māsi prathamaṃ pañcamaṃ dinaṃ sāmānyam caturthāṣṭamanavamacaturdaśapañcadaśāni dvayor api pakṣayoḥ sāmānyasāmānyam anayor ubhayor api rāśyoḥ vakṣyamāṇatattattithyucitagrahanakṣatrayoge sāmānyaviśeṣaḥ mārgaśīrṣasya prathamarātribhāgaḥ kṛṣṇanavamyām pauṣasya tu rātrimadhyaṃ kṛṣṇanavamyām māghasya rātrimadhyaṃ śuklapañcadaśyām phālgunasya dinamadhyaṃ śukladvādaśyām caitrasya śuklatrayodaśyām vaiśākhasya kṛṣṇāṣṭamyām jyaiṣṭhasya kṛṣṇanavamyām āṣāḍhasya prathame dine śrāvaṇasya divasapūrvabhāgaḥ kṛṣṇaikādaśyām bhādrapadasya dinamadhyaṃ śuklaṣaṣṭyām āśvayujasya śuklanavamīdinam kārttikasya prathamo rātribhāgaḥ śuklanavamyām iti viśeṣaparva*

## v.29
Citrā with the moon, Maghā with Jupiter, Tiṣya with the moon, Pūrva-phalgunī with Mercury, Śravaṇa with Mercury, Śatabhiṣak with the moon, Diti's two, Rohiṇī with Venus, Viśākhā with Jupiter, Śravaṇa with the moon: thus.
The Sanskrit: *citrācandrau maghājīvau tiṣyacandrau pūrvaphālgunībudhau śravaṇabudhau śatabhiṣakcandrau dityau rohiṇīśukrau viśākhābṛhaspatī śravaṇacandrau iti*

## v.30
If in Mārgaśīrṣa-first order they come in due number, excluding Āśvayuja — then special-special.
The Sanskrit: *yadi mārgaśīrṣādikrameṇa yathāsaṃkhyaṃ bhavati āśvayujaṃ varjayitvā tadā viśeṣaviśeṣaḥ*

## v.31
If another special falls on another festival, then that is an after-festival, they say.
The Sanskrit: *anyaviśeṣaś cet anyaparvaṇi tadā tat anuparva ity āhuḥ*

## v.32
And in star-and-planet conjunction, the hour is not chief.
The Sanskrit: *bhagrahayoge ca na velā pradhānam*

## v.33
The lunar day alone — from special gain; but continuity of after-rite time on festival day is chief. By the primacy of the after-rite: of festival rites, after-rite, image-rite, and wheel-rite are synonyms.
The Sanskrit: *tither eva viśeṣalābhāt anuyāgakālānuvṛttis tu parvadine mukhyā anuyāgaprādhānyāt parvayāgānām anuyāgo mūrtiyāgaḥ cakrayāgaḥ iti paryāyāḥ*

## v.34
Here the guru, his circle with offspring, the knower of reality, the maiden, the outcast woman, the courtesan, the bleeding woman, or the truth-knowing woman: in the wheel-rite the chief to be worshipped — especially all together.
The Sanskrit: *tatra guruḥ tadvargyaḥ sasaṃtānaḥ tattvavit kanyā antyā veśyā aruṇā tattvavedinī vā iti cakrayāge mukhyapūjyāḥ viśeṣāt sāmastyena*

## v.35
Here in the middle the guru, in his covering-order — from the guru down to the bound ones, hero and power in turn: thus seated in wheel or in row, then with scents, smoke, flowers, and the rest in order one should worship. Then, having contemplated the vessel as Sadāśiva-formed, having filled it with wine meditated as Śakti-nectar, having worshipped there the enjoyer-power as Śiva, having thereby sated the wheel of deities, having contemplated the meeting of the triad, man, power, and Śiva in nature, in the order of the descent of coverings — with primacy of release and enjoyment, without and within — one should perform sating; then in the order of re-circulation: thus the complete circling nourishes the wheel.
The Sanskrit: *tatra madhye guruḥ tadāvaraṇakrameṇa gurvādisamayyantaṃ vīraḥ śaktiḥ iti krameṇa ity evaṃ cakrasthityā vā paṅktisthityā vā āsīta tato gandhadhūpapuṣpādibhiḥ krameṇa pūjayet tataḥ pātraṃ sadāśivarūpaṃ dhyātvā śaktyamṛtadhyātena āsavena pūrayitvā tatra bhoktrīṃ śaktiṃ śivatayā pūjayitvā tayaiva devatācakratarpaṇaṃ kṛtvā naraśaktiśivātmakatritayamelakaṃ dhyātvā āvaraṇāvataraṇakrameṇa mokṣabhogaprādhānyaṃ bahir antaś ca tarpaṇaṃ kuryāt punaḥ pratisaṃcaraṇakrameṇa evaṃ pūrṇaṃ bhramaṇaṃ cakraṃ puṣṇāti*

## v.36
Here, having placed the universe-made vessel on the base, having sated the wheel of deities, one should sate oneself with the saluted vessel. If no vessel: auspicious folded shell-hands; or with the right hand shaped as a vessel — auspicious — with the two hands, the right laid above, made jointless, folded shell-hands; with the falling drops the vetālas and guhyakas are pleased, by the flow Bhairava. Here entry is to be given to no one; if by mistake one has entered, one should make no doubt; having made up one's mind, one should perform the wheel-rite twice over. Then fried foods, meals, and the rest should be scattered freely before them, as desired — in a hidden house or, without code-names, one should join all by the word 'deity': thus is the hero-mixture rite.
The Sanskrit: *tatra ādhāre viśvamayaṃ pātraṃ sthāpayitvā devatācakraṃ tarpayitvā svātmānaṃ vanditena tena tarpayet pātrābhāve bhadraṃ vellitaśuktiḥ vā dakṣahastena pātrākāraṃ bhadraṃ dvābhyām uparigatadakṣiṇābhyāṃ niḥsaṃdhīkṛtābhyām vellitaśuktiḥ patadbhiḥ bindubhiḥ vetālaguhyakāḥ saṃtuṣyanti dhārayā bhairavaḥ atra praveśo na kasyacit deyaḥ pramādāt praviṣṭasya vicāraṃ na kuryāt kṛtvā punar dviguṇaṃ cakrayāgaṃ kuryāt tato 'vadaṃśān bhojanādīn ca agre yatheṣṭaṃ vikīryeta guptagṛhe vā saṃketābhidhānavarjaṃ devatāśabdena sarvān yojayet iti vīrasaṃkarayāgaḥ*

## v.37
Then at the end with gifts, betel, clothes, and the rest one should satisfy them: thus this is the most excellent image-rite.
The Sanskrit: *tato 'nte dakṣiṇātāmbūlavastrādibhiḥ tarpayet iti pradhānatamo 'yaṃ mūrtiyāgaḥ*

## v.38
Even the unseen-maṇḍala one, by the image-rite worshipping the festival days, within a year alone gains the fruit stated for putrakas — without twilight practice and the rest. Thus for the old, the enjoyers, and women is this rule: when power has descended, to be taught by the guru.
The Sanskrit: *adṛṣṭamaṇḍalo 'pi mūrtiyāgena parvadināni pūjayan varṣād eva putrakoktaṃ phalam eti vinā saṃdhyānuṣṭhānādibhiḥ iti vṛddhānāṃ bhogināṃ strīṇāṃ vidhir ayam śaktipāte sati upadeṣṭavyo guruṇā*

## v.39
Now the sacred-thread rule.
The Sanskrit: *atha pavitrakavidhiḥ*

## v.40
And it, in the Ratnamālā, the Triśiromata, the Siddhāmata, and the rest, is preceded by rule and fulfills the Lord's command; and this is said in the Tantrāloka: without the sacred thread, all is fruitless.
The Sanskrit: *sa ca śrīratnamālātriśiromataśrīsiddhāmatādau vidhipūrvakaḥ pārameśvarājñāpūrakaś ca uktaṃ caitat śrītantrāloke vinā pavitrakeṇa sarvaṃ niṣphalam iti*

## v.41
Here, from bright Āṣāḍha up to the Kula-full-moon day's end, the sacred thread is to be done; Kārttika's dark fifteenth fulfills the Kula-wheel and the daily wheel: so say the knowers of the Nitya-tantra.
The Sanskrit: *tatra āṣāḍhaśuklāt kulapūrṇimādināntaṃ kāryaṃ pavitrakam tatra kārttikakṛṣṇapañcadaśī kulacakraṃ nityācakraṃ pūrayati iti śrīnityātantravidaḥ*

## v.42
Māgha's bright fifteenth: so say the knowers of Bhairavakulormi.
The Sanskrit: *māghaśuklapañcadaśī iti śrībhairavakulormividaḥ*

## v.43
The southern-course-end fifteenth: so say the knowers of the Tantrasadbhāva.
The Sanskrit: *dakṣiṇāyanāntapañcadaśī iti śrītantrasadbhāvavidaḥ*

## v.44
Here, according to one's means, having worshipped the God, having sated with oblation, one should give the sacred thread: from gold-, pearl-, and jewel-made beginning, one should make it even down to cloth, thread, cotton, and kuśa-grass-wombed.
The Sanskrit: *tatra vibhavena devaṃ pūjayitvā āhutyā tarpayitvā pavitrakaṃ dadyāt sauvarṇamuktāratnaviracitāt prabhṛti paṭasūtrakārpāsakuśagarbhāntam api kuryāt*

## v.45
And that with tattva-number knots, and with pada, kalā, world, letter, and mantra-number knots: one knee-ending, another navel-ending, another throat-ending, another on the head — thus four sacred threads to the God and to the guru, contemplating forms completely full of all paths, one should give; to the rest, one.
The Sanskrit: *tac ca tattvasaṃkhyagranthikaṃ padakalābhuvanavarṇamantrasaṃkhyagranthi ca jānvantam ekaṃ nābhyantam aparaṃ kaṇṭhāntam anyat śirasi anyat iti catvāri pavitrakāṇi devāya gurave ca samastādhvaparipūrṇatadrūpabhāvanena dadyāt śeṣebhya ekam iti*

## v.46
Then a great festival is to be held — the four-month rite, seven days and three days: thus chief and fallback courses. When there are means, monthly the sacred thread, or in the four months, or once. In not doing it, one should murmur expiation. Even the knower, even possessed of means, incurs by not doing; in greed-covered non-doing of knowledge, from the disgrace of knowledge.
The Sanskrit: *tato mahotsavaḥ kāryaḥ cāturmāsyaṃ saptadinaṃ tridinaṃ ca iti mukhyānvāpatkalpāḥ sati vibhave māsi māsi pavitrakam atha vā caturṣu māseṣu atha vā sakṛt tadakaraṇe prāyaścittaṃ japet jñānī api sambhavadvitto 'pi akaraṇe pratyavaiti lobhopahitajñānākaraṇe jñānanindāpatteḥ*

## v.47
When, even having attained wisdom, the Lord's teaching is defiled — then one needs expiation.
The Sanskrit: *yadā prāpyāpi vijñānaṃ dūṣitaṃ parameśaśāsanaṃ tadā prāyaścittī*

## v.48
Thus, by the statement.
The Sanskrit: *iti vacanāt*

## v.49
Thus: this is the sacred-thread rule.
The Sanskrit: *ity eṣa pavitrakavidhiḥ*

## v.50
At knowledge-gaining and the rest, at the end of worldly festival too — everywhere, excess of consciousness-blaze and the nearness of the deity-wheel especially come about: thus, considering such excess, just such a distinction, in after-rite and the rest, one should perform.
The Sanskrit: *jñānalābhādau laukikotsavānte 'pi sarvatra saṃvidullāsādhikyaṃ devatācakrasaṃnidhiḥ viśeṣato bhavati iti tathāvidhādhikyaparyālocanayā tathāvidham eva viśeṣam anuyāgādau kuryāt*

## v.51
Now the exposition rule.
The Sanskrit: *atha vyākhyāvidhiḥ*

## v.52
One should request the guru, complete in all scriptures, for the sake of exposition — he too, for his own disciple or even another's, should expound the scripture fit for fit refinement; even for one abiding in a lower teaching, from compassion, his heart made hopeful by the prospect of a descent of power manifested by the Lord's varied will, one should expound — excluding secret instruction.
The Sanskrit: *sarvaśāstrasampūrṇaṃ guruṃ vyākhyārtham abhyarthayeta so 'pi svaśiṣyāya paraśiṣyāyāpi vā samucitasaṃskārocitaṃ śāstraṃ vyācakṣīta adharaśāsanasthāyāpi karuṇāvaśāt īśvarecchāvaicitryodbhāvitaśaktipātasambhāvanābhāvitahṛdayo vyācakṣīta marmopadeśavarjam*

## v.53
There, exposition performed for the devoted, seated low, with speech, mind, and body restrained, becomes fruitful. First, on the ground smeared with perfumes and the rest, having drawn or resolved: a lotus-base, four-cornered, a lotus-triad, in the middle of the lotus Vāgīśī, on left and right Gaṇapati and the guru — one should worship; on the base-lotus, the wish-granting deity of exposition.
The Sanskrit: *tatra nimnāsanasthitebhyaḥ tatparebhyo niyamitavāṅmanaḥkāyebhyo vyākhyā kriyamāṇā phalavatī bhavati prathamaṃ gandhādiliptāyāṃ bhuvi ullikhya saṃkalpya vā padmādhāraṃ caturaśraṃ padmatrayaṃ padmamadhye vāgīśīṃ vāmadakṣiṇayoḥ gaṇapatigurū ca pūjayet ādhārapadme vyākhyeyakalpadevatām*

## v.54
Then, having sated the wheel with the common offering-vessel rite, one should expound: the text of aphorism, sentence, and chapter, uncontradicted before and after — first joining the position rightly, and rightly refuting; proving the provandum, revealing the drift — one should expound to the chapter's end, no more; even there, at each section's end, sating and worship — until the exposition is complete.
The Sanskrit: *tataḥ sāmānyārghapātrayogena cakraṃ tarpayet tato vyācakṣīta sūtravākyapaṭalagrantham pūrvāparāviruddhaṃ kurvan tantrāvṛttiprasaṅgasamuccayavikalpādiśāstranyāyaucityena pūrvaṃ pakṣaṃ samyak ghaṭayitvā samyak ca dūṣayan sādhyaṃ sādhayan tātparyavṛttiṃ pradarśayan paṭalāntaṃ vyācakṣīta nādhikam tatrāpi vastvante vastvante tarpaṇaṃ pūjanam iti yāvad vyākhyāsamāptiḥ*

## v.55
Then too, having worshipped, having dismissed the knowledge-seat, having smeared, one should cast it into the deep.
The Sanskrit: *tato 'pi pūjayitvā vidyāpīṭhaṃ visarjya upalipya agādhe tat kṣepayet*

## v.56
Thus the exposition rule.
The Sanskrit: *iti vyākhyāvidhiḥ*

## v.57
Now the rule-expiation.
The Sanskrit: *atha samayaniṣkṛtiḥ*

## v.58
Though for one established in the knowledge of reality there is no expiation and the rest at all, yet to grace those who partake of release through conduct alone, one should display the course of conduct.
The Sanskrit: *yady api tattvajñānaniṣṭhasya prāyaścittādi na kiṃcit tathāpi caryāmātrād eva mokṣabhāginaḥ tān anugrahītum ācāravartanīṃ darśayet*

## v.59
But the truth-unknowing one, whose enjoyment and release hang on conduct alone — if the rule is transgressed and, not performing expiation, he remains — for a hundred years he becomes an eater of raw flesh: thus the expiation rule is to be stated. There, in woman-slaying there is no expiation; elsewhere, having known strength and weakness, one should repeat the unbroken Goddess Mālinī from one time onward up to three hundred thousand, until doubt falls away. At its end, special worship; even there, the wheel-rite — for it everywhere remains.
The Sanskrit: *atattvajñānī tu caryaikāyattabhogamokṣaḥ samayollaṅghane kṛte prāyaścittam akurvan varṣaśataṃ kravyādo bhavatīti iti prāyaścittavidhiḥ vaktavyaḥ tatra strīvadhe prāyaścittaṃ nāsti anyatra tu balābalaṃ jñātvā akhaṇḍāṃ bhagavatīṃ mālinīm ekavārāt prabhṛti trilakṣāntam āvartayet yāvat śaṅkāvicyutiḥ bhavati tadante viśeṣapūjā tatrāpi cakrayāgaḥ sa hi sarvatra śeṣabhūtaḥ*

## v.60
Thus the rule-expiation rule.
The Sanskrit: *iti samayaniṣkṛtividhiḥ*

## v.61
Now the guru-worship rule.
The Sanskrit: *atha gurupūjāvidhiḥ*

## v.62
At the close of all rites, when the rite has been gathered in, on the next day one should perform guru-worship — for before, as a limb of the rite he is gratified, but not chiefly: thus, not performing it chiefly, bound by the bond of office, one remains bound.
The Sanskrit: *sarvayāgānteṣu upasaṃhṛte yāge aparedyuḥ gurupūjāṃ kuryāt pūrvaṃ hi sa vidhyaṅgatayā toṣito na tu prādhānyena iti tāṃ prādhānyena akurvan adhikārabandhena baddho bhavati*

## v.63
Thus one should perform it in every way.
The Sanskrit: *iti tāṃ sarvathā caret*

## v.64
There, having drawn a svāstika maṇḍala, there having given a golden seat, there having worshipped the entire path-course, having established that seat thereby, having performed worship to him, sating, meal, gift, and self — thus having offered, having begged the food-leavings, having bowed, having tasted oneself, one should perform wheel-worship.
The Sanskrit: *tatra svāstikaṃ maṇḍalaṃ kṛtvā tatra sauvarṇaṃ pīṭhaṃ dattvā tatra samastam adhvānaṃ pūjayitvā tatpīṭhaṃ tena adhiṣṭhāpya tasmai pūjāṃ kṛtvā tarpaṇaṃ bhojanaṃ dakṣiṇām ātmānam iti nivedya naivedyocchiṣṭaṃ prārthya vanditvā svayaṃ prāśya cakrapūjāṃ kuryāt*

## v.65
Thus? — the guru-worship rule.
The Sanskrit: *ita gurupūjāvidhiḥ*

# Tantrasara-a22 — L2 READ


## v.1
Now this entire worship is taught through the glorious Kaula method, for the fit ones mounted on firm resolve of exactly that awakened kind. On this point the Yogasaṃcāra and the rest declare: bliss is Brahman, seated in the body, established threefold at lips and lowest end.
The Sanskrit: *atha samastā iyam upāsā samunmiṣattādṛśadṛḍhavāsanārūḍhān adhikāriṇaḥ prati śrīmatkaulikaprakriyayā nirūpyate tatra uktaṃ yogasaṃcārādau ānandaṃ brahma taddehe tridhauṣṭyāntyavyavasthitam*

## v.2
Whoever abandons it, not abiding in Brahman, is cut off from bliss.
The Sanskrit: *abrahmacāriṇas tasya tyāgād ānandavarjitāḥ*

## v.3
And sacrificers of the wheel who feed on blissless, artificial food —
The Sanskrit: *ānandakṛtrimāhāravarjaṃ cakrasya yājakāḥ*

## v.4
both sink into dreadful hell. Therefore one should hold to this stance.
The Sanskrit: *dvaye 'pi narake ghore tasmād enāṃ sthitiṃ bhajet*

## v.5
So in this stance there is the Kula sacrifice, and it is sixfold: in the outer, in the Śakti, in one’s own body, in the couple, in breath, and in consciousness.
The Sanskrit: *tad anayā sthityā kulayāgaḥ sa ca ṣoḍhā bāhye śaktau svadehe yāmale prāṇe saṃvidi ca iti*

## v.6
Here each later one is higher than the last, and each earlier one exists to give a taste for what follows.
The Sanskrit: *tatra ca uttara uttara utkṛṣṭaḥ pūrvaḥ pūrvas tadrucyartham*

## v.7
For one who seeks powers, the second, the fourth, and the fifth are to be carried out in every way; for one who seeks release the sixth is chief — yet even he must perform the second and the rest on occasional rites as far as possible, and to complete the rite.
The Sanskrit: *siddhikāmasya dvitīyaturyapañcamāḥ sarvathā nirvartyāḥ ṣaṣṭhas tu mumukṣoḥ mukhyaḥ tasyāpi dvitīyādyā naimittike yathāsambhavam anuṣṭheyā eva vidhipūraṇārthaṃ ca*

## v.8
Among these, the outer consists of the bliss-full altar, the hero’s cup, a red cloth — or else the liṅga and the rest as mentioned before.
The Sanskrit: *tatra bāhyaṃ sthaṇḍilam ānandapūrṇaṃ vīrapātram aruṇaḥ paṭaḥ pūrvoktam api vā liṅgādi*

## v.9
There, with no need at all for bathing and the rest of the duties — purity being gained by resting in full bliss alone — one should first contemplate the oneness of breath, consciousness, and body, and then utter the mantra twenty-seven times over crown, face, heart, secret place, and body, in forward and reverse order, since consciousness itself has Paramaśiva’s form: the fullness of the universe’s course made complete in the Supreme Lord — in the lower, in the middle, and in the highest alike.
The Sanskrit: *tatra snānādikartavyānapekṣayaiva pūrṇānandaviśrāntyaiva labdhaśuddhiḥ prathamaṃ prāṇasaṃviddehaikībhāvaṃ bhāvayitvā saṃvidaś ca paramaśivarūpatvāt saptaviṃśativāraṃ mantram uccārya mūrdhavaktrahṛdguhyamūrtiṣu anulomavilomābhyāṃ viśvādhvaparipūrṇatā parameśvare aparatve parāparatve paratve 'pi ca*

## v.10
For so it is: from Māyā, person, nature, and intellect beginning, down to earth, there are twenty-seven tattvas — kalā and the rest included therein — and likewise, in the knowledge-power in the middle state, the qualities of the Brahman-pentad, beginning with instant-ness, born-ness, and become-arisen-ness, number just twenty-seven in form. So declare the glorious Lakulīśa masters.
The Sanskrit: *tathāhi māyāpuṃprakṛtiguṇadhīprabhṛti dharāntaṃ saptaviṃśatitattvāni kalādīnāṃ tatraiva antarbhāvāt vidyāśaktāv api parāparatve brahmapañcakasya sadyastvājātatvabhavodbhavatvādīnāṃ dharmāṇāṃ saptaviṃśatirūpatvam eva uktaṃ śrīmallakuleśādipādaiḥ*

## v.11
In the highest too the Lord is five-powered: to each power belong five forms, making twenty-five powers. Mutually undivided, they are a single power, still undivided — hence by a pervasion twenty-seven-formed one should bind the buddhi-and-breath-formed flame of consciousness-fire with a single utterance alone, so that Paramaśiva alone stands bound and it hastens toward nothing beyond the tantra. Thus penetrated, one should render the present offering-substance, dwelling, directions, and supports made of that very stuff; then one should fill the offering vessel by the tuft-binding pervasion alone and worship it, worship the altars with its drops, and — joining the left ring-finger with the thumb — worship and satisfy the mantra-wheel upon the body’s wheels with its essence. Then, up to breath; then fashion the trident-formed seat ending in the three powers upon the altar — up to Māyā, in the lettered auṃ-syllable fashion the seat ending in the three powers — up to Māyā in the lettered auṃ-syllable; and thereupon the power to be worshipped, deliberation-formed. Thus, by a single utterance alone, having effected the placing of support and supported, one should behold the universe right there — even in the consciousness that has become the supported — and that too as made of consciousness. So the universe through consciousness, and through that, her mutual enclosure comes about: arisen from consciousness, it comes to rest right there — for the universe rises from the knowable, and consciousness rests right there. Just this much is the consciousness-principle, attained through the twofold enclosure.
The Sanskrit: *paratve 'pi pañcaśaktiḥ hi parameśvaraḥ pratiśakti ca pañcarūpatā evaṃ pañcaviṃśatiḥ śaktayaḥ tāś ca anyonyam anudbhinnavibhāgā ity ekā śaktiḥ sā cānudbhinnavibhāgā ity evaṃ saptaviṃśatirūpayā vyāptyā saṃvidagneḥ śikhāṃ buddhiprāṇarūpāṃ sakṛduccāramātreṇaiva baddhāṃ kuryāt yena paramaśiva eva pratibaddhā tantrātiriktaṃ na kiṃcid abhidhāvati tathāvidhabuddhyadhiṣṭhitakaraṇacakrānuvedhena purovartino yāgadravyagṛhadigādhārādīn api tanmayībhūtān kuryāt tato 'rghapātram api śikhābandhavyāptyaiva pūrayet pūjayec ca tadvipruḍbhiḥ sthaṇḍilāny api tadrasena vāmānāmāṅguṣṭhayogāt dehacakreṣu mantracakraṃ pūjayet tarpayet ca tataḥ prāṇāntaḥ tataḥ sthaṇḍile triśūlātmakaṃ śaktitrayāntam āsanaṃ kalpayet māyāntaṃ hi sārṇe aukāre ca śaktitrayāntam āsanaṃ kalpayet māyāntaṃ hi sārṇe aukāre ca śaktitrayāntaṃ tadupari yājyā vimarśarūpā śaktiḥ ity evaṃ sakṛd uccāreṇaiva ādhārādheyanyāsaṃ kṛtvā tatraiva ādheyabhūtāyām api saṃvidi viśvaṃ paśyet tad api ca saṃvinmayam ity evaṃ viśvasya saṃvidā tena ca tasyāḥ saṃpuṭībhāvo bhavati saṃvida uditaṃ tatraiva paryavasitaṃ yato viśvaṃ vedyāc ca saṃvit udeti tatraiva ca viśrāmyati iti etāvattvaṃ saṃvittattvaṃ saṃpuṭībhāvadvayāt labhyate*

## v.12
So it has been said: having fully enclosed emission.
The Sanskrit: *tad uktam sṛṣṭiṃ tu saṃpuṭīkṛtya iti*

## v.13
Then, having offered incense, smoke, wine, flowers, and the rest, down to self-surrender, having murmured in one’s own rest and gathered it all in, one should cast it into water.
The Sanskrit: *tato gandhadhūpāsavakusumādīn ātmaprahvībhāvāntān arpayitvā svaviśrāntyā japtvā upasaṃhṛtya jale nikṣipet*

## v.14
Thus concludes the outer sacrifice.
The Sanskrit: *iti bāhyayāgaḥ*

## v.15
Now, in the Śakti. There — the goddesses and heroes of the Śakti-circle being mutually of both natures, from the awakening of Śiva and Śakti that runs from play's beginning to emission's end, through mutual activity, by the Supreme Lord's ordainment, in pure form — preeminence lies there. By this, even the distinguished wheel's Śakti-ness stands explained. There, worship is by the tuft-binding pervasion alone, upon the seat ending in the three powers; in the triangle, in the middle, the emission-power — but in the pervasion lies the distinction.
The Sanskrit: *atha śaktau tatra anyonyaṃ śaktitālāsāvīrāṇām ubhayeṣām ubhayātmakatvena prollāsaprārambhasṛṣṭyantaśivaśaktiprabodhe parasparaṃ vyāpārāt parameśaniyatyā ca śuddharūpatayā tatra prādhānyam etena ca viśiṣṭacakrasyāpi śaktitvaṃ vyākhyātam tatra śikhābandhavyāptyaiva pūjanaṃ śaktitrayāntam āsanaṃ koṇatraye madhye visargaśaktiḥ iti tu vyāptau viśeṣaḥ*

## v.16
So too in one's own body: in that very wheel there; then in the sub-wheels beginning with the crown-aperture.
The Sanskrit: *evaṃ svadehe tatraiva cakre tato brahmarandhrādyanucakreṣu*

## v.17
Now, in the couple. This is the mark of the Śakti: like that — non-difference. Then, disregarding age, birth, and the rest, out of non-attachment; for identity is born of the transgressive pair.
The Sanskrit: *atha yāmale śakter lakṣaṇam etat tadvad abhedas tato 'napekṣya vayaḥ jātyādīṃś cāsaṅgāt loketarayugalajaṃ hi tādātmyam*

## v.18
From being arisen together with cause and effect, threefold — directly, or else in arranged fashion — the two, having worshipped one another, draw bliss near by satisfying.
The Sanskrit: *kāryahetusahotthatvāt traidhaṃ sākṣād athānyathā kḷptāvato mitho 'bhyarcya tarpyānandāntikatvataḥ*

## v.19
One should worship the wheel according to its fitness, and likewise the sub-wheel that follows — outwardly with flowers and the rest, ending inward, and with perfumes, foods, wines, and the rest.
The Sanskrit: *cakram arcet tadaucityād anucakraṃ tathānugam bahiḥ puṣpādināntaś ca gandhabhuktyāsavādibhiḥ*

## v.20
Such is the state surging with the play of that bliss-mass. And the host of sub-wheels dissolves through identity with the wheel.
The Sanskrit: *evam ānandasaṃdohitatacceṣṭocchalatsthitiḥ anucakragaṇaś cakratādātmyād abhilīyate*

## v.21
In the relish of own-being at the time when each enjoyment unfolds, gradually the sub-wheel goddesses enter the middle consciousness-wheel.
The Sanskrit: *nijanijabhogābhogapravikāsamayasvarūpaparimarśe kramaśo 'nucakradevyaḥ saṃviccakraṃ hi madhyamaṃ yānti*

## v.22
That whose power is won by filling the host of rays embodied in the sub-wheel goddesses — having the nature of that power, like that — becomes mutually facing.
The Sanskrit: *anucakradevatātmakamarīcigaṇapūraṇādhigatavīryam tacchaktitadvadātmakam anyonyasamunmukhaṃ bhavati*

## v.23
The agitation born of the throbbing entry into the upper abode of that pair agitates the sub-wheels as well — and then they are made of that, not separate.
The Sanskrit: *tadyugalamūrdhvadhāmapraveśasaspandajātasaṃkṣobham kṣubhnāty anucakrāṇy api tāni tadā tanmayāni na pṛthak tu*

## v.24
Such is this couple, whose talk of difference has melted away: just when it is so, then, through gradation and excellence, she indeed is the collision of consciousness and emission.
The Sanskrit: *itthaṃ yāmalam etad galitabhidāsaṃkathaṃ yadaiva tadā kramatāratamyayogāt saiva hi saṃvidvisargasaṃghaṭṭaḥ*

## v.25
The steady supreme abode of that — the both-natured, vast world — bliss is neither the calm nor the arisen: the womb-cause of the calm and the arisen, the supreme Kaula.
The Sanskrit: *taddhruvadhāmānuttaram ubhayātmakajagad udāram ānandam no śāntaṃ nāpy uditaṃ śāntoditasūtikāraṇaṃ paraṃ kaulam*

## v.26
Desiring the undivided station, one should make her — consciousness — one's own; then the pair, calm-natured and arisen-natured, rises at once for power and for power-bearer.
The Sanskrit: *anavacchinnapadepsus tāṃ saṃvidam ātmasātkuryāt śāntoditātmakadvayam atha yugapad udeti śaktiśaktimatoḥ*

## v.27
From the interpenetration of self and other, when there is calm and other — of the two, through two-naturedness — power, though arisen like that, nourishes emission; its bearer does not.
The Sanskrit: *svātmānyonyāveśāt śāntānyatve dvayor dvayātmatvāt śaktis tu tadvad uditāṃ sṛṣṭiṃ puṣṇāti no tadvān*

## v.28
In her lies the excellent Kula. Then, through her, among men, come the taught collisions of yoga. Then — in the twofold emission, in this calm-arisen abode — those who contemplate —
The Sanskrit: *tasyāṃ cāryaṃ kulam atha tayā nṛṣu proktayogasaṃghaṭṭān atha sṛṣṭe dvitaye 'smin śāntoditadhāmni ye 'nusaṃdadhate*

## v.29
In the eastward one, mounted in the undivided station as emission-being — the arisen, too, is received mutually from mouth to mouth, from the chief mouth outward into the mouth.
The Sanskrit: *prācyāṃ visargasattām anavacchidite pade rūḍhāḥ uditaṃ ca mitho vaktrān mukhyād vaktre pragṛhyate ca bahiḥ*

## v.30
The goddess-wheel becomes satisfied, bestowing powers, knowledge, and release. In the practice of calm, one attains the calm Śiva — which here is the wheel of deities.
The Sanskrit: *tṛptaṃ devīcakraṃ siddhijñānāpavargadaṃ bhavati śāntābhyāse śāntaṃ śivam eti yad atra devatācakram*

## v.31
The void, devoid of bliss — from bliss's own abode the offering — and the humming-thrilled ones, through the taste of relishing outward states filled with one's own essence.
The Sanskrit: *śūnyaṃ nirānandamayaṃ nirvṛtinijadhāmato 'rghaṃ ca raṇaraṇakarasān nijarasabharitabahirbhāvacarvaṇarasena*

## v.32
The sub-wheel surging with inner fullness reaches the wheel; then that too surges as before. Thus this emission is threefold in its implied sense.
The Sanskrit: *āntarapūrṇasamucchaladanucakraṃ yāti cakram atha tad api ucchalati prāgvad iti trividho 'nvartho visargo 'yam*

## v.33
In this emission-abode, through relish, mantra-power — in just three ways — bears each fruit in each consciousness-womb.
The Sanskrit: *etad visargadhāmani parimarśanatas tridhaiva manuvīryam tattatsaṃvidgarbhe mantras tattatphalaṃ sūte*

## v.34
Upon the lotus that covers eternal, rising auspiciousness, resting within the triangle — the stalk never parted, rooted in the true sixteen-petalled lotus.
The Sanskrit: *koṇatrayāntarāśritanityoditamaṅgalacchade kamale nityāviyutaṃ nālaṃ ṣoḍaśadalakamalasanmūlam*

## v.35
In the fire at the course of rubbing the pair of lotuses strung upon the central stalk — from the collision, in the middle, of moon-beauty and the flood of the sun's rays.
The Sanskrit: *madhyasthanālagumphitasarojayugaghaṭṭanakramādagnau madhyasthaśaśadharasundaradinakarakaraughasaṃghaṭṭāt*

## v.36
From union with the three-petalled ruddy ray of power, in the middle — sprout-emission. Thus, by the seal of the wondrous gathering of moon and sun, in a flash.
The Sanskrit: *tridalāruṇavīryakalāsaṅgān madhye 'ṅkurasṛṣṭiḥ iti śaśadharavāsarapaticitraguṣaṃghaṭṭamudrayā jhaṭiti*

## v.37
Carrying out within the order that begins with emission, one gains steadiness in the fourth — this in the entry of the sky-going seal, mutually, of one's own power and power-bearer.
The Sanskrit: *sṛṣṭyādikramam antaḥ kurvaṃs turye sthitiṃ labhate etat khecaramudrāveśe 'nyonyaṃ svaśaktiśaktimatoḥ*

## v.38
Whoever would abide made of relish amid drinking, enjoyment, play, laughter, and the rest — with unmanifest sound, roar, outburst, hearing, tone, and tone's end.
The Sanskrit: *pānopabhogalīlāhāsādiṣu yo bhaved vimarśamayaḥ avyaktadhvanirāvasphoṭaśrutinādanādāntaiḥ*

## v.39
That mantra-power, with unbroken, unstruck ultimate realities — in the rests of going and coming, in the ear, in the eye, in contact with the double mark.
The Sanskrit: *avyucchinnānāhataparamārthair mantravīryaṃ tat gamanāgamaviśrāntiṣu karṇe nayane dvilakṣyasamparke*

## v.40
In the yoga of their closing, called the body's end — and in the couple, in the wheel — that which, throat-reaching from the heart-place between the breasts to the lip's end, is unmanifest.
The Sanskrit: *tatsammīlanayoge dehāntākhye ca yāmale cakre kucamadhyahṛdayadeśād oṣṭhānte kaṇṭhagaṃ yad avyaktam*

## v.41
Having heard that which lies between the two wheels, when agitation ends they come to rest there — and thus this is the supreme eightfold Nāda-Bhairava.
The Sanskrit: *tac cakradvayamadhyagam ākarṇya kṣobhavigamasamaye yat nirvānti tatra caivaṃ yo 'ṣṭavidho nādabhairavaḥ paramaḥ*

## v.42
Since she is light and sound, that mantra-bearing pervasion is called the highest: in every act, for the knower, there would be release while living.
The Sanskrit: *jyotir dhvaniś ca yasmāt sā māntrī vyāptir ucyate paramā karmaṇi karmaṇi viduṣaḥ syāj jīvato muktiḥ*

## v.43
Knowing that, liberated in scripture — a vessel of alien Kula wisdom is the womb — one should make the void-and-nonvoid abode, with fire and wind in the single staff.
The Sanskrit: *tajjñaḥ śāstre muktaḥ parakulavijñānabhājanaṃ garbhaḥ śūnyāśūnyālayaṃ kuryād ekadaṇḍe 'nalānilau*

## v.44
Having made the trident of one taste — essence abiding in essence, as it were — with doubt abandoned, riteless: I am not — thus contemplating.
The Sanskrit: *śūlaṃ samarasīkṛtya rase rasam iva sthitam tyaktāśaṅko nirācāro nāham asmīti bhāvayan*

## v.45
Beholding the goddesses dwelling in the body — joy, agitation, and the rest in the mass of consciousness — the host of goddesses stationed in the wheels of ear, eye, mouth, and nose.
The Sanskrit: *dehasthā devatāḥ paśyan hlādodvegādi ciddhane karṇākṣimukhanāsādicakrasthaṃ devatāgaṇam*

## v.47
Ever beholding the grasper — by Khecarī one succeeds, certainly.
The Sanskrit: *grahītāraṃ sadā paśyan khecaryā sidhyati dhruvam*

## v.48
In a far distant chasm, in a flash hurling one's own body — dwelling without violence, having drawn in both hands, gazing — one should practice this sky-going seal.
The Sanskrit: *śvabhre sudūre jhaṭiti svadehaṃ saṃpātayan vāsam asāhasena ākuñcya hastadvitayaṃ prapaśyan mudrām imāṃ vyomacarīṃ bhajeta*

## v.49
Thus this is the sacrifice of the couple.
The Sanskrit: *ity eṣa yāmalayāgaḥ*

## v.50
In the stated-pervaded breath, made of the universe — strengthening, by the taught pervasion of consciousness, through the offering of libation, food, perfume, incense, and the rest — that is breath-sacrifice.
The Sanskrit: *uktavyāptike prāṇe viśvamaye proktasaṃvidvyāptyā tarpaṇānnagandhadhūpādisamarpaṇena upodbalanaṃ prāṇayāgaḥ*

## v.51
But rest's establishment is consciousness-sacrifice — already taught before.
The Sanskrit: *viśrāntirūḍhis tu saṃvidyāgaḥ prāg eva nirūpitaḥ*

## v.52
Thus, having performed any one of these sacrifices — should the disciple become one whose heart is painted with such doubtlessness — then for him, preceded by the beholding of that sacrifice and needing no prior offering of sesame and ghee, one should perform initiation by a glance, in the order of contemplation through the aforesaid pervasion. But at the close of the occasional rite, for indirect initiation and the rest, the earlier rule alone holds.
The Sanskrit: *evam etebhyo yāgebhyo 'nyatamaṃ kṛtvā yadi tathāvidhanirvicikitsatāpacitritahṛdayaḥ śiṣyo bhavati tadā tasmai tadyāgadarśanapūrvakaṃ tilājyāhutipūrvakanirapekṣam eva pūrvoktavyāptyā anusaṃdhānakrameṇa avalokanayā dīkṣāṃ kuryāt parokṣadīkṣādike naimittikānte tu pūrva eva vidhiḥ*

## v.53
Merely by the primacy of sacrifice is this so.
The Sanskrit: *kevalam etad yāgapradhānatayā iti*

## v.54
In the guru's body lies the seventh Kula sacrifice, most excellent of all — and it too, performed but once before in the company of the sacrifices, fulfills everything. Śivam.
The Sanskrit: *guruśarīre saptamaḥ kulayāgaḥ sarvottamaḥ so 'pi prāg yāgasāhityena sakṛd eva kṛtaḥ sarvaṃ pūrayati iti śivam*

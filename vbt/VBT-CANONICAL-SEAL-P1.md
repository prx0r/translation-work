## The parable (≈90s)

> Devī keeps a bag of tricks, and the tricks are places. Put your
> mind on the ray, not the lamp: the lamp is known by what it
> lights. Put it between two sounds: the quiet that holds both
> doesn't close. Put it on the long tone that never splits; on the
> brow as a bridge you cross without moving; on the h at the
> middle of the tongue where it dissolves in calm; on the twelfth
> day when the strangeness drops away; on the breath just after
> it ends, where the eternal begins to show. She whirled her
> body till it stopped asking, and the supreme state was born of
> the stilling. She held the I-and-mine till it stopped claiming,
> and the mind went calm without support. Passion, looked at
> long enough, showed it never had a cause — reality just stayed
> there. And joy rose and dissolved like weather, and devotion
> overflowed so much it gave her mind away to Śiva. Every small
> place she stared into opened like a door. And when the question
> came — what worship pleases when there's no field to penetrate?
> — the Devī, fully content, held it out: today, the whole tantra
> in one hand. She closes the bag. It is full.

## The seal — statement

**The quiet between two sounds:** bottom: a dark slope of earth
with one small seated girl, brow low, attention-point fixed on
one tiny thing at her feet (the spot: nose-tip-class small — the
practice made as posture); from that spot upward runs a rising
path of small lit DOORS cut into the dark slope — each doorway a
different small opening with warm light through (the tricks as
places: one door half-open, one humming, one round like a tongue-
ring, one that is only a hush); the path's threads between doors
read as a strung rosary (the bag's string); crown: the Devī's
raised hand holds a FULL bag tied with a star — fully content —
and beside it a blank stamped seal (today's tantra, held, no
letters). One girl, one bag, many doors.

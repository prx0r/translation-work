# C1 — vbt-p14

## SUMMARY

Conviction completes the earlier denial: I-and-mine emptied, supportless mind falls calm; word-meditation timed to meaning aims at all-lord; the net-question resolved from firmness yields calm; self needing neither knowing nor action makes world void of both; bondage, freedom, terrors all reflections in water; senses left, self-abiding abides; one-nature lighting all gives single vision; the fourfold wasted yields Bhairava-form; a hundred-plus-twelve told in brief makes the knower-person; and yoked here one IS Bhairava whose speech performs curse and favor alike.

## FUNCTION

Clears the last metaphysical obstacles, then flips into sovereign identity — quiet as power.

## KEY TERMS

pratipatti (conviction as mechanism); śabda-abhyāsa (word-meditation); prati-bimba (reflection-model of bondage); caturka (the fourfold); sāmānya-saṃvid (one-nature lighting).

## LOCAL CONTEXT

Closes the teaching-section; the fruit-praise and japa-counts follow (p15).

## EXPLANATION

Three sūtra-pairs do final demolition: conviction removes the last holding ('I-mine-this'), word-meditation converts speech into aimed aim at the all-lord, and the net-question dissolves into firmness-given calm. Then the old question of agency returns — self needs no knowing, no action, so world-possessions are void — and bondage, freedom and terrors are all dismissed as reflections (sun in water). With the senses left, only self-abiding remains; one-nature lights knowing and known into a single vision; the fourfold (manas, consciousness, power, self) wasted gives Bhairava-form. The count-vows: hundred-plus-twelve in brief for the knower. And the turn: yoked here, one IS Bhairava — the text's highest authority — whose speech now performs curses and favors without remainder.

## BOUNDARY

The hundred-plus-twelve count is recension-sensitive (flagged in argmap) — never used to force dhāraṇā numbering.

## RELATED

Count (100+12) vs Hareesh's 116 practice-verses framing — flagged, not reconciled; sovereign-speech = sāmraṭ-bhāva, cf. Śivasūtra sovereignty themes.

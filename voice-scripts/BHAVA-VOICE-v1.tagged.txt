[calm] [warm] [slowly] A man bows in a land of desert-heat and stays bowed. Because he stays bowed, everything that passes belongs to him — all of it his, none of it else's.

[sad] [soft voice] When suffering passes he does not lift his head, so there is no fear-talk to hear. [pause] When Death comes to glare, he glares at a bowed crown — [pause] the gaze slides off, finds no face.

[cold] [low voice] The four terrors meet his one salutation and go out like lamps.

[gently] And because he never rises from the bow, he begins to see: the world shows its true face, [pause] and bliss is a door he is already inside.

[calm] When the affliction comes — as it does — it finds no one to afflict: the bow and the Lord are not two, [pause] and where they are not two, nectar falls.

[relaxed] Others do vows, gifts, cold baths; his scripture drips nectar and cools the heat of becoming where he kneels.

[slowly] Then, surprisingly, the bow begins to dance — consciousness singing over him — [long pause] and the world's desert-heat drops like a coat.

[warm] Abhinavagupta dates it: dark tenth of Pauṣa. [long pause] Signed, sealed, cooled.
